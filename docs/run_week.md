# Run-week prompt

Use this when handing a regular-season week to the runner (Claude Code; see `CLAUDE.md`).

```text
Run Week [N].

Use the repository's one-command Run Week workflow in AGENTS.md.
Resolve all internal readiness, migration/reset, background-team input, statbook, standings, snapshot and PR prerequisites yourself.
Do not ask me to edit manifests, run helper scripts, research rosters, touch Railway or perform intermediate setup.

Plan:
[From 2014 Week 6: "my Game Plan page is filled in" (or attach the .md or .txt file with its headings). Before Week 6: paste Stone's weekly plan, or write "use the already-closed ex-ante plan" for an authorized rerun]

Run Week [N] only and stop before Week [N+1].
```

The 2013 Week 1 restart (generation 3 under kernel 2013.4) is closed history: see [career/2013/migrations/week_01_kernel_2013_4_restart.md](../career/2013/migrations/week_01_kernel_2013_4_restart.md) (ledger Entry 35).

A current week needs these inputs from Stone before `python scripts/build_week_inputs.py N --season YEAR` can freeze the slate (from 2014 Week 6 `apply_game_plan.py` writes the first and the last from his Game Plan page; see the order below): the week's plan frozen as `call_sheet.json` at `SeasonPaths(YEAR).call_sheet(N)` (2014: `data/2014/Regular_Season/Games/Week_NN/call_sheet.json`; 2013: `career/2013/regular_season/week_NN_<away>_at_<home>/call_sheet.json`), without which the builder blocks; any open role decision the plan must settle, such as the weekly center evaluation; and the game-day inactives, written to `game_day_inactives` in `SeasonPaths(YEAR).depth_chart` (2014: `data/2014/Team_Operations/Team/Depth_Chart/game_depth_chart.json`; 2013: `career/2013/depth_chart.json`), where a week without a new list reuses the last one. Under kernel 2014.6 the builder reads three parts of the call sheet: `offensive_call_sheet` (required), `decisions` (Stone's standing two-point, kickoff and optional fourth-down blocks; required under 2014.6, which decides two-point tries and onside kicks) and `defensive_call_sheet` (optional, records only). The [games and statistical evidence](update_workflow.md#games-and-their-statistical-evidence) section of the update workflow gives the commands that follow. The private snapshot stays on the last merged canonical state until the public PR is merged; only a merged `main` checkout may advance it.

The [decision queue](../state/decision_queue.json) holds a week until Stone's decisions for it are recorded ([career layer](career_layer.md)). `build_week_inputs.py` and `close_week.py --close` (with or without `--continue`) refuse with exit 1 while an open item names the week or a part of its committed call sheet; the dry run of `close_week.py` lists the same items as notes. Only an item owned by Stone becomes a question for the user. When a week closes, the closing commit opens the `weekly_game_plan` item and, unless a standing instruction covers them, the `game_day_inactives` item for the next week in which Jacksonville plays; a bye week gets none. The week's packet resolves them `by: "user"`. An open item that still holds a closed week fails validation. Stone's standing fourth-down rule is in force and is frozen into each week's call sheet as its `decisions.fourth_down` block, which the game applies at the actual fourth-down distance and spot; no fourth-down item is opened for a week or carried to the next. Neither the queue nor Document 5 changes while a game is paused.

From 2014 Week 6 the week's result page is the Game Report (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).game_report(N)`, `Game_Report.md` in the week's folder; Weeks 1 to 5 keep `output.md`), written from the [Game Report template](../foundation/templates/regular_season_output_template.md). After the close: fill its box score with `render_box_score.py --season YEAR --write <Game_Report.md>`, bring the roster record up to the game, run `render_week_pages.py --season YEAR` (the Games and league-results indexes, the league-results week page, the injury report, the medical history's game entry and the new incident records), write the highlights under the league-results page's game headings, then `render_annual_record.py YEAR`. The [update workflow](update_workflow.md#games-and-their-statistical-evidence) gives the details.

## Generated-data rule

Every club dresses at most 46 players. Jacksonville's inactives are Stone's list in `game_day_inactives` of `SeasonPaths(YEAR).depth_chart`; a background club's are chosen mechanically from the bottom of its depth order (`runtime.week_inputs.game_day_actives`), never from a real historical inactive list, and the weekly gate rejects a TeamInput with more than 46 actives.

The one-command workflow keeps large reconstructed TeamInputs in the gitignored `.sim_cache/` workspace. Jacksonville/protagonist games preserve full snap/stat receipts; ordinary background games preserve `compact_stats` receipts with complete nonzero generated statistics but no background snap ledger. The runner must use these bounded-storage paths automatically rather than failing the task because the generated Git diff is too large.

**Season routing:** use the requested NFL season for YEAR, including January/February postseason games. Weekly input, closure, box-score and league-award commands require `--season YEAR`; readiness defaults to `docs/repository_map.json` active_season and can be checked explicitly with `--season YEAR`. Current record ownership follows that map separately. The 2014 release requirements remain blocking even when legacy readiness flags are VERIFIED.

## The game week from 2014 Week 6: briefing, plan, check, apply, build, close, report

Every game week runs through three pages in the week's folder (`runtime/decisions.py` names them; `runtime.decisions.week_pages` finds them through the folder map) and these steps, in this order. Opening or building a page never moves the clock or creates an event.

1. **Briefing.** When the week opens (after the previous game closes), `python scripts/render_game_week.py` issues the next Jacksonville game's `Game_Week_Briefing.md`, a blank `Game_Plan.md` and the week index (`README.md`), from stored facts only. The staff read is stored as `staff_read.json` (author, date, text) in the week's data folder (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).week_data(N)`) by whoever prepares the week; until then the briefing says "Staff read to come". Whenever the situation changes (a medical exam, a transaction, a settled decision), `render_game_week.py --check` says a new briefing is due and `render_game_week.py` issues it, keeping the old one beside it as what he saw (`Game_Week_Briefing_October_5.md`). The Game Plan is never rewritten by the builder.
2. **Plan.** Stone fills `Game_Plan.md` in place, or uploads a .md or .txt file with the same headings (`--file PATH` on the check or the apply places it as the page). Any section may say "Same as Week N"; the three decision rules may say "Standing rule", which freezes the rule in force exactly: the two-point and kickoff rules from the frozen block their instruction's record points at, the fourth-down rule from the `decisions.fourth_down` block stored once on its instruction in `state/current_job.json` (`block`; a kick is the line of scrimmage plus the bound fourth-down artifact's field-goal snap offset, so the field-goal line is the opponent's 34), with Tice's first call on each matching menu filled from the week's own calls (`prepared_calls: first-call-on-each-matching-menu`, `runtime.game_plan.first_calls`).
3. **Check.** `python scripts/check_game_plan.py N --season YEAR` says exactly what is missing or invalid, in football words, and exits 1 until the page is complete (the inactives count and roster, the depth changes, the calls against the active playbook, the call sheet under the existing validators, the clean-page rule, a played week, a briefing the situation has overtaken). Open items with others and exams still to come are listed as notes.
4. **Apply.** `python scripts/apply_game_plan.py N --season YEAR` runs the check, then writes exactly what the game path reads: the frozen `call_sheet.json` (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).call_sheet(N)`, indent 1, its defensive rows citing the page's own lines), the game depth chart's `game_day_inactives` and any order or role change in both chart copies with one update-log entry, and the week's game-plan and inactives items in the decision queue resolved by the user with the page as their record. It rebuilds the club pages, the career views and the week index. The page stays as the record of his decision.
5. **Build inputs.** `python scripts/build_week_inputs.py N --season YEAR`, unchanged: it reads the call sheet and the chart as before.
6. **Close.** `python scripts/close_week.py N --season YEAR --close`, unchanged.
7. **Report.** The Game Report (`Game_Report.md`) takes the place of `output.md` from Week 6; Weeks 1 to 5 keep `output.md` as written. After it is written, `python scripts/render_game_week.py --week N` rebuilds that week's index to link it, and the next week's briefing is issued (step 1).

Repository validation checks the open week once its briefing is issued: its index current, its Game Plan beside the briefing and its staff read well formed (`render_game_week.validation_errors`). The round trip is proved by `tests/test_game_plan.py`: Week 5's frozen call sheet and inactives, written as a Game Plan page and applied in a temporary copy, give a byte-identical Week 5 `call_sheet.json` and the same inactives.
