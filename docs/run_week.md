# Run-week prompt

Use this when handing a regular-season week to the runner (Claude Code; see `CLAUDE.md`).

```text
Run Week [N].

Use the repository's one-command Run Week workflow in AGENTS.md.
Resolve all internal readiness, migration/reset, background-team input, statbook, standings, snapshot and PR prerequisites yourself.
Do not ask me to edit manifests, run helper scripts, research rosters, touch Railway or perform intermediate setup.

Plan:
[From 2014 Week 6: "my Game Plan page is filled in" (or attach the .md or .txt file with its headings). Before Week 6: paste Stone's weekly plan, or write "use the already-closed ex-ante plan" for an authorized rerun]

Run Week [N] only and stop before Week [N+1].
```

Under kernel 2014.6 the builder reads three parts of the call sheet: `offensive_call_sheet` (required), `decisions` (Stone's standing two-point, kickoff and optional fourth-down blocks; required under 2014.6, which decides two-point tries and onside kicks) and `defensive_call_sheet` (optional, records only). The [games and statistical evidence](update_workflow.md#games-and-their-statistical-evidence) section of the update workflow gives the commands that follow. The private snapshot stays on the last merged canonical state until the public PR is merged; only a merged `main` checkout may advance it.

The [decision queue](../state/decision_queue.json) holds a week until Stone's decisions for it are recorded ([career layer](career_layer.md)). `build_week_inputs.py` and `close_week.py --close` (with or without `--continue`) refuse with exit 1 while an open item names the week or a part of its committed call sheet; the dry run of `close_week.py` lists the same items as notes. Only an item owned by Stone becomes a question for the user. When a week opens, `python scripts/open_game_week.py` opens the `weekly_game_plan` item and the `game_day_inactives` item for the next week in which Jacksonville plays, from the schedule and the standing instructions (the healthy-inactive rule covers the healthy inactives only, so the inactives item records why it is still Stone's in `standing_not_applied`); a bye week gets none, nothing is opened twice and nothing while a game is paused. `render_game_week.py` runs it before the week's first briefing, so the briefing and the desk list them, and validation fails an issued briefing whose week has no game-plan item. Until 2014 Week 5 the closing commit opened them by hand; from Week 6 the applied Game Plan resolves them `by: "user"` with the page as their record, applied once no exam the items wait on is still to come (the inactives item waits on every reassessment and projected return the medical entries name before the game). An open item that still holds a closed week fails validation. Stone's standing fourth-down rule is in force and is frozen into each week's call sheet as its `decisions.fourth_down` block, which the game applies at the actual fourth-down distance and spot; no fourth-down item is opened for a week or carried to the next. Neither the queue nor Document 5 changes while a game is paused.

From 2014 Week 6 the week's result page is the Game Report (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).game_report(N)`, `Game_Report.md` in the week's folder; Weeks 1 to 5 keep `output.md`), written from the [Game Report template](../foundation/templates/game_week/game_report_template.md) (game week version 2: Coach record, sections 1 to 7, then Full game statistics with the two box-score markers last) under the [game-output workflow](game_output_workflow.md) and the [head-coach media method](head_coach_media_method.md); Weeks 1 to 5 keep the layout of the [regular-season output template](../foundation/templates/regular_season_output_template.md). The template's markers name no game yet (`{{EVENT_ID}}`, `{{TEAM_NAME}}`): once the game has closed, the rebuild (or `render_box_score.py --season YEAR --write <Game_Report.md>`) names them from the week's closed Jacksonville game and fills the block, and validation (`runtime/game_report.py`) holds the report to the template's section order, its box score to the end of the page and its final score, line score, overtime and record to the game book. After the close: bring the roster record up to the game, draw the week's awards (`league_awards.py week N --season YEAR --close`), write the week's in-season roster moves page with its event (below), then run **`python scripts/rebuild_pages.py --season YEAR`**, which fills the Game Report's box score and rebuilds every built page in order (see "Every built page, one command" below). Store the week's highlights and reactive events in the league-results page's data twin (`data/YEAR/Regular_Season/League_Results/week_NN.json`: `games` keyed by event id, `reactive_events`), which the page renders under its game headings; the page itself is never edited by hand, and a rebuild replaces anything written on it. `python scripts/rebuild_pages.py --season YEAR --check` then reports nothing. The [update workflow](update_workflow.md#games-and-their-statistical-evidence) gives the details.

## Every built page, one command

`python scripts/rebuild_pages.py [--season YEAR] [--check]` rebuilds every page a builder owns, each a clean page with no system words, in this order: the standings (`render_standings.py`), the statistics with the statistics and records indexes (`render_season_stats.py`), the team tracker (`render_team_tracker.py`), the award pages from the recorded results (`render_award_pages.py`; nothing is drawn), the contract and cap pages from the permanent contract records (`render_contract_pages.py`), the player pages, the player ages, the club pages (team, roster, depth chart and its change history), the coach pages, the cap views, each resolved event's result page (`resolve_event.py`) and each applied roster move's Transaction page and signing records, each game page's box score where stale (`render_box_score.py`), the game-week pages (each played week's index, with its other business and a recorded week's practice table, then the open week's Game Week Briefing, blank Game Plan and index, `render_game_week.py`), the bye pages while the bye is open (`render_bye_week.py`), the week pages (the Games and league-results indexes and week pages, the injury report, the medical history's game entry and the incident records, `render_week_pages.py`), the in-season roster moves pages (`render_inseason_rails.py`), the trade pages (the folder navigation and the offer log's index of calls, `render_trade_pages.py`), the league's head-coach change pages (`render_coaching_changes.py`), the desk with its season summary and the calendar (`render_career_views.py`), the preseason statistics and last the annual record (`Record.md`). Each page has exactly one builder; the command only calls them. Write mode writes only what differs, never plays a game, draws an award, moves the clock or settles a decision, and exits 1 naming any page a builder refuses (one that would break the clean-page rule, or the career views while a game is paused). `--check` writes nothing and lists every page that is missing, stale, hand-edited or unclean.

The week's awards are drawn first (`league_awards.py week N --season YEAR --close`, which also renders the announcement pages), and the week's in-season roster moves page is written by the week's batch with its event-record line (`render_inseason_rails.py --season YEAR --week N --write --event JSON`); the rebuild keeps an event line a page already carries and writes a missing page without one. The statistical band audit the runner reads is in the data area: `data/YEAR/Regular_Season/Statistics/records/calibration_audit.md`.

## Generated-data rule

Every club dresses at most 46 players. Jacksonville's inactives are Stone's list in `game_day_inactives` of `SeasonPaths(YEAR).depth_chart`; a background club's are chosen mechanically from the bottom of its depth order (`runtime.week_inputs.game_day_actives`), never from a real historical inactive list, and the weekly gate rejects a TeamInput with more than 46 actives.

The one-command workflow keeps large reconstructed TeamInputs in the gitignored `.sim_cache/` workspace. Jacksonville/protagonist games preserve full snap/stat receipts; ordinary background games preserve `compact_stats` receipts with complete nonzero generated statistics but no background snap ledger. The runner must use these bounded-storage paths automatically rather than failing the task because the generated Git diff is too large.

**Season routing:** use the requested NFL season for YEAR, including January/February postseason games. Weekly input, closure, box-score and league-award commands require `--season YEAR`; readiness defaults to `docs/repository_map.json` active_season and can be checked explicitly with `--season YEAR`. Current record ownership follows that map separately. The 2014 release requirements remain blocking even when legacy readiness flags are VERIFIED.

## The game week from 2014 Week 6: briefing, plan, check, apply, practice week and game day, build, close, report

Every game week runs through three pages in the week's folder (`runtime/decisions.py` names them; `runtime.decisions.week_pages` finds them through the folder map) and these steps, in this order. Opening or building a page never moves the clock or creates an event.

A playoff game runs through the same three pages and steps (game week version 2, Phase C). `runtime/game_weeks.py` finds Jacksonville's game in the bracket that `runtime/postseason.schedule` builds from the closed games only, seated on the season's postseason dates (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).postseason_slots`, researched in two passes like every calendar; the 2014 file is still to be researched, and until it is on file no playoff game is set): the Wild Card games once every regular-season game has closed, each later round once every game of the round before it has closed. The pages are written to the round's folder in the postseason folder (`Postseason/Games/Week_18` for the Wild Card, then 19, 20 and 21, through the folder map), and the call sheet to its data twin, where the weekly build reads it. A first-round bye has no pages, no opponent is named before the bracket sets it, and nothing is opened after a loss. The briefing's section 1 gives the playoff game's own facts in place of the regular season's standings: the round and both seeds, the site, each club's rest since its last game (a first-round bye named as one), what a win and a loss mean, any earlier meeting this season, the 2014-era overtime rule (no tie; modified sudden death in 15-minute periods) and the game-day limit. The Game Plan may carry a regular-season week's choices ("Baseline: Carry Week 17") or an earlier round's ("Baseline: Carry Week 18").

1. **Briefing.** When the week opens (after the previous game closes), `python scripts/render_game_week.py` opens the week's two decision items (`scripts/open_game_week.py`, above), then issues the next Jacksonville game's `Game_Week_Briefing.md`, a blank `Game_Plan.md` and the week index (`README.md`), from stored facts only. The briefing follows game week version 2 ([docs/game_week_v2.md](game_week_v2.md); the layout of `foundation/templates/game_week/game_week_briefing_template.md`, sections 1 to 8): the game and its stakes from the standings, the last game's self-scout from the statistics owner, only the active-roster players under a restriction or with an exam to come (a reserve-list player only when he may practise or return before the game) with the game-day numbers and the medical authority, the opponent with and without the ball from the game records and the rosters public by the issue date, the kicking game and game management, the staff proposals, and what is owed by him with his standing directions and what the club decides. Every authored part (the staff's central question, the two things to carry forward, each coordinator's recommendation and questions, the special-teams report, the contingencies and the proposals) is stored as text with its author and date in `staff_briefing.json` in the week's data folder (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).week_data(N)`, `runtime.decisions.WeekPages.staff_texts`) by whoever prepares the week; code never writes one, a missing required part renders "To come", an optional one (what the game book does not settle, what is not established, an operational question) is left out, and validation holds each text to its form, the clean-page rule and the figures the page shows. The events inside the week (an injury replacement, a trade call, another club's head-coach change; `runtime/open_events.py`) reach the briefing as inserts under its header lines, before section 1: ACTION REQUIRED for an answer he owes, FOR YOUR INFORMATION for what was settled since the issue before, COMING UP for a dated window with nothing to answer (`runtime/event_inserts.py`, the layout of `foundation/templates/event_inserts/brief_action_inserts_template.md`); each is left out when empty, so a briefing with no inserts keeps its bytes. COMING UP shows the season's dated windows (`runtime/deadlines.py`), each holding nothing and asking nothing: the trading deadline, from the season's league dates (`league_dates.json` in the season's schedule data, sourced; 2014: Tuesday, October 28, 4 p.m. ET), in the briefings of the last Jacksonville game before it, from its first issue, and in any briefing issued after that game and before the deadline, with the trade calls then open or "No proposal is on record."; and each player's eligibility windows from the roster record's availability note ("no practice before October 20, no return before November 3"), in the briefings issued before each date. A season without its league dates shows no deadline. An issued briefing keeps the windows its issue recorded, and a change to the windows waits for the next scheduled issue. A briefing is issued again only under the one reissue rule (`docs/event_inserts.md`, section A, item 6; `event_inserts.reissue_reason`): (a) an answer the current issue does not show (a new event that needs his answer, or a game-day depth or inactives change); (b) a dated finding recorded after the issue that changes a choice still pending (a call, new terms, a counter or a medical entry on a pending event; or a medical exam, a transaction, a decision settled by someone else or a staff text that changes the briefing's own sections while the week's decisions are open; the passing of an exam's date with no recorded entry is not a finding); or (c) an answer due before the next scheduled issue that the current issue does not show. News and windows wait for the next scheduled issue; Stone's own answers never reissue it. Then `render_game_week.py --check` says a new briefing is due and `render_game_week.py` issues it, keeping the old one beside it as what he saw (`Game_Week_Briefing_October_5.md`, a later issue of the same day `Game_Week_Briefing_October_5_2.md`); the new one's status line names the briefing it replaces. A briefing is rebuilt as of its own issue (its date and the events stamped before it), so a later event never makes an issued briefing stale. Each issue is entered in the week's issue record (`briefings.json` in the week's data folder: the date, the revision, that is the n-th issue of that day, the file and the sha256 of the page as it went out; from step 5 also its time `at`, the one-rule `reason` it was issued again for, and the `inserts` it carried, each notice with its section, digest and an answer's due time), which owns the issue dates and revisions; from the second issue of a day the pages name it with its revision ("October 5, 2014, revision 2"), so two issues the same day are never mistaken for each other; validation holds every briefing, earlier ones included, to it, so a briefing is never edited by hand. Stone settling the week's own items on his Game Plan is his answer to the briefing, not a change of situation: the briefing is compared as it was before he set them, and a briefing issued later for another reason shows them "Set on your Game Plan". The Game Plan is never rewritten by the builder.
2. **Plan.** Stone fills `Game_Plan.md` in place, or uploads a .md or .txt file with the same headings (`--file PATH` on the check or the apply places it as the page; an upload for a week that is played, paused or already built for the game is refused before anything is written). The page is game week version 2's (`foundation/templates/game_week/game_plan_template.md`, sections 1 to 7, with two added fixed lines: "Opening ideas" in section 3 and "Rush rule" in section 4; a page written before October 20, 2026 with "Opening calls, in order" reads the same): every entry is one labelled line or a table row in football words, with no Opener, Returns, Family, Type or Tags column and no row id (his own direction, a line that never compiles, may run on below its label in further lines or bullets), and every line must be answered: No change keeps the last legal instruction set, None means intentionally nothing, and a line left empty is unanswered (silence never approves anything). "Baseline: Carry Week N" starts from that week's calls and defensive responses as set; the Primary calls table adds or changes calls (each by its full name, as the active book and the earlier frozen sheets name it: a shorter or partial name is asked about, "did you mean ...?", and never resolved to a longer call that would add a route, a tag or a protection; its situations in "When I want it", a variation in parentheses), "Calls not available" and the special plays remove them (a call taken off leaves a carried opening, the calls after it moving up; a call kept under Special plays cannot also be not available). Any call in the active book may be named for any situation he writes, whether or not that week's practice covered it, an earlier sheet carried it or the staff carded it (the user's ruling of October 20, 2026: "We can call anything from the playbook. It's accessible."): a call no earlier Jacksonville sheet carries is read from its own words (formation, concept and protection), one whose words a longer call's name holds is written "New call: ..." once the check has asked, and a special play may be named for any situation (a Special plays line sets the status of a call on the sheet, Keep, Remove or Practice only; a call it names that is not yet on the sheet is set in the Primary calls table with the situations he wants it for, and the check says so). Only a call from a book whose range starts after the season is refused (the playbook lock). "Opening ideas" lists the calls he wants early, in his order of preference, and any call in the book stays available: an idea not yet on the week's sheet is added to it with the opening sequence as its only situation (an earlier sheet's form when one carries it, otherwise read from its own words), and a call the page takes off is asked about, never added back. Today the order only fills the call sheet's opening slots, which name the early calls in the play-by-play and are left out of the outcome packet, so reordering the list does not move a draw; which calls are on the list (through the "opener" tag it sets), like every call on the sheet, is committed to the outcome packet and changes the game's random draws, with no football effect. The plan-level version (game week version 2's Phase D, awaiting the user's timing) will use the list by situation. Section 6, What we will practice, is information for the staff (what to practice, who takes a limited player's work) and never a condition on a call or on the plan. The defensive table's first cell names a situation the defense keys, a conditional ("If" or "When"), a counter or a presentation, and its response is read in the active defensive book's words. The three decision rules take Standing rule, Carry Week N, No change, None (fourth downs only: his live call) or "My rules, otherwise ..." above a rule table. "Standing rule" freezes the rule in force exactly: the two-point and kickoff rules from the frozen block their instruction's record points at, the fourth-down rule from the `decisions.fourth_down` block stored once on its instruction in `state/current_job.json` (`block`; a kick is the line of scrimmage plus the bound fourth-down artifact's field-goal snap offset, so the field-goal line is the opponent's 34), with Tice's first call on each matching menu filled from the week's own calls (`prepared_calls: first-call-on-each-matching-menu`, `runtime.game_plan.first_calls`), and the inactives as every player out for the game plus the deepest healthy reserve of each of the five largest healthy position groups, with the others he names after it when the week sits more healthy players than five (the briefing's ask says how many the rule leaves to him; when it sits fewer than five, a tie decides the fifth group or a unit would be short, the check says so and he names them all). His reasons, what he wants the game to look like, the stop conditions and the staff directions are author intent: kept on the page and in the applied record, never in the call sheet, so rewording them compiles to the same bytes. The page names the legal choices under each heading: the roster, the calls of the last sheet, the active books' words (`runtime/offensive_words.py` holds the offensive book's, `runtime/defensive_calls.py` the defensive book's) and the call sheet's situations.
3. **Check.** `python scripts/check_game_plan.py N --season YEAR` says exactly what is missing or invalid, in football words, and exits 1 until the page is complete; once it is, it shows back the calls on each situation's list. It refuses a week that is played, a game that is paused, and a week whose inputs or results are already built from the applied plan. It checks every line answered, the briefing he answers is the current one (its date, and its revision from the second issue of a day, on "Briefing used"), each caller line against the staff page, the inactives (the count, the roster, every player out for the game as the game reads it: his availability note or a closed game's injury inside its projected return; an unreadable note is a problem; when more are out than the count, those players only), the depth and role changes (a role goes to a player of its group and the starter, kicker and punter head it; the defensive-back seats stay valid; every role holder, new or carried, dresses), the standing inactive rule read on the chart as the page's own reorder leaves it, the compile (`runtime.game_plan.compile_sheet`: each call resolves to exactly one prepared form, or the check asks which; a new call read from its own words; an opening idea not yet on the sheet added with the opening sequence as its situation), every word of every call on the sheet against the active offensive playbook (concept, personnel, formation, motion, protection, name and tags), each call's personnel grouping against the players who dress and a variation naming a player against his dressing, a "Carry Week N" or No change that would carry no rule the game needs (and, with the fourth-down rule in force, none silently), the call sheet under the existing validators and the inputs' policy check, the clean-page rule, a heading the page does not have, and a briefing the one reissue rule has overtaken (a briefing reissued only to add or change its notices at the top leaves his answers standing: the check notes that the briefing has been reissued with new notices and his answers stand; a reissue whose own sections changed still needs "Briefing used" brought up to date). Open items with others and exams still to come are listed as notes. Nothing waits on a practice record. The complete page shows back its opening ideas in his order of preference and the calls on each situation's list. A complete page that has changed since it was applied reports "not yet set" and exits 1 until it is applied again.
4. **Apply, and commit it on its own.** `python scripts/apply_game_plan.py N --season YEAR` runs the check and, while either of the week's items still waits on an exam dated that day or later (its `waits_on`; the inactives item waits on every reassessment and projected return the medical entries name before the game), sets nothing: it prints `GAME PLAN: COMPLETE, NOT YET SET` with the exams, so a list written before them is checked against their results (silence never settles it; the call sheet is never frozen without the settled plan item). Once they are held it writes exactly what the game path reads: the frozen `call_sheet.json` (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).call_sheet(N)`, indent 1, its defensive rows citing the page's own lines), the game depth chart's `game_day_inactives` and any order or role change in both chart copies with one update-log entry (a starter, kicker or punter heads his group; a moved player's stale receiver or defensive-back label is cleared by a dated entry), the week's game-plan and inactives items in the decision queue resolved by the user with the page as their record, and the applied plan record `applied_plan.json` beside the call sheet (the page's and the sheet's digests, the inactives, the depth changes and the chart before them, the source map from every emitted call, defensive row and decision to the page line or the carried frozen item it came from, his intent with its lines and the day it was known, and the event of the apply, whose event line the week index carries and the annual record lists). The page stays as the record of his decision; validation fails while the page or the call sheet differs from what the record froze. **Commit the applied plan (the page, the call sheet, both charts, the update log, the queue, the record and the rebuilt pages) on its own, before the inputs are built**: validation requires the call sheet's freeze commit to come before the commit that adds the game's receipts (`validate_repository.freeze_order_errors`; a sheet committed with the receipts fails as well).

The mid-week record.** When an event inside the week (an injury replacement, a trade call, another club's head-coach change; `runtime/open_events.py`) is raised, answered or resolved between the week's opening and its game day, the runner records it in one commit: the event through `scripts/raise_event.py` (or its answer or resolution), with Document 5's master-date line in the mid-week form at the time of the record (step 5), its section 9 and checkpoint moved to the event's closed event line when one is written, and Document 4 when the roster changes. 5. **Practice week and game day.** Stone's standing practice instruction (the user's choice of October 23, 2026, "Spread the pads (Recommended)"; built as a job-record standing instruction in step 5 of the [rebuild plan](rebuild_plan.md)) sets the practice week: the Weeks 1 to 5 week shape (a Monday walkthrough, by the user's answer of October 24, 2026, "Monday walkthrough (Recommended)"; Tuesday the players' day off; Wednesday, Thursday and Friday practices; Saturday walkthrough); pads on the Wednesday of Weeks 7 and 9, and of Weeks 13, 15 and 17; helmets only otherwise; and Monday, November 24, 2014 an added day off in place of that week's walkthrough, so November holds the four the CBA requires (bye days not counted).
