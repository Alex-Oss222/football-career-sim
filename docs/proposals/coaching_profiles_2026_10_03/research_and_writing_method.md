# Research and writing method

Prepared October 3, 2026, for the three Alex Stone document proposals. Football evidence ends September 28, 2014. This is an editorial companion, not a new part of the career.

## What needed to change

The first proposals made the reader watch somebody constructing a document. Prompts, sample labels, numbered competencies and repeated cautions interrupted the football. The same narrator stood outside all three subjects explaining what evidence could establish. The drafts were detailed, but their detail often answered an editor's question rather than the reader's.

The revision changes what each document pays attention to. The profile follows Stone's judgment and conduct. The dossier examines his suitability for a head-coaching job. The performance review follows ownership's continuing assessment of its football operation. All three can discuss quarterback development, but they should have different reasons for doing so.

| Subject | Coaching profile | GM dossier | Owner's review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterback | What Stone hears, teaches, permits and changes | Which quarterback work he actually did and how it transfers to this job | Whether the club's quarterback is improving enough to justify continued confidence |
| Assistants | How he works with them and where he intervenes | Who he has hired, supervised and replaced; whom a new club could actually obtain | Whether the staff delivers the work the club is relying on |
| Scheme | His preferred answers and the circumstances that change them | What those answers require from the proposed roster | Whether the available players are being used effectively |
| Results | Usually outside the continuing portrait | Evidence attached to the role he held | Central to the judgment, in the context of the starting team and expectations |
| Difficulties | Established habits under pressure, including unresolved contradictions | Specific cases for corroboration and unanswered hiring questions | Problems that return, corrections that hold, and responsibility for both |

These are three populated templates. Their Stone text can be read as proposed finished documents. Reusable instructions stay here so the reader does not keep leaving the portrait or assessment to fill out a form.

## Research basis

Public NFL accounts are useful evidence of what coaches, GMs and owners say they consider. They are incomplete accounts of private processes, often published by their own clubs. They do not establish a universal internal NFL document or prove a decision was correct. The proposed formats are editorial designs informed by those accounts.

Later sources can inform the writing method. They cannot put later events, available coaches or hindsight into Stone's career. External research establishes nothing new about Stone's personality. His approved profile supplies that material; the career records supply what happened.

### Coaches explaining their work

**Andy Reid, April 18, 2014.** In [Chiefs Unplugged](https://www.chiefs.com/news/chiefs-unplugged-head-coach-andy-reid-12892089), Reid discusses being himself, keeping presentations concise, allowing individuality within team rules and continuing to adapt. The useful feature is the connection between outlook and ordinary work. For Stone, retain the established humor, open discussion and specific corrections. Do not borrow Reid's possessions, sayings or personal history to decorate another character.

**Reid and Patrick Mahomes, October 3, 2018.** The [Chiefs' account of their sideline conversations](https://www.chiefs.com/news/chiefs-notebook-andy-reid-s-in-game-conversations-with-patrick-mahomes-have-paid) describes the quarterback supplying information about what he sees and discussing calls with the coach. That is a useful model for specificity: a conversation changes the football available on the next possession. This is a later editorial reference. It is not a Stone event or dialogue to reproduce.

**Bill Belichick, January 27, 2004.** His [media-day transcript](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-media-day-transcript-135956) links fourth-down choices to the team, opponent and available play. It also discusses special teams through changing situations and individual responsibilities. The profile can therefore describe Stone's considerations without inventing a universal fourth-down threshold or a full specialist manual.

**Belichick, July 30, 2014.** His [camp transcript](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-7-30-this-is-where-we-build-our-fundamentals-199976) discusses James Develin's usefulness across varying opportunities and decisions made with incomplete practice information. A coach's treatment of a reserve and his uncertainty can reveal as much as a preferred scheme. Stone's established attention to role players belongs in the portrait even when it produces no headline statistic.

### What a GM investigates

**Dick Cass on Baltimore's 2008 search.** [Cass's account](https://www.baltimoreravens.com/news/behind-the-scenes-with-dick-cass-7748197) describes calls to coaches, scouts, front-office personnel and former players. Harbaugh's second interview covered assistants, camp, practice, discipline, difficult players and offseason participation. This supports a dossier concerned with running the entire team and corroborating claims. A polished philosophy and a famous coaching lineage do not complete that investigation. The account also recognizes the hiring organization's obligation to support its choice.

**Bill Polian, January 4, 2018.** In the [Colts' interview](https://www.colts.com/news/bill-polian-goes-behind-the-scenes-in-colts-search-for-next-head-coach-20200706), including his recollection of hiring Dungy in 2002, Polian describes testing written material with questions and asking how this particular team would play and operate in the future. An attractive plan then has to reach the work that starts upon arrival. This supports opening the dossier with suitability for a job and implementation questions, ahead of biographical detail. His preferred time horizon is not an automatic employment guarantee.

**Ted Thompson, February 2006.** In his [quarterback discussion](https://www.packers.com/news/ted-thompson-discusses-packers-quarterback-situation-2451482), Thompson says McCarthy's earlier work with Favre helped but did not decide the appointment. He separates encouraging Rodgers practice evidence from evidence obtained in games. For Stone, Brees and Cousins need different treatment. Working successfully with an established veteran cannot stand in for proof of developing an inexperienced starter.

**Mike Tomlin, September 22, 2009.** In [this interview](https://www.steelers.com/news/hearing-from-coach-mike-tomlin-956725), Tomlin discusses assistants as teachers, communicating with different players, contributions from junior coaches, delegation and intervention. That directs the dossier toward how work reaches players. Stone's choice of a quarterbacks coach and his supervision of development time matter alongside his own instruction.

**Marc Trestman's staff assembly, January 17, 2013.** The [Bears' contemporary report](https://www.chicagobears.com/news/trestman-in-process-of-assembling-coaching-staff-9383588) records completed hires, Marinelli declining to remain and Montreal assistants whose decisions were still unresolved. A proposed staff needs availability and alternatives. Stone's current coordinators are not automatically available to another employer.

**Phil Emery, January 20, 2013.** The [account of his selection criteria](https://www.chicagobears.com/news/marc-trestman-met-all-of-phil-emery-s-criteria-for-job-9395800) explains why he thought previous team leadership and adaptation to unfamiliar circumstances would transfer to Chicago. The relevant lesson is to explain the transfer. An interviewer's rationale does not guarantee that the appointment will succeed.

**Jeffrey Lurie and Chip Kelly, January 17, 2013.** Their [introductory press conference](https://www.philadelphiaeagles.com/news/chairman-ceo-jeffrey-lurie-and-head-coach-chip-kelly-9382647) includes Kelly investigating the organization and deferring quarterback conclusions until staff assembly and evaluation. Hiring has obligations on both sides. A candidate's plan depends on personnel authority, resources, expectations and people willing to join. Those matters can be examined without inventing demands Stone has never made.

### What ownership watches

**John Mara, December 30, 2013.** In the [Giants' full transcript](https://www.giants.com/news/giants-president-john-mara-reviews-season-12320466), Mara acknowledges that his own optimistic roster assessment was wrong. He retains Coughlin while questioning offensive performance, personnel decisions, depth and the delay in giving Jernigan an opportunity. He values the locker room holding together without treating that as a repair for the offense. This supports an owner voice that examines the GM and ownership's judgment as well as the coach.

**Steve Bisciotti and Ozzie Newsome, January 8, 2014.** Their [annual review](https://www.baltimoreravens.com/news/ravens-2013-season-review-press-conference-12396601) connects coaching, roster construction and contracts. Bisciotti acknowledges the difficulty of separating line, back and quarterback responsibility, and explains why a longer successful record affects his response to a poor season. The proposed owner can require a credible diagnosis without pretending to be the best technician in the building. Stone has one completed Jacksonville season, not Baltimore's preceding five-year case for continuity.

**Art Rooney II, January 8, 2014.** His [season assessment](https://www.steelers.com/news/rooney-ii-on-tomlin-ben-free-agency-12392100) distinguishes a poor start from later improvement and returns to quarterback protection raised in earlier years. An owner remembers. Stone's 2014 assessment should revisit protection, turnovers and development follow-through rather than replace them with a fresh set of generic categories.

**Shad Khan, March 19, 2013.** The [historical Jaguars interview](https://www.jaguars.com/news/khan-this-is-the-right-approach-9744637) discusses patience, spending that did not produce results and whether football leadership acts consistently with its account of the job. This informs the perspective, but the interview concerns real Jacksonville after this career diverged. It is not evidence that Khan made those statements about Stone.

**Michael Bidwill, December 31, 2012.** The [Cardinals' announcement](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/ken-whisenhunt-rod-graves-relieved-of-duties-9248504) connects several years of results and unresolved offensive problems with changes at both coach and GM. It also addresses actual candidates and hiring order. Replacing a coach creates another concrete football decision. A review should not invent an obviously superior available replacement to make dismissal sound easy.

### Writing character and judgment

**Lauren Kessler, 2023.** [Character: The beating heart of narrative](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2023/05/11/profiles-character-development-reporting-sources/) emphasizes reporting actions, speech and context while rejecting invented thoughts. Applied here, the useful material is already present: which suggestions Stone hears, what he asks on film, how he treats a quiet player and when he interrupts an assistant. Labels such as approachable or demanding become unnecessary when the conduct is clear.

**George Orwell, 1946.** [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/) warns against ready-made language substituting for thought. The editing question is what a sentence actually says. Replace an abstract claim about accountability with the person, mistake, explanation and resulting work when those facts exist. Keep precise football terminology. A protection slide is useful language, even when a general reader needs it explained.

**John McPhee, 2013.** [Structure](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2013/01/14/structure) describes arranging researched material and allowing juxtaposed facts to carry meaning. Applied here, the three documents should have different orders. Stone can give assistants independence and interrupt a correction without a sentence explaining how these traits reconcile.

**McPhee, 2015.** [Omission](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2015/09/14/omission) treats selection and cutting as substantive work. Comprehensive research does not require repeating all its findings in every document. A long profile can remain detailed while placing career totals elsewhere and removing redundant conclusions.

**Roy Peter Clark, 2023.** His [writing-tools essay](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2023/02/07/roy-peter-clark-writing-tools-narrative-craft/) describes collecting, focusing, selecting, ordering and revising, with attention to syntax and reading aloud. Dense football benefits from plain sentences. Sentence length can change with the work being explained; random variation is not a substitute for a clear thought.

**Clark, 2004.** [The Line Between Fact and Fiction](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2004/09/07/the-line-between-fact-and-fiction-3/) distinguishes selection from invention and requires honest treatment of reconstruction. The owner's first-person proposal must be labeled as an editorial rendering. It cannot manufacture a Khan quotation, meeting, private motive or employment decision.

These sources concern writing craft. They do not provide a proven recipe for escaping AI detection or measuring how human a document is. The standards here are readability, specificity, coherent perspective and fidelity to the established character.

## Building a profile that works beyond the examples

The profile needs enough recurring judgment for the reader to understand Stone in a situation the page does not explicitly list. That requires more than preferences for plays. His attention, relationships, limits and response to constraints must connect.

Consider his openness to an idea. The established material lets the writer follow it from the person who offers it to the clips, protection, expected defensive response and practice time needed. That sequence applies to a veteran's suggestion, a junior assistant's concern or an idea from outside the staff. No new anecdote is necessary. The profile remains broad because the conduct transfers; it remains specific because the work is football.

Use the same approach for unfamiliar situations. An injury can change available assignments. A young player may need a narrower job. A coordinator may disagree. A public question may invite Stone to blame someone. State the established way he diagnoses, consults and decides where the source supports it. Do not write a predetermined answer for every possible event. A profile should help portray him consistently while leaving the user consequential choices.

Coverage should include the offensive and defensive problems he notices; how he relates personnel to the weekly plan; protection, run fits and coverage responsibilities; special teams and game-day roster value; in-game communication and late-game judgment; teaching, practice and workload; staff authority; personnel disagreement; player relationships, discipline, health and family boundaries; and his public manner. These subjects deserve different amounts of space. His offensive attention is unusually well established. Manufacturing equal specialist detail everywhere would make the character less credible.

The preferred structure is an opening portrait, preparation, offense, defense, teaching and practice, the game, staff and front office, and public and private relationships. Combine subjects when the material reads better together. Current callers and roster orders belong in a short dated note. Updating a backfield hierarchy should not silently change his identity.

An intention is not an achieved result. His approved profile may describe a careful teacher while a season review records that a development period disappeared. Retain both. A failure of delivery is evidence for performance assessment; it does not automatically authorize rewriting his permanent character as indifferent to development.

## Making the career sheet useful to a GM

The front of the file should give a busy reader the case for investigating the candidate and the largest unresolved concern. Follow with head-coaching results, exact responsibility and the jobs inherited. Chronology, production tables and background then supply reference material. This order lets the reader understand why a credential matters before encountering several pages of credentials.

A serious hiring assessment needs the following questions answered somewhere in the file, without necessarily giving each a heading:

1. What is this vacancy asking the coach to do? Establish the quarterback situation, roster strengths, weaknesses, timeline and personnel authority before declaring a candidate suitable.
2. Which parts of his previous work were his? Separate title, weekly preparation, play-calling, supervision and final decision rights. Account for the people and talent already present.
3. Can he teach the quarterback this team has or expects to obtain? Use player work, not a list of famous quarterbacks. Identify the quarterbacks coach and distinguish practice progress from game performance.
4. What happens to the existing roster in his football? Examine roles, players who may not fit, the cost of immediate help and the time needed for development. The GM's acquisitions and the staff's use of them both matter.
5. Can he hire and supervise the necessary staff? Record actual availability, alternatives, succession and a case of intervention or restraint. Do not invent reference checks or carry current assistants into a hypothetical new job.
6. Can he lead the whole team while concentrating on his specialty? Examine practice organization, discipline, communication, game management and supervision of the other phases.
7. What happens when his initial answer fails? Use a documented case. An acknowledgment of error and a demonstrated correction are different evidence.
8. What do people who worked with him actually say? Attribute reports, note their context and leave missing accounts missing. Professional overlap is not an endorsement.
9. What would the organization need to provide? Authority, assistant hiring, roster priorities and expectations must be workable for both parties. Contract terms and current availability require separate verification.

For Stone, that places the New Orleans/Jacksonville contrast, Cousins, staff construction, lost development work, offensive adjustments and dual calling ahead of education and playing honors. Personal background remains available without being treated as a hiring score. Sensitive due diligence stays confined to what the established record actually says.

## Writing an owner who is watching

Begin with the position ownership holds and why. Then remember what the club hired the coach to do. Compare the inherited team with what followed, giving the coach credit for his work while keeping personnel decisions and ownership's commitments in view. The owner is responsible for employing the people making those decisions.

The strongest thread is a problem followed over time. A sack total raises a concern. The club asks for a diagnosis involving protection, quarterback decisions and personnel. A player describes the teaching he needs. A later report shows whether it reached him. Games then provide another test. The review should keep those stages connected without pretending the earlier problem is resolved because a corrective meeting occurred.

Results need enough detail to explain the judgment: scoring margin, offensive and defensive production, third downs, turnovers, protection, pressure, special-teams errors, damaging losses and postseason performance where available. Full film diagnosis, injury-adjusted comparisons and efficiency statistics should appear only when the record supports them. Missing data should not be filled by confident prose.

The employment assessment needs to distinguish continued employment, an extension, compensation and control. Support for one does not imply the others. Describe the actual contract and recorded decision first. Then explain the proposed current judgment and which recurring football problems could undermine it. Avoid a hot-seat percentage, manufactured win floor or automatic playoff ultimatum. No such threshold has been established for Stone.

An owner can care about fans, finances and public confidence, but those concerns require evidence. Do not fabricate ticket-sales pressure, sponsor complaints, private impatience or a payout estimate. Do not attribute every loss to the coach or excuse every failure through personnel. Ask whether the explanation and attempted correction account for what the club is seeing.

The reusable order is: present employment position; the assignment and inheritance; improvement and what produced it; the problems that survived; whether the staff delivers its work; the current update; the continuing employment judgment. Use the sections the evidence needs. A current update should preserve the dated prior decision rather than rewrite the past as if later results were already known.

## The Stone history that governs these proposals

Stone was in New Orleans in 2012. His interim appointment began April 16; the playing season ended January 13, 2013. The Saints went 12-4 and 1-1 in the playoffs in this career. He inherited a 13-3 contender with Brees and substantial continuity. The approximate production remains approximate. Real-world New Orleans's 7-9 result does not belong in his career.

Jacksonville went 2-14 in 2012 under the previous staff. Stone accepted its job January 15, 2013. The [official 2012 statistics](https://www.jaguars.com/team/stats/2012/reg), [season review](https://res.cloudinary.com/nflclubs/image/upload/jaguars/xdgabyhr51vldqffuruv.pdf), and [contemporary offensive roster review](https://www.jaguars.com/news/roster-breakdown-offense-9301393) establish the inherited baseline. The dossier contains the full comparison: 255 to 356 team points, 444 to 346 allowed, 4,788 to 5,777 net offensive yards, 50 to 38 sacks allowed, and a turnover differential worsening from minus-3 to minus-10 despite the eight-win improvement. Returning talent belongs in the account alongside the deficiencies.

The January 15, 2014 decision retained Stone under the original four-year, fully guaranteed agreement. There was no extension, raise or new authority. The recorded concerns included scoring margin, quarterback ball security and protection. No playoff condition was added. Lowry's departure was for Atlanta's head-coaching job; it was not a dismissal after poor special-teams results.

The September 28 update is four games: 3-1, 105 points scored, 82 allowed, five giveaways, six takeaways, eight sacks allowed and ten made. Opponents converted 34 of 68 third downs. Crennel called the first three defensive games; Stone took over in Week 4. Those facts support a mixed early update, not a completed second-year judgment.

Net passing subtracts sack losses. The 2013 career results remain official despite the equal-strength simulation's limits on causal claims about coaching. Week 4 does not retain Jacksonville defensive calls, offer a late two-point choice or individually document timeout decisions. Earlier off-sheet allegations were withdrawn as call-label artifacts. Those limitations belong in source notes, where they prevent unsupported criticism without taking over the football prose.

## Editing and updating the templates

Start with a separate evidence sheet: approved identity, recorded event, attributed report, calculated comparison, proposed interpretation or unknown. Those distinctions matter during verification. They do not need to appear as labels in every paragraph.

Draft with the intended reader in mind. Remove sentences about what the document is designed to do from its main body. Let the opening concern Stone, the appointment or the owner's team. Move author instructions and most source caveats to the end.

Read the draft for repeated moves. A paragraph that states a strength, immediately adds a balancing weakness and ends with a moral will sound manufactured when repeated ten times. So will a series of paragraphs beginning with he wants. Keep a preference word when it protects accuracy; change the subject when the football itself supplies a better one. Do not replace accurate language with random synonyms.

Cut explanations the reader has already earned. After an account of a GM disagreement, there is usually no need to announce that collaboration does not require agreement. After a missed development period, the next useful sentence concerns why it stopped or what happened later. Repeated significance statements slow the document while adding little.

Preserve the established contradictions. Do not add a flaw to balance every strength, resolve a tension into a lesson or turn an ordinary football procedure into a dramatic scene. No invented quotations, rituals, symbolic objects, bodily reactions or interior monologues are needed.

Finally, check dates, math, attribution and links. Read the profile as a portrait, the dossier as a hiring file and the review as an employer's assessment. A reader should be able to disagree with the assessment on identifiable football grounds. The source notes should make that disagreement possible without requiring the prose itself to sound like an audit.

The profile changes when the user changes or approves character. The dossier changes when responsibilities, results or verified career information change. The review changes when a completed period or material development changes the employment argument. Keep those update rules separate, so one poor game does not rewrite Stone's identity and a new scheme preference does not become a performance achievement.
