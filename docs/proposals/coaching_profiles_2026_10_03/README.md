# Proposed Alex Stone coaching documents

Prepared October 3, 2026. Repository evidence reviewed at commit `2fe74c9c59e39267bfd63a0fe9e8697f5632f260`. Current football coverage: September 28, 2014, after Week 4.

These are three separately selectable templates, with extensive Alex Stone examples and research behind the design. They do not replace the existing pages, change his approved personality or decide his employment. Each can be adopted in whole, used in part or left out later.

The bracketed prompts explain what a future version should contain. The populated passages demonstrate the intended voice and depth. They make the proposals reviewable without filling the career with new fictional meetings or unsupported statistics. These document names are choices for this project; public evidence does not establish a universal NFL internal template with these titles.

## Objective assessment of the existing pages

### The coaching profile already has a strong foundation

The profile earns its detail. It explains why Stone likes Dagger and Mesh, how he approaches protections and run fits, how practice corrections work, what he lets his quarterback change and where assistants have room to disagree. Its personal material is also concrete: relaxed meetings, humor, direct technical criticism, attention to families and a willingness to keep a relationship after reducing a player's role.

It already contains useful tensions. He gives assistants independence but sometimes interrupts their corrections. He remains aggressive on offense with a lead while becoming more cautious on defense. He believes in development strongly enough to see a usable role in a prospect whom the personnel department may regard as too expensive in time or roster space. These details make a recognizable coach without requiring an invented temper, secret insecurity or dramatic personal history.

The principal improvements are editorial. The heading says the staff context ends August 8, yet later sections contain September instructions. General backfield preference and Jacksonville's Week 4 hierarchy are repeated in multiple sections. The staff-management section preserves the older Crennel-calls arrangement while the defense section describes the new Stone-calls arrangement. A single dated application note would make the distinction between his continuing preferences and the team's current operation clearer.

There are areas that could be developed, including particular special-teams choices, handling of failed methods, self-scouting and situational tradeoffs. The template asks for those where useful without silently answering new character questions for the user. More headings alone would not improve this portrait.

### The NFL Coach Sheet is a career reference

It is not a game-day call sheet. Its closest public analogue is a detailed coaching biography and statistical career record, with less promotional language than a club media guide. Its existing chronology and distinction between coordinator title and calling responsibility are worth retaining. The net-yardage definitions and separation of postseason statistics are also strengths.

Its main omission is the team Stone inherited. The page tells the reader what Jacksonville produced in 2013 but supplies little comparison with 2012 beyond the old record. That makes it difficult to see which parts changed and which remained weak. It also mixes an August biography cutoff with September responsibilities and a current 3-1 record. Stone was 50 in early August and 51 by September 28; the proposed single cutoff fixes that ambiguity.

The proposal adds the inherited comparison, preserves every appointment and supplies a current-season subsection without prematurely closing 2014. Assistant championships stay separate from head-coaching achievements. Players acquired by Caldwell are not recast as acquisitions made by Stone.

### The performance page is accurate in several particulars but too compressed

It correctly records the retention decision and identifies specific problems. It does not currently give enough room to the assignment, the quality of the inherited roster, the staff's contribution or the case for and against sustained progress. Its strongest evidence is in the linked owner review and player exits; the page reduces much of that evidence to a short list.

The August 4 cutoff is now stale. The career has completed four regular-season games. A reader should not encounter both an assertion that he has played no 2014 game and a career elsewhere already through September 28.

The existing files do not mistakenly charge Jacksonville's 2012 losses to Stone in their career tables. The missing work is a full account of two different 2012 situations: the Saints team he ran and the Jaguars team he would inherit. The proposals make that separation explicit.

## My judgment and proposed approach

Keep the current profile as the identity source. Selectively expand it; do not replace its specificity with a personality inventory. Give the Coach Sheet a clearer career-reference structure. Rebuild the performance review at substantially greater depth using the evidence already in the career, with fresh historical research for Jacksonville's inheritance.

Human realism comes from competing obligations and consequences. Protecting a development period takes time from opponent preparation. Giving a quarterback more control requires that he and the receivers share the same answers. Retaining an assistant can preserve continuity or prolong a recurring failure. Which interpretation fits depends on what happened. None should be settled by a slogan about culture.

The profile describes his intended approach. The review can find that he failed to carry it out without declaring that the approach was never sincere. Thielen's lost development period and Kelce's unclear completion standard are already specific enough to expose that gap. There is no need to invent a flaw for every strength.

For job security, give the reader an argument. State the actual decision, the agreed assignment, the strongest reasons to keep him, the football concerns that could change the judgment and the information that is still missing. Contract guarantees, public reputation, player response, GM alignment and coaching results are related but distinct. A winning record cannot answer all of them.

## What the chronology and research establish

### New Orleans in 2012

Stone was interim head coach and offensive coordinator from April 16 through the playing season that ended January 13, 2013. He called the offense and ran the team while Payton was suspended. Carmichael remained involved in weekday passing-game work, Kromer in the line and run game, Spagnuolo called the defense and McMahon ran special teams. Stone had substantial coaching and roster-input responsibilities but no final GM authority.

The Saints were 12-4 and went 1-1 in the postseason. They entered that year after going 13-3 in 2011, with Brees, an established system and substantial organizational continuity. The evidence supports successful leadership of an existing contender during disruption. It does not describe a rebuilding job from the bottom of the league. The approximate 2012 production stays approximate, and missing playoff scores remain missing. The real Saints' 7-9 season is not Stone's season. [Prehire dossier](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/football-career-sim/blob/2fe74c9c59e39267bfd63a0fe9e8697f5632f260/library/alex_stone_character_dossier_pre_hire.md)

### Jacksonville before and after his appointment

The official 2012 Jacksonville record is the pre-appointment baseline. The roster had an unsettled Gabbert-Henne quarterback situation and injury problems in the backfield and line, but it also had useful returning players. Shorts, Blackmon, Lewis, Monroe and Jones-Drew belong in the inherited account. Describing the entire team as devoid of talent would overstate the case. [January 7 roster assessment](https://www.jaguars.com/news/roster-breakdown-offense-9301393) · [January 15 roster inventory](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/football-career-sim/blob/2fe74c9c59e39267bfd63a0fe9e8697f5632f260/career/2013/offseason/initial_roster.md)

The detailed [before-and-after table](02_nfl_coach_sheet_template.md#jacksonville-2013-first-permanent-head-coaching-appointment) shows eight additional wins, 101 more points scored, 98 fewer allowed, 989 more net offensive yards and 739 fewer net yards allowed. The points margin improved from minus-189 to plus-10. Takeaways fell from 23 to 15, however, and the turnover differential worsened from minus-3 to minus-10.

Those comparisons establish the size and shape of the team's improvement. They do not isolate an individual coaching effect. Caldwell changed the roster; Stone built and managed the coaching operation; coordinators, position coaches and players contributed. The 2013 league and Jacksonville results come from the career's own records. Real-world Jacksonville results after Stone's appointment do not fill any gaps.

### The current position in 2014

At the September 28 cutoff Jacksonville is 3-1, with 105 points scored and 82 allowed. Its five giveaways and six takeaways give it a plus-1 differential. These are early results relevant to the concerns Caldwell named. The employment decision remains the January 15 retention. Stone now calls both offense and defense, with Crennel retaining defensive coordination and transmitting the calls. That changed workload deserves examination; one game under it cannot establish its success or failure. ## Reliability checks that matter here

The Coach Sheet uses net passing yards after sack losses. The repository's generated yardage tables currently use gross passing in their displayed passing and total-yardage columns. That difference matters for historical comparison and league rankings. The proposal keeps the existing audited 2013 conversion and avoids silently labeling the current gross figure as net.

The 2013 results remain part of the career, but the equal-strength simulation limits the coaching conclusions that can be drawn from them. Current game records also have specific limits: Week 4 stores no Jacksonville defensive calls, does not offer a two-point choice on the late try and does not record timeout decisions individually. Those implementation gaps must not become fictional mistakes by Stone. Earlier Week 1 and Week 2 off-sheet allegations were withdrawn as call-label artifacts.

These notes belong outside the main coach portrait. They prevent unsupported judgments while leaving room for direct, detailed football writing about the decisions and work that are established.

## Selecting material for later adoption

The three proposals can be selected independently. The profile proposal is designed for selective additions to an existing strong portrait. The coach-sheet proposal can supply the fuller career and inherited-team structure. The review proposal can supply the substantial performance and employment account. None installs new plays, advances the season, assigns a role, creates an ultimatum or changes the established contract.
