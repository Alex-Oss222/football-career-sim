# Head coach performance and employment review

Proposed template for Alex Stone. Prepared October 3, 2026. This is a review draft, not a new club decision. Existing employment records and the adopted coaching profile remain authoritative.

## How to use this document

Write a football judgment that an owner, general manager and coach could disagree about on specific grounds. Explain the team he inherited, what changed, who did the work, what held up and what still threatens the club's progress. The employment section should then explain what the club has actually decided and how strongly the football evidence supports continuing with him.

Use the worked examples below as a demonstration of the proposed depth and voice. They are grounded in the existing career. Bracketed instructions describe the reusable template. They are not new events or obligations. Remove the instructions when producing an adopted review.

The review should grow by completed season and material development, preserving earlier conclusions at their original dates. Routine practice detail belongs in the training reports. Include it here when it changes a standing judgment about the coach.

### Worked opening: the completed 2013 assessment

Jacksonville's first season under Stone exceeded the standard set when he was hired. A club that had finished 2-14 went 10-6, reached the playoffs and won at Kansas City. The more useful organizational finding is that the staff, quarterback competition and division of responsibility with Caldwell survived a full season. Stone had previously run an established New Orleans operation as its interim head coach. Jacksonville supplied his first evidence of building and running a permanent program of his own.

The team was considerably less convincing than ten wins alone suggest. Jacksonville outscored its opponents by ten points, finished ten turnovers behind and lost three games by at least 28 points. Cousins gave the offense a starting quarterback, but his 18 interceptions and 38 sacks left substantial work for the quarterback coaches, protection staff and caller. The playoff win matters. So does the 13-point offensive performance in the loss that ended the season.

Khan and Caldwell retained Stone on January 15, 2014, on his existing terms. Neither an extension nor a raise was requested or offered. Caldwell identified scoring margin, quarterback ball security and protection as leading measures for the following year. There was no stated win minimum or playoff ultimatum. The evidence supported keeping him; it did not settle whether this staff could produce sustained contention. [Owner and GM review][owner-review]

## 2. The assignment he actually accepted

[Describe the appointment before describing the results. What was broken, what already worked, what resources and authority were available, and what did the club explicitly ask him to accomplish? Preserve the standard known at the time. Do not convert an unexpectedly good season into a promise he supposedly made before it.]

Cover the prior three seasons when they clarify the trajectory. One inherited season may suffice for a first-year review, but give the quarterback, staff and personnel context behind it. Identify major injuries or absences only where the source establishes them. Distinguish a coach taking over a strong organization during an interruption from one hired to reconstruct a failing team.

### Worked example: New Orleans, 2012

Stone did not arrive in New Orleans in 2012 to rescue a 7-9 team. He had been offensive coordinator since 2009 and had taken over the primary offensive calls during the 2011 season. The Saints entered his interim head-coaching year after a 13-3 season, with Drew Brees, a developed offensive system and much of the existing staff. Sean Payton remained the permanent head coach during his suspension. Stone's appointment gave him day-to-day responsibility for the whole team from April 16, 2012.

He retained the offensive-coordinator job and called the offense. Carmichael carried substantial weekday passing-game work, Kromer remained central to the line and running game, Spagnuolo controlled and called the defense, and McMahon ran the technical special-teams operation. Stone controlled the team schedule, meetings, practice allocation, playing time and game-management decisions. Final acquisition and contract authority stayed with the personnel operation.

New Orleans finished 12-4, second in the NFC South, won a Wild Card game and lost in the Divisional round on January 13, 2013. The established season totals are approximate: 505 points, 6,980 offensive yards and 18 offensive turnovers; Brees threw approximately 42 touchdowns and 12 interceptions. The defensive baseline is approximately 350 points and 5,900 yards allowed, with 29 takeaways and 38 sacks.

The proper assessment is successful operation of an already strong team under unusual disruption. It establishes real whole-team responsibility and a full season of offensive calling. It does not establish that he created the Saints' system, developed Brees from a novice, hired the entire staff or personally coached the defense into those results. The record does not identify the 2012 playoff opponents and scores sufficiently to write game-by-game postseason analysis. [Prehire dossier][dossier]

### Worked example: Jacksonville at the January 2013 appointment

Jacksonville was a separate assignment. Its 2012 record was 2-14. Stone became head coach on January 15, 2013, with a newly hired general manager, a quarterback evaluation to make, a staff to build and a roster requiring broad reconstruction. The hiring criteria emphasized a workable GM partnership, whole-program leadership, a credible quarterback plan, staff construction and roster-development judgment.

The inherited football problems were substantial. Jacksonville had scored 255 points and allowed 444, a minus-189 margin. It produced 4,788 net yards and allowed 6,088. Its quarterbacks took 50 sacks, and the defense made only 20. Third-down conversion was 29.6 percent; opponents converted 41.4 percent. These are the 2012 Jacksonville figures, not a second account of Stone's Saints season. The [Coach Sheet proposal](02_nfl_coach_sheet_template.md#jacksonville-2013-first-permanent-head-coaching-appointment) carries the full comparison and the official historical sources.

Stone's stated first-year standard concerned a functioning program, player development, fewer preparation and communication failures, a candid quarterback evaluation and clearer roster direction. He did not promise a playoff berth. His authority covered the coaches, preparation, depth chart and game decisions. Caldwell retained scouting, acquisitions, contracts, cap and draft authority. The review must use that division when crediting the rebuild. [Owner review][owner-review] · [Accepted contract][contract]

**Worked interpretation.** Jacksonville established a viable operation and achieved a major first-year improvement in results. Caldwell's acquisitions and Stone's use of the players belong in the same account. Treating the eight extra wins as eight wins created by coaching would erase that distinction. Treating the improvement as meaningless would also be wrong: the club's review accepted that the program functioned and that the promised quarterback evaluation had been carried out. [Owner review][owner-review]

The scoring margin improved by 199 points, from minus-189 to plus-10: 101 more points scored and 98 fewer allowed. The offense gained 989 more net yards; opponents gained 739 fewer. Protection improved from 50 sacks allowed to 38, although 38 still ranked tied 25th in the completed 2013 comparison. Takeaways fell from 23 to 15, and the turnover differential worsened from minus-3 to minus-10. Jacksonville became much more competitive while remaining vulnerable to losing possessions. That is the football account the win total alone cannot supply. [Full before-and-after table](02_nfl_coach_sheet_template.md#jacksonville-2013-first-permanent-head-coaching-appointment)

## 4. The football, phase by phase

### Offense and quarterback

[Assess scoring, net production, down-to-down operation, protection, turnovers and the quarterback's decisions in context. Examine a change in the call menu or personnel use where it is documented. Separate the design, the call, the protection adjustment, the read and the execution. A sack total cannot assign responsibility by itself.]

**Worked example.** Stone called the 2013 offense; Tice coordinated the weekly work. Jacksonville finished 12th in team scoring and 15th in total net yards. Third-down conversion was 39.6 percent, 13th. Those results support an offense capable of keeping the club competitive, but none of the listed offensive measures finished in the top ten. Cousins's 3,981 passing yards and 23 touchdowns need to be read beside the interceptions and sacks. The review should ask which losses came from protection identification, line execution, a delayed throw or a call that demanded more time than the protection could supply. [Coach Sheet][sheet] · [Cousins exit interview][cousins]

Houston supplies a concrete calling criticism. Jones-Drew ran for 86 yards on 12 carries, yet Jacksonville finished with 17 team rushes and 45 dropbacks in a 38-6 loss. The existing review records Stone's later acknowledgment that he had stopped calling what was working. That incident supports criticism of that game's call selection. Establishing a season-long tendency requires checking the other games, including score and time remaining. An unfavorable run-pass ratio by itself cannot settle the question. [Houston game][houston] · [Cousins review][cousins]

### Defense

[Describe the front, coverage and run-defense results separately from the head coach's supervision. Identify the coordinator and caller for the period. Explain whether the team can stop drives, prevent explosive gains, finish sacks and create takeaways. Do not convert low yards allowed into a complete defensive verdict.]

**Worked example.** Crennel coordinated and called the 2013 defense. Jacksonville ranked fifth in total net yards allowed and third on third down. It also finished 31st in sacks and 31st in takeaways, while team points allowed ranked tied 15th. The defense's ability to limit movement was a strength. Its shortage of possession-changing plays was a separate weakness. Stone is accountable for the staff and the overall program, while the technical work and calling credit belong to Crennel and the defensive coaches. [Coach Sheet][sheet]

### Special teams

[Evaluate field position, coverage, return decisions, protection, kicking reliability, avoidable penalties and game-day roster choices when the evidence supports them. Separate coordinator performance from a kicker's result on a single attempt. Include Stone's decisions to punt, attempt a field goal, keep the offense out or approve a fake.]

**Worked example.** Lowry's departure on January 12, 2014 was for Atlanta's head-coaching job after Jacksonville's elimination. Westhoff's appointment on February 11 filled that vacancy. Calling the move a firing or a response to special-teams failure would change the history. The review can assess continuity and the handoff without inventing a season-long special-teams ranking. For a technical example, the Houston game review flags a 26-yard punt return and a zero-yard punt downed at the line of scrimmage, both before Houston touchdown possessions. Those plays give the staff specific field-position and coverage work to examine. They do not support a verdict on Lowry's full season by themselves. A completed technical assessment needs the season's kicking, punting, coverage, protection and return evidence, which the current coaching summary does not supply. [Houston review][houston]

### Situations and game management

[Review fourth downs, two-point attempts, timeouts, challenges, two-minute possessions, four-minute possessions, end-of-half decisions, backed-up situations and sudden changes. Select the decisions that materially affected the season. Describe the information available before the decision, the reasonable alternatives, the choice and its consequences. A successful outcome does not make a poor decision good; an unsuccessful outcome does not prove the choice was wrong.]

Do not infer that Stone managed every close game well because the club won it. Where the recorded clock or decision sequence is incomplete, narrow the conclusion to what can be evaluated. His profile's preference for staying aggressive on offense with a lead and playing more cautiously on defense can guide the question. It cannot answer whether the particular call was appropriate.

## 5. Teaching, development and running the building

[Write about delivered coaching. Which player received a usable correction? Was there time to work on it? Did it hold against a changed look or in a game? Who owned follow-through? Look at starters and reserves. Do not treat every younger player on a successful team as a development success.]

### Worked example: the development promise under weekly pressure

Stone's teaching philosophy is detailed and player-oriented. The exit interviews show where its delivery fell short. Thielen's individual period disappeared after the bye without a clear explanation of whether the work had been completed or postponed. Cousins wanted the short coaching phrases paired with concrete film examples. Kelce had been working on attached blocking since spring without a sufficiently clear picture of what would close the correction. These are different problems: protected practice time, communication of a diagnosis and an observable standard for improvement. [Thielen][thielen] · [Cousins][cousins] · [Kelce][kelce]

The June 17 to 19, 2014 minicamp report supplies some evidence of a response. Bates showed Cousins the requested end-zone view of a protection error; Cousins identified the wrong-way slide and the correct answer before being supplied the coaching point. Summer sheets were delivered to the signed players. Prepared individual film packets had not been issued at that handoff. The improved instruction counts. It should not be inflated into proof that the entire development system had been fixed. This is a June finding; the packet-delivery status must be checked against later reports before describing it as an open September issue. [Minicamp report][minicamp]

[Then cover staff supervision, division of work, disagreements, discipline, player trust, medical boundaries and communication. Use actual instances. A cordial meeting is not proof that the whole locker room trusts the coach. An unpopular roster decision is not proof that he has lost it. Distinguish the coach's chosen policy from whether it was implemented consistently.]

## 6. Is the improvement likely to last?

[This is an assessment, not a forecast model. Explain the strongest reasons to expect the work to hold and the problems that could undo it. Compare multiple seasons when available. For a new tenure, acknowledge how little time has passed without repeatedly apologizing for it.]

Investigate the questions that actually bear on this team:

- Does the offense remain functional when the first answer is removed, the quarterback is pressured or the score changes the play menu?
- Does the staff return to the same unresolved problem with a better method, or just repeat the original instruction?
- Are young players retaining their assignments and techniques as their roles expand?
- Does a strong unit depend on one coordinator, one healthy player or unusually favorable field position?
- Are lopsided losses isolated combinations of mistakes, or repeated failures of preparation and response?
- Are narrow wins being supported by better underlying play? Do not label every close win luck.
- Can the staff replace a departing assistant without losing the work he owned?
- Is the roster improving within the resources Caldwell actually controls?

**Worked conclusion after 2013.** Stone earned a second Jacksonville season with a successful first year. The evidence for sustained success was less developed. New Orleans demonstrated that he could keep an established contender functioning; Jacksonville demonstrated that he could build a staff and produce a competitive first season in a reconstruction. Neither tenure yet demonstrated several years of correcting the same weaknesses, replacing his own assistants and keeping player development going through the demands of the weekly game plan.

## 7. Employment position and job security

### What the club has actually done

[State the latest formal decision, its date, who made it, the contract position and any actual conditions. An analyst's expectation belongs in the next subsection.]

**Stone example.** Retained January 15, 2014 by Khan and Caldwell on the original four-year, fully guaranteed agreement accepted January 15, 2013. No raise, extension or change in authority. Compensation and additional termination provisions are not established. No later employment decision is identified in the records reviewed for this proposal. [Contract][contract] · [Owner review][owner-review]

The guarantee describes the club's financial commitment. It does not prevent the club from changing head coaches. With salary unspecified, a review cannot calculate a firing cost. The absence of an extension also carries little independent meaning here: Stone asked for no new terms, and neither side raised an extension.

### Why keeping him makes football sense

[Write the case as an argument using the club's own assignment and evidence. Include the replacement problem only if actual alternatives, costs or organizational intentions are known. Avoid invented candidates, private loyalty or assumed endorsements.]

**Stone example.** The club had received a functioning coaching and personnel partnership, a starting quarterback selected through the promised competition, a complete staff and meaningful roles for its first group of young acquisitions. The results were well above the inherited baseline. Keeping Stone preserved an operation that had worked for a year while giving the staff a chance to address identifiable shortcomings. The recorded decision supports that rationale. It does not establish private confidence in an extension or an unlimited patience with future decline.

### What would give the club a serious reason to reconsider

[Separate issues already raised by the club from reasonable analyst questions. Explain a chain of football consequences rather than inventing a firing trigger. Do not put words into the owner's mouth.]

For Stone, Caldwell's documented concerns are the scoring margin, quarterback ball security and protection. A poor stretch would be more significant if the staff repeatedly failed to identify its causes, continued exposing the same weakness or failed to carry out corrections it had already promised. A further year of weak scoring margin despite favorable results would raise a sustainability question. Losing games while showing credible quarterback improvement and coping with established personnel losses would require a different judgment. Neither situation automatically resolves employment.

Staff performance also matters. If an assistant repeatedly fails, examine whether Stone recognized the problem, gave useful direction and made a responsible decision within his authority. Replacing an assistant is not automatically accountability; retaining one is not automatically loyalty at the club's expense. Evaluate the underlying work.

### Expectations, patience and organizational alignment

[Distinguish an explicit target, a broad ambition and the reviewer's expectation. Preserve promises made at appointment. Record any later change with its source and date. Ownership and the GM may weigh factors differently, but do not invent such a disagreement merely to make the account dramatic.]

**Stone example.** Division contention and playoff progress are reasonable subjects for a second-year assessment after ten wins. They are not a recorded playoff-or-dismissal condition. The documented GM-coach working arrangement held through 2013. Khan's London preparation request was specific; private views about Stone's long-term ceiling were not supplied. The review should say what is known at that level of specificity.

### Present assessment, separately from the club decision

[Give a plain conclusion: whether continuation is well supported, genuinely contested or difficult to justify, and why. These are editorial judgments, not fixed tiers or probability bands. Name what new evidence would materially change the view.]

**Worked judgment at the January review.** Keeping Stone was well supported by both the assignment and the first-year results. His performance had open problems, but the case for dismissal after one season was weak on the recorded football evidence. Whether the club should extend him was a separate question that had not been tested. A four-year agreement and a successful first season are reasons to continue evaluating the program, not reasons to declare the coach permanently safe.

## Evidence and editorial method

This proposal separates the author's evaluation from recorded club action. It does not reopen the January retention decision, invent meetings or assign private beliefs to Khan, Caldwell, assistants or players. Existing exit-interview dialogue is a career source; preserve any interpretive-reconstruction label in its owner rather than treating all dialogue as an independently observed transcript. The examples above summarize the reported complaints and work without adding new quotations.

The 2013 results are valid career results. Their equal-strength simulation and documented clock limitations restrict causal claims about coaching ability and late-game management. Approximate 2012 Saints figures cannot produce exact ranks or exact efficiency comparisons. These limitations belong in this source note and at the particular conclusion they affect, not in a warning after every paragraph.

The Week 4 report establishes Stone's new defensive-calling responsibility, but its game record stores no Jacksonville defensive calls. It cannot prove that a specific pressure or coverage succeeded or failed. Its late extra-point attempt was generated without offering a two-point decision, and timeout counts were not recorded as individual coaching choices. Those facts cannot be used as character flaws or bad decisions by Stone. Earlier Week 1 and Week 2 off-sheet allegations were withdrawn as call-label artifacts in the current state; do not revive them in a performance review.

Public NFL examples support the structure rather than determine Stone's verdict. [Jeffrey Lurie's January 2012 explanation](https://www.philadelphiaeagles.com/news/chairman-ceo-jeffrey-lurie-6743593) considers long-term record, recent trajectory and response within the building. [Phil Emery's January 2013 explanation](https://www.chicagobears.com/news/phil-emery-wants-bears-to-contend-on-consistent-basis-9257746) addresses persistent offensive shortcomings and sustained contention. Those are evidence for asking broader questions than whether the most recent record was winning. Neither is a universal NFL review form or a source for this club's private standards.
