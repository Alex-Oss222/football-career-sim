# Offseason training reports: football content and sources

The same work now extends through [training camp and preseason](camp_and_preseason_reports.md), including Stone's camp philosophy, the active playbooks' assessment questions and reusable camp, game and consolidated-review formats.

Prepared September 30, 2026 and revised October 1, 2026 for the user's request to rewrite the 2014 spring reports and establish reusable examples. This is report-design research, not a simulated event. The approach below is a synthesis of contemporary coaching accounts and the applicable agreement, not a claim that NFL clubs share one report format. It does not advance the career or supply practice outcomes.

## What the old reports got right and what needed changing

The original reports already contained useful football: formation changes, protection calls, route spacing, coverage exchanges, coaching corrections and later checks. Their weakness was presentation. Repeated control, legal, meeting-duration, badge and receipt language competed with the account of training. Repeated tables made distinct practices sound like the same evaluation form. The generic offseason turn template also led with job status, finances and acquisition boards, which are the wrong opening for a practice review.

The change makes the football account the opening report in each existing `training_report.md`. It explains the assignment, the observed performance, the coach's response and the remaining work. The existing detailed record stays expandable beneath it, with its original headings, metadata, individual observations and decisions preserved. This retains the evidence behind player profiles and links without making the reader work through the administrative record first. No past practice was rerun and no outcome was supplied to make a better story.

## What belongs in each stage

| Stage | Real work and setting | What the report should tell Stone |
|---|---|---|
| Phase One | Classroom install and review; conditioning, strength and rehabilitation under the restricted field rules | Explain the language being learned: formations, personnel, strength declarations, protection responsibilities and defensive help. Identify different interpretations across players or rooms. Separate classroom answers from performance-staff observations and name what must be checked outside. |
| Phase Two | Coaches take the teaching onto the field through individual work and separate-unit rehearsal | Show whether the classroom answer survives movement: alignment, cadence, quarterback drop and receiver landmark, line footwork, back track, defensive alignment and pursuit path. Explain unfamiliar jobs or combinations actually rehearsed. There is no opposing-unit matchup to judge. |
| Rookie minicamp | Orientation and first assignments for the actual eligible group on the post-draft calendar | Identify signed players and tryouts, initial positions, available teaching and actual opportunities. Describe assignment recognition, footwork, movement and response to instruction. Explain what deserves another look and what this short, non-contact sample cannot answer. |
| OTAs | Veterans and rookies working in non-contact team periods | Follow a shared call through the unit when the other side changes the picture. Who identified the change, who relayed it, and did adjacent players act on the same answer? Cover protection identification, line communication, defensive exchanges, kicking operation and reserve work alongside the passing game. |
| Mandatory minicamp | Non-contact spring work with the mandatory attendance rule for contracted players; more room for meetings and walkthroughs | Describe the football the group can presently run, corrections that survived subsequent work, installation still underway and questions awaiting pads. Show what happened when replacements carried a responsibility. Record actual summer instructions and the next camp test. |

These are reporting questions, not a compulsory curriculum. A team may install new material at minicamp; Jacksonville's existing 2014 agenda happened to emphasize recall and changed presentations. Likewise, its rookie camp deliberately used unopposed work. Do not turn those local choices into universal league rules. Phase Two and rookie minicamp can overlap on the calendar.

The original 2011 agreement controls the 2014 boundaries. Phase One field activity is strength, conditioning and rehabilitation with strength coaches; position coaches cannot run or observe field football work. Phase Two permits individual coaching and separate-unit rehearsal, without helmets or offense against defense. Phase Three allows non-contact opposing team periods but still prohibits live contact and the specified opposed one-on-one drills, including offensive-line/defensive-line pass rush and protection and receiver/defensive-back bump-and-run. Mandatory minicamp has three practice days plus a physicals day. The [existing rules research](../library/2014_offseason_phase_rules_verification.md) retains the detailed limits and source text.

Rules describe the available work, not what a player actually did. A fast team-period release is not a live bump-and-run drill; a whistle at the quarterback is not a tackle or a sack statistic. A permitted drill is not evidence that Jacksonville ran it. The closed rookie report's former physical-press conclusion was unsupported in its recorded unopposed setting; its release-plan correction remains without a contact-win claim.

## What makes the account useful

Start with phase, team, season, location, dates, completed practice days and work addressed. Distinguish days from separate sessions when it matters, and state unknown location or exposure detail honestly. Briefly explain the meaningful work the group actually did and why it mattered. The reader needs to know whether the observation came from a meeting, rehearsal, group period or team situation; an inventory of every drill is unnecessary.

Connect the players' jobs within offense, defense and special teams. A protection call involves the center, adjacent linemen, back and quarterback; a coverage exchange depends on more than the defender nearest the catch. Explain the unit's operation, then the individual work that helped or hurt it. Include the line, reserves and players doing useful work away from the ball when the record supports them. Name unfamiliar calls only where their assignments explain the play.

Follow the material questions across the block. Compare earlier and later work, identifying the coach's actual instruction and whether it held with a changed picture, another teammate or less help. A clean repeat can answer a narrow technique question without settling a role. Contradictory evidence, a repeated error and an unanswered question remain part of the report. There is no required improvement, mistake, standout or dramatic ending.

Carry forward only the questions that still matter. For each, identify the responsible coach from the staff plan, the next permitted opportunity to check it and a descriptive link to the dated observation or prior instruction. If the test is proposed, say so; if it is scheduled but unperformed, leave its result open. Keep this in the report's closing prose or a short comparison table when several items genuinely need one. Do not create a second follow-up document.

## What Hard Knocks contributes

The Falcons' official episode-two account describes Matt Ryan acknowledging his own misread of Harry Douglas's route in the receiver meeting, and Sean Weatherspoon helping Prince Shembo study. The fourth-episode account describes special-teams film corrections, a teammate's protection work used as a teaching example and individual help with the playbook. These examples support shared responsibility and specific teaching: explain whose answer was wrong, how the staff or another player helped, and what later work established. Anger, speeches, cuts and a dramatic ending are not requirements.

Access limitation: the official episode page was located, but no complete readable episode transcript was retrieved, and the full episode was not viewed in this pass. The research used the club's contemporaneous written episode account, plus primary spring-practice reporting and the original CBA. It does not claim a transcript quotation or recreate a television scene. Hard Knocks follows training camp; its contact work is not evidence that the same drill belongs in OTAs.

Both episode accounts are later than the August 1, 2014 checkpoint used for this design revision. They are authoring context only, excluded from simulation evaluation. No Falcons personnel result, later player development or actual Jaguars performance enters the branch reports.

## How the chat uses the templates

The [template index](../foundation/templates/offseason_training/README.md) routes five distinct templates. It is linked from AGENTS.md, the general offseason template, the training workflow and all five spring folders. The general offseason template's existing season-closeout, finance, draft and free-agency sections remain available for those tasks; spring training uses the phase report instead.

For an existing report request, the LLM reads the phase's closed evidence and presents the opening football account. It uses the expandable record for player-specific follow-ups. For a request to run new work, it first follows the existing calendar, practice-resolution and decision rules, then writes the result using the matching template. Neither path gets to turn a plan into completed work. There is no application UI or new model endpoint to deploy in this repository; the chat behavior is controlled by the checked-in instructions and records.

Render the completed report directly in the LLM chatbox, with a link to its saved owner. The headings organize the account; the user is not asked to fill a form. Depth follows the actual work: a classroom and conditioning phase need not be stretched to camp length, while a substantial practice block may need fuller treatment. Shared writing and evidence rules live in the template README and assessment method; individual templates supply the phase-specific prompts.

Each phase has one report owning its coaching findings and dated event reference. There are exactly two full annual player assessments: the opening baseline, maintained with dated source-linked observations, and a separate final assessment after the season. Material findings update the existing opening card; actual contested jobs update their existing battle cards. No phase generates another whole roster of assessment files.

Evaluate the player's actual taught job in the active book. An archetype describes a player; it does not lock his identity or restrict study of the book. Development depends on the player, correction and subsequent evidence, without fixed weeks-to-technique or months-to-position timers. The supplied development outline's permission to use later real-life outcomes privately is not adopted. These instructions guide coaching interpretation and presentation, not a replacement result engine.

The verification pass separately retrieved the original agreement and the Packers and Steelers accounts after the initial NFL, Browns, Titans and Falcons searches. It confirmed the central distinction between classroom explanation, rehearsal and non-contact opposition. Unknown conditioning measurements, film deliveries and reasons for individual errors were left unknown.
