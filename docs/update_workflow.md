# Keeping the career records current

The actual event owns its dated facts. [Document 5](../state/05_Current_Season_State.md) owns the current checkpoint, and the [repository map](repository_map.json) names current paths and dependencies. [Document 6](../foundation/06_Event_Records_and_Handoff.md) governs event records and handoffs. `Record.md` is a generated one-line index of those owners, not another narrative or a place to resolve an event.

Write names, dates and football findings in ordinary language. Descriptive event metadata and compatibility aliases connect evidence internally; readers should not have to decode entry numbers. Software releases and migrations belong in their technical owners and do not appear as football events.

## Write once, update the affected views

| Completed event | Authoritative record | Views to update when their facts change |
|---|---|---|
| Practice or camp work | The phase's `training_report.md`, including its assessment | Dated player updates, an actual position-battle card, availability, current state and any changed role or medical instruction |
| Signing, release, tender or trade | The actual contract or transaction record | Roster, contract register, cap and future obligations, depth chart, pick ownership, player development cohort and Documents 4 and 5 |
| Draft selection or rookie contract | Drafted or undrafted result record | Player control, contracts, finances, pick ownership, working player record, development cohort and current state |
| Outside staff request, permission, interview or outcome | Dated staff request/outcome record; preserve frozen method and result artifacts. A contact with Stone himself (a rumor, a call, a permission or interview request, an interview, an offer, an accepted job) is a dated entry in `contacts` of his coach record, `data/people/coaches/alex_stone.json`, with its stage, deadline and source | Staff timeline and the decision queue (`state/decision_queue.json`); for Stone, the Coach page and Contract and jobs (`render_coach_pages.py`); employment and payroll only if a departure or appointment occurred |
| Staff hire, departure or delegated responsibility | Staff hiring/departure/decision record, then a dated `hired`, `role_changed`, `contract_signed` or `departed` entry in the coach's permanent record (`data/people/coaches/<slug>.json`, `runtime/coaches.py`), which owns his job, unit and contract terms | The Staff page and the Coach page (`render_coach_pages.py`), the coaching payroll (`render_jaguars_cap_tracker.py`), authority where affected and Documents 4 and 5 |
| Head-coach contract signed, amended, extended or ended | A `contract_signed` or `contract_amended` entry in Stone's coach record. An amendment is dated and applied by its day within the career (`in_world`), with the real-world day it was written in `recorded`; a page shows the contract as it stands on its own date, so an amendment applies only from its `in_world` day, and validation fails one dated after the master date. A signed salary is null (none stated) or names every season of the term, and so does the contract as amended; a term completed from comparable contracts is marked a reconstruction with its basis (`text`, `from`, `source`). The job record's `contract_record` points at it; nothing stores the term twice. Every coach record is listed in the repository map's required files | The Coach page, Contract and jobs, the coaching payroll and Document 5's current-job block (`render_coach_pages.py`, `render_jaguars_cap_tracker.py`, `render_career_views.py`) |
| Change in who calls a phase, a relay or an emergency lead | `delegation` in `state/current_job.json`, with the dated event record as its source; a `calling_changed` entry on each affected coach's record dates it in his history | Document 5's current-job block and the desk (`render_career_views.py`) and the Staff page (`render_coach_pages.py`). The staff register (`coaching_staff.md`) is history through October 5, 2014 and is no longer written |
| Stone decision or standing instruction | Its event record: the weekly packet, game output or transaction record | The decision-queue item, marked `RESOLVED` with that record, or the `standing_instructions` entry in `state/current_job.json`; then `render_career_views.py` |
| Game week decisions (from 2014 Week 6) | Stone's Game Plan page in the week folder (`Game_Plan.md`), checked by `check_game_plan.py`; the week's two queue items opened by `open_game_week.py` (run by `render_game_week.py` before the week's first briefing; the inactives item waits on the exams the medical entries name before the game) | `apply_game_plan.py`, once no exam the items wait on is still to come (until then it sets nothing): the frozen call sheet, the game depth chart's inactives, order and roles with one update-log entry, the week's inactives record (`inactives.json`) and applied plan record (`applied_plan.json`, the page's and the sheet's digests, the source map of every emitted call, his intent and the apply's event, carried on the week index), and the week's game-plan and inactives queue items resolved; then every built page (`rebuild_pages.py`). Commit the applied plan on its own before the inputs are built. The Game Week Briefing is re-issued by `render_game_week.py` whenever the situation it shows changes (Stone's own settlement of the week's items is not such a change), the old one kept beside it |
| Game Week Briefing (from 2014 Week 6) | The week's data folder (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).week_data(N)`): the staff's authored parts of the briefing (`staff_briefing.json`: each part with its author and date, rendered "To come" when absent), written by whoever prepares the week, and the issue record (`briefings.json`: each issue's date, file and sha256), written only by `render_game_week.py` | The briefing, any earlier briefing kept as he saw it and the week index (`render_game_week.py`); validation holds each briefing to its issue |
| League-results highlights (from 2014 Week 6) | The league-results page's data twin (`data/YEAR/Regular_Season/League_Results/week_NN.json`: `games` keyed by event id, `reactive_events`), written by whoever writes the roundup | The league-results week page (`render_week_pages.py`); an entry naming no other game of the week stops the build |
| Medical decision | Dated medical record or the phase/game record that received the instruction | Current availability and permitted work; role or roster changes only if separately decided |
| Game | Game output and public result receipt | Box score, relevant season statistics and standings, awards when due, actual injuries/roles, player updates and current state |
| Bye | That week's output | Actual practice, recovery, self-scout and decisions; no game receipt |
| Technical or foundation correction | The affected technical or rule owner | Source hashes, references and readiness when affected; no fabricated football event |

The [career layer](career_layer.md) describes the current-job record, the decision queue, its decision classes and the scripts that refuse while an item is open.

Plans retain intended teaching, priorities and authorized limits. A report owns observed work. The calendar retains dates, opponents, windows and deadlines, with sources and dated corrections. Do not append completed-event narratives, cap snapshots, role assessments or engine status to the calendar. A historical financial as-of date remains correct until a financial event changes it.

**The club records (rebuild step 3b, October 19, 2026).** From 2014 the roster's only owner is the roster record, `SeasonPaths(YEAR).roster` (`data/YEAR/Team_Operations/Team/Roster/roster.json`, `runtime/roster.py`): each controlled player's position, list (active, reserve or practice squad), position group, status with at most one dated parenthetical, and the club's current medical communication on his availability, each with its source, plus every dated departure with how control ended and its rails disposition. A signing, release, reserve placement, promotion or medical change edits that record; nothing reads the roster page back. Every reader of club control reads the record (the weekly build, the exclusivity gate, the preseason build, the in-season rails' Jacksonville control, the progression roster, the player pages, the cap tracker, the ages page and validation); 2013 keeps its roster page as its own owner. A depth decision changes the game depth chart (and Stone's dated role decisions on the working chart) and appends one dated entry, with its sources, to `data/YEAR/Team_Operations/Team/Depth_Chart/update_log.json`. Then `render_club_pages.py` rebuilds the team page, the roster and the depth chart; its `--check`, run by validation, fails a stale, hand-edited or unclean page. The club pages are dated by the master date (`runtime/player_bios.master_date`), as the desk and the coach pages are: the results, the standings, the next game, the staff on game day and the ages come from the closed games up to that date, each matched to its fixture by week and clubs (never by event id), so they move when Document 5's master date moves past a closed game. Rebuild them after every closed game and every master-date move.

*How a game week updates the club records.* When a game closes, the same commit (1) brings the roster record up to the game: every availability the game's medical record changed (injury, removal, hold or clearance, in the club's words, with its source), every status a transaction changed, and `as_of` (the ISO `date` and the `event` it follows, for example "after the Week 6 game at Tennessee"); `render_club_pages.py --check` fails while `as_of.date` is earlier than Jacksonville's last closed game; and (2) appends one entry to `update_log.json` for the game's availability notes and one for each depth decision of the week, oldest first. An update-log entry holds exactly `date` (as the page shows it: the long form of `recorded`, "October 12, 2014", or built from it: a window ending on it, "October 6 to 11, 2014", a same-day "review" or "correction", or the day it happened with "(recorded October 12)"), `recorded` (ISO, never earlier than the entry before and never after the master date), `event`, `change` and `sources` (each a `label` and a `page` that, once normalized, is an existing Markdown page under `career/`, with an optional anchor; `career/../AGENTS.md` fails), and optionally `events` (the closed event ids it belongs to) and `as_written` (an entry's original wording, kept unrendered when it is reworded for the page); the check fails any other key, an unordered, undated or future entry, a shown date not built from `recorded`, or a source outside `career/`. Links inside an availability value are written relative to the roster page (`career/YEAR/Team_Operations/Team/Roster/roster.md`), and links inside a log entry's change relative to the depth chart page, because the pages show them as written. `as_of.event` is the roster's as-of label: it feeds the progression roster's as-of line (`build_player_progression_roster.py`, through `runtime/roster.as_of_label`) and nothing in a game draw; the pages date themselves by the master date. A value reworded for the page keeps its original words in `as_written` on the record row too; it is never rendered.

*The season handoff.* `season_handoff.py` carries the roster as its record (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).roster`): the carry-forward manifest names it, the opening and staging checks parse it, and staging opens the successor's record unchanged; the built roster page is never copied (the successor's is built from its record). The staff carry is the coach records folder (`data/people/coaches`, `runtime/season_layout.COACH_RECORDS`), permanent records that need no copy, and the opening staff-and-authority review covers the coach records and `state/current_job.json`; the frozen staff register is not carried. From 2014 a reader that parses the 2013 roster page layout (the weekly build, the exclusivity gate with `--roster`, the in-season rails) refuses a Markdown roster with no plain player rows, so the built page can never be misread as the owner.

Jacksonville's contract inputs live once in the data area (`data/finances/financial_inputs.json`, `runtime/season_layout.PLAYER_FINANCES`); the file left at the old `career/finances/supporting_records/` path holds no facts and only keeps the pre-Week-6 links resolving. An append-only history page (the medical history, the annual record, the roster decisions) may gain entries after Week 5: the clean-page check reads only what follows its recorded pre-Week-6 bytes (`data/pages/history.json`, "lengths"), and any change to those bytes makes the whole page a new one. An appended entry keeps its event-record line (`<!-- event-record: {...} -->` on a line of its own, `runtime/events.EVENT_META`); the check treats that whole line as an invisible marker. The page history map is pinned by its sha256 (`runtime/season_layout.PAGE_HISTORY_SHA256`): validation fails when the map changes without the pin, and a page written from Week 6 on is never added to it. The clean-page check and the link check read every link form: inline links, links with a title (`[x](path "t")`) and reference definitions (`[x]: path`). The player cap views keep every figure the contract inputs hold; their notes are reworded for the reader in the builder (`render_jaguars_cap_tracker.py`, `RECORD_NOTES` and `READER_WORDS`), never in the inputs. The ages page lists the other clubs from the active season's Week 1 rosters (`SeasonPaths(YEAR).background_depth`) once the master date reaches the season's first game; a listed player with no verified birth date shows none.

Trades and free agency are year-round areas under `Team_Operations`; their position in the file tree does not limit when transactions can occur. Staff records in `Early_Offseason` likewise keep their dated sources when an event falls later. Update an existing owner instead of creating a duplicate phase-specific transaction history.

## Close an event

1. Read current state, the team's calendar, the applicable plan and the relevant sources. Respect the active playbook iteration and dated information gates. The [2014 NFL calendar](../library/2014_nfl_calendar.md) provides league research; [Jacksonville's calendar](../career/2014/Calendar.md) supplies the team route. Actual historical opponents, dates, venues, byes and published amendments control every year. Branch standings determine draft order and playoff qualification.
2. Write the completed event in its domain owner. Keep its actual date, authority, conditions, uncertainty and consequences. A mixed date window may contain several separately dated events. Preserve prior facts and identify a correction where needed; do not silently alter a result or present a proposal as completed.
3. Attach the descriptive event metadata to that owner and its closure metadata to the record that closes the bounded progression. New phase metadata names the source event through `event_ref`. Legacy identifiers are resolved by the single compatibility map, not displayed as a second history. Generate the year's `Record.md` from the owners.
4. Update each genuinely affected current view, and open or resolve the affected decision-queue items, in the same commit. Reconcile control, contracts, known financial effects, medical instructions and roles even when an unrelated amount remains unknown. Never use an unknown figure as zero. Preserve unexecuted plans and frozen pre-result choices.
5. Reconcile Documents 4 and 5 checkpoints and source pointers. Document 4 may retain its older content version if none of its facts changed; Document 5 must name that exact version. After a foundation edit, refresh its Git-blob hash in Document 5 using `git hash-object`.
6. Refresh player ages after a master-date or roster change, and refresh the development cohort after any signing, departure, trade, draft addition or tender resolution. Verify a new player's identity and birth date before a game. Review updated reports and assessments against their evidence, run validation and the appropriate tests, and commit the complete dependency set through a pull request.

Phase metadata uses `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE`, its actual evidence-through date and the descriptive source event. Future reports keep null evidence dates and event references. From 2014, assessment is inside `training_report.md`; no separate phase assessment or summary receipt is created. Legacy 2013 phases that still have separate summaries retain their SHA-256 source receipts. For those only, review the actual summary first and then use `refresh_summary_receipt.py PHASE --reviewed`. That command acknowledges review; it does not generate observations or prove the prose correct.

## Games and their statistical evidence

`Run Week N` remains the complete one-command regular-season workflow defined in `AGENTS.md`. Read the week's actual plan, current roster and medical instructions, calendar, active books and existing output. Internal readiness preparation, an already-authorized migration, input construction, closure, statistics, PR bookkeeping and snapshot handling belong to the task. The user does not have to operate helper scripts. A genuinely new consequential Stone decision still returns to the user.

The [game-output workflow](game_output_workflow.md) governs the separate writing pass after the evidence is settled. The full statistics remain generated in the existing marked block. Football narrative and media dialogue are authored from selected supported events, with original questions and the [media method](head_coach_media_method.md), then reviewed for factual and voice fidelity. A presentation-only revision preserves the result receipt, event metadata, actual decisions, dated observations and current career state. Keep sole-source detail accessible at its existing owner, and collect technical qualifications once instead of repeating them in the narrative. Do not rerun a game or advance the clock to improve its report.

A template/process request changes the instructions for future authorized outputs; an existing report cited as an example remains untouched unless its rewrite is separately requested. Review both game templates and the shared method for conflicting instructions when changing the process. The editorial pass must check the account and complete media exchanges before delivery, even when all repository and statistical checks pass. Keep this review out of the football report itself.

Before a draw, `build_week_inputs.py N --season YEAR` freezes the full TeamInput package and checks player exclusivity, legal game-day roles and the scheduled game count. An ad-hoc package must pass `check_week_input_exclusivity.py PACKAGE --expected-games COUNT`. Jacksonville's actual control overrides conflicting historical personnel rails. Preserve the frozen `call_sheet.json`; a permitted technical rerun reuses its pre-result decisions.
