**Evidence cutoff:** Jacksonville's stored October 5, 2014 position after Week 5. The 2014 season has not been completed in this record. This page is neither a January 2015 brief that has been issued nor a decision Stone has made.

**Why this example exists:** To demonstrate how the review should read from real career facts, expose relevant football questions, and leave decisions open instead of supplying generic praise or predetermined departures.

## 2. Material football questions one might bring to Stone

The examples below are grounded in games that have already occurred, but they are **questions for the later complete-season review**, not judgments about how the rest of 2014 turns out.

**Quarterbacks and protection.** Against Pittsburgh, Kirk Cousins threw three interceptions in a game where he was not sacked. This does not identify whether Bates failed to prepare a read, the quarterback missed it, a route changed, or Pittsburgh created a turnover with correct coverage. Bates, Tice and Yarno's linked preparation records and the actual cutups should distinguish them before anyone assigns individual responsibility. If a cause remains absent, the staff report may say so without pretending the coach did not notice it.

**Tight ends.** Travis Kelce caught one ball on three targets in Week 5 and was the intended receiver on two intercepted throws. Zernhelt owns the tight-end room, but a target and an interception do not establish a bad route, release or instruction. The staff should examine which of the practiced receiving-Y roles actually reached the field and what was taught across the season. It must not conclude that Zernhelt succeeded or failed from this sample.

**Defense and calling authority.** Stone began calling Jacksonville's defense on September 28, 2014. Crennel continues to prepare, advise and transmit calls; he is not the live decision maker on those snaps. Pittsburgh converted 4 of 14 third downs, but finished with an eleven-play, eighty-yard touchdown drive without a pass attempt. The later review may examine Crennel's run-fit preparation, position-coach teaching and Stone's actual call choices separately. A poor defensive sequence does not make every staff member its cause.

**Special teams.** Westhoff calls the kicking game. The Week 5 punt coverage included a fifteen-yard face-mask penalty; Scobee made one of two field goals. Those are documented outcomes, not an established failed coaching instruction or operation fault. The staff review should examine the actual kicking and coverage responsibilities, not automatically assign them to Westhoff as individual errors.

The finished January report would then add each later game and the 2014 postseason if actually played, preserve contrary evidence, and identify which issues really warrant a staffing decision.

## 3. Sample of how an issued briefing could ask for a decision

> **Bates: quarterback-coach contract**  
> **Contract:** Runs through the 2014 season; exact expiration day must be verified.  
> **Football question:** How well did the quarterback room's protection identification and coverage preparation hold up over the full season? Who owns the different parts of recurring mistakes?  
> **Proposed decision:** Pursue new terms, choose not to pursue renewal, or defer until the specific missing cutups/review are available.  
> **Authority:** Stone determines whether Bates remains in his coaching plan; an extension needs actual contract terms, budget confirmation and Bates's agreement.  
> **Your response due:** Not established until contractual timing is verified.

This is a demonstration of format only. There is no supported conclusion that Bates should be renewed or dismissed.

## 5. Why there are separate staff results

Suppose that during a future **hypothetical** January week, the club concludes four distinct events:

- A renewal with Coach A is actually executed on Monday.
- Coach B declines a proposed extension on Tuesday, but remains employed until his valid existing term ends.
- A valid outside interview-permission request involving Coach C is granted on Wednesday.
- Coach D's existing contract actually expires on Friday without renewal.

The week has **four separate dated result files**, with the canonical contract/staff/permission owner for each. `Staff_Moves.md` displays four separate links. Coach B's declined extension is *not* a premature dismissal. A second club's new interview request for Coach C is another separately identifiable event, not a rewrite of Wednesday's decision.

This example intentionally avoids naming actual Jaguars or simulated 2015 teams for these imagined outcomes. No club hiring or future game result has been decided.

## 6. Related 2014 precedent already closed in this career

Alan Lowry left Jacksonville to become the Atlanta Falcons' head coach on **January 12, 2014**, creating a vacancy. Jacksonville hired Mike Westhoff on **February 11, 2014**. Those are two separate historical career events with their own existing records. They illustrate the desired result ownership: a departure is not the same as a replacement hire, and neither should be folded into a generic staff-update paragraph.
