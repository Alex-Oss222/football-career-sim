## OTAs and minicamp: repository assessment and blockers

### Assessment

**Well supported as a design:** Jacksonville's spring archive provides formation-specific, assignment-specific evidence, delayed retests and consequences for camp. The existing templates already distinguish Phase One classroom, Phase Two unopposed work, separate rookie minicamp, non-contact veteran OTAs and mandatory veteran minicamp. Stone's powers and spring teaching/medical boundaries are well established.

**Not implemented as the proposed workflow:** `runtime/decisions.py` registers `otas` with the exact fixed three names, but `implemented=False`, and its place resolves to the same `Offseason_Training/` folder as the April program. The existing file system contains the 2014 plans/reports, not the proposed three-page decision lifecycle for a future active season. No practice resolver exists, and no closed OTA issue/plan/report transaction has been built. The closed 2014 OTA and mandatory-minicamp reports record that the spring had no engine injury draw for practices and recorded only communicated medical facts; the Phase One, Phase Two and rookie-minicamp reports do not address the draw. The reports apply evidence separations in their observations (the OTA report's May 27 to 29 receivers row records "a release-timing loss with the assignment intact") but do not state a method. The authored, protagonist-blind method with the label-swap check was the Q-P proposal's own description; the user decided Q-P on October 20, 2026 and the facts-only method was adopted instead (blocker 1 below).

**Risk if implemented naively:** The third new page could copy every observation and become a second owner of practice evidence. Instead `OTA_Report.md` must be a **built view** of the two real phase owners: it shows or links their sections, with no observation or editable finding of its own.

### Specific blockers and decisions for implementation

1. **How 2015 practice is recorded: decided by the user, October 20, 2026.** The lead's method is adopted ([design, section 5](otas_and_minicamp.md#how-2015-practice-is-recorded)): each session records its date, type, equipment, participation as the medical staff communicated it, installs and periods run, and time on the field, and nothing about how a player looked. No practice resolver is needed. Session facts are not collected from the user. Never roll generic improvement or fill session reports from plan language.
2. **CBA calendar/attendance legality.** Enforce ten announced OTA slots versus days actually held, three plus three plus four phase-week limit, six-hour/two-hour OTA limit, separate physicals/minicamp limits including the two-a-day rules, the rookie program window, voluntary status, valid participation agreements and medical exceptions.
3. **Roster and staff as of each session.** End-of-2014 season and 2015 handoff have not occurred. A 2014 player/assistant cannot be automatically inserted into a 2015 scene just because his past page is available. In the [coach records](../data/people/coaches/) every 2014 position coach's contract ends with the 2014 season, while Tice's, Crennel's and Westhoff's run through 2015; who coaches in 2015 comes from the January staff decisions.
4. **Brief reissue and freeze.** `docs/event_inserts.md` requires immutable issued copies for consequential changes, not silently edited current briefs. The OTA Brief uses the one phase-brief issue history keyed by phase that the February and March briefs need, not a mechanism of its own; date and source cutoffs must remain available.
5. **Decision queue.** The generic `practice_plan` class has no spring-session block. Add a block kind for a spring session (season and date) and let independent league or contract work proceed.
6. **Department result routing.** The OTA Report links each rejected, accepted or abandoned trade, contract and staff result page, each built on the shared event result in its owner's folder. Each result is written on its own date, never deferred to the OTA Report or the end of minicamp, and never duplicated in the training folder.
7. **Open medical reports.** Unresolved treatment/return dates do not let a coach clear a player or put him in a period. Medical staff decide.
8. **Handoff:** The June minicamp close cannot authorize coached football through the summer; only the narrow Article 21 §6 exceptions and rookie development work inside its window are possible. Camp must reopen on its own actual schedule, named only once announced.
9. **Page hygiene:** The three pages in `Offseason_Training/OTAs/`, My Responses and the brief's saved editions need the clean-page rule, with machine identifiers and technical hashes solely in data or invisible validated metadata.
10. **Legacy immutability:** The 2014 ten OTAs, three mandatory-minicamp practices, dated observations and existing medical decisions have already occurred. Leave their bytes and historical claims intact.

### Potential conflict in older instructions

The 2014 spring archive records four players on unsigned tenders kept out of the program. The source is the user's May 1, 2014 instruction, given as Stone, which names Bradfield, Pasztor, Clemons and Brown and gives the reason that they were not under contract ([memo amendment](../career/2014/Early_Offseason/stone_to_caldwell_2014_offseason_decisions.md#september-30-2026-amendment-unsigned-tenders-and-the-cross-training-list)). This was an **individual 2014 decision**, not a categorical labor rule. The 2011 CBA allows an unsigned-tender player or an expired-contract UFA to participate after invitation and a signed participation agreement. From 2015, Stone decides whom to invite and Caldwell's office executes the agreement; the brief asks about each such player. A 2015 validator must check the agreement instead of importing the 2014 exclusion for all such players.
