# Writing a game turn

Adopted October 18, 2026 at the user's request to overhaul game reporting and media while preserving the existing career. This is an authoring workflow, not a new resolver, data schema or event owner. The [regular-season template](../foundation/templates/regular_season_output_template.md) retains Coach info and seven sections; the [preseason template](../foundation/templates/preseason_output_template.md) retains eight. Keep the complete receipt-derived statistics in their established place.

## 1. Read the event before writing

Read the applicable instructions, current season state, approved preparation and decisions, game receipt and public play record, medical changes and existing output. Use the actual season paths and active playbook. For a rewrite, work from the closed event: do not redraw, advance time, revise a user decision or add an activity that was never recorded.

Identify the football facts that matter, the authorized decisions, and any material unknowns. Retain preparation dates, already-recorded practice work, user wording and supporting sources. A style revision may reorganize that evidence; it must not silently turn documented work into an omission or use a plausible weekly schedule to invent missing work.

Keep event metadata and statistical markers intact. The full box score is generated from the receipt by the supported renderer. Existing factual record tables and score summaries may also be generated and verified. No script should fill narrative paragraphs, coaching explanations or dialogue by reciting fields.

## 2. Author each part for its purpose

Preparation explains the actual work and choices that brought the team to kickoff. Preserve the dated sequence and material restrictions without an administrative recital.

The game narrative explains how the result happened, using the template's chronology. Choose the sequences and numbers that make the account intelligible. The full scoring and drive accounting is already in the statistics. Do not infer blocking, route, coverage or communication causes from a result alone.

The coaching review develops supported findings about the units, actual personnel combinations and decisions. Give detailed work space when there is evidence for it, including contrary evidence and the opponent's contribution. A missing technical cause does not require the same disclaimer under every player or unit. State a shared evidence limit once in supporting notes; mention it in the analysis where it changes the conclusion.

Media follows the [head-coach media method](head_coach_media_method.md): original questions from information public at that time, responsive follow-ups and Stone's authorized positions in his established voice. A rewrite can improve the language of already-authored routine answers; it cannot overwrite the user's actual words or invent consequential choices.

Carry-forward names the actual or proposed work resulting from the review, with the responsible coach and next opportunity when established. Personnel sections show roles, availability and material changes. They do not repeat every statistical line or replace the roster owner. Closure gives the current record, useful links and next event. Internal execution details stay with their existing technical owners.

Keep the main section order. Subordinate prompts are questions for the author, not content quotas. Omit empty optional findings, repetitive lists and stock explanations. Do not force a controversy, a player award, praise and criticism in equal amounts, a certain number of questions or a target word count.

## 3. Use research where it helps

The [media research](head_coach_media_method.md#5-research-basis-and-limits) and [game-report research](preseason_game_reports.md) supply inspected references for form and football explanation. Use those references without pretending to have researched them again. Read an additional relevant source when an unfamiliar situation calls for it; research and verify a material rule or factual gap under the existing sourcebook requirements.

Do not copy transcript questions and swap names. Do not borrow real results, diagnoses or player assessments to fill this career. A real transcript is evidence of its own conversation only. Keep new writing references in compact supporting notes outside the dialogue, together with existing evidence links. No per-question citations, per-section source quotas or routine research-status lines are needed.

## 4. Review the football, voice and record separately

First read the authored account without the statistics. Can the reader understand the game and the work? Cut repeated statistical summaries, generic significance claims and machine terminology. Read the Q&A aloud: questions should seek information, answers should respond, and follow-ups should depend on what was said. Natural length variation is allowed; artificial terseness is not a quality check.

Then verify the actual claims. Check score and chronology, names and participation, the information available at each session, medical authority, announced roles, and the meaning of Stone's decisions. Preserve uncertainty where it matters. Do not add a claim merely because a heading suggests it.

Finally use the existing repository and statistical checks. Preserve exact generated-box-score agreement and source metadata. A presentation-only edit retains the event identity, result and career date. Update dependent source bindings only where the existing validation rules require it. Do not refresh a summary hash without reviewing the summary against the revised output. Follow [the update workflow](update_workflow.md); no new approval gate, linter or state format is introduced here.
