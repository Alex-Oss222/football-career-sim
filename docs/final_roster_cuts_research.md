### Two independent verification passes and explicit conflict resolution

**Pass 1:** the original Article 29, 30 and 33 text, the NFLPA's August 19, 2014 expansion announcement and the league's 2015 schedule bulletins as the library records them (J and K). **Pass 2:** the cutdown and September 6 times checked against separately retrieved calendar transcriptions (L and M) and the library's own two-pass record. The NFLPA agreement controls the ten-player practice squad, not a modern website's current rules.

The library records the one conflict in its own words: "An inaccessible Raiders PDF search extract showed a conflicting final-cutdown hour; it does not override the agreeing contemporaneous bulletin copies." The design uses **4 p.m. ET** and keeps the discrepancy for anyone checking a league-office original. For the designated-for-return window the library says: "L supplies September 6 noon claims, the approximately 1 p.m. personnel notice, and IR-return placement after 4 p.m. M independently confirms the IR date. Do not use September 8 from another season." It would be wrong to choose a conflicting hour silently or to use a 2016 or 2022 calendar.

### Necessary football decisions, as opposed to roster paperwork

A roster cut raises real football problems, not a universal theory of who should be cut. An injury can raise the value of a lineman who can cover both tackle sides and keep the offense working. Similar questions exist for center, quarterback, defensive communication, nickel and dime, and the core coverage units. A fourth-team receiver's summer catch count without exposure to coverage or returns cannot by itself decide his roster worth. The pages must show what job is lost with a particular cut, the evidence actually available and who has the authority.

Stone, under his head-coach role, decides depth, roles and game-day inactives. Caldwell decides player retention, contracts, claims, practice-squad signings, trades and cutdown compliance, on Stone's recommendation. The medical staff decide clearance. Other clubs' waiver claims and free-agent signings follow the rails, and a player's choice between Jacksonville and a club he really signed with is decided only by the market draw. A user recommendation may shape Caldwell's information; it never grants a contract by assertion.

### Error boundaries that materially affect results

- No real 2015 Jaguars cuts, retirements (except the repository's date-gated league-wide retirement rule), practice-squad names, draft or free-agency additions are copied into 2015 career personnel.
- Other clubs' players follow the dated rails. Jacksonville players are protected from being overwritten; a player who leaves Jacksonville takes his real next move only when it is the same kind of move in the same window, and is otherwise unplaced until an actual authorized move.
- 'Waived with the injured designation' is not the same event as 'placed on injured reserve': a claim can intervene. Medical diagnosis is not a coach's narrative choice.
- The first 75 stage can produce **zero cuts**; it still requires a legal count. The final 53 is a roster limit, not a requirement to carry a fixed number of quarterbacks, backs, linebackers, corners or specialists.
- Each independently concluded event gets its own result, including a failed claim, a rejected trade, a withdrawn offer and a practice-squad approach lost in the market draw. Final 53 is the roster as filed on September 5, frozen, not an omnibus disposition report.
- The 2015 game-day, practice-squad and reserve rules must be the **2015** rules: no later roster elevations, 48-player game day or later IR return rules. The 2015 game-day figure is Unverified until the new rules entry (46 of 53 is confirmed for 2014).

## Final Roster Cuts: repository assessment and unresolved implementation requirements

This assessment records the repository as the process check found it on October 20, 2026. The one list of what the build must add, with its steps, is [section 3 of the design](final_roster_cuts.md#3-repository-inspection-and-the-build); this section does not keep a second one.

### Actual established functionality

- `runtime.decisions.REGISTRY` names `Roster Cutdown Report`, `My Cut Recommendations` and `Final 53` as the `final_cuts` three-page workflow, **not implemented**. Its shared place resolves to `Training_Camp_and_Preseason/`, while the folder map keeps roster decisions in `Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Roster_Decisions/`.
- The 2014 career has one completed `final_roster_cuts.md` with the real August 26 and 30 to 31 dates, a separate `roster_decisions.md` for Stone's depth and leadership choices, and linked roster, trade and medical records. They stay as written and are not a 2015 answer key.
- The six 2014 position-battle cards closed August 30. The depth data own the current depth (`game_depth_chart.json`, `working_depth_chart.json` and `update_log.json` in `data/<year>/Team_Operations/Team/Depth_Chart/`), and `render_club_pages.py` builds `depth_chart.md` from them; the permanent roster and contract records own the other facts. A new `Final_53.md` is built from those sources at the filing, never kept as a second editable roster.
- `runtime.events` and `scripts/render_annual_record.py` support dated events at their owner and a built annual index. `docs/event_inserts.md` sets the one reissue rule and one result per resolved event. The routing for 2015 cutdown events and the new queue blocks is **not demonstrated**.
- `state/current_job.json` holds authority: final roster cuts belong to Caldwell as a labelled inference from his personnel authority (row 7), Stone's practice authority is also a labelled inference (row 11), and Stone's depth control is an explicit contract term (row 9).

### True blockers before an implementation could be declared complete

1. **Destination conflict:** the `final_cuts` decision resolves to the parent `Training_Camp_and_Preseason/`, not `Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Roster_Decisions/`. A route for this decision only is needed, without moving the camp or preseason-game pages.
2. **The 2015 roster does not exist yet at a playable in-world date.** The rest of 2014 is unplayed, and no 2015 signings, draft selections, medical results, camp work or decisions have occurred. The tool must refuse to populate them from the real Jaguars' history.
3. **No cutdown writer or gate.** The repository has the 2014 roster moves and their owners, but no 2015 pipeline that takes Caldwell's decision, distinguishes waived, released, waived with the injured designation and reserve rights, validates each contract, commits the effects and writes each player's result in one step.
4. **Claims.** The method for other clubs' claims on Jacksonville's waived players exists: a waived Jaguar goes to a club only if his real next move was a claim in that window, and otherwise clears (the 2014 record, August 31; rails rule 6). That is a reusable rule, not a reused outcome. No method existed for Jacksonville's own claims on players other clubs waived; the design now decides them by the waiver order (design rule 18), and the order itself still needs its two-pass rules row and its computation from the career's standings.
5. **Three service counts:** Article 29 and Article 30 use Plan credited service; the finance inputs hold an Article 26 credited-season count, which reads "Unresolved" in 43 of its 90 rows; the practice-squad class uses accrued seasons. A validator that checks the wrong count can turn a termination into a waiver, or the reverse. The 2014 record's accrued-season test is not copied; John Parker Wilson, waived in 2014 with at least three accrued seasons, has four Article 26 credited seasons in the finance inputs, and the 2014 record stays as written.
6. **Two deadlines, not one.** The September 1 count must be complete before the September 3 game. The September 5 filing and the September 6 waiver and practice-squad stage have distinct dated statuses. The preseason roster limit today is 90 for every preseason game; game four must be checked against 75.
7. **The next game is not the 53.** The 53 can hold medically restricted or unavailable players, while the Week 1 game-day list has its own limit and Stone's inactives. A removed player's role goes to his ranked successor, or to Stone when no successor is ranked (design rule 9); nothing on Final 53 clears a player medically.
8. **Individual event records and idempotence:** one result per release, per rejected trade, per failed claim and per practice-squad approach lost in the market draw. Each is readable at its own date and survives later changes. The phase index cannot stand in for 22 releases.
9. **Financial effects:** each effect comes from the recovered or adopted schedule, labelled a reconstruction where adopted; a field is unresolved only where no term exists. Termination pay cannot arise at the reductions; it applies only after the first regular-season game.
10. **Roster and count classification:** unsigned tenders, PUP and NFI, reserve lists and waivers must be classified for 2015 from the rules and the player record. The 2014 '74 signed and four unsigned rights' result is not a 2015 formula.
11. **Publication and clean-page rules:** the pages ask about the coach's real choice, with no identifiers, code or invented personnel grades. Every issued edition of the Roster Cutdown Report is kept under the one reissue rule.

### What should **not** be altered by integration

The 2013 and 2014 results, the completed 2014 cut names and dispositions, Stone's 2014 depth decisions, the real league-date sources, the 2014 Week 6 readiness gates and the game engine. Do not trade correctness for a shorter workflow: a date, a roster count and an independently resolved player case cannot be invented or skipped.

**Conclusion for integration review:** the design has the period rule structure and the verified 2014 precedent to build later, but the repository does *not* yet hold the 2015 cutdown writer, the 2015 roster, the claim and waiver-order computation or a live user decision that would close this workflow. Tests have been specified, not run.
