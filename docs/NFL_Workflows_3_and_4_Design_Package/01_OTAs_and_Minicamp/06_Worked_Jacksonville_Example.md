# Jacksonville example: completed 2014 spring, with 2015 left open

**Illustration, not a new in-world briefing or amended decision.** Every football finding below comes from already closed 2014 Jacksonville reports. The example shows how the proposed three pages would have read when their respective facts became available. It must not change the actual 2014 files, put the 2014 class into 2015, or create an unplayed 2015 OTA.

## A. OTA Brief, modelled as of May 23, 2014

# OTA Brief | Jacksonville Jaguars | 2014

**For:** Alex Stone  
**From:** Mike Tice, Romeo Crennel, Mike Westhoff and their position coaches  
**Preparation date in this example:** May 23, 2014  
**Next work:** May 27-29; June 2-3 and 5; June 9-10 and 12-13; mandatory minicamp June 17-19

### What the coaches need to see

The first two phases have given Jacksonville one agreed language for identifying the protection point and for handling the free defender outside Boot Flood's launch path. In the classroom, Bates and Yarno removed a disagreement about the double A-gap picture. During the permitted separate-unit work in May, Cousins, Brewster and the backs could identify the shared point while moving without a defense playing against them. That is where the evidence ends.

The first OTA periods can put a changing defense in front of the offense. What matters is whether the center makes the correct point, whether the guard and back echo what changes their job, and whether the whole group sends HALF toward the actual overload. The coordinator should identify the front change and record the answer without supplying it first. This work will still be non-contact. The staff cannot decide which guard wins a block from it.

On Boot Flood, Tice and Bates now have the common free-edge instruction from the April review. The next test is a live presentation by Crennel's defense, particularly whether the tight end's release changes the deep and intermediate timing from Wing. Lewis's release and the quarterback's throwaway are linked assignments, not separate target totals.

On defense, the May 14 bunch rehearsal had exposed two answers about the point receiver; Oden corrected the rule and the group gave one answer on May 15 and again May 20. Opposing receivers in the OTA periods should test whether the correct exchange survives a different release. Crennel's group also needs to relay the full call with Posluszny and Stanford as the communicators. Whether a defender actually finishes the tackle remains a camp question.

Westhoff can put the snap, kick, protection alignment and coverage/return units together legally for the first time. Both Cain and Kreiter have separate unopposed operation evidence; neither should be declared the long snapper merely from a clean delivery without a contact rush.

**Stone's choice:** Specify the OTA installation and repetitions for these tests, including the first and alternate quarterback/line combinations. No roster move is requested, and no medical status is to be decided by Stone.

### The rule and schedule

Ten announced OTA days, helmets without shells, legal seven-on-seven, nine-on-seven and eleven-on-eleven; no live contact or opposed one-on-ones. The May 16-17 rookie minicamp has its own record. The veteran minicamp is June 17-19 after its own June 16 physicals.

## B. My Install Plan: established decisions rather than an invented response

There is **no separate May 23 written answer from Stone in the current records** that could be quoted as if he submitted this new form. The authorized directions for the completed 2014 program are already recorded in his April 18 decision and the staff plans that flowed from it:

- Keep Mike Tice responsible for coordinating offense and running an approved practice if Stone is absent; Romeo Crennel is the second emergency lead. Neither acquires new depth or installation authority simply because of Stone's absence.
- Continue the shared center-quarterback-back protection teaching. Carry Boot Flood's free-edge answer to the field rather than treating the board solution as game execution.
- On defense, identify TAKE, GIVE and ANSWER for each call; rotate the full communication responsibility among the designated personnel without changing the actual playing roles. Daryl Smith remains the primary defensive communicator in this period, with Posluszny and Stanford the stated backups under the then-current roster.
- Cross-train selected players only in a bounded secondary responsibility after their primary job; newer veterans start with their first Jacksonville job.
- Westhoff owns the punt operation and the four game-situation families; evaluate operation, kick and coverage separately.
- Keep injured players involved in the mental work the medical department permits. Voluntary participation and optional family gatherings do not become an indirect depth-chart test.

The exact later June 2014 install actually executed includes Counter Boot, Sprint Flood and Y-Cross, according to the completed OTA evidence. This example is not a claim that Stone personally dictated an unrecorded May 23 priority statement naming those three concepts. Implementation must preserve that distinction.

## C. OTA Report: facts known at the June 20, 2014 handoff

# OTA Report | Jacksonville Jaguars | 2014

**OTAs held:** Ten non-contact sessions, May 27-June 13  
**Minicamp:** June 16 physicals; practices June 17-19; staff handoff June 20

### Offense

The separate teaching of Boot Flood survived the first OTA look. But on May 28 a walked-up defender affected Lewis's release from Wing, and the deep and intermediate routes lost their timing. Zernhelt revisited the release. The adjustment held the following day and after the June 2 break. This establishes the release and timing answer in the recorded non-contact periods, not a win against press contact.

Protection presented two different problems. Turner failed to echo a late front change until the cadence began on May 28; the group retaught point, echo and confirmation, and his answer was on time May 29 and June 2. On June 9, during a two-minute period, the HALF slide went the wrong way against a late overload. An unblocked rusher reached the launch point before the whistle; the record did not establish a full-contact sack. The later June 12 check was correct. Minicamp was required because the line had asked for sideline drawings and still had to demonstrate the late change unaided.

On June 17, Cousins, Brewster and the guards set and echoed the protection answer before the snap against the late picture. They reproduced it June 19 without a drawing or prompt. Linsley and Henne still had a separate late-front timing problem on one relief repetition. The first correction did not erase the second.

Y-Cross from 12 personnel had put the cross and dig at the same depth on June 5. Bates and Drake corrected the stagger; the routes held their separation June 9 and 12 and again at minicamp from 11 personnel. This is a dated route-combination finding, not a guaranteed receiver development claim.

### Defense

On May 28, Poyer and Moore took the same stack crosser and left the flat open. Oden and Bush corrected the exchange, which was carried on May 29, June 2 and June 12. Stanford's fast-motion relay was late May 29, then on time June 10 with the more senior communicators out of his group. Crennel's new five-man Nickel Even pressure gave up the short flat on June 5; the report identifies that pass as its planned concession, not a coverage bust.

Minicamp raised a different coverage concern: Stanford lost leverage on the back's wheel route June 18. His repeat was sound June 19; the club also demonstrated its Cover 3 alternative. That does not certify the matchup against an opponent at game speed or after contact.

### Special teams

Westhoff's recorded Cain and Kreiter operation remained without an identified snap or hold fault in the non-contact spring work. Both remained candidates; contact rush and game operation were still missing.

### Questions handed to camp

Protection adjustment under physical rush, left-guard and right-guard leverage against contact, receiver press releases, Kelce's attached blocking, edge rush with contain intact, the wheel-route matchup, tackling and long-snapper operation under an actual rush. The completed 2014 reports did not award any of these competitions merely because spring ended.

### Individual events

This short example does not invent a June transaction, medical release or hiring search. Any genuine separately resolved event that had happened in the window would appear as its own dated document at its transaction or staff owner, with a link here. The spring practice accounts are separate phase owners and not substitutes for those event results.
