# Training camp and position battles: 2011 CBA and contemporary source register

**Research scope:** NFL practice rules and coaching evaluation methods applicable during 2014–15. Sources establish era rules, actual league scheduling, the difference between spring and camp evaluation, and how teams publicly discuss contested roles. They do **not** import real 2015 Jacksonville draft selections, injuries, personnel, results or coaching decisions into Stone's simulated career.

## Primary governing agreement

**NFL and NFLPA, [2011 Collective Bargaining Agreement, signed August 4, 2011](https://nflps.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/collective-bargaining-agreement-2011-2020.pdf)**. Original contemporary agreement hosted by the NFL Physicians Society, not the later 2020 CBA or a current article transcription.

- **Article 23, §6, printed pages 140–141, PDF pages approximately 155–156 in one-based indexing:** camp first day is examinations, meetings, classroom work and permissible conditioning, not team drills or pads; no contact/pads on days two and three; subsequent practices restricted to four on-field hours per player, one padded period not more than three hours, second session walkthrough only and the required intersession rest. Consult the actual PDF pagination when implementing. The rule grants eligible practice procedures, not guaranteed contact work at the first opportunity.
- **Article 21, printed pages 131–135:** voluntary offseason work/Phase Three contact restrictions differ sharply from camp. Non-contact spring protection and coverage against an opposed team can identify assignments but is not a padded camp rep. Keep earlier report evidence within the permitted spring inventory.
- **Article 23, reporting/roster and rest clauses:** check camp reporting, veteran/rookie exemptions and off-day obligations in the original selected-era agreement. Modern club media or a future CBA summary cannot authorize an outdated schedule.

**Important source warning:** The present-day Over The Cap webpage titled “Article 23” at `https://overthecap.com/collective-bargaining-agreement/article/23` publishes later CBA terms (e.g., days two through five restrictions and different practice lengths). Do not use that current transcription in place of the original 2011 rules for the simulator's 2014–15 era.

## Uncertainty and source hygiene

1. The 2011 CBA has a legally meaningful year-specific practice inventory. Do not silently substitute today's NFLPA/Over The Cap wording. The original 2011 PDF is controlling; a 2020-era online transcription is a warning, not independent confirmation.
2. Other clubs' historically real roster transactions operate under the user's restricted historical-league-rails exception and date gate. This cannot supply Jacksonville's 2015 player assessments or injury decisions.
3. An unknown 2015 late-August daily camp timetable stays unknown. Public initial camp announcements stop August 12. The simulated program can only schedule additional lawful activities on actual recorded Stone decisions.
4. No private coach conversations, player feedback, diagnostic details or unrecorded film outcomes can be cited as if recorded. Dated public football context and dated simulated private evidence are distinct sources.
