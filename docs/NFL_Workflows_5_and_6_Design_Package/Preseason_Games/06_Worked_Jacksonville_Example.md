## Example A: game one, August 8, 2014, Tampa Bay at Jacksonville

### Scouting/staff briefing material legitimately available before that game

**Venue:** EverBank Field. **Kickoff:** Friday August 8, 7:30 p.m. ET.

The existing camp plan gave Stone concrete comparisons, not a demand for a generic 'strong performance'. The first two Cousins possessions were intended to change two receiver/guard arrangements while preserving the quarterback and other support: initially Nicks at X, Adams in the WR3 work and Bitonio at left guard, then Adams at X, Hawkins in WR3 work and Norwell at left guard, with Turner remaining at right guard. That work sought route timing, protection assignments and blocking against an outside opponent.

Crennel's defense sought first-group edge work for Mincey and Branch, then communicators Daryl Smith, Posluszny and Stanford in the units actually available. Westhoff planned Cain to begin snapping, with Kreiter receiving live specialist opportunities. These came from Stone's recorded Game 1 plan and frozen game inputs, not a staff meeting invented for this illustration.

**Decision boundary:** A report could legitimately request Stone's game plan, rotation and legal use of medically available players. It could not force a final WR1, left-guard or long-snapper decision ahead of the exhibition.

### Stone's recorded game plan, not a new answer

Stone's authored plan placed Cousins in the two comparison possessions subject to the recorded snap ceiling; Henne and the later quarterbacks followed in named blocks when possessions allowed. The retained offensive menu was reduced and frozen after the August 5 to 7 practices. The first defense kept the narrow carried menu and the relay sequence. No outside draft/player target was signed by the game plan.

### What the actual game report supports

**Tampa Bay 30, Jacksonville 21 (career result).** The two Cousins possessions produced six snaps and two sacks, both at the right-tackle slot. This did **not** test the intended X or left-guard receiving comparison as designed: neither X had a target in his respective first-group possession, and the sacks did not identify which part of the protection caused them. It would be a factual error to score Nicks/Adams or Bitonio/Norwell from those drives as if each played a comparable passing-game sample.

The existing 2014 account records three first-defense touchdowns allowed, three later Jacksonville scoring possessions led by Henne, Wilson and Shaw, seven Jacksonville sacks, and three Jaguars turnovers. The game generated medical incidents for Lane Johnson, Cameron Brate and C.J. Anderson. Their exact availability and any roster ramifications belong in the medical and personnel records, not an automatic deduction from the final score. No position competition was closed on August 8.

**A useful coaching finding:** Jacksonville needed a right-tackle correction with the protection call and quarterback response, not a new verdict about left guard. The staff could carry the receiver comparison into Chicago if legal and desired, and Stone could choose a new plan. No argument about 'momentum' changes what the six first-offense snaps showed.

## Example B: game four, August 28, 2014, Atlanta at Jacksonville

### The actual brief-to-plan question

The closed August 23 to 27 preparations put the starters' first-quarter operation together, and Stone's game-four instruction kept Cousins through the second quarter behind a developmental offensive line after the starters left. In that young group Leno, Norwell, Linsley, Feiler and Lucas were to work together, with Kelce as a receiving component. The first defense was to give way gradually to reserves and rookies. Kreiter began the specialist operation; Cain followed.

Before kickoff the 2014 reduction to 75 had already been satisfied with **74 contracted players**, without a transaction. Four unsigned tenders remained outside the signed roster count. Their lack of a signed participation basis was an actual 2014 career fact, not a universal statement that an unsigned tendered player can never participate.

### Completed result and narrow conclusions

**Atlanta 31, Jacksonville 13 (career result).** The starting offense's first quarter ended in three punts. Cousins also worked with the younger group in the second quarter and finished **12 of 20 for 123 yards** across his work. His developmental group had an 80-yard touchdown drive finished by C.J. Anderson, and Jacksonville added Scobee's field goal. Kelce caught two passes on three targets for 26 yards in the receiving work. Henne led a third-quarter field-goal possession; Atlanta's second unit scored twice against Jacksonville's backups.

Christian Jones forced and recovered an Atlanta turnover. Edebali had Jacksonville's recorded sack. Scobee made two field goals and a try. Hurns returned a punt 36 yards. Those observations support specific performance and opportunity, not universal player-development gains.

The game reported injuries to Hakeem Nicks, A.J. Bouye, Gator Hoskins and Jeris Pendleton. Nicks's multi-week restriction materially affected the subsequent X decision, but the game record itself assigned no receiver fault that proved Adams was the better X. Four preseason games between Cain and Kreiter yielded 40 combined specialist snaps without a charged fault; Kreiter's later appointment was Stone's depth decision with Caldwell's roster transaction, not a fabricated statistical tie-break.

**At the end of August 28:** all six actual position competitions remained open; Caldwell's final roster reduction and Stone's season depth decisions were still ahead. The career resolved them August 30 and recorded them at their own owners. Do not move those decisions backward into the preseason report.
