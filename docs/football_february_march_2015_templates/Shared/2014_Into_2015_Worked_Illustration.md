## How an individual output differs from a phase result

The 2014 career already contains verified examples of **independent** events:

- Eugene Monroe received a 2014 franchise designation, later replaced by a five-year signed agreement dated March 11, 2014. The designation and contract are two separate legal actions, each with its actual date and rights/accounting effect.
- Golden Tate declined Jacksonville's recorded five-year proposal of up to $32.5 million, and Jacksonville did not acquire him. That is a concluded negotiation, not proof that every proposed free agent becomes a Jag.
- Julian Edelman likewise declined Jacksonville's recorded four-year proposal up to $19 million and stayed with New England. He did not occupy a Jacksonville roster slot or create a Jaguars cap charge.
- Alterraun Verner signed a four-year, $29 million Jacksonville agreement in the 2014 career. The contract has actual dated status, economic terms and a club-control consequence.

These 2014 completed transactions are from `career/2014/Team_Operations/Free_Agency/signings.md` and its negotiation owners. **They are history, not templates to copy into future player decisions**. Their substantive lesson is that a GM can sign, counter, lose a bidding contest or deliberately end talks, and those outcomes have different roster and cap consequences.

## What March 2015 should ask without predicting it

If the fully reviewed 2014 roster no longer has a dependable outside corner reserve, the Pro Personnel Report should say which actual reserve combinations were tried, what assignment the new corner must handle, whether it is a starting or depth job, and which *legally reachable* players meet it. It should not announce a future free-agent name or a 'three-year $12 million' offer without sourcing that market estimate.

Stone's My Free-Agent Priorities could say, *after seeing that real brief*: 'Look first for a reserve who can handle outside alignment and our match coverage, but don't guarantee a starting role. I'd prefer to solve another position first if the difference in price is large.' The front office can pursue, pass, or fail to reach agreement. No signing happens because a sentence in My page names the job.
