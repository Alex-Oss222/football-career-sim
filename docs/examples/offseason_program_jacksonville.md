## A. Illustrative Offseason Program Brief, Jacksonville, before April 21, 2014

# Offseason Program Brief

**Jacksonville Jaguars | 2014 offseason program**  
**Prepared for:** Head Coach Alex Stone, before the April 21, 2014 start  
**Presented by:** Mike Tice, Romeo Crennel, Mike Westhoff and the performance staff  
**Program:** Phase One begins April 21; Phase Two begins May 5

### The work in front of us

This offense already has an established run and passing-game vocabulary. The specific spring questions are more useful than starting another installation from scratch. The offense includes Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Stick, Drive, Mesh and Boot Flood. Boot Flood has a point of stress at the free edge of the rollout path. The staff has to teach one answer for the quarterback and the intermediate and flat receivers and agree on how that answer relates to the protection. The previous 2013 examples are available for film study; the April teaching has not happened yet.

The protection identification work also needs to make the quarterback, center and backs consistent. The center's declaration must identify the point, and the other players should repeat only changes that affect their assignments. A board discussion can reveal disagreement about two A-gap threats. It cannot show whether that recognition will survive an actual rush. This is a classroom problem first, an unopposed movement question in May and a different test once offense and defense can legally work against each other.

On defense, Crennel wants each front and coverage call taught with three questions: what we remove, what we concede and what answer changes when the offense changes its look. The communication work involves Daryl Smith, Paul Posluszny and Julian Stanford, including how the group passes motion and strength information. Westhoff is responsible for punt teaching, with a field-position punt, a plus-territory punt, a backed-up punt and an end-game punt treated as different decisions and operations. The special-teams film review will separate the snap and operation, the kick and the coverage.

### Who can work

The spring participant plan cannot treat an unsigned tender as an executed veteran contract. The existing 2014 status record identifies four unsigned tender players, Cameron Bradfield, Clemons, Brown and Austin Pasztor, who are not to be given program work without a valid basis. The record also identifies Will Rackley's current upper-extremity restriction. That is not authority to excuse him from permitted classroom learning or to assign him physical work contrary to the medical staff. Posluszny was cleared April 5; his actual availability is still subject to any later communication.

The Phase One room may study plays and discuss film. Strength, conditioning and rehabilitation work is run by the permitted staff, separately from position-coach physical observation. There will be no coached punt operation or snapping on the field during Phase One. Phase Two can move formation, protection, pursuit, routes and kicking-unit work onto the field on a separates basis, still without helmets, contact or offense versus defense.

### What needs the head coach's direction

The staff needs the priority order for the limited periods, the emergency practice lead if Stone is absent, the defensive communication backup, the extent of injured-player classroom work, which existing players can carry an emergency second assignment, and the review rhythm. Stone's existing contract gives him the practice and coaching decision, not the signing authority over the unsigned tender players. No coach is authorized to convert classroom work into a promised depth-chart decision.

## B. Illustrative My Program Plan, from Stone's adopted April 18, 2014 direction

# My Program Plan

**Jacksonville Jaguars | Alex Stone | Direction recorded April 18, 2014**

### Football I want taught

Keep the currently authorized offensive and defensive books in front of the players. Teach protection as a connected quarterback, center and running-back responsibility. Return Boot Flood to the room, including the free-edge answer. Crennel's packages must each have a defined TAKE, GIVE and ANSWER, so the player understands what the call protects and what it deliberately leaves available. These are not reasons to install an entirely new system in April.

Westhoff owns the punt unit; I own the decision to punt. Teach the four different purposes for a punt: field position, plus territory, backed up and end game. Grade every punt in three separate pieces: operation, kick and coverage. On the spring progression, in my April 18 words:

> The spring progression has to respect the CBA. In Phase One, punters can kick but cannot work with a snapper/holder, while long snappers may snap into a net. Phase Two permits individual and "perfect play" work without offense-vs-defense competition; Phase Three permits non-contact unit work against an opposing look.

*The verified Phase One rule allows no footballs, so all Phase One punt and snap instruction stayed in the classroom; Phase Two moved the legal separate-unit work onto the field.*

### How the staff operates

If I am absent from a scheduled practice, Tice runs the approved session. If Tice also cannot do it, Crennel takes that responsibility. The emergency lead keeps the periods on time and handles ordinary situational adjustments. He cannot take the occasion to change the depth chart, make a role or personnel decision or introduce a new family of calls. Crennel runs his defensive work, Westhoff the special teams work, and medical personnel have the final say about availability.

Daryl Smith is the first defensive communicator, with Posluszny and then Stanford in the established backup progression when they are medically and tactically available. Stanford should receive full-call and motion-check work, not merely recite terminology. Sio Moore gets responsibility for his own packages but is not automatically installed as the whole-defense backup.

An injured player stays involved in the permitted football teaching. If the limitation is physical only, the player gets the full meeting-room program and medical personnel control the physical work. If the medical team limits cognitive work, then the amount of film and classroom work changes accordingly. There is no coaching override of medical instructions.

Use secondary positions selectively. Bradfield can work at both tackles when eligible. Pasztor's guard and emergency identification work is a targeted evaluation, not a promise of a new position. Thielen, Kelce, Poyer, Sio Moore, Prosinski and Trawick have specified secondary looks in the established April 18 list. Primary work takes the large majority of useful teaching opportunities, particularly for newcomers learning Jacksonville's terminology for the first time. These looks do not settle a depth chart.

Keep a short first-active-day meeting with Tice, Crennel and Westhoff, follow useful field sessions with a focused review, and close each week with the coaches who must fix the next problem. Player feedback has a place. Optional dinners and family events remain genuinely voluntary; nobody keeps attendance for football judgment. The club and staff still operate inside all 2014 CBA limits.

**How a later change is recorded.** This page is answered once and kept. Under the new format, the May 1 instruction on the four unsigned tenders, which kept them out of the program and its social events until they signed, closed Bradfield's and Pasztor's lanes and revised the cross-training list, would be a dated entry on the April My Responses page; the plan above stays as written.
