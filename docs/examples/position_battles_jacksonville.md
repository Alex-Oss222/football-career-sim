# A. Position Battle Report

**Jacksonville Jaguars | Training camp, 2014**  
**Prepared for:** Head coach Alex Stone  
**Presented by:** Mike Tice and Romeo Crennel, the coordinators, Mike Westhoff for special teams, and the relevant position coaches  
**Issued:** August 29, 2014, after the Atlanta review  
**Edition:** First issue at this review point  
**Next decision point:** The coaching depth chart and Caldwell's separate reduction to 53 on August 30

## The decisions at issue

Six actual comparisons remain open pending Stone's depth call: X/WR1 (Hakeem Nicks and Davante Adams); Z/WR3 (Adams and Andrew Hawkins); left guard (Joel Bitonio and Andrew Norwell); the conditional right-guard alternative involving Trai Turner; Edge 1 (Jeremy Mincey and Andre Branch); and long snapper (Jeremy Cain and Casey Kreiter). Their open status does not mean the staff has evidence that every starter should change. A trial in one preseason game is not itself a depth-chart appointment.

## Offense

**X receiver: Nicks or Adams.** Nicks's spring and camp record has the steadier timing in selected quick work. Adams improved his release-to-route timing after the correction noted in camp, but his individual press reps remained mixed. Both took an X series with Kirk Cousins in the preseason; the game record did not isolate a cause for the interceptions thrown toward Nicks or produce a comparable receiver matchup tally. In the August 28 Atlanta game Nicks caught his early short target and sustained a multi-week injury. Adams caught three balls at X with Cousins's young group, including two third-down conversions. That is not equivalent opponent, teammate or opportunity context. It does mean Stone needs a workable early-season X arrangement while the medical staff's restriction on Nicks continues. The coaches cannot clear him.

**Z receiver: Adams or Hawkins.** Adams also worked this second role; Hawkins kept the relevant assignment and route depth when used at Z/slot, and Westhoff used him in the return operation. Comparing one player's X work with another player's Z work would misstate the test. Whichever arrangement Stone selects must account for the rest of the receiving unit rather than assume one player's performance at WR1 settles WR3.

**Left guard: Bitonio or Norwell.** Bitonio, beside Mike Brewster in the first group, anchored against Sen'Derrick Marks in the July 30 controlled-contact work, but over-set once and gave up an inside path; George Yarno corrected the set angle, and it held on the later August 1 and August 2 work. Norwell carried an ACE combination with Brewster to the linebacker. His initial leverage loss came against Roy Miller on July 30, and he recovered without the pocket collapsing; in the August 2 series Marks walked him back once in protection, and he recovered before the whistle. In four preseason games neither was charged with a loss at left guard. Bitonio had first-unit work, Norwell also had first-unit and relief combinations. This evidence does not produce a statistical winner. It lets Stone choose the five-man combination he wants, with Norwell still providing useful guard depth.

**Right guard:** Trai Turner kept the starting trial. The conditional alternative discussed earlier was tied to a demonstrated problem; the recorded August 1 and preseason material did not trigger an automatic switch. Moving Bitonio or Norwell across would also change the left-guard comparison and available backup pairings.

## Defense

**Edge 1: Mincey or Branch.** Mincey's documented work was steadier in keeping contain while rushing. Branch produced meaningful rush wins, including the Tampa Bay preseason sack, and also lost the quarterback escape lane in camp series. The coaches corrected the lane; later controlled work held the responsibility. The play-by-play names the sack and the rush slot, not the lane on every play. A good rush and sound contain are separate observations. Stone must decide which complete job he wants first; a single sack does not automatically award the place.

## Special teams

**Long snapper: Cain or Kreiter.** Across the four preseason games, the two completed **40 combined recorded specialist snaps with no assigned operation fault**. They alternated who entered first, including punt and place-kick work, and the recorded operations held in both orders. The record does not demonstrate that Kreiter was the more accurate snapper or that Cain had deteriorated. Stone needs one first assignment for the opening chart, while the distinct roster call belongs to Caldwell.

## Evidence that would be unsafe to invent

The four game outputs contain no receiver-release chart establishing exactly why Nicks-targeted interceptions occurred, no named-game contact-loss count at left guard, no complete edge escape-lane grading, and no snapper accuracy separation. A decision may be necessary under that uncertainty, but the missing findings remain missing. The August 28 Nicks medical restriction is not a reason to manufacture an injury diagnosis or a lasting player decline.

## Timing and authority

Stone decides X, Z, the guards, first edge and long snapper, and may choose temporary arrangements. Caldwell makes the August 30 transaction and reduction decisions. The injury restriction remains with medical staff. The season game chart is issued against whoever is actually on Caldwell's resulting 53; the process must not assume a player survives merely because Stone names him for a trial.
