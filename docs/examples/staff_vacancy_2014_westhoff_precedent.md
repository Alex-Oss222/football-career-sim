# Worked precedent and next-cycle setup: Jacksonville assistant vacancies

**Scope:** Grounded in the already completed 2014 special-teams search. This is *not* a new search, new interview, actual 2015 vacancy or authorization to hire anyone. Nothing in this file changes the 2014 career. The design it illustrates is [docs/staff_vacancy.md](../staff_vacancy.md).

## Why this older event is a useful test

The actual branch record shows that Alan Lowry departed to become Atlanta's head coach on January 12, 2014. The special teams coordinator position was open. Stone covered the kicking game temporarily. At the February 2 planning checkpoint Stone had a target order and terms; his first target could not be reached, so the authorized sequence continued.

| Date in 2014 | What the actual branch records |
|---|---|
| January 12 | Lowry leaves Jacksonville, opening the special teams coordinator job. Stone directs special teams on an interim basis. |
| February 2 | The working plan selects Bobby April first, Mike Westhoff second, Bruce DeHaven third, with bounded terms already authorized. |
| February 3 | April's 2014 employment, unknown in the record, is settled under the search's frozen method: Oakland had re-signed him (drawn at the labelled probability 0.8125). Jacksonville requests permission to speak to him. |
| February 4 | Oakland refuses the request as a lateral move, under the symmetric rule the search applied to every club. No interview with April occurs. |
| February 5 | Jacksonville contacts unattached Mike Westhoff. No employer permission is needed. |
| February 7 | Westhoff agrees to an interview (willingness drawn at the labelled probability 0.25). |
| February 10 | Westhoff interviews. The record does **not** contain a verbatim interview transcript or individual answers, so no invented dialogue belongs in the result. |
| February 11 | Caldwell confirms funding within the plan's ceiling; the club offers $625,000 per season for 2014-2015 on the approved terms; Westhoff counters at $750,000; Jacksonville meets the ceiling Stone had already authorized and he accepts. |
| After acceptance | The job is filled, and the special teams calls Stone held as interim cover pass to Westhoff on his effective date. DeHaven is not approached; no fictional refusal is needed. Westhoff's coach record carries the job, the contract and the change of calls; the Staff page and the coaching payroll are built from it. |

Under the corrected design, the February 11 authority to meet the counter is Stone's own My Hire answer (in 2014, the user's written search plan), not a standing instruction, and an amount above Lowry's $625,000 is Caldwell's decision (design section 3); in 2014 he confirmed it under that search's own reading of the promised budget.

## How the proposed Candidate Report would have read on February 2, 2014

### Special teams coordinator opening

**From:** David Caldwell, general manager. No special teams coordinator could advise, since his was the open job.

**Why the job is open:** Alan Lowry departed January 12. Stone is temporarily directing the kicking game.

**Work to assign:** Punt and punt-return units, kickoff and kickoff return, place-kick protection and operation, personnel preparation, coverage-lane teaching, specialist coordination and game-day special teams calls. Stone retains team-level game management and Caldwell retains player acquisitions.

**Reference figures:** Lowry's former salary of $625,000 and the eleven remaining assistants' scheduled 2014 salaries of $6,950,000 (the [staff plan](../../career/2014/Early_Offseason/staff_changes/staff_plan.md)). Neither is a ceiling. Under the corrected design the report proposes no opening or ceiling: the $700,000 opening and $800,000 ceiling for April, and $625,000 and $750,000 for the fallbacks, would be Stone's own entries on My Hire, as the user's selections of September 28 and 29, 2026 adopted them.

**Bobby April:** Established NFL special-teams coaching record. Current Oakland contract/permission requires confirmation before an interview. He is Stone's first target, not a guaranteed appointment.

**Mike Westhoff:** Veteran former Jets special-teams coordinator, retired after 2012. Whether he wishes to return must be established by contact; he is not presumed willing.

**Bruce DeHaven:** Experienced coach currently associated with Carolina in the source record, but exact branch contract status and permission need checking if the search reaches him.

**Decision requested:** Set the target order and the financial bounds on My Hire. Do not treat 'call April' as 'hire April'. No named answer to an interview or financial counter can be supplied in advance.

This illustration uses the actual plan known at the relevant branch checkpoint. It does not insert the later February 11 result back into the February 2 briefing.

## Separate dated outputs that would have existed

The original closed search recorded these stages together in `hires.md`, under three event records (April's request, Westhoff's approach and interview, and the hire). Under the corrected format, each concluded event of a fixed kind (design section 5) would point to its own dated readable result:

| Resolved event | Kind | Individual result, as a naming illustration |
|---|---|---|
| April's 2014 employment settled, February 3 | Employment check | `Employment_Check_Result_2014-02-03_Bobby_April.md` |
| Oakland refuses April interview permission, February 4 | Permission | `Permission_Result_2014-02-04_Bobby_April.md` |
| Westhoff agrees to interview, February 7 | Contact | `Contact_Result_2014-02-07_Mike_Westhoff.md` |
| Interview completed, February 10 | Interview | `Interview_Result_2014-02-10_Mike_Westhoff.md`, with no fabricated interview answers |
| Offer and counter close with Westhoff acceptance, February 11 | Appointment | `Staff_Hire_Result_2014-02-11_Mike_Westhoff.md` |

The permission request of February 3, the contact of February 5, and the funding confirmation, offer and counter of February 11 are process entries: they appear on Staff Hire and in the results they lead to, not as pages of their own. These are **design examples**, not files to backfill into the closed 2014 career. The old source owner stays authoritative. The first four outcomes are small stage results; the final appointment is a separate completed hire with its actual effective date and signed terms. The Staff Hire index may link all five, but cannot replace them.

## January 2015: what the system actually knows

As of the career's current date, October 5, 2014 (after Week 5):

- Mike Tice and Romeo Crennel have 2015 contractual salaries and ongoing coordinating jobs. Stone calls the offense (since the 2013 season) and the defense (since September 28, 2014); Tice and Crennel prepare their sides, and Crennel sends in the defensive call. A new coordinator appointment must not silently transfer Stone's call control.
- Mike Westhoff has a 2015 contract with non-guaranteed 2015 salary, and he prepares and calls special teams. That is a real existing agreement, not an automatically vacant position. If he left, Stone would take interim direction of special teams automatically, with no clock stop (design section 4).
- Nine other assistants' recorded terms run through 2014. A pending renewal or contract year boundary is **not yet** proof of a vacancy; a completed departure/expiry/termination must actually create it. Their contracts end on the later of January 31, 2015 or the day after Jacksonville's last 2014 game, a labelled reconstruction the user's decision of October 20, 2026 settled and the coach records hold ([January staff review](../january_staff_review.md), section B, item 5).
- No 2014 end-of-season coaching result, staff departure, interview, January 2015 candidate interest, offer or hiring outcome has been established. Therefore no new 2015 Jacksonville hire may be written now.
- An outside candidate's 2015 employment would start from his last verified job before the divergence; no real 2015-cycle staff news is read. Another club would answer Jacksonville's request for one of its assistants by the design's symmetric method rule: a real step up granted, a same-title move refused.

**If a vacancy actually occurs:** Open an ACTION REQUIRED insert in the active offseason/season briefing, then issue a Candidate Report after the controlling vacancy and Stone's staff authority are established. The search would live at `career/2015/Early_Offseason/staff_changes/<vacancy-key>/` through the folder map, once 2015 is staged. The next stage is Stone's My Hire instructions, not an immediate candidate draw and automatic contract.

## A football interview for a specific role, without prewriting the answer

For a hypothetical *quarterbacks-coach* vacancy after the coach's existing terms legally end, a serious interview might include:

- Explain the quarterback's protection identification against a six-man pressure, including the line/backs' responsibilities and the built-in throw answer.
- Describe how you teach an early throw against off coverage and how you distinguish quarterback footwork from receiver route depth when timing fails.
- Show how you would coordinate with Tice and Stone given that Stone calls the offense.

For a hypothetical *defensive-backs* vacancy:

- Explain a Quarters match answer to motion into a bunch, including the safety and nickel communication and the force player against the run.
- Describe how a position coach takes a repeated exchange mistake from film to individual work and later checks it with a different presentation.

These are proposed interview questions, not actual 2015 interviews or verified candidate demonstrations. No candidate needs a numerical score, and no performance improvement is assumed from hiring a recognizable name.
