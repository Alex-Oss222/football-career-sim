# Jacksonville example: completed 2014 spring, with 2015 left open

**Illustration, not a new in-world briefing or amended decision.** Every football finding below comes from already closed 2014 Jacksonville reports. The example shows how the proposed three pages would have read when their respective facts became available. It must not change the actual 2014 files, put the 2014 class into 2015, or create an unplayed 2015 OTA.

Corrected after the process check of October 20, 2026: the brief follows the corrected template (the insert slot, then tables built from the records, then each staff read with its author and date, then the proposal, the standing instructions and who decides what); the plan section reads the emergency lead as the job record now holds it and treats the 2014 player lists as lists the staff propose again each year; and the OTA Report is shown as the built index it is, quoting or linking the two training reports and linking the window's two contract results.

### Staff reads

These reads are modelled from the closed reports; no such page was issued in 2014.

**Offense | Mike Tice, May 23, 2014.** The first two phases have given Jacksonville one agreed language for identifying the protection point and for handling the free defender outside Boot Flood's launch path. In the classroom, Bates and Yarno removed a disagreement about the double A-gap picture. During the permitted separate-unit work in May, Cousins, Brewster and the backs could identify the shared point while moving without a defense playing against them. That is where the evidence ends. The first OTA periods can put a changing defense in front of the offense. What matters is whether the center makes the correct point, whether the guard and back echo what changes their job, and whether the whole group sends HALF toward the actual overload. The coordinator should identify the front change and record the answer without supplying it first. This work will still be non-contact. The staff cannot decide which guard wins a block from it. On Boot Flood, the next test is a live presentation by Crennel's defense, particularly whether the tight end's release changes the deep and intermediate timing from Wing. Lewis's release and the quarterback's throwaway are linked assignments, not separate target totals.

**Defense | Romeo Crennel, May 23, 2014.** The May 14 bunch rehearsal had exposed two answers about the point receiver; Oden corrected the rule and the group gave one answer on May 15 and again May 20. Opposing receivers in the OTA periods should test whether the correct exchange survives a different release. The group also needs to relay the full call with Posluszny and Stanford as the communicators. Whether a defender actually finishes the tackle remains a camp question.

**Special teams | Mike Westhoff, May 23, 2014.** The snap, kick, protection alignment and coverage/return units can come together legally for the first time. Both Cain and Kreiter have separate unopposed operation evidence; neither should be declared the long snapper merely from a clean delivery without a contact rush.

Ten announced OTA days, helmets without shells, legal seven-on-seven, nine-on-seven and eleven-on-eleven; no live contact or opposed one-on-ones. The May 16-17 rookie minicamp has its own record. The veteran minicamp is June 17-19 after its own June 16 physicals.

## B. My Install Plan: established decisions rather than an invented response

There is **no separate May 23 written answer from Stone in the current records** that could be quoted as if he submitted this new form. The authorized directions for the completed 2014 program are already recorded in his [April 18 decisions](../../career/2014/Offseason_Training/stone_april_18_2014_decisions.md), his later instructions and the staff plans that flowed from them:

- **Practice if Stone is out.** Mike Tice runs an approved practice if Stone is absent; Romeo Crennel is the second emergency lead. Neither acquires depth, installation, role, disciplinary or personnel authority because of Stone's absence. The job record now holds this assignment from April 18, 2014 (`delegation.emergency`), and a 2015 page reads it from there.
- **Protection and Boot Flood.** Continue the shared center-quarterback-back protection teaching. Carry Boot Flood's free-edge answer to the field rather than treating the board solution as game execution.
- **Defensive intent and the relay.** On defense, identify TAKE, GIVE and ANSWER for each call; rotate the full communication responsibility among the designated personnel without changing the actual playing roles. Daryl Smith remains the primary defensive communicator in this period, with Posluszny and Stanford the stated backups under the then-current roster. The method carries into 2015; the named succession does not, and the 2015 staff propose it again against that roster.
- **Cross-training.** Cross-train selected players only in a bounded secondary responsibility after their primary job; newer veterans start with their first Jacksonville job. The named list was revised on May 1, 2014, when Bradfield and Pasztor came off it; like the succession, a 2015 list is proposed again.
- **Punts.** Westhoff owns the punt operation and the four game-situation families; evaluate operation, kick and coverage separately.
- **Injured players and family events.** Keep injured players involved in the mental work the medical department permits. Voluntary participation and optional family gatherings do not become an indirect depth-chart test.
- **Already decided before the brief.** Stone's May 12 rep starting points (Nicks at WR1, Adams at WR3, Bitonio at left guard, Turner at right guard, Mincey at Edge 1), each an open competition ([memo amendment](../../career/2014/Early_Offseason/stone_to_caldwell_2014_offseason_decisions.md#september-30-2026-amendment-starting-roles-entering-otas)); and the four unsigned tenders kept out while not under contract. From 2015 a role call like this is applied to the depth chart, and Stone decides whom to invite among unsigned tenders, with Caldwell's office executing any participation agreement.

The exact later June 2014 install actually executed includes Counter Boot, Sprint Flood and Y-Cross, according to the completed OTA evidence. This example is not a claim that Stone personally dictated an unrecorded May 23 priority statement naming those three concepts. Implementation must preserve that distinction.
