## What the research actually shows

These are official club accounts containing coaches' and players' comments. They establish what those people discussed, not access to their private grading sheets or a universal NFL report form. The format below is our application to Stone's existing system. Later-dated articles inform the writing method only; their player results never become evidence about a player in this career.

**A unit result needs an explanation across positions.** After Kansas City's August 16, 2013 game against San Francisco, Andy Reid spread responsibility for seven sacks beyond the offensive line. Alex Smith identified recognition, getting the ball out, outside execution and communication as parts of the problem. Dunta Robinson attributed Frank Gore's long run to defenders pursuing too far and leaving the other side exposed. That is a useful model for tracing a failure through connected jobs instead of blaming the nearest player. [Chiefs, August 17, 2013](https://www.chiefs.com/news/chiefs-share-game-plan-for-improvement-10854863).

**Good play and a correction can belong in the same assessment.** After reviewing the 2014 Cincinnati opener, Reid praised Eric Fisher's physicality but wanted more consistent hand placement. He described Zach Fulton's work in terms of sustaining blocks and reaching linebackers. The opening third-down run failed when a receiver and a lineman could not maintain their blocks. This supplies a concrete standard for discussing line play, reserve combinations and contributions away from the ball. [Chiefs, August 8, 2014](https://www.chiefs.com/news/reid-pleased-with-physical-ol-play-13427241).

**Communication is a football task with consequences.** In his August 22, 2014 review, Bill Belichick discussed Jamie Collins handling defensive communication, how that responsibility affected the rest of a player's work and why familiarity with the complete call mattered. He evaluated defense through coverage working with the rush, and used an end-of-half catch and field-goal opportunity to discuss decisions by both sides. The report therefore needs evidence of shared calls, situational awareness and execution, beyond a generic chemistry verdict. [Patriots postgame transcript](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-postgame-transcript-i-thought-we-took-another-step-in-our--200691).

**The audition includes ordinary football work.** Pittsburgh's 2010 opener preview set out Mike Tomlin's interest in legal blocking and tackling technique. Its quarterback questions included accuracy, huddle command and presnap communication; it also identified special teams as an evaluation that live games could expose more fully than drills. This supports looking beyond passing totals and scoring plays. Its year-specific roster and usage details do not govern 2014. [Steelers, August 13, 2010](https://www.steelers.com/news/things-to-watch-in-preseason-opener-2594564).

**A game cannot guarantee the planned test.** Belichick's August 8, 2014 explanation distinguishes practice situations the staff can arrange from game opportunities it cannot guarantee. He also discusses different combinations and opponents across the work. A reserve's narrow or unusual sample needs that context, while camp remains part of the assessment. [Patriots transcript](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-i-m-looking-forward-to-this-week-200276).

These written sources were inspected. No film viewing, private coaches' report or complete Hard Knocks transcript is claimed. The earlier [camp research](camp_and_preseason_reports.md#what-the-research-contributes) separately identifies the official Hard Knocks episode recap used there.

## What Stone and the coaches should learn

The report first tells how the game developed. The review then explains whether the team could perform its assigned football, who helped or hurt that work, and what the staff should change. Useful assessments contain evidence and a football judgment; they do not need a numeric grade.

| Part of the review | The useful coaching question |
|---|---|
| Whole team | What repeatedly sustained or stopped the team? How did turnovers, field position, possessions and situations connect the three phases? Does the score conceal a recurring problem or exaggerate one isolated failure? |
| Offense | Did the formation, protection, quarterback decision, routes and run blocking work together? Which actual combinations kept the prepared offense functional? What happened after the first answer was taken away? |
| Defense | Did the front, fit, force player, rush and coverage support one another? Who defeated a block or maintained leverage, and who left another defender a problem? Were tackles finished when the record establishes that work? |
| Special teams | Did protection, specialist execution, lane responsibility, blocking and return decisions meet the kick's objective? Who contributed without making the final tackle or return? |
| Communication | Did the call reach the group, and did its members act on the same instruction through motion, substitution or tempo? Was a failure in communication, recognition, technique or an unresolved cause? |
| Individual players | What did he actually do well or poorly in his assigned job? Did the game confirm or challenge the camp assessment? What help, workload or narrower assignment shaped the performance? |
| Coaches | Was the instruction clear and consistent? Did the call, personnel and support give the player a usable answer? What adjustment was made, and did the later work support it? |

Apply those questions to the active book. In Stone's Iteration I offense, HALF protection links the point declaration, slide/man responsibilities, the back's scan and the quarterback's pressure answer. Power joins secured inside blocks, the puller's path and the back's read. In the defense, a BOX assignment and a SPILL assignment do not ask the edge to produce the same result; motion changes still have to preserve the fit and coverage help. The [2014 staff/assignment map](../career/2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/staff_plan.md#what-the-active-playbooks-ask-of-the-players) names the responsible coaches. Evaluate the version actually taught and called.

Stone's physical standard means he wants the assigned block sustained, the defender to handle contact without abandoning his responsibility, and the technique to hold through the work. His emphasis on varied personnel makes the performance of combinations important. It does not make a reserve's rep identical to a starter's, nor turn a violent collision into good execution. His [profile](../career/coaching_profiles/alex_stone.md#how-he-wants-training-camp-run) already contains the requested approach; this follow-up makes its game assessment explicit.

## What the user will read in chat

The complete output retains coach status, preparation, pregame media, game, postgame media, coaching takeaways, personnel/availability and closure. Inside the game section, the narrative is followed immediately by Report to render: what the staff wanted to see; how the game unfolded; how the offense, defense and special teams played; communication and game operation; players who stood out and questions still open; Stone's decisions and the next practice; the full generated box score. The game header supplies the date and score once. The report develops the assessment from the narrative without telling the same game twice.

Write connected prose under those headings. The story names the action that mattered. The unit review explains the relationships. Individual paragraphs deepen a consequential assessment instead of repeating every catch, sack and touchdown. A player's useful work and his limitation can appear together. An established starter receives the same scrutiny as a player trying to make the team. A reserve is neither dismissed for playing late nor promoted because of one highlight.

The next-work section identifies a real correction and who owns it. For a shared protection problem, the quarterback and line rooms may need to reconcile their instruction before another team period. For a correctly identified block lost physically, the position coach needs the relevant technique and matchup work. These are different responses. Name an actual delivered correction only when recorded; recommendations remain recommendations.

## Evidence the LLM needs

The assistant reads the game's prepared menu, dated camp questions, actual participation, public receipt/play record, medical changes and recorded staff observations before writing. It checks a representative sequence against that record and uses the active book to interpret the job. Research supplies evaluation questions and prose examples, never missing events.

If the receipt records a sack but no cause, report the sack and the supported consequence. Review the protection, quarterback timing and route answer as open coaching questions; do not invent a missed slide. If the record documents a correct identification and lost leverage, make that distinction plainly. If an archived summary has no defensive detail, it cannot support a claim that the entire secondary communicated cleanly. The worked extract demonstrates that limit without filling it with generic praise.

Store the complete report in the game's existing output owner, with its generated box score and supporting records linked. A bulk preseason turn uses the same full preseason output template, repeating the game block for each completed game. Use the [consolidated review guide](../foundation/templates/training_camp_and_preseason/preseason_review.md) within section 6 for the assessment across games, keeping team execution, communication and named-player substance. A table of scores is not the review. Follow-up questions use the saved evidence without changing the game or inventing a new observation.
