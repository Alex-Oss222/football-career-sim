# Repository rebuild plan

Adopted October 19, 2026, from the user's instructions of October 18 and 19, 2026. This file governs every rebuild task. Read it first. Its decisions stay as written until the user changes them; a change is recorded in the change log at the end with the date and the user's words, never made silently.

## What the rebuild is

The rebuild changes how the career is organized, stored, presented and decided. It does not change the game engine, the private service, how games are played or any result.

The user's goal, in their words: a complete process from start to finish, realistic to football, clean pages with no system clutter, and a brief that says what is needed before every decision.

## Rules that never change

1. **The new system starts with 2014 Week 6.** Everything written up to the close of 2014 Week 5 may be moved to its place in the new folders, but its content is not rewritten to fit the new process. From Week 6 on, everything follows this plan.
2. **The engine is untouched.** Games, draws, the private service and outcomes are outside the rebuild.
3. **Historical dates.** Every season runs on the real NFL dates. Before a new season opens, its calendar is researched and checked in two independent passes.
4. **Clean pages.** A page the user reads is football only. It never shows internal codes or identifiers (for example 1C, 5A, 7D), file paths, script or module names, system notes, disclaimers, version labels or words such as engine, branch, receipt, kernel, ledger, snapshot or checkpoint. An assessment carries one dated line, nothing more: "Staff view, <date>." (the latest dated entry the summary draws on: the assessment's own date, or its latest dated update up to the summary's date) or, when it cites figures from games after the assessment's date, "Staff assessment originally dated <date>; <season> results current through <cutoff>." (and should those figures end before the staff view's date, "Staff view, <date>; <season> results current through <cutoff>."). A check in repository validation fails any page that breaks this rule.
5. **One fact, one place.** Every fact is stored once. Pages are built from those stored facts, so a page can never disagree with another page.
6. **Realistic authority.** Stone only decides what his job lets him decide. Everything else is decided by the person who owns it and reaches him as news, or as a request for his input when a real coach would be asked.
7. **One step at a time.** Each build step is its own pull request, merged only after its tests, repository validation and the clean-page check pass. The next step starts after the merge.

## How the stored facts are kept

These rules keep the year-to-year move small and the structure safe to change:

1. **People and contracts are stored once, not per season.** Each player, coach and contract has one permanent record under the data area that grows by dated entries (signed, traded, injured, assessed). Nothing is copied into a new season; only what belongs to one season (games, decisions, the calendar) lives in that season.
2. **One folder map.** All code finds files through one map of the layout. A test fails if a script writes a folder name or a year into a path itself.
3. **The same shape every season.** Every season has the same folders, created from one template, never copied from the year before.
4. **Built pages are never the source.** Pages, statistics and standings are built from the stored facts and game files, and a check confirms they match. Nothing built is carried forward or kept in sync by hand.
5. **Closed seasons are packed and locked.** When a season closes, its game files are packed into one compressed file with a checksum, read by the same tools, and the season becomes read-only.
6. **Document 5 is a short summary built from the stored facts:** the date, the job, open decisions, availability and the next events, so it changes only when a fact changes.
7. **Safeguards.** Before each real year change, a copy of the repository runs the whole move into the next season first. Tests work out the current week from the stored facts instead of naming a date.

## How a decision works

A decision is one of two kinds, and each has its own pages.

**A phase decision has three pages** in the folder where it happens:

1. **The brief.** Named for whoever really brings it: the GM, the scouting department, the medical staff, the agent. It tells Stone what he needs to know and what is being asked of him, in the voice that person would use. Tables first, built from the stored facts: injuries, how players played, contracts, needs, who is calling. Then a short read from the staff member who gives it, which may only cite what the page shows. Then who else decides what, and the deadline.
2. **His page.** A fill-in page with fixed headings, named for what it is (Game Plan, My Draft Recommendations). He fills it in place, or uploads a .md or .txt file with the same headings. Choices are limited to what is legal: players on the roster, calls in the active playbook (any of them, practiced that week or not; only a call from a later book is refused). Before anything runs, a check tells him exactly what is missing or invalid. His page stays as the record of his decision.
3. **The result.** Named for where it is decided (Game Report, Draft Room Results). It says what happened and what changed.

**An event inside a week or phase does not get three pages of its own** ([event inserts](event_inserts.md)). An injury replacement, a trade call, a staff permission request, a contract matter or another club's call appears as an insert at the top of the active brief (the Game Week Briefing, the Bye Week Review or the phase's brief): ACTION REQUIRED for an answer Stone owes, FOR YOUR INFORMATION for news and for what a standing instruction or someone else has already answered, and COMING UP for a window or deadline ahead with nothing yet to answer. He answers every inserted event on that week's or phase's one My Responses page (`My_Responses.md` in its folder), which is separate from the frozen Game Plan; the season's own page is My Season Response, apart from any week's. Each resolved event (accepted, rejected, withdrawn, lapsed, granted or refused) gets its own dated result page in its owner's folder: roster moves in Team_Operations/Team/Roster, signings in Team_Operations/Free_Agency, trades in Team_Operations/Trades, staff in Team_Operations/Staff, and Stone's own career in career/Stone. The week's or phase's index links each one, and the weekly report links it and never pastes it: N resolved events make N result pages, never one combined page. A new event that needs his answer, a finding that changes a choice still pending, or an answer due before the next scheduled issue reissues the active brief, and the old one stays as what he saw.

Decisions covered by a standing rule he has given, and decisions that belong to someone else, ask him nothing: they reach him as FOR YOUR INFORMATION. Only an answer due before kickoff (in a bye, before the bye's scheduled work) holds the game; unrelated business never holds one, and business that does not hold Jacksonville's game never holds the week's slate. In a bye week the other clubs' games close while Stone's bye answers are open. Closing the other clubs' games while Jacksonville's own pre-kickoff answer is open in a playing week has not been ruled on: it would need a new ruling from the user before it is written as a rule or built, and until then the weekly build and close hold the whole slate (`scripts/build_week_inputs.py`, `scripts/close_week.py`). Silence never counts as yes or no. Opening a page never moves time or creates an event. If the situation changes after a brief goes out, a new brief replaces it, and the old one stays as what he saw.

Contacts from other clubs are always named for what they are: a rumor, a call, an interview request, an offer, or an accepted job. Shad Khan alone decides another club's request to contact or interview Stone while he is under contract: he grants it, refuses it, or asks for compensation under the head-coach exception (Document 3 row 1a). Stone is not open to job offers, so such a call reaches him only as news, never as a decision. For his assistants, Jacksonville's standing instruction grants a head-coach interview and a position coach's coordinator interview and refuses a move to the same title elsewhere, alike whatever the asking club; the request and Jacksonville's answer reach Stone as news, and he is asked only about a request the rule does not cover.
