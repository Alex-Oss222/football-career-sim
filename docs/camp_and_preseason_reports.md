## What changes in the report

The report should tell Stone how his team practiced: the formation and call, the job a player had, how he performed it, what the coach corrected and whether the next opportunity answered the question. Camp adds the physical tests that spring could not provide. Preseason adds an outside opponent, game operation and situations the staff cannot reset on demand.

The camp report owns the football account and its dated observations together. Its retained detail preserves supporting evidence, decisions and anchors. A presentation edit does not add a mistake, successful rep, coach quotation or padded result. Material individual findings belong as dated updates to the opening annual player card; an actual competition updates its existing battle card. Neither requires another camp assessment document.

For example, Hawkins's existing record supports this account:

> From a reduced split on July 26, Hawkins cut the Y-Cross dig a yard short. The read was intact, but the route missed its taught depth. Drake corrected it, and Hawkins reached the right depth on the repeat and on the July 29 retest.

The assignment, coaching and later response are the story. “Improved execution” would leave the reader guessing what changed.

That example describes the non-contact opening, not the whole camp. The July 30 and August 1 record adds controlled-contact wins, losses, corrections and retests while leaving the named competitions open. Preserve each observation's date and setting. Future authorized practice resolution must establish new work before the writer can use it; the template requires neither a struggle nor a standout.

The report begins with the team, location, dates, completed days and actual work addressed, distinguishing sessions and contact levels where necessary. Briefly explain the significant periods and why the staff used them. A reader comparing two players needs their jobs, teammates, opponents, assistance and opportunities, not just a verdict that one looked better. Exposure that was not recorded stays unknown.

## What a camp day contains

The daily order and duration come from the club's permitted schedule. These are functions to plan, not a claim that each occurred on every recorded day.

| Work | What happens | What the report should explain when observed |
|---|---|---|
| Meetings and film | Review earlier work and teach the day's calls, technique and situations. Reconcile a shared instruction across rooms. | What the player understood, what needed clarification, and whether the coaches supplied one usable answer. |
| Walkthrough | Line up, make the declaration, move through a shift or substitution and rehearse the assignment at the required pace. | Whether the call reaches everyone and the jobs still fit after something changes. |
| Individual periods | Position technique such as line footwork and hands, a receiver's release, a defender's transition or a back's protection steps. | The particular technique and correction. Winning a later team rep is a separate observation. |
| Group periods | Inside-run work joins blockers, backs and fitters; pass skeleton tests distribution; permitted one-on-one work isolates a matchup. | What the drill includes and removes. Seven-on-seven provides no live line protection; a one-on-one rush lacks the full game's help and escape constraints. |
| Team periods | Put assignments together against an opposing unit, with the script's actual pace and contact instruction. Change a formation, pressure look or player combination for a reason. | Whether the whole call survives. Explain who helped whom and whether a loss came from identification, technique, physical execution or the call itself. |
| Situations and kicking | Rehearse a clock/field-position problem, substitution, punt instruction or another assigned operation. Include the staff's communication. | Whether the group achieved the stated objective, and what happened when the usual player or first answer was unavailable. |
| Recovery and review | Apply medical/workload instructions, review film and assign the next correction. | What the coaches actually told the player and what they will check next. Promising film is not delivering it. |

These applications come from the active books and existing camp plan, checked against contemporary coaching accounts. They are not a universal timetable or a requirement to schedule every drill daily.

## Follow the football across the block

Report the unit's connected execution before separating individual findings. On offense, explain whether protection, the quarterback's decision and the route adjustment supplied the same answer. On defense, connect the front, fit, coverage help and communication. In the kicking game, follow the operation and assignments beyond the kick or return. A successful result can conceal a bad assignment; a physical loss can occur after a correct call.

Explain personnel experiments by their purpose. A reserve beside established players may reveal whether his problem was his own technique or the surrounding operation; removing the usual communicator may expose a shared dependency. Name what the changed combination actually tested and what the opportunity left unanswered. Different support and opposition matter to the comparison. Mixing units does not itself establish versatility, equal opportunity or a winner.

Use the block's earlier work to show meaningful change, persistence or contradiction. Follow the staff's correction into a later session when one exists. Separate an immediate repeat from retention after an interruption, a different look or reduced coaching help. If the coaches changed their instruction, technique emphasis or combination, explain why the recorded evidence supports that response. Do not require every player to improve or every issue to close before new material can be taught.

End with the remaining football questions, each tied to its dated source, responsible coach and next useful test. Distinguish an assigned follow-up from a proposed one and an actual result from a future opportunity. These questions stay in the report; annual cards and live battle cards receive only the material updates they own. No additional question tracker or assessment roster is needed.

## The 2014 practice boundary

The original August 4, 2011 CBA controls this season. Article 23 §6 limits the first camp day to examinations, meetings/classroom work and running/conditioning; days two and three have no contact or pads. Thereafter, the daily limit is four field hours, with at most one padded practice of no more than three hours, a break of at least three hours, and a second session limited to the remaining time as a walkthrough. Article 24 §1(c) counts required helmets and shoulder pads as padded practice. “Shells” cannot be used to evade that definition. [Original agreement, printed pp. 140–143](https://nflps.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/collective-bargaining-agreement-2011-2020.pdf).

The CBA counts camp days, not three football-practice days after reporting. Jacksonville's recorded choice to keep July 25 to 29 non-contact was more conservative. Its first completed padded sessions were July 30 and August 1, with July 31 preserved as the recorded day off. Neither the legal permission nor a later template changes what those sessions contained. Contemporary [July 25 coaching remarks](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-we-ll-get-into-pads-tomorrow-199821) distinguish teaching before pads from later physical competition. Do not substitute current-CBA camp rules for 2014 or treat permission for contact as proof that a particular rep used it.

## Stone's standards and assessment

The user supplies Stone's personal philosophy. The research does not claim that every NFL coach follows it, and the uncertain Rams comparison is not needed to define it.

His [updated profile](../career/coaching_profiles/alex_stone_coaching_profile.md#how-he-wants-training-camp-run) and [operative plan](../career/2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/staff_plan.md#stones-camp-standard) now say that all eligible contracted players report on their applicable dates, veterans included. Players and coaches, including Stone, must meet written physical standards appropriate to their jobs and medical circumstances. Maximum participation means completing assigned work and remaining involved in the permitted teaching when limited. It does not give a veteran a standing exemption or a coach authority to overrule medical clearance. No coach's test result has been invented.

Physicality is expressed in the permitted work: securing blocks, defeating them, protecting, handling contact on releases, fitting runs, keeping the ball and executing the instructed tackle finish. Mixing units gives the staff a reasoned comparison: a reserve alongside regulars, a starter beside a replacement, a different opponent or the usual communicator removed from a drill. It creates evidence rather than predetermined winners or equal snap quotas.

The [scheme assessment table](../career/2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/staff_plan.md#what-the-active-playbooks-ask-of-the-players) translates the actual Iteration I books into coaching questions. HALF connects the center's point to the guard, back and quarterback. Power and Counter require coordinated block paths. An H or Y assignment includes the work attached to that role. BOX and SPILL demand different edge results. Cover 1 needs the actual matchup and help to work. Staff judgments must explain those jobs, not simply call a player a good fit.

Position coaches identify the technique and correction; coordinators judge shared execution and the call's costs; Stone weighs the usable combinations, situations, support and fairness of the opportunity. The report can also identify a teaching failure. A player following inconsistent instructions has exposed a staff problem that needs resolving.

The shared [assessment method](../foundation/templates/training_camp_and_preseason/player_assessment.md) applies across those stages with different evidence limits. It guides findings within the report, not another full personnel assessment. There are exactly two full annual assessments: the opening baseline with dated observations added during the year, and a separate final review after the season. Preserve that baseline; assess the assigned job rather than mastery of uninstalled material. Development has no fixed timetable and later real-world outcomes are never a private answer key.

Keep the template's section order and rebuild the content from that year's people, active books, assigned work and results. Shared prose and evidence rules belong in the template README and assessment method; phase templates concentrate on the work that makes their stage different. Report length follows the material evidence, without padding a short block or cutting a substantial one to an arbitrary word count.

## How this works in the LLM chatbox

AGENTS.md, the full output templates and the season's camp/preseason indexes route the assistant to these files. The assistant reads the event's completed evidence and relevant taught playbook entries, then renders the filled report as normal Markdown. Game reports preserve the full output layout, with the assessment immediately below the narrative. The headings organize prose; the user is not asked to fill a form. Narrow follow-ups use the saved detail rather than replaying the whole report. There is no new application endpoint or frontend to deploy.

The [Tampa Bay folder](../career/2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Preseason_Games/Game_01/README.md#questions-coming-out-of-the-camp-opening) contains a preparation preview carrying the camp questions. After that game is legitimately played, its report will state the result, explain the material game sequences, include the generated box score, assess the work against those questions and assign the next corrections. Compare starters and reserves in the situations they actually faced, including communication and team execution. Explain what the game added to practice evidence; a game does not automatically answer a question it never tested. The future game cannot be illustrated with invented player results today.

The existing preseason bulk process, material-decision pauses and release requirements still control execution. This guidance changes presentation and explains coaching method. It changes no master date, roster, medical state, role or game result.
