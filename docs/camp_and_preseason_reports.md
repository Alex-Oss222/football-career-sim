# Training camp and preseason: research, reports and Stone's approach

Prepared September 30, 2026 at the user's request. This extends the [spring report work](offseason_training_reports.md). The career remains at July 29, 2014: camp has completed five practices without full pads, and no preseason game has been played.

## What changes in the report

The report should tell Stone how his team practiced: the formation and call, the job a player had, how he performed it, what the coach corrected and whether the next opportunity answered the question. Camp adds the physical tests that spring could not provide. Preseason adds an outside opponent, game operation and situations the staff cannot reset on demand.

The [rewritten camp report](../career/2014/training_camp_and_preseason/training_camp/training_report.md) and [player assessments](../career/2014/training_camp_and_preseason/training_camp/player_assessments.md) now open with that football account. Their original detailed records remain expandable, preserving the old observations, dates, decisions and anchors. No new mistake, successful rep, coach quotation or padded result was added to make the prose livelier.

For example, Hawkins's existing record supports this account:

> From a reduced split on July 26, Hawkins cut the Y-Cross dig a yard short. The read was intact, but the route missed its taught depth. Drake corrected it, and Hawkins reached the right depth on the repeat and on the July 29 retest.

The assignment, coaching and later response are the story. “Improved execution” would leave the reader guessing what changed.

The current record is overwhelmingly assignment-correct. A presentation edit cannot honestly turn it into a camp full of battles, dropped passes, injuries and breakthroughs. Future authorized practice resolution must establish those observations before the writer can use them. The template does not require a struggle or a standout.

## What a camp day contains

The daily order and duration come from the club's permitted schedule. These are functions to plan, not a claim that each occurred on every recorded day.

| Work | What happens | What the report should explain when observed |
|---|---|---|
| Meetings and film | Review earlier work and teach the day's calls, technique and situations. Reconcile a shared instruction across rooms. | What the player understood, what needed clarification, and whether the coaches supplied one usable answer. |
| Walkthrough | Line up, make the declaration, move through a shift or substitution and rehearse the assignment at the required pace. | Whether the call reaches everyone and the jobs still fit after something changes. |
| Individual periods | Position technique such as line footwork and hands, a receiver's release, a defender's transition or a back's protection steps. | The particular technique and correction. Winning a later team rep is a separate observation. |
| Group periods | Inside-run work joins blockers, backs and fitters; pass skeleton tests distribution; permitted one-on-one work isolates a matchup. | What the drill includes and removes. Seven-on-seven provides no live line protection; a one-on-one rush lacks the full game's help and escape constraints. |
| Team periods | Put assignments together against an opposing unit, with the script's actual pace and contact instruction. Change a formation, pressure look or player combination for a reason. | Whether the whole call survives. Explain who helped whom and whether a loss came from identification, technique, physical execution or the call itself. |
| Situations and kicking | Rehearse a clock/field-position problem, substitution, punt instruction or another assigned operation. Include the staff's communication. | Whether the group achieved the stated objective, and what happened when the usual player or first answer was unavailable. |
| Recovery and review | Apply medical/workload instructions, review film and assign the next correction. | What the coaches actually told the player and what they will check next. Promising film is not delivering it. |

These applications come from the active books and existing camp plan, checked against contemporary coaching accounts. They are not a universal timetable or a requirement to schedule every drill daily.

## The 2014 practice boundary

The original August 4, 2011 CBA controls this season. Article 23 §6 limits the first camp day to examinations, meetings/classroom work and running/conditioning; days two and three have no contact or pads. Thereafter, the daily limit is four field hours, with at most one padded practice of no more than three hours, a break of at least three hours, and a second session limited to the remaining time as a walkthrough. Article 24 §1(c) counts required helmets and shoulder pads as padded practice. “Shells” cannot be used to evade that definition. [Original agreement, printed pp. 140–143](https://nflps.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/collective-bargaining-agreement-2011-2020.pdf).

The CBA counts camp days, not three football-practice days after reporting. Jacksonville's recorded choice to keep all July 25–29 practices non-contact is more conservative; its selected July 30 full-pad date remains the branch plan. Neither that choice nor a future template rewrites the completed sessions. Contemporary [July 25 coaching remarks](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-we-ll-get-into-pads-tomorrow-199821) also distinguish teaching before pads from the later physical competition. Do not substitute the current CBA's camp rules for 2014.

## What the research contributes

The official July 25, 2014 Belichick transcript describes reinstallation after the break, fine points shared across positions and an install calendar working toward preseason and opening day. It also describes players working beside and against changing groups. This supports reporting retained teaching, communication and combinations before claiming a physical verdict. [Transcript](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-we-ll-get-into-pads-tomorrow-199821).

His August 8 transcript makes a useful distinction for preseason assessment: practice can deliberately create third-down, red-zone, two-minute and pressure situations, while a game supplies whatever situations occur. Evaluate both bodies of work, with their different combinations and opportunities. His August 16 explanation adds that assignment and execution across the player's work matter beyond the headline statistics. [August 8](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-i-m-looking-forward-to-this-week-200276); [August 16](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-transcript-8-16-the-team-played-with-good-energy-and-compe-200501).

That is a useful model for a scrimmage testing independence and game operation. It does not establish any result for Stone's August 2 scrimmage, which has not happened. [Club account](https://www.jaguars.com/news/jaguars-mock-game-hc-gus-bradley-pleased-with-qbs-13374339).

## Stone's standards and assessment

The user supplies Stone's personal philosophy. The research does not claim that every NFL coach follows it, and the uncertain Rams comparison is not needed to define it.

His [updated profile](../career/coaching_profiles/alex_stone.md#how-he-wants-training-camp-run) and [operative plan](../career/2014/training_camp_and_preseason/training_camp/staff_plan.md#stones-camp-standard) now say that all eligible contracted players report on their applicable dates, veterans included. Players and coaches, including Stone, must meet written physical standards appropriate to their jobs and medical circumstances. Maximum participation means completing assigned work and remaining involved in the permitted teaching when limited. It does not give a veteran a standing exemption or a coach authority to overrule medical clearance. No coach's test result has been invented.

Physicality is expressed in the permitted work: securing blocks, defeating them, protecting, handling contact on releases, fitting runs, keeping the ball and executing the instructed tackle finish. Mixing units gives the staff a reasoned comparison: a reserve alongside regulars, a starter beside a replacement, a different opponent or the usual communicator removed from a drill. It creates evidence rather than predetermined winners or equal snap quotas.

The [scheme assessment table](../career/2014/training_camp_and_preseason/training_camp/staff_plan.md#what-the-active-playbooks-ask-of-the-players) translates the actual Iteration I books into coaching questions. HALF connects the center's point to the guard, back and quarterback. Power and Counter require coordinated block paths. An H or Y assignment includes the work attached to that role. BOX and SPILL demand different edge results. Cover 1 needs the actual matchup and help to work. Staff judgments must explain those jobs, not simply call a player a good fit.

Position coaches identify the technique and correction; coordinators judge shared execution and the call's costs; Stone weighs the usable combinations, situations, support and fairness of the opportunity. The report can also identify a teaching failure. A player following inconsistent instructions has exposed a staff problem that needs resolving.
