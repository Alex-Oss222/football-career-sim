## Main findings, supported and unsupported

### Phase boundaries and legality

The controlling source is the [August 4, 2011 NFL/NFLPA collective bargaining agreement](https://nflps.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/collective-bargaining-agreement-2011-2020.pdf), Article 21, printed pp. 131-137 and Article 22 (minicamps); Appendix G covers offseason-workout conduct. PDF pages 146-152 in the one-based viewer for Article 21. The agreement in force for 2014-15 is **not** the later 2020 CBA.

| Claim | 2011 CBA basis | Operational effect |
|---|---|---|
| Program is voluntary, including classroom instruction | Article 21 §1; §5(a) | Never penalize voluntary nonattendance, demand justification or assign a commitment grade. Separate one permitted mandatory veteran minicamp under Article 22. |
| New head coach club may start first Monday April; returning head-coach club third Monday at earliest | Article 21 §2(c) | 2014 Jaguars returning start April 21; 2015 returning start April 20. New-head-coach exception does not arise solely from drafting a new page. |
| Program scheduled and filed by the mutually agreed date, no later than 21 days before start; changes advanced in writing | Article 21 §2(c) | Derived latest Apr 21 2014 filing date Mar 31; Apr 20 2015 latest Mar 30. Actual accepted filing dates and transmissions require their own proof. |
| Program divided into Phase One first 2 weeks, Phase Two next 3, Phase Three last 4 | Article 21 §2(b) | April decision handles first five weeks; OTA workflow starts Phase Three. |
| Phase One strength, conditioning and physical rehab only for physical activities | Article 21 §2(b)(i) | No position-coach field practice, observation, coached snapping or kicking, helmets or ordinary footballs. Narrow quarterback-to-uncovered-receiver exception is not a coached field drill. |
| Phase One **classroom** football coaching is possible | Article 21 §§1, 2 and §5 distinguish classroom instruction from restricted on-field work; 2014 Chiefs first-hand explanation below | Teach protection, call language and film on lawful program days, not pre-program and not on the field. |
| Phase Two individual and separate-unit instruction without opposition | Article 21 §2(b)(ii) | No offense against defense, receiver/DB competition, OL/DL or contact; no helmets. The punt operation may be rehearsed separately from a return team. |
| Phase Three OTAs allow 7-on-7, 9-on-7 and 11-on-11, with no live contact | Article 21 §2(b)(iii); Appendix G | The first legal offense-versus-defense tests are later, and even these are non-contact. |
| Time maximum and player control of hours | Article 21 §5(b) | In non-OTA/non-minicamp program days: at most 4 h in facility, 4 weekdays/week, 90 min field/day; at most 2 h/day prescribed by club. Validate actual individuals and club calendar. |
| Required Tender/UFA participation agreement | Article 21 §§3,9 | Tender alone is not proof a player may work; confirm signed contract or lawful offseason participation agreement. |
| Record filming and head-coach compliance exposure | Article 21 §§5(c),8 | Club retains field-session footage for NFLPA review through thirty days after season starts; illegal coaching creates real club/head-coach liability, not a reward for effort. |
| No post-program organized coached football outside allowed exception | Article 21 §6 | Do not covertly extend April or Phase Three workouts into the pre-camp break. |

**Important correction from the repository's own history:** Stone's April 18, 2014 direction includes the proposition that Phase One punters could kick and snappers could snap into a net. That sentence conflicts with Article 21's prohibition on footballs in Phase One except the narrow QB provision. The actual recorded 2014 Phase One correctly kept all kicking and snapping teaching in the classroom. Preserve the *rest* of Stone's intent: Westhoff controls punt units, four game situations are distinguished and the three parts of a punt receive separate technical attention. Do not replace the legal rule with Stone's older informal reading.

The CBA is not a daily schedule. It controls what may happen. A club's posted program schedule, actual filing and executed sessions supply whether and when that specific activity occurred. A staff outline is not a completed workout, and scheduled OTAs are not necessarily ten held practices.

### 2014 historical Jacksonville and contemporaneous NFL practice

The April 3, 2014 [Jacksonville club notice, *OTA, minicamp dates set*](https://www.jaguars.com/news/ota-minicamp-dates-set-12840120) directly verifies Jacksonville's returning-head-coach offseason start **Monday Apr 21**, ten scheduled OTAs on **May 27-29, June 2-3, June 5, June 9-10, June 12-13**, and mandatory minicamp **June 17-19**. An independent [NFL all-club announcement](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-offseason-workout-program-dates-announced-0ap2000000339264), likewise published that April, supports the program phases and the Jacksonville dates. The [Jaguars rookie minicamp report, Apr 30, 2014](https://www.jaguars.com/news/jaguars-open-three-minicamp-practices-12936883), reports two Jaguars on-field rookie days, **May 16 and 17**, even though a three-day rookie weekend could legally have been used. Do not invent a third day.

The first-hand Apr 21, 2014 [Chiefs report with Andy Reid](https://www.chiefs.com/news/chiefs-begin-phase-one-of-offseason-program-12898503) explicitly distinguishes Phase One classroom football coaching from quarterback/receiver throwing without coach participation. The [Chiefs 2014 program explanation](https://www.chiefs.com/news/kansas-city-chiefs-begin-offseason-program-12894182) gives the two/three/four-week field boundaries. A club-level [Packers 2013 explanation by Mark Murphy](https://www.packers.com/news/offseason-program-in-the-home-stretch-10256085) corroborates the first phase as classroom installation plus limited physical preparation. The original 2011 CBA, rather than each reporter's shorthand, remains the legally controlling source.

A [Jaguars Apr 22, 2014 veteran program report](https://www.jaguars.com/news/14-offseason-veterans-report-for-offseason-program-12902026) describes real NFL club physical and logistical practices. It is useful contemporary evidence of work type and how a team described the opening program, **not** permission to import the real Jaguars' athletes, players' choices or Gus Bradley's staff outcomes into the Alex Stone career.

The existing repository also contains unusually detailed **career-specific 2014 records**, not just historical research: `career/2014/Offseason_Training/Phase_One/staff_plan.md`, `Phase_One/training_report.md`, `Phase_Two/staff_plan.md`, `Phase_Two/training_report.md`, Stone's dated April 18 direction, and `library/2014_offseason_phase_rules_verification.md`. Those files show precisely what was completed in this separate career through its actual spring: Phase One eight legal classroom and physical-work days; Phase Two twelve unopposed field days; actual football corrections and their evidentiary limits. They also show the separation of contractual participation from mere tender rights. These records are already closed, so they support the 2014 illustration only and remain unchanged.

## Source hierarchy and source discipline

1. **Governing labor document:** original Aug 4, 2011 CBA text, especially Article 21 and Article 22.
2. **League and direct club calendar announcements:** original NFL/Jaguars notices issued in the relevant year, with their actual publication and change dates.
3. **Contemporary practice explanations:** 2013 Packers and 2014 Chiefs and Jaguars primary firsthand reporting. These explain ordinary coaching procedure but cannot override the CBA.
4. **Jacksonville career ownership:** the simulator's committed Stone decisions, staff plans, completed Phase One/Two training reports and current data owners. These govern *this career*.
5. **Research chronology:** `library/2014_offseason_phase_rules_verification.md`, `library/2015_historical_calendar.md`, `docs/rebuild_plan.md`, `docs/event_inserts.md`, `docs/season_structure.md`. The source registers distinguish when evidence was published, when it was later corroborated and what never belonged to Stone's actual career.

Do not use a later club coach's retrospective assessment to set an earlier player judgment. Do not turn a schedule announcement into a record of participation. Do not treat the CBA's allowed activities as proof anyone performed them. And do not assume that a failed business deal left no record: the program summary links its own concluded result even when the signed roster and cap stay unchanged.
