# Week 6 at Tennessee: practical illustration (not a submitted Game Plan)

These examples use only facts available in the existing October 5, 2014 briefing and the closed Week 5 record. They are **illustrations**, not Stone's new decisions, completed scouting observations, invented press quotes or a played Week 6 result.

## How the staff brief could read

**Week 6: Jacksonville (3-2) at Tennessee (1-4).** Sunday, October 12, 2014, LP Field, 1 p.m. ET. Jacksonville lost to Pittsburgh 27-10 after leading 10-6 in the third quarter. It gained 360 yards, allowed no sack, turned the ball over four times, and Pittsburgh turned those giveaways into 17 points. The problem this week is not simply gaining more yards. It's avoiding the field-position and scoring losses that erased productive drives.

**Availability before the week:** Christian Jones has an October 6 reassessment after a minor upper-extremity problem. Brynden Trawick is due for an October 7 reassessment; the current projected return is October 11. Jordan Poyer is still held pending the independent medical process, with October 9 projected but no clearance. The game-day secondary and seven inactives cannot be final until the relevant medical information is known.

**Tennessee with the ball:** The Titans have rushed for 687 yards in five games. Shonn Greene has 81 carries for 387 yards; Jake Locker has thrown for 995 yards, seven touchdowns and five interceptions. Kendall Wright has 33 receptions for 288 yards. These numbers identify people and production. They do not establish which front Tennessee prefers to attack, its protection declarations, or its response to a specific disguised pressure. Crennel should prepare run fits, coverage responsibility and a practiced answer if the offense changes the formation rather than announcing an unverified pressure tendency.

**Tennessee defending:** Its opponents have gained 1,287 passing yards and 618 rushing yards through five games, but those totals do not, by themselves, establish a weakness against Mesh, 12 personnel or shifts. Tice can recommend early presentations that make defenders declare who travels with the second tight end and who widens with the back. That is a plan to learn something, not a report that the staff has already seen Tennessee fail that test.

**Stone's two decisions:** Give an executable game plan and specify the inactives the standing policy does not cover. The staff's medical update determines the eligible list; Stone determines the football roles. No roster move is implied or silently authorized.

## Better way to document a retained special play

Instead of: `Flea Flicker retained; opener 16; returns 24 and 28.`

Write: `Keep the Ace Flea Flicker on the available second-and-short special-play card. It is not an automatic call. I want the defense to have seen our regular 12-personnel run action and shown the safety reaction that makes the throw worth attempting. If that reaction doesn't appear, we stay with the base call. Keep Jones-Drew in his established exchange role.`

This is a *sample choice*, not an instruction actually submitted for Week 6. The game must not report the safety reaction as having happened unless it actually records one.

## Better way to discuss a real Week 5 play

**Weak version:** `The runner's third-and-long screen went for 15 yards, creating the 4th-and-1 policy exception. The team retained 10 points off five trips.`

**Football version, grounded in Week 5:** `Jacksonville reached the Pittsburgh 3 early in the third quarter, then lost yardage on an intentional-grounding penalty. On third-and-16 from the 17, Jones-Drew took the slow screen 15 yards to the 2. That left fourth-and-1. The sideline did not revisit the field-goal decision that had been set before the third-down snap. After a timeout and a delay-of-game penalty, Scobee kicked from the 7. Jacksonville led 10-6, but it had passed up a short-yardage decision at the goal line.`

This explains the exact football problem without claiming unrecorded Pittsburgh coverage or an unobserved blown protection. The loss itself is not being rerun or rewritten here.

## Better example of a press exchange

The following is an **illustrative invented exchange, not a historical quotation**:

**Reporter:** You were at the two, fourth-and-one, and you kicked. Was that the call you wanted?

**Stone:** No. We'd made the kicking decision before third down. Maurice got us to the two and we didn't change it. That part is on me.

**Reporter:** Is that a communication issue or a philosophy issue?

**Stone:** The situation changed. The decision didn't. We'll make sure the fourth-down call is made with the actual down and spot from now on.

The meaningful point is the follow-up. The second question responds to his first answer instead of feeding him another statistic. It does not invent a new Week 6 policy: the actual standing fourth-down instruction already exists in the project's current job record.
