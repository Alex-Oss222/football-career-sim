# Alex Stone | Ownership review

**Jacksonville Jaguars · Ownership perspective · October 5, 2014**

**Last employment decision:** January 15, 2014. Alex Stone was retained on his existing contract.

[Coach profile](README.md) · [Coaching philosophy](Coaching_Profile.md) · [2014 season](../2014/README.md)

## Our view of Alex Stone

We hired Alex because we believed he was the right head coach for Jacksonville, not because we needed someone to hold the job until the roster got better. We liked his football background, his approach to teaching and the staff he wanted to build. We believed he and Dave Caldwell could work together without confusing who made the football decisions and who controlled personnel.

Alex has justified that choice. He took over a two-win club and gave us a ten-win team, a playoff appearance and a postseason victory in his first year. There were growing pains and some bad games. We didn't expect every problem to disappear in a season. What matters is that Jacksonville became a better football team far more quickly than we had reason to expect.

## What changed in his first season

We finished **2-14 in 2012**. With Alex as head coach, Jacksonville finished **10-6 in 2013**, second in the AFC South, and went to Kansas City and won the Wild Card game. We lost the Divisional game at Tennessee. That result was disappointing because we had earned the opportunity to keep playing, but it doesn't erase what the club accomplished.

The improvement was visible on both sides of the ball. Jacksonville scored **356 points**, compared with 255 the year before, and allowed **346**, down from 444. The offensive line surrendered fewer sacks, the running game improved and the defense was far better at getting off the field on third down. Some losses were ugly, and the overall turnover margin still needed attention. Those are football problems for the next season, not reasons to downgrade an eight-win improvement and a playoff victory.

Alex finished the season as a Coach of the Year finalist. Maurice Jones-Drew was Comeback Player of the Year. Those honors followed a turnaround that everybody in the building contributed to.

## The quarterback and the young roster

Dave controls trades, contracts and draft decisions. He acquired Kirk Cousins and moved Blaine Gabbert. Alex and his staff ran the quarterback competition, gave Cousins the starting job and coached the offense around him. That is how a general manager and head coach are supposed to work together. Credit belongs to both sides.

Cousins threw for **3,981 yards and 23 touchdowns** in his first season with Jacksonville. His 18 interceptions and the protection breakdowns were real. He was also a productive starting quarterback for a club that entered the offseason without a settled answer at the position. The right response is to keep developing him, not pretend the year was a disappointment.

The draft class gave us useful football immediately. Lane Johnson started at right tackle. Travis Kelce, Jordan Poyer, Sio Moore and Bacarri Rambo earned roles. Adam Thielen came in undrafted and became our second receiver. The personnel department found players, and the coaches gave those players work they could handle. Their development is part of why we have a foundation for the next few seasons.

## The building Alex runs

Alex established **The Prowl** and its *Good. Better. Best.* standard. His staff teaches assignments, asks players to explain what they see, and returns to problems rather than treating one meeting as a correction. He expects coaches to be prepared and to accept the same scrutiny they give players. Those methods showed up in the team's preparation and in young players earning meaningful roles.

His manner matters too. Alex invites disagreement, keeps communication open and knows the people he coaches. He can be relaxed in a meeting without accepting sloppy football. He takes responsibility publicly, and his players know an error will still be addressed when the film comes up.

He built a coaching staff capable of carrying those standards into practice and games. Mike Tice's preparation, Romeo Crennel's defense and the work of the position coaches deserve their share of the credit. Alex leads that group. He and Dave have also worked through personnel disagreements without requiring ownership to settle ordinary differences of football judgment.

## Where Jacksonville stands now

We are **3-2 through Week 5 of 2014 and first in the AFC South**. The club has lost two straight. The Pittsburgh game was a poor performance, particularly with the turnovers. That deserves correction. It is not a fresh question about whether Alex belongs in the job.

This roster is still being built. We need a more dependable passing game, fewer giveaways, stronger protection and better defensive execution on third down. We need to stay healthy and continue developing the young players. Those are normal responsibilities for the football department, with Dave responsible for personnel and Alex responsible for coaching and game-day decisions.

Jacksonville has already shown it can reach and win a playoff game under this staff. The work now is to make that level of football sustainable. We are building on a successful first season rather than starting over.

## Our position

**Alex Stone is our head coach, and we support the direction of the club.**

We retained him on January 15, 2014, under the contract we originally agreed to. Neither side asked for a raise, an extension or a change of authority at that review, and no later employment action has been recorded.

We have every reason to let Alex and Dave continue the work they started. They have earned that confidence with football results and a functioning partnership. We expect the problems to be addressed as they arise, but we do not treat one difficult Sunday as a referendum on the coach.
