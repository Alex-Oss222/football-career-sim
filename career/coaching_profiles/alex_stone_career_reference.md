**Current through August 4, 2014.**

This is the factual reference for Stone's identity, experience, record and authority. The profile describes his coaching; the assessment log explains changes in that judgment.

## Identity and background

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Professional name | Alex Stone |
| Full name | Alex-Lamar Stone |
| Born | September 21, 1963, Miami, Florida; age 50 at this cutoff |
| Current job | Jacksonville Jaguars head coach, appointed January 15, 2013 |
| Family | Married to Inbar Zsela-Stone; no children |
| Languages | English native; Spanish conversational; Arabic, French and Japanese limited/basic |
| Playing position | Tight end; 6 ft 4 in, 235 lb playing weight |

Stone played at Miami from 1982 through 1985. He was a sophomore on Howard Schnellenberger's 1983 national championship team; Jimmy Johnson coached his final two college seasons. He then played ten seasons for the San Diego Chargers, 1986 through 1995, retiring after the 1995 season.

His established playing record is 150 games, 488 receptions, 5,912 yards and 44 touchdowns, with five Pro Bowl and two first-team All-Pro selections. He played in the 49-26 Super Bowl XXIX loss to San Francisco on January 29, 1995. These are the character's established career facts; his addition does not subtract from real players' records or change that game's result.

## Education

| Qualification | Institution | Year |
|---|---|---|
| BS | University of Miami | 1986 |
| MBA | University of San Diego | 1996 |
| PhD, Exercise Physiology | University of Miami | 2002 |

The doctorate concerned motor-learning acquisition and retention. Stone was in residence in 1996 and 1997 while a football graduate assistant, spent 1998 on qualifying exams and the dissertation proposal, and completed the dissertation part-time during his early coaching years. The field is exercise physiology, not neuropsychology. The degree does not confer medical authority.

Through the completed 2013 season, Stone had seventeen coaching seasons, thirteen in the NFL, and two completed NFL head-coaching seasons. His assistant championships were with New England in the 2003 and 2004 seasons and New Orleans in 2009. They are team accomplishments in the stated roles.

Before Jacksonville, Oakland interviewed him twice after the 2011 season without making an offer; Indianapolis interviewed him once without a second interview. Tampa Bay and Miami interest went through his agent, with no interviews established.

## Jacksonville employment and authority

Stone accepted the original four-year, fully guaranteed contract on January 15, 2013. The January 15, 2014 review retained him without an extension, raise or authority change. Compensation was not specified in the accepted offer and is not supplied here.

| Responsibility | Current arrangement |
|---|---|
| Coaching staff, program and football roles | Stone selects coaches and controls coaching assignments, the depth chart and game-day decisions. |
| Offense | Stone calls plays and has the final offensive menu and game-plan decision. Tice coordinates the weekday process. |
| Defense | Crennel coordinates and ordinarily calls the unit under Stone's head-coach oversight. |
| Special teams | Westhoff coordinates the unit following his February 11, 2014 appointment. Stone's temporary coverage after Lowry's departure ended that day. |
| Delegation | Stone may call, delegate or take back any phase. The current division remains in force until he changes it. |
| Personnel and finance | Caldwell has final authority over scouting, acquisitions, the draft, contracts and the cap. Leading quarterback commitments require Stone's written projection and consultation; Caldwell retains the final call. |
| Medical decisions | Clinicians control clearance and restrictions. Coaching plans must respect them. |
