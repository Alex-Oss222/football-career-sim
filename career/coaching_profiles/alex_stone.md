## Current synthesis

Stone is now a head coach with a full season of building and operating his own Jacksonville program, not only a former coordinator taking over somebody else's team. He assembled a staff, installed a teaching method, held an open quarterback competition, coached Cousins through an entire starting season, assigned roles to young players and experienced a complete regular-season/postseason preparation cycle. Those are established experiences. They do not, by themselves, establish exceptional teaching, roster judgment or game management.

**Working interpretation:** the clearest supported direction is a head coach who works through detailed teaching and adaptable assignments, keeps a substantial personal role in the offense, and delegates real operational responsibility to experienced assistants. Thielen's movement between taught receiver jobs, Cousins's retained spring operation and the staff's distinct room responsibilities show more than a résumé claim. The useful idea is continuity of the football job while the presentation changes. Credit belongs to the actual teachers and players, not exclusively to Stone.

The uneven part is sustaining that individual approach under weekly pressure. The exit record contains parked evaluations, missing individual feedback and unresolved cutup promises. His own Houston review identified a gap between the intended running plan and the calls he made. A later self-check is a response to that problem; the record has not established that it reliably changed his behavior. His offensive-caller, whole-team and interim special-teams responsibilities also create competing demands that need visible delegation and follow-through.

He may become a more adaptable caller, a better teacher of assistants, a more effective whole-team decision maker, or some combination. The next phases should reveal that. Do not force him into a fixed “players' coach,” disciplinarian, offensive genius or system-coach label. Preserve his established football background and let his choices and their supported effects change the working assessment.

## Stone's own recorded view and room for revision

The January review records his acknowledgment of the Houston run/call problem and his responsibility for the offensive menu. Cousins's exit records a preference for correcting the particular bad throw and raising call-confidence questions before the sheet is final. Thielen's exit records that the same concepts across new jobs helped him, alongside the disappearance of his individual development home after the bye. These perspectives can change Stone's approach; they are not unanimous proof that his philosophy worked.

The user can edit the following working direction without inventing a new event:

- **Preserve:** specific teaching, consistent job language, meaningful player responsibility, independent medical authority and actual coordinator delegation.
- **Explore:** how Cousins and other players see the problem; whether a different explanation, role or support arrangement opens a useful answer; what assistants can own more clearly.
- **Repair and verify:** closed-loop individual feedback, shared protection/coverage instructions, parked evaluations, caller self-scout and complete situation information.
- **Leave open:** future offensive identity, Cousins's ceiling, the ideal division of weekly work and calling, and whether an apparent pattern survives more reliable evidence.

These are assessment priorities, not new calls, hires, promises, medical permissions or player role assignments. The active playbook and separately authorized decisions govern the football work.

In the offseason program, review whether the prepared cutups and compatible cues answer the players' actual questions within the lawful contact rules. At OTAs, observe delayed transfer and new player solutions. At minicamp close, compare individual written feedback with unresolved promises. During the season, keep the caller and teaching review alive after the bye and when results are disappointing.
