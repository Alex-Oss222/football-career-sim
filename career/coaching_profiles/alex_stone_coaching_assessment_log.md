## January 14, 2013: entry assessment

Stone arrived as an experienced offensive coordinator and caller with a full New Orleans interim head-coaching season. He had already worked in quarterback, secondary and tight-end rooms. The open questions concerned building a permanent staff, developing a young starting quarterback in his own program and sustaining responsibility across seasons. They were not questions about whether he had ever called a game or led an NFL team.

The [prehire dossier](../../library/alex_stone_character_dossier_pre_hire.md) remains a frozen account of that starting point. His playing honors, education and assistant championships establish background, not the quality of every subsequent decision.

## 2013: building and operating his own program

The [staff hires](../2013/offseason/staff_building/hires.md), [OTAs](../2013/offseason/otas/output.md) and [minicamp](../2013/offseason/mandatory_minicamp/output.md) established actual Jacksonville experience. Coaches taught assignments, corrected them and returned to them under a changed look. Cousins retained parts of the spring operation; Thielen later described familiar concepts helping him take on different receiver jobs. The evidence supported this teaching method in particular assignments. It did not make every position coach's work Stone's personal achievement.

The [camp competition](../2013/offseason/training_camp/output.md) and [roster decisions](../2013/offseason/training_camp/roster_decisions.md) added responsibility for choosing and developing a starter. During the season, Stone kept Johnson at right tackle with help, used Brewster at center rather than moving several linemen, and retained Brewster when Meester returned. Thielen kept an expanded role after Blackmon returned. These decisions support a willingness to change support and let younger players' work alter roles. They do not prove the superiority of every choice from the win-loss record. See [Seattle](../2013/regular_season/week_03_jacksonville_at_seattle/output.md), [Denver](../2013/regular_season/week_06_jacksonville_at_denver/output.md) and [London](../2013/regular_season/week_08_san_francisco_at_jacksonville/output.md).

Crennel retained defensive calling responsibility while Tice ran the weekday offensive process. Stone had now built and operated his own staff. How well he would replace assistants, develop coaches and maintain that division over time remained open.

## November 24 to December 5, 2013: the caller departs from his plan

At [Houston](../2013/regular_season/week_12_jacksonville_at_houston/output.md), the intended running emphasis became seventeen runs and forty-five dropbacks. In [Cleveland preparation](../2013/regular_season/week_13_jacksonville_at_cleveland/output.md), Stone acknowledged abandoning a run game Houston had not stopped. He introduced a reason-for-the-next-pass question after three consecutive called passes. The [Houston rematch](../2013/regular_season/week_14_houston_at_jacksonville/output.md) kept the run involved.

**Assessment:** a specific calling error and an attempt to correct it were established. Cousins later raised a six-pass Cleveland sequence, leaving the consistency of that self-check unresolved. A run quota would not answer the underlying question: did the opponent, situation and available execution justify the next call?

Absence or an inactive decision alone could not prove a character judgment or successful team culture.

## February 2, 2014: preserved assessment baseline

The assessment compiled September 29, 2026 concluded that Stone had gained a full season of building a staff, operating his program and developing a young NFL starter. Adaptable assignments, substantive delegation and specific teaching were supported. Incomplete individual feedback, unresolved cause review and a caller's departure from his own plan were the principal weaknesses.

At that checkpoint, Lowry's January 12 departure had left Stone covering special teams while a replacement search remained open. The added responsibility did not establish specialist technical expertise. Late-game management also remained uncertain: the [engine defect register](../../runtime/defect_register.md) documented clock and chain limitations, preventing a clean judgment from the endings alone.

The useful next evidence was delivery of promised material, compatible coaching cues, delayed retests, fair opportunities and calling decisions made against a reliable situation record. These were assessment questions, not completed repairs or new football instructions.

## February 11, 2014: special-teams responsibility transferred

The [Westhoff appointment](../2014/01_Early_Offseason/staff_changes/hires.md), reflected in the February 17 review, closed the search after Oakland retained Bobby April. Westhoff became coordinator and Stone's temporary coverage ended.

**Changed:** the staffing gap closed. The hire itself did not show how the partnership would work or add special-teams teaching expertise to Stone's assessment.

## May 23, 2014: spring corrections and written feedback

The [Phase Two report](../2014/02_Offseason_Training/Phase_Two/training_report.md) records work on Boot Flood, the protection cue and the Cover 1 bunch rule. Where players received conflicting answers, Stone had the coaches agree on the instruction before reteaching. Later reps held the corrected answer under a changed look. Boot Flood was back in teaching; describing it as still permanently removed would now be stale.

The [rookie minicamp report](../2014/02_Offseason_Training/Rookie_Minicamp/training_report.md) also preserved a weakness: split families were described before being drawn. Players asked for adjustments to be explained at installation. Each signed Phase Two participant received written notes at the handoff.

**Changed in scope:** actual teaching and some written follow-up answered part of the January concern. Conflicting or compressed instruction still occurred. These were unopposed reps, with no evidence yet of contact transfer or game use. Film packets remained unissued.

## June 13, 2014: delayed retests against opposition

The [OTA report](../2014/02_Offseason_Training/OTAs/training_report.md) adds opposed, non-contact work. A late interior echo, stack crosser switch, Y-Cross stagger and wrong-way slide each led to a shared answer, another rep and a later retest without the prompt. Stone did not remove the concept in response to those failures. Installation followed the prepared schedule, including the June 3 movement and Y-Cross work.

**Assessment retained with broader evidence:** the correction method now had opposed practice behind it. The five May 12 starting points remained open because the evidence did not settle the contested jobs. The late-change protection answer still needed contact. Planned work without a usual communicator tested operation; it did not create an injury or establish game management.

## June 20, 2014: spring follow-through partly delivered

The [minicamp report and handoff](../2014/02_Offseason_Training/Mandatory_Minicamp/training_report.md) record the requested sideline drawings, Cousins's end-zone angle on the promised day and a written summer sheet for every signed participant. Stone set the examination before the practices and kept the test intact when difficulties appeared. The linebacker matchup with the back's vertical still needed help; the five starting points stayed open.

**Changed:** it was no longer accurate to describe player follow-up solely as a promise. Specific requests and written feedback had been delivered. Film packets remained unissued, and non-contact work could not settle blocking, finish or whether the method would survive a crowded game week.

## July 29, 2014: camp opening

The [camp report](../2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/training_report.md) records the non-contact opening block, a correction to Hawkins's dig depth and a delayed retest. Camp-entry retest findings reached every signed player in person. Stone retained the starting points and arranged the padded work around the coordinators' questions.

**Assessment retained:** specific correction and follow-up continued. The camp opening added another delivery point, but the evidence still could not decide the physical competitions. No film packet had been issued.

## August 1, 2014: first padded practices

The July 30 and August 1 entries in the [camp report](../2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/training_report.md) add controlled contact with protected quarterbacks and no period live to the ground. Mixed groups addressed named evaluation questions. Eleven corrections were given in the position rooms and retested in the relevant periods. No starting point changed after two padded days containing both successful and unsuccessful reps.

**New evidence:** the promised Kelce blocking assessment began. His assignment and initial movement were sound, but his base rose later in the period and the issue recurred. It was now observed work under correction, not an untouched promise or a closed weakness. Position coaches owned the technical instruction; Stone owned the opportunity and follow-up.

## August 4, 2014: scrimmage and block review

The [August 2 scrimmage and August 4 close](../2014/04_Training_Camp_and_Preseason/Training_Camp/training_report.md) ran series with coaches on the sideline, calls through coordinators and corrections after the series. Two substitution-timing errors were treated as operation problems; Tice and Crennel clarified the taught responses. The mixed evidence did not settle the open competitions.

Kelce's base rose late in the scrimmage and held in the shorter August 4 period. Zernhelt explicitly kept the repeated-effort question open. Other recurring corrections were retested, including one that the scrimmage had not presented. Every signed participant received the block review in person. Film packets remained unissued.

**Current assessment:** follow-through is better evidenced than in January, and the camp structure has now operated with controlled contact and coaches off the field. The scrimmage was against Jacksonville's own units, with prescribed contact and no score. It cannot establish opponent adjustment, reliable play-calling under a deficit, a competitive finish or season-long teaching consistency. No 2014 preseason game has been played at this cutoff.
