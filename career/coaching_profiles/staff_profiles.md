# Assistant coaches: current work and developing approach

[Method](README.md) | [Stone](alex_stone.md) | [2013 staff and responsibilities](../2013/coaching_staff.md) | [2014 room plans](../2014/03_offseason_training/coaching_methods/unit_plans.md)

**Evidence through February 2, 2014; compiled September 29, 2026.** These are editable working assessments of the eleven remaining assistants, with Lowry's departure preserved. They synthesize the staff record, dated 2013 work and player exits, not new interviews or annual coach ratings. Each coach has a full Jacksonville season of additional experience; no actual post-divergence real-world career is used to supply his future development.

The linked exits and [player assessments](../2014/00_team/player_development/roster_profiles.md) distinguish observed work, retrospective interpretation and missing evidence. Player requests are evidence of a feedback need, not automatic proof of a technical coaching defect. A prepared packet does not prove delivery or retention.

## Mike Tice: offensive coordinator

**Current responsibility:** weekday offense, run/protection coordination, installs, personnel and self-scout. Stone remains the offensive caller. His prior head-coach/line/coordinator experience is context, not an ability bonus.

**Working synthesis:** his Jacksonville year adds experience coordinating this particular young quarterback, line and receiving group under another offensive caller. The program has a defined division of labor; the unresolved issue is whether the weekly plan, protection instructions and live calls are reconciled consistently. Stone's Houston review and the question of Kelce's protection-versus-route opportunity belong in that joint examination. The engine's rushing or sack totals cannot isolate Tice's contribution.

**Next observation:** reconcile one shared protection rule across QB/center/back, document a disagreement before the call sheet freezes, and review a real change of game direction with Stone. Identify what Tice recommended and what was actually called. Sources: [January review](../2013/season_review/stone_2013_review_and_exit_interview.md), [Cousins](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/kirk_cousins.md), [Kelce](../2013/exit_interviews/core/travis_kelce.md).

## Romeo Crennel: defensive coordinator and caller

**Current responsibility:** defensive plan, installation, calls and staff coordination. Stone's authored defensive system does not remove that calling authority.

**Working synthesis:** a full year with this front and secondary adds concrete experience beyond his extensive entry résumé. The records describe useful front/fit operation and communication during teammate absences. They also leave questions about when the front call arrived and how help was communicated. Those are specific shared-job issues, not a verdict from points allowed or sack totals. Smith's relay work is distinct from Crennel's sideline call.

**Next observation:** the front, linebacker and secondary independently describe the same call/help exchange after a changed look; log a late/incompatible instruction as a staff contribution where supported. Resolve backup relay assignments in their proper decision record. Sources: [Marks](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/sen_derrick_marks.md), [Miller](../2013/exit_interviews/core/roy_miller.md), [Smith](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/daryl_smith.md), [Grimes](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/brent_grimes.md).

## Jeremy Bates: quarterbacks

**Current responsibility:** daily quarterback teaching and assessment, with Skalaski support and Tice/Stone on shared protection/menu questions. The old QB plan's “Greg Bates” heading is a known name error, not a different coach.

**Working synthesis:** Cousins's repeated spring operation and immediate corrections support specific teaching that worked. His exit preference for a concise cue paired with contrasting examples gives Bates useful information about this starter now. Henne's recurring operational prompts and the unclear explanation of Bray's reduced work require their own responses. Neither can be resolved by repeating the starter's packet or diagnosing a general lack of talent.

**Next observation:** align the cue and examples with the QB's actual question, preserve an effective aggressive answer, then check delayed transfer without a prompt. State fair backup evaluation conditions. Sources: [Cousins](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/kirk_cousins.md), [Henne](../2013/exit_interviews/main_core/chad_henne.md), [reserve offense](../2013/exit_interviews/summaries_offense.md).

## George Yarno: offensive line and run game

Assignment/communication evidence can support a useful teaching contribution; contact technique and a changed front require their own evidence. Individual linemen asked for their own review. ## Tim Spencer: running backs

**Working synthesis:** track, protection and outlet work provide a more useful basis for assessment than rushing averages. Retained corrections and recurring questions differ by back. Fumbles require separate carriage/contact/assignment review; a public correction or a repeated ball-security period does not establish the cause or that it was repaired. Rotation communication also belongs to the staff.

**Next observation:** establish the assigned track/threat, compare a correct example with the disputed rep, and verify the response later. Explain an actual role change through Tice/Stone's authority.
