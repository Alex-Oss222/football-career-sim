## 2. Week setup and game preparation

**Opponent context:** Indianapolis came in 2-1 with the strongest defense Jacksonville had faced in the branch. It had allowed 13.3 points and 251 yards a game (152 passing, 99 rushing), and opponents had converted 14 of 42 third downs. It had 14 sacks from nine players, four by Cory Redding, and five takeaways. On offense it had turned the ball over seven times, including five Andrew Luck interceptions, and allowed six sacks. Reggie Wayne had 20 catches for 234 yards and four touchdowns, and Vick Ballard had 68 carries and 18 targets. Stone's reading was that the pressure came from the structure, not from one player, so the answer was protection identification, not a chip on one rusher.

**Stone's decisions:** Mike Brewster stayed at center after the zero-sack game in Seattle. Brad Meester's projection cleared on September 27, and he dressed as the reserve center. Stone called that a continuity decision, not a demotion, to be revisited on Monday. With Paul Posluszny out, Daryl Smith and Russell Allen were the base linebackers, Smith took over as the communication lead, and Stanford and Moore were next. Inactives: Pasztor, Mosley, C.J. Wilson, Posluszny, Edwards and Rutland; Blackmon remained on Reserve/Suspended. The edge order stayed Babin, Mincey, Branch and Davis. The offense was built on 12 personnel with a smaller menu. 20 Draw was added as the answer to the pass rush, Power Pass Post-Cross (MAX) and Mills (MAX) were the only shots, and Boot Flood and 10 Empty stayed off.

**Practice / teaching emphasis:** Monday classified the Seattle mistakes: Sherman's interception on Dagger, Jones-Drew's fumble, and how the defense corrected after Seattle's three early scores. The zero-sack protection was kept as it was. Monday and Tuesday built Indianapolis's defensive map under Mike Tice. Wednesday repped the core runs, the quick game and both screens, with Brewster on odd-front communication. Thursday was a pressure period for every group, built on protected, hot or kill, plus situations. Friday deleted uncertain calls, and Saturday was mental work.

## 3. Pregame media Q&A

**Reporter:** Brad Meester is back. Why isn't he starting?

**Stone:** Mike played a clean game in Seattle against a defense that had been sacking people. Brad dresses and is ready. We'll look at the position again Monday. It's not a demotion.

**Reporter:** Indianapolis has 14 sacks. How do you slow that down?

**Stone:** Know where it's coming from before the snap. It's been nine different players, so it isn't one guy we can chip.

**Reporter:** Who replaces Paul Posluszny?

**Stone:** Russell Allen takes the bigger role, and Daryl Smith runs the communication. We're not rebuilding the defense around one absence.

### Game narrative: selected highlights

Indianapolis scored on the first snap of the game, a seven-yard Luck pass to Reggie Wayne, after Scobee's opening kickoff went for a touchback. The ledger has no field position to reconcile that possession with a touchback; see section 8. Jacksonville's first possession went three plays and a punt. Its second reached field-goal range on two 12 Doubles Stick throws to Cecil Shorts, for 19 and 18 yards, and Scobee kicked a 38-yarder.

The shot from Stone's opening board came next. Indianapolis punted, and Cam Johnson sacked Cousins on 20 Doubles Drive. On the next snap Cousins hit Toney Clemons for a 20-yard touchdown on 12 Ace Right Power Pass Post-Cross with MAX protection, and Jacksonville led 10-7 after one quarter. Brent Grimes then intercepted Luck on a throw toward Ballard. Two possessions later Montell Owens caught a 26-yard Stick pass, and Jonathan Grimes scored from three yards on 21 Pro Left Counter R. It was 17-7 at halftime.

Jacksonville received the second-half kickoff and drove 78 yards in 11 plays. Shorts caught a 23-yard Spacing throw, and Jones-Drew scored on a nine-yard run on 11 Trips Right Inside L. Adam Vinatieri missed a 32-yard field goal on Indianapolis's next possession. Jacksonville then gave the ball back when Vontae Davis forced and recovered a Clemons fumble after a Spacing catch.

Indianapolis gave it straight back: Brent Grimes forced and recovered a Ballard fumble after a catch. Jonathan Grimes ran for 16 on Inside L, and Clemons caught a 15-yard Spacing touchdown for a 31-7 lead with 11:28 left. Vinatieri's 23-yard field goal was Indianapolis's only other score.

The team numbers read like the plan. Jacksonville converted 7 of 10 third downs against a defense that had allowed a third of them, and Indianapolis, with 14 sacks in three games, got Cousins once. Cousins completed 21 of 31 for 167 yards and two touchdowns without an interception. The run game produced 87 yards on 23 carries and scored twice. Luck completed 32 of 41 for 224 yards, 11 of them to Wayne for 83, and Indianapolis ran for 65. Jacksonville's giveaway came after the lead was 17 points.
