## 2. Week setup and game preparation

**Opponent context:** The staff had no completed Kansas City defensive scouting report entering the week, so no Chiefs front, coverage or pressure tendency was assumed. The opening possessions were built to find those answers: box count and overhang behavior against 12 personnel, man or zone rules against Bunch and Trey motion, and the pressure picture against Empty.

**Stone's priorities:** Execute what Cousins and the unit had already shown in camp and preseason. Get Cousins clean early information through quick game and HALF/SOLID protection before asking for long-developing throws. Shots only on a trigger: a safety fitting the run, an isolated matchup or a coverage reaction. 11 and 12 personnel as the base presentations, 21 as the physical counter, 20 as the matchup package. No six-lineman package in the opener with Pasztor still on medical hold. Jones-Drew leads the backs; Anderson and Grimes rotate by practiced concept and protection responsibility, with no touch quota.

**Practice / teaching emphasis:** Wednesday selected and taught the weekly core, the protection alerts and the first 15. Thursday rehearsed every run with its complement, third down by distance, red zone and screen timing, with Stone calling a mock game from the sheet. Friday removed anything that had not been clean, and Saturday was recall only. Mills and the extra-lineman package did not make the final sheet.

**Workload / recovery:** Pasztor (medical hold) and Mosley (medically unavailable) were ruled out. Inactives: Pasztor, Mosley, Mark Asper, Mike Brewster, Ryan Davis, Lavar Edwards and Kevin Rutland.

### Game narrative: selected highlights

The first quarter was scoreless. Jacksonville's opening possession moved on a 16-yard Texas completion to Marcedes Lewis before stalling into a punt, and the third drive ended when Brandon Flowers intercepted Cousins on a throw for Cecil Shorts. Kansas City punted on each of its first two possessions.

Alan Ball returned the favor early in the second quarter, intercepting Alex Smith on a pass for Cyrus Gray. The drive that followed ran 16 snaps. Its biggest gain came on a shot call: out of 13 Heavy, Cousins hit Shorts on Post-Cross for 34 yards. Jones-Drew finished it on the Inside/Bubble access call, taking the handoff inside for a 15-yard touchdown with 4:35 left in the half. On the first snap of Kansas City's next possession, Jordan Poyer forced a Jamaal Charles fumble and recovered it.

Josh Scobee's field goal early in the third quarter made it 10-0. Ryan Succop answered for Kansas City, and Jacksonville's reply went 10 snaps: Jonathan Grimes ran for 15, and Cousins found Lewis for a one-yard touchdown out of Bunch on Mesh. The fourth quarter opened with a 19-yard Smash completion to Shorts and a three-yard Jones-Drew touchdown on Power with the Smoke access, for a 24-3 lead.

Kansas City scored its only touchdown on the next drive. Anthony Sherman caught a 33-yard pass and Charles ran in from the three. Eric Berry then intercepted Cousins on a Boot Flood throw for Montell Owens, and Succop's second field goal cut the margin to 24-13 with 4:33 left.

Jacksonville's last possession started with 1:20 to play. Grimes ran for 16 on Split Zone, and Cousins threw a nine-yard touchdown to Adam Thielen with 18 seconds left.

Protection remained the offense's recurring cost. Kansas City sacked Cousins three times, with Johnson charged with two and Meester with one, and Jacksonville faced 25 third downs, converting eight. Cousins finished 27 of 47 for 225 yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions. The run game carried more of the load: 180 yards on 35 carries, with Grimes gaining 77 on nine carries to Jones-Drew's 76 on 21. Kansas City converted two of ten third downs, and its two giveaways were Ball's interception and Poyer's strip.

## 5. Postgame media Q&A

**Reporter:** Two interceptions and three sacks. How do you grade Cousins's day?

**Stone:** I'll grade it after the tape. The first interception and the one on the boot are throws I need to see with the coverage in front of me before I say whose they are. He also led four touchdown drives.

**Reporter:** Lane Johnson was charged with two of the sacks.

**Stone:** He was. Both are on the tape and we'll fix the technique the same way we did in camp. He's our right tackle.

**Reporter:** Grimes had almost as many yards as Jones-Drew on less than half the carries. Does that change the rotation?

**Stone:** Maurice is our lead back. Jonathan ran well when his concepts came up. Nobody gets a quota, so the carries go where the calls go.

**Reporter:** Twenty-five third downs. Is that a concern?

**Stone:** Eight conversions is not good enough. We lived on third down because first and second down didn't get us far enough, and that's where we start this week.

**Reporter:** The defense didn't have a sack.

**Stone:** It had two takeaways and held them to two of ten on third down. Romeo will tell you he wants the rush to finish, and so do I.

## 6. Coaching takeaways and next-week carry-forward

**What held up:** The run game (180 yards, 5.1 per carry) and the one shot taken, the 34-yard Post-Cross to Shorts. On defense, Ball and Poyer produced both takeaways and Kansas City converted two of ten third downs.

**What must be corrected:** Right-side pass protection: Johnson was charged with two sacks and Meester with one, the same physical and leverage losses as the preseason. Both interceptions need film review to separate the read, the throw and the route. Early-down efficiency left the offense in 25 third downs. The defense recorded no sack; Marks had two pressures.

**Next-week carry-forward:** Oakland prep starts from the Johnson protection cutups and the two interceptions. The Week 1 menu stays; nothing is added until the corrections hold in Wednesday and Thursday work.
