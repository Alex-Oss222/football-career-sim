## 2. Week setup and game preparation

**Opponent context:** Jacksonville entered without a completed branch Kansas City defensive tendency report. Staff therefore used only period-available film to build an identification map and did not treat a guessed front, coverage, pressure or motion tendency as fact. The offensive task was to execute the retained system, protect Kirk Cousins with clean information and use early personnel, formation and motion presentations to identify Kansas City's actual answers. Crennel owned the defensive call and reported the three team-level problems: controlling Jamaal Charles, keeping Alex Smith from extending manageable downs and preventing Kansas City from living in favorable third downs.

**Stone's priorities:** Stone approved 11 and 12 personnel as the base presentations, 21 as a physical counter and 20 as a matchup package. The opening menu was diagnostic rather than a mandatory sequence. Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Outside Zone and Split Zone anchored the run plan; Stick, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Spacing, Smash and Texas supplied the primary pass menu. Shots required a declared safety fit, isolated matchup or coverage reaction. NORMAL tempo opened the game, HOLD followed useful declarations, and PRESS remained conditional on communication and a trapped personnel package.

**Practice / teaching emphasis:** Wednesday installed the weekly selection and the protection alerts. Thursday paired the major runs with their complements, rehearsed the opening menu, worked third down and red zone, and tested the screen answer against aggressive rush. Friday removed rather than rescued calls that still required repeated correction; Mills and the extra-lineman package did not make the game sheet. Saturday was recall only. Bates drilled KILL/HOT and zero-pressure communication with Cousins; Yarno kept the line on the concepts it could execute without new teaching; Tice coordinated run complements and the short-yardage menu. Crennel and Lowry retained control of their respective units.

**Workload / recovery:** Qualified medical communication kept Austin Pasztor on medical hold and C.J. Mosley unavailable. Both were inactive. No other Jacksonville restriction changed the Week 1 practice or game plan.

## 3. Pregame media Q&A

**Reporter:** Without a complete Kansas City tendency report in the branch, how much can you really script?

**Stone:** We can script what we do. The early calls give Kirk clean answers and give us information about their front, leverage and pressure. If the picture changes, the situation sheet wins; we are not running fifteen calls because somebody numbered them.

**Reporter:** Does the protection trouble from preseason force you to shrink the offense?

**Stone:** It forces us to be honest about what is ready. We kept the quick game, the run complements and the protections the group communicated cleanly. Anything that needed a Friday rescue is not on the sheet.

**Reporter:** Who has the final word on the defensive plan?

**Stone:** Romeo calls the defense. I need the problems, the situations and any personnel decision that reaches the head coach. I did not hire an experienced coordinator to stand next to me while I call his game.

## 4. Game

### Week 1: Kansas City Chiefs at Jacksonville Jaguars

**Final:** Kansas City 30, Jacksonville 27 | Loss
**Location:** EverBank Field, Jacksonville

### Game narrative: selected highlights

Kansas City took the opening possession to a field goal. Jacksonville's diagnostic start immediately produced the first adverse result instead of the desired clean completion: the opening offensive possession ended in a Cousins interception. The defense limited the immediate damage only briefly; Kansas City finished its next possession with a touchdown for a 10-0 lead.

Jacksonville answered rather than abandoning the approved structure. The offense finished consecutive possessions with touchdowns, using its run-and-complement menu rather than forcing the vertical alert before Kansas City declared the necessary picture. The defense intercepted Alex Smith on each side of that stretch, and Jacksonville moved ahead 14-10. That was the cleanest expression of the plan: information first, then calls earned by the observed response.

The game tightened around protection and possession value. Jacksonville allowed three sacks, with generated evidence locating leverage losses at center, guard and the reserve-tackle spot rather than an unrecognized assignment. Kansas City's Tamba Hali recorded two sacks and Justin Houston one. Jacksonville still accumulated 475 yards, but it did so while giving away three possessions.

Kansas City reclaimed the lead with a touchdown late in the second quarter and added a field goal after halftime. Jacksonville's own next scoring chance ended with an interception, leaving the offense chasing the game despite moving the ball. Stone stayed with the prepared quick answers and protected complements rather than turning the call sheet into repeated long-developing dropbacks.

A Scobee field goal made it 20-20, and Jacksonville later finished a touchdown possession to lead 27-23 with 9:20 remaining. Maurice Jones-Drew produced 121 yards from scrimmage, while Toney Clemons became the high-volume receiving outlet with nine catches for 126 yards. Cousins accounted for 274 of Jacksonville's 327 passing yards.

Kansas City answered with a field goal, then Jacksonville punted with 5:41 left. Crennel's defense forced another punt, giving Stone's offense one final possession with 3:55 remaining and a 27-26 lead. That possession ended in Jacksonville's third interception with 1:14 left. Kansas City converted the short final opportunity into the decisive touchdown and a 30-27 final.

The kernel attributed isolated passing work to Chad Henne and John Parker Wilson as well as Cousins, including one interception apiece. No generated injury or permanent quarterback-role event accompanied those series. Cousins remains QB1; the usage is a Week 1 game-management correction and is not carried forward as a rotation.

## 5. Postgame media Q&A

**Reporter:** You moved the ball for 475 yards. Why did that not become a win?

**Stone:** Three possessions ended with us giving them the football. Yardage does not buy those back. We had the lead late, got another possession, and did not finish it.

**Reporter:** Kansas City put 335 scrimmage yards through Jamaal Charles. What failed?

**Stone:** We did not control the back in either phase. There are run-fit plays, coverage plays and tackle-finish plays on that tape. Romeo and I will separate them before we decide whether the answer is assignment, call or execution.

**Reporter:** The protection allowed three sacks after it was the main preseason correction. Did the weekly plan work?

**Stone:** The communication was better than the result in several spots, but identified assignments still ended in leverage losses. That is not good enough, and it is also different from pretending the whole protection was unidentified. We will coach the actual problem.

**Reporter:** Why did all three quarterbacks record attempts?

**Stone:** Kirk is the starter. The extra series did not help us and there is no role change coming out of it. The substitution and communication process belongs to me, and we will correct it.

**Reporter:** What carries to Oakland?

**Stone:** The offense had answers and finished three touchdown possessions. We keep the answers. We correct the turnovers, the protection finish and the way we handled the quarterback operation.

## 6. Coaching takeaways and next-week carry-forward

**What held up:** The prepared menu created enough functional offense for 475 yards, three touchdowns and two field goals. The run game produced 148 yards, the offense found productive outlets beyond the nominal lead receivers, and the defense generated four sacks and two takeaways. The quick answers and complements allowed Jacksonville to keep moving without forcing unearned shots.

**What must be corrected:** Jacksonville lost the turnover margin and the last possession. Cousins' interception reflected a late decision on generated evidence; the two backup interceptions compound a substitution/operation failure rather than creating a quarterback competition. Protection assignments were generally identified, but Meester, Nwaneri and Bradfield each lost leverage on a sack event. Defensively, Charles' combined rushing and receiving production requires separate review of fits, coverage ownership and tackling rather than one generic effort judgment.

**Next-week carry-forward:** Cousins remains QB1. Stone and Bates will reduce the quarterback substitution path to emergency-only absent a new approved decision. Yarno retains the protection identification language but adds technique work on leverage and finish. Crennel will lead the Charles cutup and separate run-fit, underneath-coverage and tackle-finish corrections. The offense carries forward the diagnostic opening principle, quick pressure answers and trigger-only shot rule; Oakland preparation and its plan have not begun.
