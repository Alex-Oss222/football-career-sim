## 2. First-half review

Stone's framing to the team was "6-2" and "0-0": the six wins cannot beat Tennessee. The review was organized as Good, Better, Best.

**Through eight games** (branch receipts; team passing yards are gross):
- **Offense:** 191 points (23.9 a game); Cousins 1,871 passing yards and 14 touchdowns; 39.8% on third down (45 of 113); 10 giveaways; 19 sacks allowed (3, 3, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 3 by week).
- **Receivers:** Shorts 55 receptions; Lewis 36 for 420 yards.
- **Defense:** 126 points allowed (15.8 a game); opponents 31.7% on third down (32 of 101); 9 sacks; 6 takeaways.
- **Kicking:** Scobee 14 of 15 field goals.

**Keep:** the run defense, third-down defense and communication; the fast-start offense; Brewster at center; Thielen's development; Kelce's widening role; Blackmon's return completed.

**Correct:**
- **Turnovers.** Each of the four Week 8 interceptions was classified separately (read, leverage, protection, route, timing, decision, placement), with no single "bad game" label.
- **Protection.** Tice's staff sorted every sack by cause (physical loss, identification, communication, late pressure, overload, holding the ball, route timing, call against the look).
- **Third-down distance.** The staff charted what creates long third downs, so that fewer are needed.
- **Defense.** More disruption without losing structure: rush finish, tackling through the ball, backs and tight ends in space, and explosive-play prevention. A cut-up of every 20-yard play allowed was studied without score or opponent attached.
- **Stone.** He included his own game management in the self-scout, marked "STONE" against any decision that was his, and had Tice chart his play-calling tendencies.

## 3. The week

- **Monday:** medical evaluation and treatment on return from London; the team meeting; position film; no practice.
- **Tuesday:** the one practice. Periods were ball security, protection (the largest block), third down at fixed distances, the core runs and 6OL, Crennel's run fit, rush finish, back and tight-end coverage, and third down. Special teams were kept short.
- **Pasztor and Mosley:** both worked in their first practice since their availability was restored (Entry 46). Pasztor had interior technique, communication and conditioning work; Mosley competed for rotational tackle snaps. Neither was moved on the depth chart. Their Week 10 roles are Stone's and Crennel's decisions from that evidence and Week 10 practice.
- **Leadership council:** Cousins, Jones-Drew, Monroe, Lewis, Posluszny, Daryl Smith, Lowery and Scobee were asked what wastes practice time, what is unclear and what should not change.
- **Wednesday to Sunday:** players off. Each left with a first-half summary, two things to keep, two to correct and recovery instructions.
- **Staff:** Wednesday self-scout and separate meetings with Tice, Crennel and Lowry; Thursday morning second-half planning, including a Houston short-week skeleton for the Week 14 Thursday game; Stone away Thursday afternoon to Saturday.

## 4. Trade deadline

Before the Tuesday, October 29 deadline, Stone gave Caldwell his assessment: no need at quarterback, receiver, running back or center; pass rush, offensive-line depth and defensive-line depth only if value existed; no move before the staff had evaluated Pasztor and Mosley. Caldwell holds transaction authority. No trade proposal was open, and the deadline passed with no Jacksonville transaction.
