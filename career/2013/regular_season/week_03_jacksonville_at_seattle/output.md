## 2. Week setup and game preparation

**Opponent context:** Seattle beat Carolina 23-17 and lost 31-28 to San Francisco. Through two games its defense had seven sacks, four takeaways and a 6-for-33 third-down record against it (18.2%), while allowing 151.5 rushing yards and 231 passing yards a game. Six of the sacks and three of the takeaways came against Carolina. Seattle's offense had allowed five sacks, four of them to San Francisco, averaged 86 rushing yards and converted 12 of 26 third downs (46.2%); Sidney Rice had 183 receiving yards and two touchdowns, and Golden Tate 115 yards in Week 2. Stone's scouting drew no conclusion about Seattle's coverage or blitz distribution from those two games. His conclusion was narrower: Seattle could end a drive with pressure or a takeaway, and third down against it had been very hard to convert.

**Stone's decisions:** Mike Brewster started at center for Brad Meester, as the only other healthy center, so that no second position had to move. Mark Asper was active as interior reserve. Lane Johnson stayed at right tackle with structural help (tight-end chips, back scans, SOLID and MAX protection, quick-game timing), and Cameron Bradfield was the in-game contingency with no pregame rotation. With C.J. Wilson out, the edge order was Babin, Mincey, Branch and Davis, with Lavar Edwards inactive. Inactives: Pasztor, Meester, Mosley, C.J. Wilson, Edwards and Rutland. Blackmon remained on Reserve/Suspended. The offensive priority was to win first and second down and stay out of long third downs. Boot Flood and 10 Empty came off the primary sheet, and Dagger was restricted to SOLID protection.

**Practice / teaching emphasis:** Monday's film covered Johnson's two Oakland sacks, Nwaneri's sack, both Boot Flood sacks, the final fumble and every Meester communication duty Brewster now inherited. Tuesday and Wednesday were center week for Brewster: front identification, the MIKE point, protection calls, exchanges, twist pickup, cadence and road communication, with Johnson's protection work built around tight-end and back help. Thursday was full speed on pressure, third down, backed-up and sudden-change situations, crowd-noise communication and the first 15. Friday removed anything still heavy on communication. Saturday was mental execution, including silent-count and emergency center communication.

## 3. Pregame media Q&A

**Reporter:** Who starts at center with Brad Meester out?

**Stone:** Mike Brewster. He's the center we have healthy, and we're not moving three guys to fill one spot.

**Reporter:** Lane Johnson has been charged with four sacks in two games. Is he still your right tackle?

**Stone:** Yes. We know why those happened, and we're giving him help that makes sense. We're not changing the player without a better answer.

**Reporter:** Seattle's defense has held opponents to 18 percent on third down. What's the answer?

**Stone:** Don't get to many of them, and don't let the ones we do get be long.

### Game narrative: selected highlights

Seattle scored on three of its first four possessions. Stephen Hauschka kicked a field goal on the opening drive, and Marshawn Lynch finished a short possession with a three-yard touchdown run. Jacksonville's second possession ended on a Dagger throw to Cecil Shorts that Richard Sherman intercepted. Hauschka's second field goal, early in the second quarter, made it 13-0.

Jacksonville was still scoreless at halftime. Mike Brown's 22-yard catch on 20 Doubles Drive opened a possession that ended in a punt, and the next one ended when Bobby Wagner forced and recovered a Maurice Jones-Drew fumble on Power R.

Seattle did not score again after Hauschka's second field goal. Late in the third quarter, Cousins hit Toney Clemons for a 19-yard touchdown on 13 Heavy Left Post-Cross, the MAX shot from the heavy picture. Scobee's first field goal made it 13-10 early in the fourth. Seattle answered with a 46-yard completion to Lynch, but Sen'Derrick Marks sacked Russell Wilson on the same drive, and it ended without points.

The tying drive was the plan as written. Jacksonville ran Split Zone for 12 and 16 yards and Outside Zone for Jonathan Grimes for 10, Shorts caught an 18-yarder on Drive with SOLID protection, and Scobee's second field goal tied it 13-13 with 5:41 left. Seattle's last possession included completions of 44 yards to Rice and 24 to Jermaine Kearse, then stalled, and Jon Ryan punted with 1:06 left.

Jacksonville took over with 58 seconds left. Cousins completed a 21-yarder to Shorts on Y-Cross, 12 yards to Adam Thielen on the same call and a short Spacing throw to Marcedes Lewis. Scobee's third field goal came on the final snap.

Seattle did not sack Cousins. Jacksonville faced 11 third downs and converted five, against a defense that had allowed 6 of 33, and ran 31 times for 148 yards. Cousins completed 23 of 30 for 184 yards with the touchdown and the interception. Both turnovers were Jacksonville's. Wilson completed 17 of 37 without a touchdown pass, and Lynch ran 20 times for 56.

## 6. Coaching takeaways and next-week carry-forward

**What held up:** Protection. Seattle had seven sacks in two games and none in this one, with Brewster starting at center and Johnson charged with none. The run game gained 148 yards and carried the tying drive. Jacksonville converted 5 of 11 third downs. The defense held Seattle scoreless after its first three scoring drives, and held Wilson to 17 of 37 without a touchdown pass.

**What must be corrected:** Ball security. Sherman's interception on Dagger and Wagner's forced fumble on Power R both came in the first half, and Jacksonville has had at least one giveaway in each game. Seattle's early drives reached points three times in four possessions, and its last possession produced two completions of more than 20 yards.

**Next-week carry-forward:** Posluszny is out for the Indianapolis game on the medical projection. Who replaces him in the linebacker operation is an open Stone and Crennel decision. Meester's projection clears on September 27. Whether he returns to center or Brewster stays there is an open Stone decision. As the depth chart stands, Brewster is the Week 3 center and Meester is on the Week 3 inactive list, and a list that is not reset carries forward. Blackmon remains on Reserve/Suspended through Week 5.
