# Uche Nwaneri, G: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; George Yarno (offensive line).
**Status going in:** Starting left guard all season; dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both postseason games. Active 53; available, no communicated restriction; exit physical with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract for 2014 (2010 five-year extension; the register marks the final year disputed, 2014 or 2015, and his 2014 status is unaffected); 4+ accrued seasons (entered 2007). Age 29 (born March 20, 1984).

## The season in the record

He entered 2013 wearing 77, with a 2012 record of 15 games and 15 starts. Stone's January-February 2013 roster evaluation placed him in the offensive core.

Spring and preseason. OTAs, June 4-5: "Rackley and Nwaneri were late once transferring the interior combination, but the line corrected without a coach supplying the second answer." Preseason Game 1 against Miami (August 9, won 33-17): he was one of four linemen who "identified their protection jobs but lost leverage physically; each continued the rep after the loss." No minicamp or camp record names him individually.

Season. Left guard in every game: beside Meester in Weeks 1 and 2 and beside Brewster from Week 3 (Brewster confirmed as the starter in Week 6), in a line "locked as Monroe, Nwaneri, Brewster, Rackley, Johnson" at the bye, recorded "unchanged" in Weeks 12 and 13, with "no reshuffle" for the Wild Card and "normal jobs" in the Divisional plan. At Oakland in Week 2 the week's correction line read "Right-side pass protection, for the second week: Johnson was charged with two sacks and Nwaneri with one," and the Monday film before Seattle "covered Johnson's two Oakland sacks, Nwaneri's sack." No classification from that review is written down; the bye review sorted every sack by cause, but its result for this one is not recorded. After Week 3 the week files name him only in line listings.

Unit context, credited to no lineman individually: 180 rushing yards in the opener, 148 at Seattle and at Denver, 179 in the Wild Card; Jones-Drew 282 carries, 1,334 yards, 8 touchdowns; 38 sacks allowed by the team in the regular season.

His individual receipt line: 4 sacks allowed (Week 2 at Oakland, L 13-17; Week 14 against Houston, W 21-20; Week 15 against Buffalo, L 16-45; Week 17 at Indianapolis, L 22-23), none in the postseason. The 2013 charge was drawn at random across dressed linemen (defect register) and is not used as evidence. No receipt injury, no weekly-award shortlist, no in-season honour.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Availability: he dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both postseason games and held the left-guard job in each.
- Assignment and communication. Miami has him identifying the protection job. The OTA interior transfer was late once and then corrected by the line without a coach. Stone said that exchange, a unit correction he was part of, shows one part of the Better description, communicating without prompting; the record credits the line, not him alone, and does not show the correction retained.

Development targets:

- Leverage after a correct identification. Miami records that he lost leverage physically after identifying the job, and continued the rep; there is no effort finding.
- The in-season record is thin. After Week 3 he appears only in line listings, and the one individual item, the Oakland sack, was reviewed before Seattle and sorted with every other sack at the bye, but no result for it is written down. Stone said that is a gap in the staff's work, not a finding about him.

Teaching state: Good on assignment and communication in spring and preseason; one part of Better (communication without prompting) shown by the line, not by him individually, on the OTA interior exchange. No in-season record supports a state either way.

## The player's view

Nwaneri was even and specific. The run game was what he would keep: the downhill calls fit the room, and Counter, which was slow in camp, was "ours by December." That matches the record: in camp Power and Inside Zone survived first contact better than Counter, which became usable from its simplest presentation, and by Weeks 14 and 16 Counter was on touchdown drives. He said the center change in Week 3 cost the line less than people expected.

His criticism was the Oakland week, and it was pointed. The correction was headed right-side pass protection and still had his name in it; he plays left guard. The sack was on the Monday film before Seattle, and nobody told him afterwards what the film decided. "If it's mine, tell me it's mine. If it isn't, don't put my name on it." Stone agreed with what the record shows: the charged sack was named in writing before any film review, the bye sort's result for it is not written down, and nothing records anyone going over it with him. He treated it as a staff failure first, not a complaint to manage.

Asked what the staff should know about coaching him, he said he wants a few lines of individual notes each week on the interior calls, not only the unit correction. Stone recorded it as veteran information the staff needs.

## Future and contract

He asked plainly whether he is in the plans for 2014. He said he turns 30 in March and asked whether he should expect the club to bring in guards. Stone said he is under contract for 2014; that contract, roster, draft and free-agent decisions are Caldwell's; and that he gives Caldwell football evaluations but would not say what his contains or what the personnel department is preparing. He gave him the standard as written: "A veteran does not lose his place because a coach wants to prove a cultural point," and a young player does not wait because someone older arrived first. Left guard in 2014 is decided by 2014 evidence. He made no role or roster commitment. Nwaneri said it was the answer he expected.

Stone reminded him that Player Development's resources stay available on the same confidential terms, without asking whether he uses them.
