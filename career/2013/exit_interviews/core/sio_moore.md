## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Corrections held. Every spring correction the record names for him (the rookie-minicamp landmark, the OTA coverage landmark, the changed-distribution handoff) held on the retest. Rookie minicamp recorded Good. By the framework's definitions, retaining corrections and executing against changing looks is Better, and that is what the OTA and minicamp record describes on those coverage jobs. The record does not use the word; Stone said so.
- Speed that shows up in the right places: "Pursuit movement and space change-of-direction" at rookie minicamp, "pressure and pursuit were useful" at the scrimmage.
- Available every week of his first season.

Development targets:

- Fit discipline at full speed. He overran a fit in camp, took the correction, then overran one again at the scrimmage, and Crennel narrowed his assignment. Bush's camp correction was a leverage correction, which points to technique at speed rather than assignment; the scrimmage overrun itself is not classified in the record, and Stone said so. Stone's reading on that job was Good, not yet Better: the correction held once and the overrun came back once.
- The season itself is unclassified. Stone would not put a teaching state on sixteen games of package work that no record breaks down.

## The player's view

Moore credited the spring directly. "One thing, then the tape, then the same look again. I always knew what I got wrong and what right looked like." He said the season was different: he played his packages, and the Monday corrections were the unit's. He never heard whether the assignment Crennel narrowed after the scrimmage was widened again, or what his package work was being judged against.

His sharpest point was the base reserve order. "When Paul went down, Julian was next in the base. I'm not saying he didn't earn it. Nobody told me what he did that I didn't." Stone did not dispute that the reason was never given to him. He told Moore the record states the order, not the reason, and he would not reconstruct one in the room; Crennel and Bush owe him the evidence with film.

He also said he was drafted to rush and cover, rushed in camp, and recorded no pressure in the season; he wants to know whether rushing is part of his job. And he wants a special-teams job: "Put me on every unit you'll let me. I'd rather run down on kicks than stand there."

## Future and contract

No contract question; he is under his rookie contract through 2016. He asked whether he will get base linebacker reps in the spring and a real chance at the job. Stone made no commitment either way: no job is promised to anyone in the room, roles are earned on 2014 evidence, and what he will be taught and evaluated at in the spring will be told to him before April 21. Moore: "Then tell me what the evidence is, and I'll bring it." On special teams, Stone said coverage jobs are the special teams coordinator's to set from camp evidence; with that job vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12, they are Stone's until it is filled. He promised nothing.

All voluntary. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and declining any voluntary work costs nothing.

- Film, his to keep: Bush's cutup of his camp fit reps (the overrun, the corrected rep, the scrimmage overrun) beside his in-season package reps, classified by the state/05 categories, correct reps marked correct. No quiz.
- Assignment clarity: Crennel and Bush tell him his 2013 package menu, whether it widened after August 3, and what he will be taught and evaluated at in the spring, including whether rush work is in it. A teaching answer, not a role.
- Special teams: the special teams coordinator (vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12; Stone until the job is filled) explains what camp's coverage jobs were built on ("Communication, lane leverage and pursuit") and how 2014 jobs are earned.
