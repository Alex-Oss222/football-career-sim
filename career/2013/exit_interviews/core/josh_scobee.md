## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- The kicks that tied games or won them went in: the Week 3 tying kick with 5:41 left and the winner on the final snap, the third-quarter 47 that tied Week 5, the Week 13 overtime winner, and the Week 14 extra point that put Jacksonville ahead with 3:34 left.
- The finish. He made every field goal and extra point he attempted from Week 14 through the Divisional, including five field goals in Week 17.
- The first half of the season: 14 of 15 at the bye, with the weekly record showing no operation issue in any of those games.

Development targets:

- Apart from the 51 at Denver, the misses clustered from Week 10 to Week 13: 22 and 27 at Tennessee, 30 against Arizona, 48 and an extra point at Cleveland. All five went on the weekly correction lists, the Tennessee pair as "Two missed field goals inside 30 yards." None is classified by cause. Stone told him he would not call any of them a kick problem, a snap, a hold or protection until the special-teams review says which, and told him the Week 10 to 13 tape had not been broken down that way.
- Teaching state: the record gives none for a kicker, and Stone did not build one from make totals.

## The player's view

Stone opened by telling him plainly that Lowry left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach and that the special teams coordinator job is open. Who replaces Lowry is not decided, and Stone made no promise about who, when or what would change. Scobee called his season "a good year with a bad month" and did not argue with the misses. "I know how each of them came off my foot. I want to see the snap and the hold on every one before anybody writes down what it was." He said the operation feedback through the bye was specific and that after the bye he heard results, not operation.

He raised the Week 10 postgame answer, "Six turnovers and two missed short field goals." "Both true. But one of those things lost that game, and it wasn't me." Stone said the answer was factual and named no one. Scobee said it didn't need to. Stone accepted that putting unclassified misses next to the turnovers made them read as a cause, and recorded it against himself.

His other point was the offense. "Five in Indianapolis and we still lost. I'd rather kick extra points." Stone agreed that is the offense's problem and his, as the play-caller, and said the stalled drives are going into his self-scout.

What he needs: the operation reviewed kick by kick with Cain and the holder, and the council asked again during the season, not once.

## Future and contract

Scobee asked whether the club will bring another kicker to camp. Stone said signings and camp roster spots are Caldwell's, that he did not know and would not promise either way, and that the kicking job, like every job, is earned on 2014 evidence. Scobee: "If somebody comes in, fine. I've kicked against guys before." No contract question; he is under contract through 2015.

## Development plan

All voluntary. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and declining any voluntary work costs nothing. His kicking routine before then is his own.

- Film, his to keep: a special-teams review (Stone until the coordinator job is filled) of every 2013 field-goal and extra-point attempt, regular season and postseason, each miss classified by phase (snap, hold, kick, protection, other), with a made kick from a comparable spot beside each miss where one exists.
- Operation: when the program permits football instruction (Phase Two onward), the first special-teams kicking periods are snap, hold and kick timing with the specialists together, built from what the review finds, not from the make totals.
- Physical: his own routine; performance-staff input on request within CBA limits. Medical: today's physical belongs to the medical staff.
- Support: Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support go on no report, without asking whether he uses them.
