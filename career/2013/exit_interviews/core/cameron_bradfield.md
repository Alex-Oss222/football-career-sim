# Cameron Bradfield, OT: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; George Yarno (offensive line).
**Status going in:** Swing tackle all season, and the sixth offensive lineman in the 6OL package from Week 5. Active 53; available, no communicated restriction; exit physical with the medical staff today. Contract: 2011 undrafted rookie contract, three years, final year 2013; restricted free agent when the 2014 league year opens on March 11 (2 accrued seasons before 2013, with 2013 accruing in the branch). Age 26 (born September 14, 1987).

## The season in the record

He entered 2013 on the 2012 final active roster, wearing 78, with a 2012 record of 14 games and 12 starts. In 2011 he was one of three undrafted rookies on Jacksonville's opening 53.

Spring and camp. At mandatory minicamp "Johnson leads the right-tackle work with Bradfield challenging." In the first padded camp work "Bradfield produced a sound response and remained the challenger," and he then took "the second sequence and swing evaluation." Preseason Game 1 against Miami (August 9, won 33-17): "Pasztor, Nwaneri, Rackley and Bradfield identified their protection jobs but lost leverage physically; each continued the rep after the loss." Camp close: "Johnson's communication and cumulative transfer led; Bradfield retained swing value." Johnson started; Bradfield became the swing tackle.

Season. He dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both postseason games. At Seattle in Week 3 he was "the in-game contingency with no pregame rotation" for the right side, and "The Bradfield contingency was not used." From Week 5 at St. Louis he was the sixth lineman in the new 6OL package (Heavy Power, Heavy Counter away, Wing TE Delay), and the bye plan kept "Bradfield swing and 6OL." The package stayed in the plans to the end: its first call gained 20 at St. Louis; in Week 12 it was one of the ways the plan helped the tackles against Watt; in Week 14 it produced runs of 11, 12 and 12 and one Grimes fumble; Jones-Drew scored from a yard on 6OL Counter in Week 16; it appeared on two Wild Card touchdown drives and in the Divisional plan. The week files name the package, not the eleven on the field, so none of those gains or losses is his.

The receipts give him one individual line: 3 sacks allowed (Week 6 at Denver, W 26-10; Week 12 at Houston, L 6-38; Week 17 at Indianapolis, L 22-23), none in the postseason. The 2013 charge was drawn at random across every dressed lineman, backups included (defect register), so it is not used as evidence here. No receipt injury, no weekly-award shortlist, no in-season honour.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Assignment. The first padded camp work records "a sound response," and Preseason Game 1 has him identifying his protection job. That meets the Good description at camp and preseason contact level, and Stone told him so.
- Two jobs held through the season: swing tackle, and sixth lineman from Week 5 through the Divisional plan. Under the program's rule a poor package can shrink or disappear, and the 6OL package kept its place. Stone said the record does not separate his share of that from the other ten players', and did not hand him the yards.

Development targets:

- Leverage after a correct identification. The one technique note (Miami) records that he lost leverage physically after identifying his protection job, and continued the rep. There is no effort finding.
- The record itself. Nothing written evaluates his 6OL snaps or any tackle work after camp. Stone said the evaluation would be no thicker than that until Yarno's film sort exists.

Teaching state: Good on assignment in camp and preseason. Nothing in the record shows Better either way.

## The player's view

Bradfield was direct. He came into camp as a tackle who had started 12 games and wanted to start again. He thought the July padded work was closer than the final decision read, but he accepted that it was made on the whole camp and that he was told what was being judged: "I knew what the test was. I just wanted it to be harder for them." He liked the 6OL job: "Every week I knew exactly what I had."

His criticism was the swing job. He said the in-season work he remembers was the 6OL periods, and he could not remember a period built for him at either tackle spot, including the Seattle week. "If Lane goes down in that game, I'm going in on reps from August." The week files record 6OL periods (the Week 5 install, the bye practice, the Week 10 personnel day) and no period for the swing tackle. Stone said that is either work the staff did not do or work nobody wrote down, and both are the staff's; he treated it as a planning failure first.

He also asked about the three sacks, saying he was not sure he was on the field for all of them. Stone said the charge sheet is not reliable about whose sack it was, it is not part of his evaluation, and Yarno's sort will say what the film shows.

## Future and contract

He raised it himself: would Jacksonville tender him, and, with Monroe a pending free agent, would left tackle be open? Stone said the tender decision is Caldwell's, due before 4 p.m. ET on March 11; that he will give Caldwell his football evaluation and would not tell Bradfield what it is; and that he would not discuss another player's contract. Every 2014 job, at either tackle spot, is decided by 2014 evidence. He made no starting, swing or roster promise.

Bradfield said he wants to start again, here or elsewhere, and if another club offered that chance he would listen. Stone said that is his right under the rules and did not advise him on it. He reminded him that Player Development's resources, including financial education, remain available on the same confidential terms, without asking whether he uses them.

## Development plan

Voluntary throughout; nothing is required before April 21 or in any voluntary phase, and declining any suggestion costs him nothing.

- Film: Yarno's sort of his 2013 snaps (6OL, any tackle snaps, the three charged sacks in with everything else, nothing in his column by default), tagged by the bye-review causes and paired with a correct rep where one exists. Delivered and discussed when and as the CBA permits.
- Technique, his choice of method: leverage and finish through the rep after a correct identification (Miami).
- If he is under Jacksonville control when the program opens: individual work on what the sort shows. His request for work at both tackle spots goes to the program planning; no promise was made.
- Physical: the strength and performance staff's input on request.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding belongs to the medical staff.
