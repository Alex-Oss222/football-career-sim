## Stone's evaluation

Stone said at the start that the record on Owens is thin, and that the evaluation would be no thicker than the record.

Strengths:

- Three of his catches after October came on touchdown drives: 34 on 22 Heavy Snag against Houston, 19 on the same call at Indianapolis, 29 at Kansas City.
- No fumble in 18 games.
- He dressed for every game. The Wild Card injury was cleared by the medical staff at its projection; Stone treated it as a medical fact, not a toughness finding.

Development targets and gaps:

- The fullback's main jobs are not in the record. No phase record evaluates his lead blocks, his protection or his coverage work. Stone did not guess at any of it, favorably or otherwise, and said the staff owes him that evaluation from tape.
- Carries: 15 for 63. None is classified by call or by cause, and Stone drew nothing from the minus-2 carry against Buffalo.
- The Week 1 interception is not charged to him. The self-scout did not write down whose it was.

Teaching state: no phase record applies Good, Better or Best to him, and Stone assigned none.

## The player's view

Owens's main point was the gap. "Nobody writes down what a fullback does. You've got fifteen carries and eight catches on me. That's not my job." He said he believed he blocked well this year and wanted the staff to put it on tape and tell him if he was wrong.

He said the 22 Heavy Snag calls late in the year were the best part of his season: "Put me in 22 and throw me the Snag. It worked." On the coaching, he said he always knew his job in the group, but never once sat down with a coach about his own blocks during the season, and would have wanted that at the bye.

He asked why he was not among the coverage-unit players Lowry listed, and said he wanted to be evaluated for them. On his health he said he felt fine and had played at Tennessee. Stone said his exit physical is today and the medical staff decides.

## Future and contract

Owens asked whether Jacksonville wants him back. Stone said contract decisions are Caldwell's, that he will give Caldwell the football evaluation recorded here, and that he would not say what he will recommend or hint at it. Stone did not discuss the contract's terms; the register has not established them, and nothing in this interview assumes them.

Owens asked whether there is a fullback in next year's offense. Stone said no personnel group is owed a share. The 2013 plans used 21 personnel as the physical counter from Week 1, ran the Week 16 offense downhill through 12, 21, 22 and 6OL, and carried the Divisional run-pass conflict through heavy personnel. What 2014 uses will come from the self-scout and the 2014 work, and he made no commitment either way. Owens said his agent would handle the rest.

## Development plan

Voluntary. The program may begin no earlier than April 21; until then nothing is required, and declining costs nothing. His contract status is unresolved, so nothing here assumes where he works in the spring.

- Blocking and protection: Spencer's written tape evaluation comes first. Any individual work follows from what it finds, not from a guess now.
- Keep: his receiving job on 22 Heavy Snag.
- Special teams: the special teams coordinator's evaluation of his 2013 coverage work, if any exists on tape. The job has been vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12, and Stone holds it until it is filled.
- Physical: his own program; the performance staff will build an individualized recommendation on request.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding belongs to the medical staff. Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support exist and go on no report, without asking whether he uses them.
