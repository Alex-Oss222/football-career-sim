## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- London. Ahead 20-13, right after the interception at 3:27, his sack of Kaepernick came on the possession that ended in a San Francisco punt. Stone said that is the first rep he would put in any cutup of the season.
- Houston away. The 10-yard loss on Foster on Houston's first series was part of the stop that produced a punt from the Houston 16, in a game Jacksonville lost 38-6.
- Availability. Dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games, second in every recorded edge order, no receipt injury.

Development targets:

- The record. A 16-game starter in 2012, he is not named in any 2013 spring, camp or preseason record, and the week files describe two of his plays. Stone told him plainly that he could not give him a teaching state from that, and would not invent a flaw to fill the gap.
- The room's open questions apply to him as much as anyone: finish (the bye correction) and containment on the directive weeks (Kaepernick's 30-yard run in Week 8, Schaub's scrambles of 9 and 10 in Week 14, Locker's 13-yard scramble in the Divisional game). None of those records names an edge; all of them wait for the film.

Teaching state: none recorded. Stone did not assign one.

## The player's view

Mincey was measured and direct. He came to camp as the returning starter expecting to compete for the first spot, came out second, and was never told what decided it. He did not dispute the camp result: "Jason earned it in camp. I'm not arguing that." His complaint was what followed. The recorded order did not change, nobody told him what would move him up or down, and after a while he "stopped asking." Stone checked it in the room: the week files record the edge order through Week 8 and, beyond C.J. Wilson's injury, never record the reason for it. He said the order was the staff's decision, the reasons should have been told to him, and that is a staff communication failure before it is anything about him. Pleasant said he coached the room on the week's plan and did not sit down with Mincey about where he stood.

He also said the rotation made it hard to settle into a game because he did not know his series before kickoff. No defensive snap or series counts are recorded, so Stone said he could neither confirm nor dismiss it from paper, and asked Pleasant to include the series pattern in the film sort if the tape shows one.

What worked: the London week, when "everybody knew what we were taking away," and Pleasant's individual drill work. What the staff misunderstands about him: that a veteran does not need to be told where he stands. What he needs: the film and an honest read of his standing, in season and not only in January.

## Future and contract

He asked it straight: at 30, under contract through 2015, is he going to be on this roster in September, or is he going to be released or traded? Stone said release, trade and contract decisions are Caldwell's; that he will give Caldwell his football evaluation of Mincey and would not tell him what it is or hint at it. He made no promise in either direction. Mincey said that if he is not in the plans he would rather know early, so he can find a place to play. Stone said he understood, that the timing of any decision is Caldwell's, and that he would not get ahead of it. Asked about his role if he stays, Stone said the 2013 order is a fact about 2013 and not a ranking for September; every 2014 job is decided on 2014 evidence. Mincey said he would get ready either way.
