# Jordan Poyer, CB: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Tony Oden (defensive backs).
**Status going in:** Rookie nickel corner and coverage-unit player (roster role); the weekly records place him only at nickel, and he stayed there after Alan Ball's injury. Dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: branch #64 rookie contract, four years, final year 2016; under contract; 0 accrued seasons before 2013, with 2013 accruing in the branch (contract status register). Age 22 (born April 25, 1991).

## The season in the record

Drafted #64 on April 26. The board's case was "recognition and technique that might reduce coverage mistakes"; its written "best objection" was "A player can understand a route and still lack the recovery margin to defend it." The draft record: "Tony Oden begins him at corner; any safety cross-training must be earned rather than presumed."

His teaching record:

- Rookie minicamp (May 3-5): "Communicated the basic corner leverage and coverage call reliably"; "one late finish came from footwork rather than confusion"; "Self-identified the footwork issue and applied the correction." One of the staff's three strongest teaching tapes; next step, "Add veteran receiver pictures."
- OTAs: "communicated cleanly through a changed receiver distribution" (May 13-15); "retained leverage and communicated the base exchange cleanly" (May 20-21); at the June close he "earned broader outside/inside evaluation."
- Mandatory minicamp (June 11-13), with Moore and Rambo: "Changed-distribution and exchange communication survived retest."
- Camp: "communicated well from outside and inside positions" (July 26-29); "earned nickel/outside competition" (July 31-August 2); "handled nickel/outside movement cleanly" at the August 3 scrimmage, after which "Poyer would continue nickel/outside evaluation." Camp closed with "Poyer at nickel" and defined coverage-unit jobs. Preseason Game 3 vs Philadelphia (L 20-31): "executed communicated coverage-unit responsibilities."

Regular season, 16 games: 35 tackles (19 solo, 16 assisted), 7 passes defended, 1 interception for 2 yards, 1 forced fumble, 1 fumble recovery; 2 punt returns for 24 yards and 1 kickoff return for 12 (36 return yards). Postseason, 2 games: 7 tackles (6 solo, 1 assisted), 1 sack, 1 tackle for loss, 1 pressure, 3 passes defended.

- Week 1 vs Kansas City (W 31-13): on the first snap of Kansas City's possession after Jacksonville's touchdown, forced a Jamaal Charles fumble and recovered it; standout list, "from the nickel spot." Held up: "Ball and Poyer produced both takeaways."
- Weeks 2 to 8, nickel row: "Steady" in every week but Week 5 ("Two passes defended"). Week 8: 5 tackles.
- Week 11 vs Arizona (W 29-7): intercepted Palmer in the second half; Scobee's field goal followed, 26-0. Stone that week: "Mike Harris outside. Jordan stays at nickel. One new job, not two."
- Week 17 at Indianapolis (L 22-23): 5 tackles; unit correction "Late pass defense."
- Wild Card at Kansas City (W 38-14): 4 tackles, 2 passes defended; unit correction "Underneath pass defense: Avery caught 12 of 13 targets."
- Divisional at Tennessee (L 13-20): sacked Locker, 1 pass defended; standout list.

No weekly-award shortlist, in-season honour or receipt injury. Evidence caveat: every 2013 fumble credited one defender with both the force and the recovery (defect register item 12); interceptions, passes defended and sacks were credited by role share (`runtime/play_detail.py`; item 19); 2013 returners were drawn afresh on every kick, and no coverage tackles were credited, so his returns and teams work are not read from the statbook.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Coverage communication and leverage, retained across every phase from rookie minicamp to camp. On those items the spring and camp record meets the framework's Better description: corrections retained and the job executed through changing looks (a changed receiver distribution in OTAs, a retest at minicamp, outside and inside positions in camp). That is a teaching state for those phases, not a label.
- Self-correction: he found his own footwork fault in May.
- He held the nickel job as a rookie, with no recorded change through the Divisional game, and dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games, with takeaways in Weeks 1 and 11 and the Divisional sack.

Development targets:

- The draft board's objection is still open: recovery when a route beats his leverage. The season record neither answers it nor shows a failure; the tape has to.
- The outside evaluation. Camp said it would continue; the weekly records carry him only at nickel, and no record closes it.
- His reps inside the Week 17 and Wild Card corrections are classified before anything is assigned to him. No in-season teaching state is recorded.

## The player's view

Poyer was direct and a little impatient. He liked the spring: "You showed me what I did right, not only what I did wrong," and finding the May footwork fault himself, then fixing it, was "how I learn." He argued Week 11. He had spent camp in the nickel and outside evaluation, and when an outside job opened "it went to Mike, and the reason was one new job, not two. I get it. It still means I'm the nickel because I'm good at nickel, not because anybody decided I can't play outside." He wanted to know which it is.

Stone said the Week 11 decision was about how many jobs changed in one week, not a finding that he could not play outside, and that no record compares him outside with Harris during the season. Oden said nobody told him afterwards whether the outside evaluation was closed or parked, and that was his. Poyer's second point: the weekly corrections, like "underneath pass defense," were the unit's, "and underneath is where the nickel lives. I want to know if it's me." Stone treated both as possible teaching failures first.

## Future and contract

He asked whether he will compete outside in the spring, and whether the club sees him at safety. Stone said no 2014 role is promised to anyone; where he is taught and evaluated in the spring is a teaching answer Crennel and Oden will give him before April 21; and the draft record's rule stands: safety cross-training is earned, not presumed, and the 2013 record shows none. He did not raise his contract.
