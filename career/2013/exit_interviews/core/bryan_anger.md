## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Operation. Every recorded read of the specialist operation, from the spring through Week 8, is clean. From Week 10 no operation read was kept, so Stone called the rest of the season unread, not clean.
- Available for every game.
- The Week 2 held-up line, the Wild Card fair catch at the 13, the Divisional punt downed at the 9 and the late Divisional punt after which Tennessee started at its own 10 are noted, not claimed: without the special-teams film review Stone could not separate them from the spot and the coverage.

Development targets:

- Plus-territory punting. Lowry's own rematch note named "plus-territory punt execution" after the punt downed at the line from the Houston 49. That punt is the one the record names; its cause (snap, protection, contact or kick) is not recorded, and Stone did not name one.
- Punt and coverage on the three returns that went on correction lists: Martin 26 (Week 12), Martin 33 (Week 14, after "better punt lanes" was the plan) and Hilton 26 on the last snap at Indianapolis. Each is classified as punt (hang and placement against the intended punt), coverage (lane and leverage) or call.
- Teaching state: none is recorded for him; the records speak to the unit, and Stone did not build one from the averages.

## The player's view

Stone opened by telling him plainly that Lowry left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach and that the special teams coordinator job is open. Who replaces Lowry is not decided, and Stone made no promise about who, when or what would change.

Anger was even and specific. The numbers, he said, "look fine and tell me almost nothing. I want to know if I did what the call asked, and nobody wrote down what the call was." Stone agreed the record keeps punts as counts and distances with no intended punt attached.

On Houston: "Everybody in the building knew 'plus-territory punt execution' meant me. My name wasn't on it, and I never saw it broken down." He wants the play reviewed with Cain and the protection before anyone decides what it was. Stone accepted that a note naming a problem without a cause reads as naming the player, and recorded it as a staff item.

On the returns, he said punt and coverage are one play: "If I outkick the lanes, that's mine. If I hang it and they miss the lane, that's theirs. Right now it's all one line on a list." On Indianapolis, three seconds left: "That ball can't be returnable." He asked whether there is a rule for that snap. Stone said the record holds no end-of-half or end-of-game punt rule, and the last possession at Indianapolis is his to own, like every end-of-game call.

What worked, he said, was the spring and camp operation work with Cain and Lowry. What did not was the second half of the season: after the bye he heard counts, not operation, which Scobee also raised.

## Future and contract

He did not raise his contract; he is under contract through 2015. He asked whether the club judges its punter on gross average, and whether another punter will be in camp. Stone said camp roster spots and signings are Caldwell's, that he did not know and would not promise either way, and that the job is earned on 2014 evidence; the evidence he will use is the classified film, punt by punt, not the average. Anger asked that the intended punt be recorded with every punt in 2014 so that is possible.

## Development plan

All voluntary. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and declining any voluntary work costs nothing. His punting routine before then is his own.

- Film, his to keep: a special-teams review (Stone until the coordinator job is filled) of every 2013 punt, regular season and postseason, each tagged with the situation, the snap and operation, placement and any return, with the intended punt stated where the staff can establish it. Order: the Week 12 punt from the Houston 49, then the three returns on correction lists, then the end-game punts at Indianapolis and in the Divisional.
- Operation: when the program permits football instruction (Phase Two onward), the first special-teams punt periods are snap, operation and plus-territory punts with Cain, built from what the review finds, not from the averages.
- Physical: his own routine; performance-staff input on request within CBA limits. Medical: today's physical belongs to the medical staff.
- Support: Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support go on no report, without asking whether he uses them.
