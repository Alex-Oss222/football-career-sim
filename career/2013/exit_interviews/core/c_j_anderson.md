## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- The back's job. Anderson reached Good in rookie minicamp, then kept the track and check responsibility in every OTA session the record covers (May 13-15, May 20-21, May 23, June 4-7) and through the June 12 changed pictures at mandatory minicamp. Stone told him that is Better in that job: retained, and correct against a changed look. In camp he found the correct scan, lost one pickup physically, corrected his base, and the correction held.
- Special teams. He held a defined coverage job from camp through the season, and the two longest returns of his year, 68 at Houston and 27 at Nashville, both put the offense in the other team's half.
- When his runs were called late in the year, the records show gains on the core calls: 10 on 6OL Power at Houston, 12 on Counter at Nashville.

Development targets:

- Ball security. Rookie minicamp recorded Good on ball security; the Buffalo fumble reopened it. Nothing written classifies the cause, so Stone did not assign one.
- Protection in games. No in-season record evaluates his protection in writing. The camp record shows one physical loss, then a correction that held; that is the last written evidence.
- Contact balance. Rookie minicamp could not test it, and no later record evaluates it.
- Screen and checkdown landmarks, the next step set in rookie minicamp: two targets all season. There is not enough game evidence to evaluate.
- Running efficiency: 17 carries is too small a sample to say anything, and Stone said so rather than drawing a conclusion from 3.9 a carry.

## The player's view

Anderson was direct about the fumble. "That one's mine. We're down 14-3 and I give them the ball at the 12." Stone said he would not accept that as a finding until the tape was classified, and would not reject it either. Anderson said the week after mattered to him: technique work, no benching, and carries again against Tennessee.

His frustration was the offensive workload. "Three carries at most when I got any. Some weeks none. You can't find the speed of it like that." He said he understood RB3 was where he started and that no workload was promised when he signed, but he wanted Stone to know he does not think of himself only as a special-teams player.

On the teaching, he said the protection work from rookie minicamp was the most useful thing he got: "identify it, get corrected, do it again." What he missed was contact. He asked for more live work in camp to show his balance. Stone said contact in camp follows the CBA and the camp plan, and he would not promise extra live work.

He asked to be considered for the return job, pointing to Houston and Nashville.

## Future and contract

Anderson did not ask about his contract; he is under contract through 2015. He asked what it would take to move up the depth chart, and whether Jones-Drew's situation changed that. Stone would not discuss another player's contract. He said 2014 roles are earned in 2014 work, that a young player does not wait simply because somebody older arrived first, and that a veteran does not lose his place to prove a point. He promised no role, touches or depth position. On returns, Stone said returner evaluation belongs to the special teams coordinator. Lowry left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach and the job is open, so until it is filled that evaluation comes to Stone. He said the 2013 weekly records assign the return jobs "by situation," and that Anderson would get the evaluation directly. No return job was promised.

## Development plan

Voluntary. The program may begin no earlier than April 21; until then nothing is required, and declining any suggestion costs him nothing.

- Ball security: review the Week 15 fumble with Spencer and classify it first. A carriage cause is drill work he can do on his own.
- Protection: Spencer's written evaluation of his 2013 protection from tape, then an optional cutup and teaching sheet on scan identification and the physical pickup from camp, for home study.
- Receiving: study the screen and checkdown landmarks in the 2013 book; practiced work waits for the program.
- Special teams: the special teams coordinator's evaluation of his coverage and return work, with whatever individual study that evaluation suggests. The job has been vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12, and Stone holds it until it is filled.
- Physical: his own program; the performance staff will build an individualized recommendation on request. Contact balance is tested in practice, not assigned as offseason homework.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding belongs to the medical staff. Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support exist and go on no report, without asking whether he uses them.
