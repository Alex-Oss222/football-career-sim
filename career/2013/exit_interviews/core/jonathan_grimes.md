## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- He produced when his calls came: Wham touchdowns against San Diego, Arizona and Tennessee, the Counter touchdown against Indianapolis, Draw at Kansas City, and the 28- and 39-yard catches. Four rushing touchdowns and a receiving touchdown came out of an RB2 workload.
- One lost fumble all season. His assigned route work held from camp into the preseason games.

Development targets:

- Ball security. The Week 14 fumble came at the Houston 18 in a game Jacksonville won by one point. The correction list names it; nothing written classifies the cause (carriage, contact, a second defender, the call). Stone would not guess.
- Low-yield games. Denver, 10 carries for 12; Houston in Week 12, 2 for 0. The records do not say whether those runs were the blocking, the track, the read or the call, so Stone did not put them on Grimes.
- Protection. No camp, preseason or in-season record contains a written evaluation of his pass protection. Stone told him he could not evaluate what nobody wrote down, and that protection and ball security are inputs the framework uses to decide how a back is used.
- Receiving. Eight catches on 15 targets; the targets that were not caught are not classified, and none is charged to him.

Teaching state: the preseason record supports Good on his assigned route jobs. Nothing recorded establishes Better (a correction retained against changing looks, unprompted communication), and no state was assigned for run track or protection because none was written.

## The player's view

Grimes said the year showed he can carry more than he was given. "I did what was asked every time they called my number. I'd like it called more." He accepted the no-quota principle but said his work came in uneven amounts, one carry at St. Louis and two at Houston, and that a back is hard to keep sharp on one or two carries. He said he usually knew which runs were his by the Thursday mock game, not always by Wednesday.

On the fumble he did not argue that it was harmless. "Joseph got a hand on it. I want to see whether I gave it to him or he took it." He asked to see it with Spencer rather than be told.

He said the route teaching in camp was the clearest part of his year. Asked what the staff gets wrong about him, he said: "You see a change-of-pace back. Nobody's asked me to prove I can pick up a blitz." He asked for protection work and a written evaluation of it.

## Future and contract

Grimes asked whether he would be back and whether Stone had talked to Caldwell. Stone said contract decisions belong to Caldwell, that he will give Caldwell the football evaluation recorded here, and that he would not say what he will recommend or hint at it either way. The register has not established the contract's terms, and Stone did not discuss them.

Grimes asked whether the lead job would be his if Jones-Drew did not return. Stone would not discuss another player's contract. He said the lead job is not vacated or awarded in January: "roles move when the football evidence says they should move," and a veteran does not lose his place to prove a point either. That announced no competition and denied none. Grimes said that did not give him much to go on; Stone said it was the honest answer. Grimes asked that his agent hear from Caldwell early. Stone said he would pass that on and that the timing is Caldwell's.

## Development plan

All of it is voluntary. Jacksonville's program may begin no earlier than April 21; until then, and in any voluntary phase, nothing is required and declining costs him nothing. His contract status is unresolved, so nothing here assumes where he works in the spring.

- Ball security: review the Week 14 fumble with Spencer and classify it before choosing any work. A carriage cause is self-directed drill work he can do anywhere.
- Protection: Spencer's written evaluation from 2013 tape comes first. If Grimes wants it, Spencer will add a short cutup and teaching sheet on the back's scan and pickup jobs in the 2013 book for home study. Practiced installation waits for the program.
- Keep: the Wham and Counter tracks and his receiving jobs on Post-Cross, Snag and Spacing.
- Physical: his own program; the performance staff will build an individualized recommendation on request.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding, restriction or clearance belongs to the medical staff. Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support exist and go on no report, without asking whether he uses them.
