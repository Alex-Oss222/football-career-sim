# Austin Pasztor, G: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; George Yarno (offensive line).
**Status going in:** Game-day interior offensive-line reserve (from Week 10), eighth on the season-end line order. Active 53; available, no communicated restriction; exit physical with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: signed to Jacksonville's practice squad September 17, 2012, promoted to the 53 on December 14, 2012; pending exclusive-rights free agent (register; its 2013 final year rests on one source and is marked Unverified). The register records him on the 53 all season, with 2013 accruing in the branch. Age 23 (born November 26, 1990).

## The season in the record

2012: 3 games, 3 starts. Mandatory minicamp left him and Rackley "a genuine right-guard competition." Camp: "Rackley communicated the combinations more consistently while Pasztor had the better isolated recovery after losing initial leverage; neither owned the job"; Rackley took the first work "because his communication held late in practice, but Pasztor remained a live challenger"; "Pasztor's best rush pickup kept the contest live." Preseason Game 1 (August 9, won 33-17): he identified his protection job, lost leverage physically and continued the rep.

At Preseason Game 2 at the Jets (August 17) he entered an independent medical hold (head/neck) and was removed from football work. The right-guard battle closed as "Rackley held the first-unit operation; Pasztor was medically interrupted." He was kept on the 53 while held; "availability was not treated as effort."

On October 27 the club's record was corrected (ledger Entry 46). Only the injury's class and restriction had been kept, not its projected return, so the hold "could never clear on a date." The recovered projection was minor, two days: August 19, 2013. He was "held out of Weeks 1-8 by the recording defect."

He practised at the bye ("interior technique, communication and conditioning work") and was not moved on the depth chart; Stone told Caldwell there would be "no move before the staff had evaluated Pasztor and Mosley." The second-half plan locked the starting five, together since Week 3. From Week 10 he dressed as the primary interior reserve in place of Asper, through both playoff games; Rackley stayed the starting right guard.

Statistics: 8 regular-season games dressed (Weeks 10 to 17) and 2 postseason. Sacks allowed charged: 4 (two in Week 10, one each in Weeks 14 and 17), none postseason. The 2013 engine charged each sack allowed to a randomly drawn dressed lineman (`runtime/defect_register.md`), so these are not evidence; the record does not show whether or when he played a snap. No weekly-award shortlist, no in-season honour, no receipt injury.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Recovery and rush pickup, the two individual positives camp recorded, and what kept the competition live.
- Assignment identification: Preseason Game 1. Preseason protection notes came in part from automated sack attribution (Preseason Game 3 record), so this one is read alongside the camp record, not alone.

Development targets:

- Combination communication. Camp separated the two guards on Rackley's communication holding late in practice. That is communication, not a physical finding.
- Initial leverage: lost before recovering in camp and in Preseason Game 1. Technique, not effort.
- Stone was plain that the in-season record is thin: one bye practice, then eight regular-season and two playoff games dressed as reserve with no individual evaluation recorded.

Teaching state: Good (assignment identified, a live competitor through camp). Better, which asks for unprompted communication and technique reproduced through changing looks, is not established: combination communication was the part camp called less consistent, and the season added no individual evidence.

## The player's view

Pasztor was frustrated and said so. The competition ended at the Jets, not in practice, and then he sat eight games on a hold whose projected return, in the club's corrected record, was two days after it began. "Every week the answer was I was with the medical staff. You said the same thing to the press." He asked whether the eight weeks count against him anywhere.

Stone did not defend it. The failure was in the club's record, not a medical finding about him and not anything he did: "The standard goes upward. That one's ours." He pointed to the camp standouts and cutdown records: the hold is recorded as a medical limitation, "not effort or character evidence."

Pasztor's second point: once available he had one practice before the line was locked, and right guard was never reopened. "I'm not asking you to bench Will. I'm asking whether it was ever open again." Stone answered that it was not; he kept the five who had started since Week 3, and that was his decision, not a finding against Pasztor. When Pasztor noted that Rackley had been listed limited since early in the season, Stone declined to discuss another player's medical status, as he would decline to discuss Pasztor's.

On coaching, he said the camp competition was fair and he knew what separated them, but as the dressed reserve he did not know week to week which spots and calls he was being prepared for. Stone treated that as a teaching problem first.

## Future and contract

He asked whether he will be tendered and whether right guard is open in 2014. Stone said tenders are Caldwell's (exclusive-rights tenders are due before 4 p.m. on March 11); he will give Caldwell his football evaluation and did not say what it is. On the job, no promise: roles move when the 2014 football evidence says they should, for him and for the starter.
