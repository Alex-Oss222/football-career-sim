# Allen Reisner, TE: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; John Zernhelt (tight ends).
**Status going in:** TE3, the third tight end in 13 personnel; third on the season-end tight-end depth chart (Lewis, Kelce, Reisner). Active 53; available, no communicated restriction; exit physical with the medical staff today. Contract: 2011 Minnesota undrafted contract, carried by waiver claim (December 24, 2012); final year 2013; restricted free agent when the 2014 league year opens on March 11 (2 accrued seasons before 2013, with 2013 accruing in the branch). Age 25 (born September 29, 1988).

## The season in the record

He entered 2013 on the 2012 final active roster, wearing 87, with a 2012 record of 2 games and 2 starts. In camp, "Reisner held assignments" and "repeatedly held assigned routes"; he was retained as TE3. Preseason: against Miami, "assignment-correct route work from Reisner, Lewis, Grimes and Murphy sustained scoring drives"; at Atlanta he was among the players who "sustained assigned route responsibilities."

He dressed for all 16 regular-season games: 2 targets, 2 receptions, 14 yards, no touchdown, no fumble; longest 9. Postseason, both games: 1 target, 1 reception, 3 yards.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Assignment reliability on routes. Camp and two preseason games record the same thing: he ran the route he was given. That meets the assignment part of the Good description (knows where to line up, knows his assignment), and Stone told him so; the record holds nothing on his communication or technique, so Stone took the reading no further.
- Availability. He dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games, and held the 13-personnel job the weekly records assign him.

What the record does not hold:

- Stone was direct that the written record on him is thin. There is no recorded evaluation of his blocking, his protection or any teams work, and 2 regular-season targets plus 1 in the playoffs cannot evaluate his routes. Stone did not manufacture a flaw to fill the gap, and he did not hand out a grade the tape has not supplied.
- The Better items (changing looks, adjustments, reproducing technique through repeated reps) are untested in the record either way.
- Development target: first, an honest evaluation from film. Zernhelt will write one.

## The player's view

Reisner was even about the season. He said he knew the job when he walked in, third tight end and be ready in 13, and he believed he did it: "I was where I was supposed to be. I dressed every week." He said Zernhelt's room was clear about what each week's job was.

His criticism was about feedback. Most of the individual time in the tight-end room in season went to Kelce's development, and he said he understood why: the rookie had the new calls. But he said almost everything he heard about his own snaps came in the group meeting, and he asked for an individual evaluation of his blocking. "If there's something wrong with it, I'd rather hear it in January than find out in March." Stone treated it first as a possible staff failure to schedule depth-player evaluation, not as a complaint to manage.

He asked what separated him from Kelce in the staff's eyes. Stone answered from the record: in camp, Kelce's route and teams work transferred and earned TE2; Reisner's record shows held assignments. There is no comparison grade beyond that, and Stone did not invent one.

## Future and contract

Reisner raised it directly: he is a restricted free agent, and he asked whether Jacksonville would tender him and whether Stone wanted him back. Stone said the tender decision is Caldwell's, due before 4 p.m. on March 11; that he will give Caldwell his football evaluation; and that he would not tell Reisner what it is, in either direction. He made no roster or role commitment.

Reisner asked whether being around the building in the spring would help. Stone said no: the offseason program cannot begin before April 21, after the March 11 deadline, and declining voluntary work never counts against anyone in this building. Reisner said that was at least a straight answer. Stone reminded him that Player Development and the confidential support resources stay available to him on the same terms, without asking whether he uses them.

## Development plan

Voluntary throughout; nothing is required before April 21 or during any voluntary phase, and declining any suggestion costs him nothing.

- Evaluation first: Zernhelt's written evaluation of his 2013 snaps from tape (13-personnel blocking, protection, routes), given to Stone, and to Reisner when and as the CBA permits.
- If he is under Jacksonville control when the program opens: individual work in Phase Two on whatever that evaluation shows, taught through the same cycle as everyone else.
- The complete active playbook remains his to study.
- Physical: any program is the strength and performance staff's, on request.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding belongs to the medical staff.

## Follow-ups

- Zernhelt: written evaluation of Reisner's 2013 snaps from tape, to Stone before Stone gives Caldwell his evaluation, and to Reisner when and as the CBA permits.
- Stone: football evaluation of Reisner to Caldwell before the RFA qualifying-offer deadline (4 p.m. ET, March 11).
- Medical staff: exit physical, January 14. No finding is recorded in this interview.
- Teaching note: the third tight end has no individually recorded development work or evaluation in season; the in-season individual time in the room went to the rookie's development calls. Depth players need a scheduled evaluation, not only group correction.
- User decision: what football evaluation, and any recommendation, Stone gives Caldwell on Reisner before the March 11 RFA qualifying-offer deadline. Nothing was said to Reisner about it.
