# Travis Kelce, TE: exit interview

**Date:** Tuesday January 14, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; John Zernhelt (tight ends).
**Status going in:** TE2, second on the season-end tight-end depth chart (Lewis, Kelce, Reisner). Active 53; available, no communicated restriction (his Week 13 minor injury cleared when its projected return of December 3 was reached); exit physical with the medical staff today. Contract: branch #33 rookie contract, four years, final year 2016; under contract (register label: Confirmed); no accrued season before 2013, with 2013 accruing in the branch. Age 24 (born October 5, 1989).

## The season in the record

Jacksonville drafted him #33 on April 26, Caldwell accepting Stone's "higher-than-public valuation." He began with "a bounded TE2 job alongside Marcedes Lewis," with Zernhelt owning his blocks, releases and route menu, and camp confirmed him as TE2 on August 24.

He dressed for all 16 regular-season games: 17 targets, 8 receptions, 105 yards, no touchdown, no fumble; 13.1 yards a catch, longest 36 (team statistics view). Postseason, both games: 3 targets, 2 receptions, 60 yards. No weekly-award shortlist.

## Stone's evaluation

Strengths:

- Alignment and assignment. Rookie minicamp recorded Good on the attached/wing alignment menu. In OTAs he "handled attached-to-flex alignment changes correctly," and the standouts record reads "Alignment knowledge traveled." No phase record shows an assignment error; every recorded correction was technique.
- Route and movement work that transferred. Camp: "Kelce's best movement/route work earned a defined second-group role." Preseason: held his route assignment on a touchdown drive against the Jets; transferred his assigned route job against Philadelphia; sustained it at Atlanta. He carried three weeks of in-season additions and was in the regular call structure from Week 8.
- His four catches from Week 10 through the Wild Card went for 36 at Tennessee, 14 against Buffalo, 28 against Tennessee and 53 at Kansas City.

Development target:

- One correction, the same one since May: base, hand placement and pad level on the attached run-block fit, especially after an alignment change. Rookie minicamp: in-line leverage "less consistent when his first strike landed high." OTAs: improved in individual work, lost again when the alignment changed. Mandatory minicamp: base "narrowed on a block-fit rep before the corrected return." Camp: "pad level/base varied in attached run work." Stone classified it as technique, not assignment and not effort.
- Stone was plain that the in-season record is thin here. After Denver he said publicly he would grade the blocks and releases on tape; no such grade is in the game records, and he did not substitute one from memory.
- Seventeen targets are not an evaluation of his routes. Lewis was the first tight end all year, and, as Stone said before San Diego, the ball goes where the coverage says.

Teaching state: Good on alignment and assignment, recorded. The block fit has not reached Better, which requires reproducing the technique through repeated reps and changing looks: the OTA close called it "improved but remains open," mandatory minicamp left it "an open correction," and camp recorded "continued inline inconsistency."

## The player's view

Kelce said the year was not what he pictured at #33, but he did not blame the depth chart: "Marcedes had the year he had. I'd have thrown it to him too." He said Weeks 6 and 7 were the hardest stretch: two weeks of new work, no ball, and a reporter asking about it. He had heard Stone's answer that his job that week was not catches and did not dispute it. "It's still a long week when your development week is a zero."

On coaching, he said Zernhelt's cutups pairing the narrow base with the corrected rep were the most useful thing he got all year: "I see it faster than I hear it." His complaint was about the same correction: it was called open at every stage, and he never had a clear picture of what closed looks like. Stone treated that as a teaching problem first.

He asked where his in-season blocking actually landed, citing the Denver comment; Stone told him the grade is not in the written record and the cutup will be the answer. He also asked for padded reps before camp. Stone said the offseason phases carry no live contact, so the first padded answer to the correction comes in camp.

## Future and contract

Kelce asked directly whether he would be "more than a two" in 2014, and whether the club was looking at tight ends. Stone made no role or depth-chart commitment: roles move when the football evidence says they should, and the 2014 evidence has not happened yet. He said the principle he wrote before the draft still cuts both ways: Kelce was not drafted as a prewritten replacement plan for anyone, and an earned role is not tenure for anyone, Lewis or Kelce. Acquisitions are Caldwell's; Stone did not discuss draft or free-agent targets. Kelce's contract runs through 2016, and he asked nothing about it.

## Development plan

Voluntary. The offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21; nothing is required before it or in any voluntary phase, and declining any suggestion costs him nothing.

- Block fit: Zernhelt's cutup of his 2013 attached blocks and chip-releases from tape, paired with corrected examples, reviewed together once the program permits position instruction.
- A written description from Zernhelt of what the corrected fit looks like through an alignment change, so he knows what closes the correction; given to him when the CBA permits.
- Routes: the complete active playbook remains his to study. Tape: the voluntary 12 and 22 personnel session offered to Lewis on January 13, with both of them, if both want it; no role or usage follows from it.
- Physical: any program is the strength and performance staff's, on request. Stone did not tie it to the block-fit correction, which the records classify as technique.
- Medical: exit physical today; any finding belongs to the medical staff. Stone reminded him that Player Development and confidential support are there and are not personnel evidence, without asking whether he uses them.

## Follow-ups

- Zernhelt: 2013 attached-block and chip-release cutup with corrected examples; review when position instruction is permitted.
- Zernhelt, with Stone: a written closing standard for the base and hand-placement correction.
- Tice and Zernhelt: voluntary 12 and 22 personnel tape session with Lewis and Kelce, when permitted.
- Medical staff: exit physical, January 14. No finding is recorded in this interview.
- Teaching note: a technique correction set as rookie minicamp's next step stayed open through the OTA, minicamp and camp closes without a stated closing standard. Open corrections need an observable finish line.
- Teaching note: Stone's Week 6 public statement that he would grade Kelce's blocks and releases on tape has no recorded result. A development week graded on tape needs its grade recorded and returned to the player.
