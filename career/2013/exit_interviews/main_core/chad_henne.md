# Chad Henne, QB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Jeremy Bates (quarterbacks); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** QB2 behind Kirk Cousins, with John Parker Wilson QB3. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: veteran contract signed March 2012, two years ($6.75M), final year 2013; pending unrestricted free agent, 5 accrued seasons before 2013 (2008-2011 Miami, 2012 Jacksonville) with 2013 accruing in the branch. Age 28 (born July 2, 1985).

## The season in the record

Henne dressed as QB2 for all 16 regular-season games and both postseason games. The game receipts credit him with no statistic in any of the 18: no dropback, pass attempt, rushing attempt or other counted figure. Every Jacksonville pass play in those receipts names Kirk Cousins as the passer. No receipt injury, no weekly-award shortlist and no in-season honour. Before this staff arrived he played 10 games with 6 starts for Jacksonville in 2012.

Games dressed, regular season (10-6): Week 1 Kansas City, W 31-13; Week 2 Oakland, L 13-17; Week 3 Seattle, W 16-13; Week 4 Indianapolis, W 31-10; Week 5 St. Louis, L 24-26; Week 6 Denver, W 26-10; Week 7 San Diego, W 30-24; Week 8 San Francisco, W 20-13; Week 10 Tennessee, L 11-41; Week 11 Arizona, W 29-7; Week 12 Houston, L 6-38; Week 13 Cleveland, W 22-19; Week 14 Houston, W 21-20; Week 15 Buffalo, L 16-45; Week 16 Tennessee, W 38-27; Week 17 Indianapolis, L 22-23. Postseason: Wild Card at Kansas City, W 38-14; Divisional at Tennessee, L 13-20.

The recorded evaluation of his year comes from the offseason, camp and preseason:

- OTAs, May 13-15: generally assignment-sound and accurate in the short taught menu; slower to reset the protection answer on changing pictures; retained the simplified communication on the final day.
- OTAs, May 20-21: corrected the changed-picture delay often enough to keep the higher-difficulty presentation work; one late reset remained, "improved rather than closed."
- OTAs, May 23: handled the comparable reset promptly and kept Stick and Drive on schedule; the clean transfer "materially narrowed the operational gap."
- OTAs, June 4-5: correctly reset the protection picture on which Cousins held the ball, delivered on time, and earned the next first exposure.
- OTAs, June 6-7: the earned first exposure was orderly until a two-minute clock prompt, when Bates had to restate the situation. Close decision: Cousins narrowly first, Henne immediately behind.
- Mandatory minicamp, June 11: matched the base menu; late once resetting the back in protection. June 12: corrected the reset and ran the best early changed-picture series. June 13: normal and backed-up periods clean; a hurried substitution needed a second call.
- Training camp, July 26-29: matched several core periods; one late hot answer against an overload presentation. July 31-August 2: accurate in the reduced passing menu; needed a repeat on one formation/substitution call; Stone moved from alternating first exposure to a provisional Cousins-first, Henne-second order. August 3 stadium scrimmage: ran the second group cleanly at normal tempo, then lost time to one protection/substitution mismatch. August 5-7: corrected the mismatch.
- Preseason: Miami (W 33-17), Henne, Wilson and Bray moved the offense after Cousins' lone interception without another giveaway; Jets (L 17-24), "Cousins and Henne operated cleanly"; Philadelphia (L 20-31), "meaningful competent work." August 24: Cousins named QB1 and Henne QB2 on the full offseason and camp body, correction retention and clean Games 2-3 operation. Camp standouts: "Henne sustained the closest starting challenge."
- In season: the weekly game records for Weeks 1-8 list him as the dressed QB2; the records from Week 10 through the Divisional round do not name him. None contains an evaluation of his practice work.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Thanks for coming in. Medical has you for the exit physical after this. Whatever comes out of that is between you and them, and we hear only what football needs. This is the review of your year. I'll go through what our record shows, then I want yours, then we'll talk about what comes next as far as I can talk about it.

**HENNE:** Okay.

**STONE:** I'll start with the part I can't do. You dressed eighteen times and you didn't take a dropback. So I don't have 2013 game tape of you, and I'm not going to pretend I do. What I can evaluate honestly is spring, camp and the preseason. The in-season part is thinner than it should be, and I'll come back to that.

**HENNE:** That's fair. There's nothing to watch.

**STONE:** Spring first. First OTA week you were generally assignment-sound and accurate in the short menu. When the picture changed, you were slow to reset the protection. By the last day of that week you were using the simplified language. Next block you corrected the delay often enough that we kept giving you the harder presentation, with one late reset still in there. May 23 you handled the comparable reset promptly and kept Stick and Drive on schedule. Then June 4 and 5, Kirk held the ball when the picture moved the Mesh outlet, and you reset that same picture and threw it on time. That's what earned you the first look at the end of that week.

**HENNE:** And then the clock.

**STONE:** And then the clock. Two-minute period, Jeremy had to restate the situation for you. Minicamp: June 11 you were late once resetting the back. June 12 you fixed it and ran the best early changed-picture series. June 13 the normal and backed-up periods were clean, and a hurried substitution took a second call. Camp: one late hot answer against the overload look in the opening full-team practices. July 31 to August 2, accurate in the reduced menu, and you needed a repeat on a formation and substitution call. The scrimmage, the second group was clean at normal tempo until the protection and substitution mismatch. You had that fixed by the 7th. Preseason, no giveaway in Miami after you came in, clean at the Jets, competent work against Philadelphia.

**HENNE:** I'm not arguing with any of that.

**STONE:** Here's what I take from it. The protection reset on a changed picture was a real correction in May, and you closed it. By June it was holding: you reset the picture and threw on time, and at minicamp you fixed the one late reset overnight. In our language that's Better: you kept the correction and ran it against a look that changed. What kept coming back is the communication when the clock or the substitution speeds up. The two-minute prompt in June, the substitution at minicamp, the camp repeat, the scrimmage. Four separate times, and you corrected the scrimmage one by the 7th, but the same kind of miss showed up in each phase. Nothing in the record makes that an effort question. What it shows is the situation or the substitution call having to be supplied or repeated at pace. That's still Good, because Better means you communicate it without the prompt.

**HENNE:** Yeah.

**STONE:** And I'll be plain about the decision in August. The difference was operation and retention: huddle, substitution, two-minute, and fixing a mistake inside the period. Your accuracy wasn't the issue.

**HENNE:** I get that. Can I say how it looked from my side?

**STONE:** That's why you're here.

**HENNE:** The order got set in May, when all of us were learning a new language. After that, every time I closed something, the word was that I'd narrowed the gap. In the spring I got the first look once. I'm not saying Kirk didn't earn his. I'm saying cumulative is a hard thing to catch from second.

**STONE:** That's an accurate description of how the spring read. Two things. In camp the first look alternated until the July 31 to August 2 practices. That's when the order was set, after Kirk corrected a third-down protection error inside the period and ran the cleanest two-minute. And what decided August was situational operation. Your misses there ran from June through the scrimmage. It wasn't the May reset that decided it.

**HENNE:** Okay. I still think the first week followed me.

**STONE:** You might be right about part of it. The plan says that when two quarterbacks' reps aren't equivalent, we write down how. I want Jeremy to go back through the two-minute and substitution periods and check whether the second group got the same situations at the same pace. If it didn't, then your record in exactly the area that decided it was built on a different test, and that's our problem.

**BATES:** I can do that from the practice cutups. It isn't a long job.

**HENNE:** I'd want to know what you find.

**STONE:** You'll hear it from Jeremy.

**HENNE:** The part that bothers me more is after August. I dressed every week. I don't know what anybody thought of anything I did from September on.

**STONE:** Neither do our records, and that's the honest answer. Through the first eight weeks the weekly reports carry you as the dressed QB2 and nothing else, and after the bye they don't name you at all. There's no evaluation of your practice week written down. We tell backups to prepare as if the role can change on the next snap. Then we owe the backup the same honesty about his work that we owe the starter. If you went the whole season without hearing where you stood, that's on the staff before it's on anyone else.

**BATES:** That's on me as much as anyone.

**HENNE:** Did you ever think about making a change? After San Francisco. After Tennessee.

**STONE:** Going into the Tennessee game I kept Kirk at QB1 without qualification, and he stayed there after it. That was my call. I'm not going to take you through Kirk's season, the same way I wouldn't take him through yours.

**HENNE:** Fair enough.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**HENNE:** The cut-down reset language in May worked. When the terms got simpler I stopped translating in my head. That's the first week I felt like I was running it instead of reciting it.

**TICE:** That simplification came out of that first block. Good to hear it carried.

**HENNE:** What didn't work: Bray got his own cutups and his own teaching sheet. The interior line and Kelce got their own work too. My thing kept showing up in the same spot, the two-minute and the substitutions, and I got it corrected in the room with everybody else. I'd have used my own tape on it.

**STONE:** That's a fair hit. A correction that shows up four times across three phases is what the individual supplement is for, and we didn't build you one. When the same problem keeps coming back, we're supposed to ask whether the teaching is the problem before we ask whether the player is.

**TICE:** There's a second question for us on the call side. At tempo the quarterback is carrying the situation, the substitution and the protection point at once. I want to look at whether our operation asks him to say too much at the same moment. If it does, that's the call structure, and it won't be yours alone.

**HENNE:** That's how it felt in the scrimmage. Two things in the same breath.

**STONE:** Then Mike looks at it. Now the part you came in wanting to ask.

**HENNE:** My deal's up. Do you want me back?

**STONE:** I'll tell you exactly what I can say. Dave Caldwell makes the contract decisions. I'll give him my football evaluation of you, and it'll be the same evaluation you heard in this room. What I recommend to him stays between him and me, and I'm not going to hint at it in either direction.

**HENNE:** That's a careful answer.

**STONE:** It's the only honest one I have. If I told you what I'm going to recommend, I'd be making you a promise that isn't mine to make, or taking one away that isn't mine to take.

**HENNE:** If I'm here, is the job open?

**STONE:** Every role here moves on evidence, and a starting job isn't tenure. That's true in every room, including this one. It's not me telling you there's a quarterback competition in April, and it's not me telling you there isn't one. 2014 roles get decided on 2014 evidence, and none of it exists yet.

**HENNE:** I'm 28. I want to be somewhere I've got a real chance to play. I'm going to sit down with my agent about what's out there.

**STONE:** That's between you, your agent and Dave. I'm not going to steer you.

**HENNE:** Okay.

**STONE:** So here's the development side, and none of it is required. Our program can't start before April 21. When it does, the workouts are voluntary. Until then there's nothing we can require of you, and whatever you decide doesn't cost you anything here. This is what I'd work on if I were you, wherever you are in April.

**HENNE:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** One, the tempo operation. Put a clock on your own study. Say the situation, the call and the protection point out loud before anybody has to tell you the situation. That's the June two-minute rep and the camp repeat. Two, the overload look. The changed-picture reset you already fixed. The one protection item the record doesn't show being retested is the late hot answer against the overload look in the opening full-team practices of camp. Three, the body. Your own program, and if you want an individual recommendation from the performance staff, ask, and they'll put one together within what the rules allow before April. Four, anything medical belongs to the medical staff, starting with today.

**BATES:** And I'll write up your correction record this week. Each date, what the call asked, what happened, whether it held on the retest. Then you've got the spring and camp in one place, and you'll have the comparability answer on top of it.

**HENNE:** I'd like that.

**STONE:** What else do you need from us?

**HENNE:** That's it. The write-up and the answer on the reps.

**STONE:** One more thing, and it's not a question. Player Development is there for anything off the field, and so are the confidential resources. None of it is football evidence, and I'm not asking whether you use any of it.

**HENNE:** Understood.

**BATES:** I'll have the write-up to you by the end of the week. Medical's expecting you next.

## Development plan

All of it is voluntary. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and the club cannot require voluntary offseason work, before the program or during its voluntary phases. Henne is a pending unrestricted free agent; nothing here assumes where he plays in 2014.

- Tempo communication (the recurring item): self-directed study with a clock, stating the situation, the call and the protection point aloud before a prompt. Evidence: June 6-7 two-minute prompt, June 13 hurried substitution, July 31-August 2 formation/substitution repeat, August 3 protection/substitution mismatch (corrected August 5-7).
- Protection against overload: review the one late hot answer against the overload presentation (July 26-29), which the record does not show being retested. The changed-picture reset is recorded as corrected (June 4-5, June 12) and needs only retention.
- Retain what is already Better: the changed-picture protection reset and on-schedule Stick and Drive timing (May 23, June 4-5, June 12).
- Physical: his own offseason program; an individualized recommendation from the performance staff is available on request, within what the CBA permits before the program opens.
- Medical: exit physical with the medical staff on January 13. Any finding, restriction or clearance belongs to the medical staff.
