# Daryl Smith, LB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Frank Bush (linebackers); Romeo Crennel (defensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Base linebacker and defensive communication lead; dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: branch re-signing, two years ($6.00M), final year 2014; 2014 base not guaranteed; under contract; accrued seasons not status-determining (entered 2004) (contract status register). Age 31 (born March 14, 1982).

## The season in the record

Before the season. The free-agency board listed him as an own-player negotiation: "Long Jacksonville tenure. Limited 2012 availability because of injury. Substantial prior starting tape." His 2012 line entering the snapshot was 2 games, 2 starts. The coaching-staff note was that "Crennel/Bush should evaluate him as a football player in the new defense rather than retain him for institutional familiarity," and Stone's view was a short deal only if the medical evaluation was satisfactory, the football role clear and the guarantee controlled. Caldwell would "explore a short Daryl Smith return without treating prior service as an obligation." The club's medical review "was satisfactory for a short return," his representative "sought more than a one-year rehabilitation bet," and the sides closed on two years with only the signing bonus guaranteed "and no starting guarantee."

Spring and camp. Qualified medical personnel cleared him before the May 13-15, May 20-21 and May 23 OTA work and he completed it without a communicated restriction; in the June 4-7 block, instructions were refreshed before each workday, all 64 controlled players participated in their assigned work, and no restriction was communicated. In the first block "Paul Posluszny stabilized front and fit calls, and Daryl Smith executed his assigned non-contact work without a communicated limitation." At mandatory minicamp the defense's first exchange was late; Crennel removed the decoration and restored the look, and "Posluszny and Daryl Smith stabilized the second level" (standouts: "Stabilized front, fit and second-level communication. Base defense enters camp on firm language."). In camp "Crennel's base front and fit held behind Posluszny and Daryl Smith"; at the August 3 scrimmage "Crennel's first defense was sound against the run when the front call arrived early. Posluszny and Smith stabilized fits." Crennel retained "Posluszny/Smith at linebacker" at camp close. A minor upper-extremity restriction at the end of the preseason "cleared through medical re-evaluation" before the September 4 checkpoint. The four preseason game records do not otherwise name him.

Regular season and postseason. Crennel called the defense. With Posluszny out in Week 4, Stone made Smith and Russell Allen the base linebackers and Smith the communication lead, at the time "for Posluszny's absence only"; when Posluszny returned in Week 5, "Daryl ran the communication well last week, so he keeps it." Smith was on the bye-week leadership council, asked "what wastes practice time, what is unclear and what should not change"; the record keeps the questions, not the answers. After Posluszny's Week 13 injury, Smith stayed base linebacker and communication lead with Allen starting beside him from Week 14 ("Don't replace Paul. Play your job." "Crennel may reduce the linebacker menu."). From Week 8 on, most of Stone's recorded weekly directives to Crennel named the opponent's tight end.

## Conversation

**STONE:** You've got the physical after this?

**SMITH:** Right after.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. Frank coached you every day and Romeo called every snap you played, so they're both here. I'll go through what the record says, then I want your side, then you ask what you want.

**SMITH:** I've got one question. It'll keep.

**STONE:** I'll start in March, because it tells you how I'm going to talk to you today. When we built the free-agency board, the staff note on you said Romeo and Frank should evaluate you as a football player in this defense, not keep you because you'd been here a long time. My note said a short deal only made sense if the medical was right, the role was clear and the guarantee was controlled.

**SMITH:** I played two games in 2012. I'd have written the same thing.

**STONE:** The medical came back satisfactory. Your side didn't want a one-year deal and you got two. That part's done. What's left of it is the standard: football, not familiarity. Same today.

**SMITH:** That's what I'd want anyway.

**STONE:** Spring. Before every OTA block the medical staff cleared you, and you finished the work with no restriction. The spring notes have Paul stabilizing the front and fit calls. At minicamp the first exchange was late, Romeo took the decoration off, and the note says you and Paul stabilized the second level: front, fit, second-level communication, base defense into camp on firm language. In camp the base front and fit held behind the two of you, and at the August 3 scrimmage the defense was sound against the run when the front call arrived early.

**STONE:** In our language that's Good. Better asks for communication without a prompt, and a correction you were given and kept when the look changed. The communication is in the notes. A correction with your name on it isn't, so I won't give you that part off paper, and I won't take it away either.

**BUSH:** If I corrected him in the room, it didn't go down on paper by name. That's a note-keeping problem, and it's mine.

**STONE:** Week 4. Paul's out for Indianapolis and I tell the press you'll run the communication. At the time it was for Paul's absence only. They ran for sixty-five and the note says the linebacker operation held. Paul came back for St. Louis and you kept the calls.

**SMITH:** I thought I was handing them back that week.

**STONE:** I said at the time why you weren't: you ran it well, so you kept it. That's the only way anything moves here. Did it cost you anything, calling it and playing your own job?

**SMITH:** St. Louis I'd want to look at. First week doing it with Paul back beside me, and their tight end has a hundred and two. After that it was part of the job.

**STONE:** Arizona, their third snap, you pick off Palmer, bring it back thirty-eight and we kick a field goal off it. Kansas City in the playoffs, fourteen tackles, and your sack starts a three and out. Saturday you get Locker.

**SMITH:** Saturday's doesn't do much for me.

**STONE:** Houston at home is the one I want to spend time on, because it's the best version of your job we saw. Thursday night, short week, Paul hurt the Sunday before, Russell starting next to you. The plan had you as the central voice on Daniels. Foster ran seventeen times for forty. Down 20-14, you, Tyson and Brent stop three straight runs behind the line, they punt from their 10, and we score from their 40. Then Johnson catches an eight-yarder at our 39 with a minute fifty-six left, and you take the ball off him and recover it. Thirteen tackles. Russell had eight next to you, three for loss.

**STONE:** That's the closest the record gets to what we call Best: the personnel around you changed, you had the calls all night, and the man next to you did his job. It's one game, and the record doesn't show your own fits rep by rep, so I'm not calling a season Best off it.

**SMITH:** Russell did what he was told. That makes it easy.

**CRENNEL:** That was the whole message that week. Don't replace Paul, play your job. It was for you as much as for him.

**STONE:** Two things on the sheet I'm not going to lean on. A hundred and nine tackles in the season, twenty-one in the two playoff games. That tells me you were on the field and near the ball. It doesn't tell me whether the fit was right a second before. And six pressures with no sack in the season, then two sacks in two playoff games. I don't know from paper how often Romeo sent you, so I'm not reading anything into either number.

**SMITH:** Tackles aren't nothing.

**STONE:** They're not nothing. They just don't answer the question I have.

**STONE:** Here's the question. Four of our losses have the run defense on the correction list. Tennessee in November: 210, and Johnson's fifty-two-yarder in the fourth quarter. Houston away: Foster twenty-nine for 133, with runs of thirty, fourteen and fourteen. Buffalo: 237 at 7.2 a carry, ten days after Houston went right. Saturday: Johnson twenty-four for 103, and they had the ball thirty-seven oh six. None of those notes has your name on it. They're the defense's. But you made the calls in all four, so what I want to know is whether the front and the fit were right before the snap and whether they held after it. Paper doesn't tell me that.

**SMITH:** Buffalo's the one I think about. We had Houston right, and ten days later they ran it like we'd never seen a run. Romeo, after Paul, did we get smaller? The Houston week the plan said you could trim the linebacker menu. By Buffalo it felt smaller to me.

**CRENNEL:** The plan gave me room to. What I called is on the sheets. I'll put Houston's next to Buffalo's and you'll see what changed and what didn't. If it got smaller and they had it figured, that's mine.

**STONE:** And if it didn't get smaller, we look somewhere else. Either way it's a staff answer before it's a player answer. I'm not going to hang a call on you that Romeo sent in.

**SMITH:** I'm not asking you to take it off me. I'm asking to see it.

**STONE:** You'll see it. What didn't work in how we coached you?

**SMITH:** The tight end. Every week the tight end was a communication note.

**STONE:** [reads from the season's directive sheets] London, full tight-end preparation for Vernon Davis. Arizona, cover Housler and the backs. Houston away, Paul and you carry Daniels. Cleveland, Cameron identified every snap. Houston at home, you the central voice on Daniels. Buffalo, clean Chandler communication. Tennessee here, clean tight-end exchanges. Kansas City, clean Fasano communication. Saturday, account for Washington and Stevens.

**SMITH:** Communication tells everybody who has him. It doesn't make the man who has him able to run with him. Some weeks that's me. Some weeks it's a safety in the wrong spot, some weeks it's the call. If it goes on the sheet as communication every time, then every time it reads like the linebacker who talks didn't do his job.

**STONE:** That's fair to raise, and I'll treat it the way I treat anything like it: as ours before it's yours. Here's what the game book has. Cleveland, Cameron one catch. Kansas City, Fasano three for seven yards. Tennessee here, Stevens three for twenty-seven. Then St. Louis, Cook nine for 102. Houston away, Daniels's touchdown. Houston at home, Daniels sixteen and nineteen on their touchdown drive. Buffalo, Chandler's touchdown on a short field. Indianapolis, thirty-six to Dwayne Allen on the last drive. Saturday, Stevens for twenty-three to the 14. The game book tells me who was thrown to. It doesn't tell me who had him.

**CRENNEL:** He's right about the word. When the note says communication, it tells the unit who owns the tight end. It doesn't say what we do when the man who owns him can't carry him. Whether my calls said it, I'll check.

**BUSH:** I'll pull every tight-end target of the year. Who owned him on the call, whether the call got to everybody, then technique, leverage and whether it was a physical loss. A clean rep beside the bad ones where we have one.

**SMITH:** Put Cleveland in. And Kansas City.

**BUSH:** Both.

**STONE:** If it comes back as your coverage, I'll tell you that straight. If it comes back as the call or the structure, I'll say that too.

**SMITH:** You're going to hear I've lost a step. I'll be thirty-two in March.

**STONE:** I haven't heard it, and I won't take it from anybody without tape. Age isn't a football finding. If there's a step on the tape, the tape is the finding, and you'll be holding the same tape.

**SMITH:** Fair.

**STONE:** What worked?

**SMITH:** Friday. If we weren't sure of it, it was gone. The less I'm carrying on Sunday, the faster I get it out. And Monday. You sort it before anybody yells. Nobody ran because he missed a fit. We looked at why.

**STONE:** At the bye I put you on the council and asked three things: what wastes time, what isn't clear, what shouldn't change. What I have kept is the questions, not your answers. Give them to me again for the whole year.

**SMITH:** Shouldn't change: Friday and Monday. Isn't clear: the tight end, which I've said. Wastes time, I'd have to think about it, and I'm not going to make something up so I've got three. One more that isn't on your list. After Paul went down, if I'd gone down too, who's making the calls? I didn't know. I don't think Russell knew. That gets settled in camp, not in December.

**CRENNEL:** The record has Russell starting beside you and Stanford as the first base reserve. It doesn't say who had the calls behind you. It should.

**STONE:** Then that's on the staff for 2014. Be ready before you're needed applies to us.

**STONE:** Saturday.

**SMITH:** Eleven of twenty on third down. That's us.

**STONE:** We went one for nine. Thirty-seven oh six doesn't happen without that, and that part's mine.

**SMITH:** I'm not giving it to the offense. Twice we made them kick field goals from inside the 11. Then they go sixty-two yards to go ahead and we can't get off the field.

**STONE:** Your question.

**SMITH:** My second year isn't guaranteed. Am I going to be here to play it?

**STONE:** You'll get the true answer, and it's short. I'll give Dave my football evaluation of you. What goes in it is what you've heard in here: the calls, Houston at home, the four run games, the tight ends and whatever Frank's tape shows. The contract, and anything that happens with it and when, is Dave's decision. I'm not going to tell you what I recommend, and I'm not going to hint at it.

**SMITH:** That's about what I expected. Then this. If something's going to happen to me, I want to hear it from somebody in this building before I see it on the ticker.

**STONE:** I'll give Dave that request today. How the club tells a player is his process, and I'm not going to promise you his part of it.

**SMITH:** And when Paul's back. Do I keep the calls?

**STONE:** Paul's health belongs to the medical staff, and I'm not going to talk about another man's situation with you. Nobody's 2014 job is set, and that includes who talks in the huddle. You got the calls in Week 4 because of Week 4 and kept them because of what you did with them. Next year it's the same process with next year's evidence.

**SMITH:** I'd rather hear that than guess.

**STONE:** Between now and April, what you do is yours. There's no club program before April 21, and when there is one it's voluntary. I'm not going to call something voluntary and then count it. What do you want from us?

**SMITH:** Frank's tape and Romeo's sheets.

**BUSH:** End of the month. The tight-end targets, sorted the way I said. Then your run-fit reps from Tennessee in November, Houston away, Buffalo and Saturday, tagged by whether it was the call, the communication, the technique or a physical loss, with Houston at home, Tennessee here and Kansas City beside them for the reps that held. It's yours to keep. Nobody's going to quiz you on it.

**STONE:** How you train is your business. If you want the performance staff's read on anything, ask; it's there inside the rules. What comes out of the physical is the medical staff's, not mine. And what I tell everybody: Player Development is here all offseason. Financial education, confidential counseling, family and life support. You don't have to tell me whether you use any of it.

**SMITH:** I know where it is. One thing. When the program opens, if the young linebackers want to learn the calls, I'll sit with them.

**STONE:** If you want to. It's not on anybody's sheet, and it doesn't count for you or against you.

**STONE:** Anything else you need from us?

**SMITH:** Romeo's answer on the menu before the end of the month.

**CRENNEL:** You'll have it. Frank and I will bring it together.

**STONE:** Then go see the doctors.

Delivered by January 31, 2014. No quiz or follow-up required.
- Run-fit reps, same cutup: his reps in the fit, and the front calls he communicated, from Week 10 (fourth quarter), Week 12, Week 15 and the Divisional game, each tagged by the state/05 execution categories (assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing, medical limitation, coaching or teaching failure), beside reps that held from Week 14 (Foster 2.4 a carry), Week 16 (20 runs for 77) and the Wild Card (47 rushing yards). No individual fault is assumed before the tape is classified; the weekly records assign these corrections to the unit.
- Coverage technique: an individual correction is offered only if the tight-end sort shows losses that are his technique or physical losses. If the sort points to the call or the structure, no individual coverage correction is offered and Stone says so.
- Keep: the communication work as recorded in Week 4 ("Without Posluszny the linebacker operation held") and Week 14 ("Daryl had the calls all night and then made the play. Russell did his job next to him."; "Smith and Allen in the reshaped base defense" held up). This describes 2013 evidence; it is not a 2014 role.
- Physical: his own offseason training. The performance staff's input is available on request within CBA limits; nothing is required.
