# Marcedes Lewis, TE: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; John Zernhelt (tight ends); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Lead TE. He was the lead tight end in every weekly personnel record for Weeks 1 to 8 and TE1 in the bye's second-half personnel plan, and he is first on the season-end depth chart (Lewis, Kelce, Reisner). Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract; 2011 five-year veteran contract, final year 2015; accrued seasons 4+ (entered 2006), with 2013 accruing in the branch (on the 53 for 16 games). Age 29 (born May 19, 1984).

## The season in the record

Lewis dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Travis Kelce, the #33 pick in April, began with "a bounded TE2 job alongside Marcedes Lewis," with Zernhelt owning the initial blocks, releases and route menu; Allen Reisner was TE3 in 13 personnel.

Regular season (game receipts): 95 targets, 61 receptions, 720 yards, 4 touchdowns; 11.8 yards a catch and a longest reception of 72 (team statistics view); no rush and no fumble. Through eight games (bye review): 36 receptions for 420 yards. Through Week 17 he was second among the league's tight ends in receiving yards (league leaders view: Jeff Cumberland 751, Lewis 720).

Postseason: 13 targets, 9 receptions, 108 yards, 2 touchdowns; his longest was 33, in the Wild Card game.

Recorded moments:
- Draft: Stone's own pre-draft board made the case for Kelce as "what Lewis and Kelce could let us do together" and stated: "Lewis keeps an earned role; this is not a prewritten replacement plan."
- Spring and camp: the OTA records do not name him. At mandatory minicamp, "Shorts and Lewis stabilized formation communication." In camp, "Lewis remained the most dependable all-around tight-end operator"; the position-battle record reads "Lewis remained the complete lead." In the first preseason game, against Miami, "assignment-correct route work from Reisner, Lewis, Grimes and Murphy sustained scoring drives." The other preseason records do not name him.
- Week 1: 7 of 11 for 50 and a 1-yard touchdown; a 16-yard catch on the opening possession.
- Week 2 at Oakland: 6 of 9 for 150, including the 72-yard touchdown with Jacksonville down 10-3, a 19-yarder on a 13-snap possession that ended without points, and a 24-yarder on a 66-yard drive that ended in a punt with 3:15 left. Stone afterwards: "The plan was to use two tight ends and let the coverage decide who gets the ball. Today it was Marcedes."
- Weeks 3 to 5: the weekly personnel tables record the tight ends as "Used in protection and the short game" (Seattle), "Protection and formation role" (Indianapolis) and "Delay and protection roles" (St. Louis). At Seattle, a short catch on the drive that began with 58 seconds left and ended in the winning field goal.
- Week 6 at Denver: a 14-yard touchdown to finish a 13-play fourth-quarter drive, the first of the two that put the game away. That week's development emphasis was Kelce and Thielen; Wednesday included "Kelce's chip landmark and release timing." Kelce's development work stayed on the Wednesday schedule in Weeks 7 and 8. Week 7: catches of 31 and 21. Week 8 (London): a 34-yard catch.
- Bye: member of the leadership council, which was asked what wastes practice time, what is unclear and what should not change. The Tuesday practice gave protection its largest block. The second-half plan: "Lewis TE1, Kelce TE2 in the regular call structure"; a Thursday complement period and "Friday deletion of anything the team cannot execute."
- Week 10 at Tennessee (11-41): 1 of 3 for 15. The plan that week made 12 personnel the base, with every pass concept's protection attached before it entered the plan.
- Week 11 vs Arizona: no catch on one target; Jacksonville ran for 267. Stone after the game: "The line and tight ends made Arizona fit the run. Maurice finished it."
- Week 12 at Houston: 23 on Sprint Flood on a 62-yard drive that ended in a field goal, 7-6. The rematch notes record one of the three sacks, by Mays, on TE Delay.
- Week 13 at Cleveland: 7 of 9 for 110, including 42 on Spacing on the 89-yard first possession.
- Week 16 vs Tennessee: a 10-yard touchdown on Texas two plays after Dwight Lowery's interception, 38-13.
- Wild Card at Kansas City: 5 of 7 for 91 and two touchdowns: 13 on Texas to finish the five-pass, 76-yard drive (14-0), 32 on Sprint Flood (21-0), and 33 on Mesh on the second half's opening touchdown drive.
- Divisional at Tennessee: 4 of 6 for 17. The eight drives after the second-quarter touchdown gained 62 yards; Jacksonville was 1 of 9 on third down. Zach Brown's 9-yard fourth-quarter sack came on TE Delay.
- Weekly-award shortlists: none. In-season honours: none. Receipt injuries: none; available every week.

## Conversation

**STONE:** You've got the doctors after this?

**LEWIS:** This afternoon.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of that is between you and them. This is the football part. John coached you every day and Mike ran the offense's week, so they're here. Before I say anything, how do you see your year?

**LEWIS:** Mixed. The numbers are fine. Kansas City was as good a playoff game as I've had. But Saturday I had four catches for seventeen yards and we kicked one field goal after the half. And there were whole games this year I didn't feel like part of the passing game. Arizona, one target. Houston at home, one. I want to know if that was the plan or if that was me.

**STONE:** Fair question, and I'll get to it. I'll start with what I'd keep and where it comes from. Minicamp: you and Cecil steadied the formation communication. Camp: the staff wrote you down as the most dependable all-around tight end we had, and the complete lead in the room. Against Miami in the first preseason game, the review names your route work as assignment-correct on the scoring drives.

**LEWIS:** Okay.

**STONE:** Then the season. Sixty-one catches, 720 yards, four touchdowns, and not one fumble. Through Week 17 that's second among the tight ends in this league in receiving yards. A hundred and fifty at Oakland, including the seventy-two when we were down 10-3. Seven for 110 at Cleveland, with the forty-two on the first drive. The ten-yard touchdown against Tennessee two plays after Dwight's interception. And Kansas City: the thirteen to make it 14-0, the thirty-two on Sprint Flood, and the thirty-three on Mesh to start the second half.

**LEWIS:** Kansas City was fun.

**STONE:** On the program's scale, steadying the communication around you is part of what Best means for a phase. The minicamp and camp records don't describe enough of your own reps for me to put a label on either phase, so I'm not going to. Nothing in the season record puts an assignment error on you.

**ZERNHELT:** I went back through the weekly records for this. There isn't one.

**STONE:** Now the part that isn't finished, and most of it is ours. I can't give you an evaluation of your blocking or your protection this year, because we never wrote one. The weekly records say what the tight ends were used for. Seattle, protection and the short game. Indianapolis, protection and formation. St. Louis, delay and protection. For Nashville the plan made 12 personnel the base and put a protection on every pass before it went on the sheet. After Arizona I said in public that the line and the tight ends made them fit the run. That's the whole written record of you as a blocker. For the lead tight end all year, that's thin, and it's on the staff.

**LEWIS:** That's what I came in to say. You know what I got all year? Game plans. Travis got a development emphasis. Travis got Wednesday periods on his chip and his release. I'm not complaining about a rookie getting coached; he should. But I'd like to know what you thought of my blocking, because I did a lot of it.

**ZERNHELT:** He's right. The individual time in my room went to Travis: his correction work in the spring, my prep in the break before camp, and Wednesdays from Denver through London. Marcedes's week was the plan. What I said to him about blocking in season, I said in the room, and none of it is written down where he can look at it.

**STONE:** Then it's a teaching problem before it's anything about you. An established player's work needs a home in the week too, not only the young player's. John, build it from tape: run blocks, protection, chips, by week. If a snap was right, it gets written down as right. If something needs fixing, the snap and the corrected rep go side by side.

**LEWIS:** And I want it in my hand. Not in a room with the whole group.

**ZERNHELT:** In your hand.

**STONE:** Now the quiet games. Seattle, Indianapolis, St. Louis: five catches, then two, then two. From Nashville on: one, none, two, then seven at Cleveland, then one against Houston. Saturday, four on six targets for seventeen. I can't tell you today how much of that is you. Some of those weeks the sheet asked the tight ends to protect. Some of it is the quarterback and what the coverage gave him. Some of it is my calls. I'm not going to guess from memory, and I'm not going to charge an incompletion to you because it has your name on the target.

**TICE:** At the bye, protection got the largest block of the practice, and from Nashville on 12 was the base because we were giving up sacks. That's where some of your routes went. I don't know that it's all of it.

**LEWIS:** I understand protection. I've been in this building since '06. I've blocked for a lot of backs. But look at it from my side. You take a tight end with the thirty-third pick, you give him the development emphasis, and my targets go down in the second half. You'd be asking the same question.

**STONE:** I would. So here's what I wrote before the draft, on my own board. The case for Travis was what you and he could do together. It said you keep an earned role, and it said it wasn't a replacement plan. That was April. You were our first tight end from camp through Saturday.

**LEWIS:** And next year?

**STONE:** Next year works the same way for both of you. I don't protect a job because somebody's been here a long time, and I don't hand one to somebody because he's young. The evidence in 2014 decides it. That isn't a promise about September either way, for you or for him.

**LEWIS:** That's about what I figured you'd say.

**STONE:** It's what I say to everybody, so nobody hears two versions.

**LEWIS:** Then the other thing. I've got two years left. I know what I count and I know I'm twenty-nine. Dave's going to look at that this spring. Is something coming?

**STONE:** Contracts are Dave's. If you or your agent have a question about yours, it goes to him, and I'm not going to guess at his answer.

**LEWIS:** You're going to talk to him about me.

**STONE:** I'm going to give Dave my football evaluation of every player on this roster, you included. What I tell him stays between him and me. That isn't a hint either way.

**LEWIS:** If there's a conversation about my deal, I want it straight. I don't want to hear it from my agent reading something.

**STONE:** I'll pass that to Dave: if there's anything about your contract, you want it to come to you directly. How and when he handles it is his. What I can talk about with you is the football side.

**LEWIS:** All right.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**LEWIS:** Spring worked. Minicamp was the best this offense talked all year, I thought. The Friday thing in the second half, cutting whatever we couldn't run, I liked. Veterans like that. What didn't: I never knew what you thought of me except from the targets. John tells Travis where his base is every day. Nobody told me mine.

**ZERNHELT:** That's mine.

**STONE:** What should I know about coaching you that I don't?

**LEWIS:** Ask me. I see things at the line that don't show up upstairs. Saturday, by the third quarter, I thought they were playing our heavy calls before the snap. Maybe I'm wrong. Nobody asked me on the sideline, and nobody asked me on the Thursday before.

**STONE:** Whether Tennessee was sitting on the heavy looks is a film question, and yours goes on the list. The Divisional plan is Mike's and mine to review, and I'm not going to answer it two days later in this room.

**TICE:** The heavy calls, 22 Power, 22 Snag and 6OL, were supposed to carry the run-pass conflict. If they were sitting on it, the tape will say so. I'll want your notes when we watch it.

**STONE:** And you're right about asking. We put three questions to the leadership council at the bye: what wastes time, what's unclear, what shouldn't change. I don't have a record of asking them again. How often we do that next year is a decision I haven't made. Answer them now, for your room.

**LEWIS:** Wastes time: not much, after the bye. Unclear: TE Delay. We got sacked on it at Houston and again Saturday. I'm not saying it's the call and I'm not saying it's the protection. I want to know which. Don't change: the Friday cut, and keeping the menu small the way we did in the playoffs.

**TICE:** Both TE Delay sacks go on the protection review by cause, the same way we sorted the first-half sacks at the bye. Nobody gets charged by default, including the tight ends.

**STONE:** The offseason. None of it is required. The program can't start before April 21 and it's voluntary until minicamp. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me or with John.

**LEWIS:** I'll do my own work through the spring. I'll decide about Phase One when I see the schedule.

**STONE:** That's your call. Here's what we'd offer.

**ZERNHELT:** First, the blocking and protection evaluation from this year's tape, written, whenever the rules let me send it to you or sit down with you. Dave's office tells us what's allowed before April. Then in Phase Two, when we can coach on the field, individual work on whatever the tape actually shows, at the speed the rules allow. If the tape says it was right, we keep it, and I don't invent a correction to fill the period.

**TICE:** And the two-tight-end tape with you and Travis together, if you both want it. What we ran from 12 and 22, what worked with both of you on the field and what didn't. That's part of how the staff decides what the package looks like next year, and I'd rather have your eyes on it than not.

**LEWIS:** I'll do that. If Travis wants to watch the run tape with me, I'll do that too. That's part of the job.

**STONE:** It is. On the physical side, nothing in the record: you were available every week and no game receipt has an injury for you. Whatever comes out of this afternoon is the doctors' to tell you. When the program opens, the strength staff builds a plan for anybody who wants one.

**LEWIS:** I'll talk to them when it opens.

**STONE:** Anything else you need from us?

**LEWIS:** The blocking evaluation in writing. The TE Delay answer. And somebody asking the older guys before the third game against the same team.

**STONE:** The first two, yes, when the rules allow. The third is part of that decision, and I haven't made it. One more thing, and I say it to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason for anything away from football. You don't go through me and I don't need to know.

**LEWIS:** Understood.

**ZERNHELT:** I'll call you when Dave's office tells me what I can send.

**LEWIS:** Text first. I'll call you back.

## Development plan

- Blocking and protection evaluation (voluntary to receive): Zernhelt writes an evaluation of his 2013 in-line blocking, protection and chip work from tape, by week, keeping assignment, technique, communication and physical loss separate. Correct snaps are recorded as correct; any correction is shown as the snap beside the corrected rep. Given to him individually, only when and as the CBA permits. Evidence: the weekly records assign the tight ends protection roles (Weeks 3 to 5) and make 12 personnel the base with a protection on every pass (from Week 10), and they contain no individual blocking evaluation.
- Target review: the low-output games sorted by cause (protection assignment, route, coverage, read, throw, call), with Stone's calls marked and nothing charged to the receiver by default. Games: Week 4 (2 catches on 6 targets), Week 5 (2 on 4), Week 10 (1 on 3), Week 11 (0 on 1), Week 14 (1 on 1) and the Divisional (4 on 6 for 17), alongside the weeks the sheet assigned the tight ends to protection.
- TE Delay: the two recorded TE Delay sacks (Week 12, Mays; Divisional, Zach Brown) go on Tice's protection review by cause. He has asked for the answer.
- Two-tight-end tape: the 2013 12 and 22 personnel tape watched with Lewis and Kelce together if both want it, as input to the staff's package decisions. No role, usage or snap commitment follows.
- Phase Two (the program may open no earlier than April 21; Phase Two follows Phase One's two weeks): individual work only on corrections the tape establishes, retested later without a prompt. If the tape shows correct work, nothing is added.
- Keep: formation communication with the players around him (minicamp), dependable all-around tight-end operation (camp), assignment-correct route work (preseason Game 1), ball security (no fumble).
- Physical: nothing indicated in the record. Exit physical today with the medical staff; any finding, restriction or clearance is theirs. Strength and conditioning available when the program opens, voluntary. He plans his own work through the spring and will decide on Phase One later; that carries no consequence.
- All of the above is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; declining any of it carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
