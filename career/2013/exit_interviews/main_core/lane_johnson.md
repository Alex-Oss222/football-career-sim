# Lane Johnson, OT: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; George Yarno (offensive line); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Starting right tackle (won in camp, confirmed on the right side August 24, 2013; Bradfield swing tackle). Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract; branch #2 rookie contract signed May 2, 2013, four years, final year 2016, plus the CBA first-round fifth-year club option. Age 23 (born May 8, 1990).

## The season in the record

Drafted #2 on April 25, 2013, after Kansas City selected Eric Fisher; Caldwell accepted Stone's preference within the premium tackle group. Johnson began at right tackle under Yarno, with Tice integrating his protection and run assignments and Monroe staying at left tackle. The draft card set the test: "Test inside counters, speed-to-power, combination-block timing and communication," and "Johnson's athleticism must translate into assignment execution; it cannot stand in for technique." Public pre-draft record: a former quarterback and tight end with comparatively little time on the offensive line; technique and experience were the developmental questions.

Regular season: dressed for all 16 games, listed as a Jacksonville tackle starter in Weeks 1 to 8 and 10 to 17. The only individual statistic the receipts give him is sacks allowed: 8 charged. Postseason: dressed for both games, none charged; no line reshuffle.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Physical today?

**JOHNSON:** After this.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. This is the football. George had you every day and Mike fit your protection and run assignments into the plan all year, so they're both here. How do you see your year?

**JOHNSON:** I started every game, and from Seattle on the five of us didn't change. We ran it at Kansas City in the playoffs. Saturday we couldn't stay on the field, one for nine on third down. And the first three weeks I was a press-conference question every week. I'm not going to pretend I liked that part.

**STONE:** I'll get to that. I want to start with what I'd keep, because it's the reason you were out there. Rookie minicamp you reached Good on base alignment, formation identification and combination communication, and yours was one of the three strongest teaching tapes we had: the movement, and you kept the hand-placement fix in the repeat period. In OTAs you kept your assignment and your pass-set landmark when we changed the front on you, and the first changed-front series held because Brad made the declaration and you echoed it. First time in pads, you held your set and your run assignment against different edge guys. The camp standouts had you and Will as the best cumulative communication on the first unit. Cameron gave you a real fight and you still won it on that.

**JOHNSON:** He did give me a fight.

**STONE:** On assignment and communication, spring through camp, that's Better on our scale. You kept the correction and you kept the job when the picture changed. I don't have a spring rep where you didn't know who you had.

**YARNO:** Neither do I.

**STONE:** Same record, other side. Rookie minicamp, your hands and your pad level came and went on a few of the controlled power reps. Camp, occasional leverage losses. We wrote those down as technique, not effort, and that's what they were. The draft card said we'd test you on inside counters, speed-to-power, combination timing and communication. Communication passed. Through camp, your technique against power was Good. It wasn't Better yet.

**JOHNSON:** I know where I lose. I'm not arguing that.

**STONE:** Now the part you brought up. Eight sacks got charged to you. Two in the opener, two at Oakland, two at St. Louis, one against San Diego, one at Houston. I'm not using that number to evaluate you.

**JOHNSON:** You used it in September.

**STONE:** I did. After Kansas City I said both were on the tape and we'd fix the technique the same way we did in camp. After Oakland I said it didn't get fixed. Both of those came weeks before the bye, when Mike's staff sorted every sack by cause. I was talking ahead of the sort.

**JOHNSON:** So they weren't mine?

**STONE:** I'm not telling you that either. The charge sheet puts a name next to a sack. It doesn't say why it happened, and it isn't always right about whose it was. In Nashville it charged two to our dressed interior reserve, and the record doesn't say when he'd gone in. The bye sort went by cause. What we didn't do was write down, player by player, what it found. That's ours.

**TICE:** Physical loss, identification, communication, late pressure, overload, the ball held, route timing, a call against the look. Some of the ones with your name on them may land in your column. Some may not. It isn't written yet.

**JOHNSON:** Some of them were mine. I'm not going to sit here and say eight is wrong and none is right. But the Monday before Oakland it was my cutup. The Monday before Seattle it was my cutup again. Week 1 you told them I'd get what the protection calls for, same as anyone. Week 3 you told everybody in Seattle I was getting help. And before Nashville you told them protection is eleven people's job. In September it was mine.

**STONE:** The help at Seattle was my decision, and so was saying it at the podium. "We're giving him help that makes sense." The decision I'd stand by. The telling was more than they needed to hear.

**TICE:** The protection side of it we built with George: chips, back scans, SOLID and MAX, quick game. They'd had seven sacks in two games. With the same film I'd recommend the same week.

**JOHNSON:** I'm not saying don't help. Were you going to pull me that day?

**STONE:** Cameron was the in-game contingency. No rotation planned, and we didn't use it. I said we weren't changing the player without a better answer, and we didn't have one. Seattle didn't sack us.

**JOHNSON:** Okay. Did the help ever come off? Houston, Buffalo, it felt like somebody was always coming my way.

**TICE:** It followed their rusher. At Houston the plan was help for the tackles, plural, against Watt, by alignment. Against Buffalo it was chip and back help on Mario Williams wherever he lined up. That's a plan against the man across from you. It isn't a note about you.

**STONE:** And here's what I can't give you. After Seattle, the week files name you in the line and nowhere else. No note on a set, a run block, a call. That's a record we didn't keep, not a finding about you. We ran for 180 in the opener, 148 at Seattle and at Denver, 179 at Kansas City in the playoffs. That's five linemen, the backs and the tight ends. I'm not handing you those yards any more than I'm handing you the sacks.

**YARNO:** I'll cut every snap of his season. I'd rather go through it with him than describe it.

**STONE:** Good. What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**JOHNSON:** The spring worked. George walks it, I do it, he fixes one thing, I do it again. The hands at rookie minicamp stuck because I did it right the next period. I haven't played tackle that long. I learn it by doing it right straight after I do it wrong.

**YARNO:** Right rep next to the wrong one.

**JOHNSON:** Yeah. And center week before Seattle. Two days of Brewster's point and the protection calls, his first start of the season, and we went up there and didn't give one up. That's the best week we had as a line.

**STONE:** What helps you play your best football?

**JOHNSON:** Knowing early. When the point comes out before the snap and I've echoed it, I'm not thinking, I'm just setting. The weeks I played worst in my own head were the weeks I was thinking about the last loss instead of the call.

**TICE:** That's what the spring said too. Communication was your strength from rookie minicamp on.

**STONE:** What didn't work?

**JOHNSON:** In the season, Monday was the ones I lost. I'd watch the loss over and over and go out Wednesday thinking about the loss. If there were snaps where I did it right against the same guy, I didn't see them on Monday.

**YARNO:** The Mondays after Kansas City and Oakland both had your cutups in them. That was the plan both weeks. If the right rep wasn't sitting next to the wrong one, that's on me.

**STONE:** Then it's a teaching problem first. In the spring you got the pair and the retest. In September you got the half that hurts, two Mondays running. We fix the method, not you.

**JOHNSON:** That's all I'm saying.

**STONE:** What do you think we misunderstand about you?

**JOHNSON:** That I'm a project. Everybody who wrote about the draft said athlete, not much time at tackle. Fine, that's true. But I knew my job every week. I want credit for that, and I want the hands fixed. Those are two different things.

**STONE:** They are. Through camp, the record says the first one. In the season we didn't write it down either way, which is why George is cutting the film. Knowing the job and winning the rep are separate columns here. The second is the work.

**JOHNSON:** Can I ask about next year?

**STONE:** Ask.

**JOHNSON:** Am I the right tackle?

**STONE:** You were the right tackle for all sixteen games and both playoff games because camp put you there and nothing in the season gave us a better answer. That's a fact about 2013. It isn't a promise about September. Roles move on evidence here, and they moved for you in August.

**JOHNSON:** What about left?

**STONE:** There's no left tackle decision, and I'm not making one in January. Whatever Eugene's situation is, that's his and Dave's, and I'm not going to talk about another man's contract with you. If that question ever comes up, it comes out of what we see in the spring and in camp, and you'd hear it from me before you read it.

**JOHNSON:** Is Dave bringing in tackles?

**STONE:** Dave builds the roster. I'll give him my evaluation of the whole line, you included, and what I tell him stays between him and me. I'm not going to tell you who is or isn't on his board, because I don't decide it.

**JOHNSON:** I'm signed. I'm not worried about that part. I just want to know where to put the work.

**STONE:** Then I'll tell you where I'd put it. None of it is required. The program can't open before April 21, and until minicamp every piece of it is voluntary. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me.

**JOHNSON:** I'll be around.

**YARNO:** First the film. Your whole season by cause, the eight charges in with everything else, nothing in your column by default, and a right rep next to every loss where we have one. When I can send it depends on what the rules allow before the program opens. Dave's office tells us that. I'm not going to guess.

**TICE:** Then when we're allowed to coach on the grass, power. Speed-to-power and the inside counter, two of the four things the draft card said we'd test. Hands inside, pad level, and the anchor after eight straight snaps, not just on the first.

**JOHNSON:** Do you want me heavier?

**STONE:** I'm not giving you a number. The performance staff sets your range from how you move and how you anchor. If you move the way you do and hold up against power, the scale isn't the point. If the movement goes, the range changes. That's theirs to work out with you, and it starts when the program does.

**JOHNSON:** Okay.

**STONE:** Anything you need from us?

**JOHNSON:** Two things. The film before I start lifting, if the rules let you. And in September, if they ask about me, say less.

**STONE:** The first, if the rules allow it. The second is already how we're supposed to work: the correction belongs to the team before it belongs to the public. After Oakland I said more than they needed. That one's mine.

**JOHNSON:** Fair.

**STONE:** If you want anything set up away from football, Player Development is here all offseason. You don't go through me and I don't need to know.

**JOHNSON:** Got it.

**YARNO:** I'll call you when Dave's office tells me what I can send.

## Development plan

- Full-season film review, voluntary to receive and to discuss: every pass set and run assignment, with the 8 charged sacks sorted alongside everything else by the bye-week causes (physical loss, identification, communication, late pressure, overload, holding the ball, route timing, call against the look). Nothing goes in his column by default; each loss is paired with a correct rep against the same rusher or look where one exists. Yarno builds it, Tice on the protection side. Delivered only when and as the CBA permits.
- Technique against power, the open item (state through camp: Good): hand timing and placement, pad level, leverage and anchor against speed-to-power and the inside counter. Evidence: the draft card's test list, the rookie-minicamp controlled power reps, the camp leverage losses. Taught as pairs (the loss beside the correct rep, then an immediate re-rep, the method he says works), then retested unprompted later in Phase Two and OTAs at the contact level those phases permit.
- Keep, do not re-teach: assignment and communication (Better through spring and camp): the point echo, changed-front calls, the twist-exchange spacing. No new job added because the calendar advanced.
- Repeated effort: technique held late in a drive, not only on the first snap (readiness standard section 1), built into the performance staff's plan.
- Physical: exit physical today with the medical staff; any finding, restriction or clearance is theirs. Playing-weight range and functional-strength work set by the performance staff from how he moves and anchors, from April 21 at the earliest. Stone sets no weight target.
- All of the above is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; declining any of it carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
