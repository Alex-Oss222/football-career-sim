# Brent Grimes, CB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Tony Oden (defensive backs); Romeo Crennel (defensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Starting cornerback, outside with Alan Ball through Week 10 and with Mike Harris from Week 11; dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: branch free-agent signing, one year ($5.50M, fully guaranteed), final year 2013; pending unrestricted free agent, 4+ accrued seasons (entered 2006; played 2012 under an Atlanta franchise-tag contract and signed from outside as a free agent in March 2013), 2013 accruing in the branch (contract status register). Age 30 (born July 19, 1983).

## The season in the record

Before the season. The free-agency board's public-fact lines were "High-level prior starting tape" and "Entering market after a torn Achilles cost him most of 2012." Stone's view: Grimes was "not the default second premium signing" but a contingency if Jacksonville did not spend its major money on Bennett. After Bennett declined, Jacksonville "completed its medical review and offered Grimes a one-year, $5.5M contract with the full amount guaranteed. Grimes accepted the short structure and an immediate opportunity to compete for a major role. The club priced the Achilles uncertainty through term rather than asserting a guaranteed recovery or future performance."

Spring, which the records treat as medical placement and not football evidence. In the first OTA block (May 13-15) he "participated in meetings and medically directed rehabilitation/individual work, but was withheld from offense-versus-defense team periods." After reassessment he was cleared May 21 "for a limited set of controlled, non-contact team-period repetitions, with the medical staff retaining control of the volume." May 23 was the same with group and base team repetitions added; from June 4 he had "a broader but still medically controlled non-contact team assignment" and stayed out of the longest competitive segment. At mandatory minicamp he "received an expanded non-contact team assignment but remained medically restricted from the longest competitive period." The first OTA block's closing decision was "no coach-created timetable applies"; the OTA standouts file called his progress "medical progression only"; at minicamp "Stone did not treat medical limits as character evidence." No setback was communicated at any point.

Camp. At the July 25 report qualified medical personnel "cleared Brent Grimes for full football participation, with ordinary workload monitoring but no football restriction; this was a medical decision, not a coaching acceleration." In the first physical work, "Grimes' full-work return was technically sound, but Stone and Crennel did not convert clearance into automatic status; Ball, Harris, Poyer and the other corners continued comparable role-specific work." Then "Grimes and Ball handled the leading outside work, with Harris in the next band." At the August 3 stadium scrimmage he "completed the scrimmage without a communicated setback and combined sound leverage with clear exchanges." He completed camp work through August 8 without a communicated setback, and at camp close "Crennel retained ... Grimes/Ball outside." The four preseason game records do not name him.

Regular season and postseason. Ball was hurt at Tennessee in Week 10 and was out beyond the regular season; Harris started outside from Week 11 with Poyer at nickel. Crennel called the defense. Stone's directives to Crennel that touched the corners: Week 8 "finish through the catch against Boldin"; Week 11 "identify Fitzgerald every snap without leaving Harris alone"; Week 12 "know Andre Johnson's location without leaving Harris isolated"; Week 14 "change the help location on Andre Johnson without abandoning Hopkins"; Week 15 "don't lose Graham deep while helping Johnson"; Week 16 "know Britt's location"; Week 17 "know Wayne every snap; no free Hilton vertical"; Wild Card "no free intermediate explosives to Avery" and "rally and tackle underneath"; Divisional "change Britt's picture by situation without distorting the front." The bye review's defensive correction was "More disruption without losing structure: rush finish, tackling through the ball, backs and tight ends in space, and explosive-play prevention," studied on "a cut-up of every 20-yard play allowed."

- Postseason, 2 games: 7 tackles (5 solo, 2 assisted), 1 pass defended, 1 punt return for 3 yards.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Tony's here because he coached you every day. Romeo's here because he called every defensive snap you played. The medical staff has you after us.

**GRIMES:** You're not going to ask about the Achilles?

**STONE:** No. That's between you and the doctors. If there's something football needs for availability, they tell us that and nothing else. I'll go through what the record says about your year, and then I want yours.

**GRIMES:** I've got one question and you know what it is. Go ahead with the tape first.

**STONE:** Start with March. You signed for one year after the club's medical review, and Dave priced the uncertainty through the length of the deal. That's the contract side, and it's his. The football side starts in May. The first week of OTAs you were in meetings, rehab and individual work and out of team periods. May 21 the medical staff cleared you for a limited set of non-contact team reps, June was broader, and at minicamp you were still out of the longest period. None of that is in your football file as anything. It was medical placement, and nobody on this side of the building set a timetable for it.

**GRIMES:** Nobody tried to hurry it. I noticed that.

**STONE:** July 25 the doctors cleared you for full work. Their decision. In the first physical work, July 26 to 29, the note is that your return to full work was technically sound. And then Romeo and I kept Alan, Mike, Jordan and the other corners on comparable work anyway.

**GRIMES:** I noticed that too.

**STONE:** How did it land?

**GRIMES:** I'd been cleared. I'd been in every meeting since May. And I'm splitting reps like I walked in off the street. I knew why you were doing it. It still bothered me for about a week.

**CRENNEL:** The clearance was the doctors'. The job wasn't theirs to give, and it wasn't mine to give off a medical form.

**GRIMES:** I'm not arguing it. He asked how it landed.

**STONE:** And I'd do it the same way. August 3, the scrimmage: no setback, and the note is sound leverage with clear exchanges. At the end of camp Romeo had you and Alan outside. In our language, camp was Good without any question: the leverage was sound, you communicated, and the technique held at camp speed. Clear exchanges point toward Better. The rest of Better is holding a correction and handling a changed look, and I can't give you that off paper, because nobody wrote a correction down for you.

**ODEN:** We wrote about the room. I can't show you whether there weren't any or whether I didn't write yours down.

**STONE:** The season. Sixteen games dressed, both playoff games, nothing on an injury sheet. Indianapolis at home, you picked Luck on a throw to Ballard. In the second half we put the ball on the ground, and on their next possession you take it off Ballard after the catch and come up with it. Clemons scored on the drive after. I said that night you got it back for us right away.

**GRIMES:** That was a good day.

**STONE:** Houston away. Their third snap of the second half, you intercept Schaub and bring it back to our 46. Romeo's notes from that night say two things made Houston change anything all game: their first possession and your pick.

**CRENNEL:** That's what I wrote.

**GRIMES:** And we got five yards.

**STONE:** Five yards and a punt downed at the line. That's the offense, and the offense is mine. Houston at home, late: you, Tyson and Daryl stop three straight runs for losses, they punt from their 10, and Shorts scores four plays later. That three-and-out is on the held-up list.

**GRIMES:** That one I liked more than the pick.

**STONE:** Sixty-seven tackles. For a corner that number can mean two different things, and the sheet can't tell me which.

**GRIMES:** One of them's good.

**STONE:** And the other one means the ball's getting caught in front of you. I'm not reading it either way until Tony has pulled the tape. Denver's the same kind of question. On paper it's one of your two quietest days, one assisted tackle. It's also 87 passing yards allowed and 0 for 13 on third down. A quiet line from a corner isn't a bad day.

**GRIMES:** Tell that to my agent.

**STONE:** Now the part that isn't comfortable. Houston away: Andre Johnson, eight for 134 and three touchdowns. Buffalo: Manuel threw four touchdowns, two to Graham, the week the directive was don't lose Graham deep. Tennessee at home: Britt, fifteen catches for 187 on 24 targets, and Greene's fifty-yard touchdown. Indianapolis: the late pass defense, Luck 28 of 38 and Wayne eleven catches. Kansas City: Avery twelve of thirteen underneath. None of those notes has your name on it. After Houston I told the press I wasn't putting Johnson on one player, and I'm not going to write your name in here when the file doesn't. The file also doesn't say who had whom on any of those snaps. You were the starting corner for all of it. What's your read?

**GRIMES:** Start with the plan. The first two weeks after Alan went down, it was about Mike. Arizona, identify Fitzgerald without leaving Harris alone. Houston, know where Johnson is without leaving Harris isolated. I heard that in the room. I'd have done the same thing; Mike was new. Nobody ever told me in words what it meant for my side.

**CRENNEL:** The directive was about Mike. What it did to your side is on my call sheets, snap by snap. I'd rather show you than tell you from memory.

**ODEN:** And if you didn't hear it from me in the room every week, that's mine to fix, not yours.

**STONE:** That's a teaching question before it's anything else. If a call protects one corner, the other corner hears what it leaves him, in words, before Sunday. Keep going.

**GRIMES:** Britt. Fifteen catches. I don't know yet what my part of that is, and I want to. Then Saturday, Romeo moves Britt's picture around by situation, and he catches eight for 58 and nothing longer than nineteen. On Saturday I knew before the snap what I had.

**CRENNEL:** Change his picture by situation. That was the directive for Saturday.

**STONE:** Then the question for the staff is why it took a playoff game to get there. Tennessee had Britt for ten catches and two touchdowns in November. That one's ours.

**GRIMES:** Kansas City I'm not losing sleep over. We were up 38-7.

**STONE:** We were. It's still on the list. They went 80 and 84 in the second half, and Avery caught twelve of thirteen. Put it in the cutup. If it's what you'd expect at 38-7, you'll see that and so will I.

**GRIMES:** Fine. Put it in.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you?

**GRIMES:** Spring. I was on the side for most of the team work and I still walked into camp knowing the book, because the meetings were real meetings. Tony didn't treat me like I was on vacation. And the medical side. Nobody came up to me every day asking when I'd be back.

**STONE:** What didn't?

**GRIMES:** What I said about Mike's side. And the film. I learn off my own clips with the call on them: where the help was, where I was, what I saw. Room film is fine for the room. It's not how I fix my stuff.

**STONE:** What should I know about coaching you?

**GRIMES:** Tell me straight and show me the clip. I've been in this league since 2006. If it's my technique, I'll own it. If it's the call, say it's the call.

**STONE:** That's the standard anyway. If the call was bad, the coach says so. You've been in secondaries a long time. What should we be teaching that room that we aren't?

**GRIMES:** Same thing. Say the help out loud before the snap, every snap, so the young guys hear it. Mike will hear it. Jordan will. I'd hear it too.

**ODEN:** I'll take that.

**STONE:** Your question.

**GRIMES:** I took one year because of the Achilles. Everybody knew why. I played every game. Are you bringing me back, and is anybody going to talk to my agent before the 11th?

**STONE:** I'll answer it inside what I'm allowed to answer. I'm going to give Dave my football evaluation of you. What you've heard in this room is the record I work from: camp, Indianapolis, Houston twice, Denver, and the corrections I just read you. Whether there's a contract, what it looks like and when anybody calls your agent are Dave's. I'm not going to tell you what I recommend, and I'm not going to hint at it.

**GRIMES:** So you can't tell me if you want me.

**STONE:** I can tell you what I think of the football, and I just did. What I say to Dave stays between me and Dave. If I told you something here and Dave did something else, I'd have made you a promise I can't keep.

**GRIMES:** I played 2012 on a tag in Atlanta. Is that on the table here?

**STONE:** The tag is a contract tool, and it's Dave's. The dates are public. The window is February 17 to March 3. Your agent can talk to other clubs from noon on March 8, and the league year opens March 11. Anything between your agent and this club before then is Dave's.

**GRIMES:** Does the Achilles come into it? Thirty, off an Achilles.

**STONE:** Not from me. I'm not a doctor, and I'm not putting a medical opinion into a football evaluation. What I evaluate is what you did on the field. How Dave weighs anything else is his to say, not mine.

**GRIMES:** And if I'm back, am I starting?

**STONE:** Nobody's 2014 job is set. You started this year because camp put you there and the games kept you there, and we made you earn it in August after you'd been cleared. It'd be the same process next year with new evidence.

**GRIMES:** I figured you'd say that.

**STONE:** It's the same answer everybody in the building gets, because it's the true one.

**GRIMES:** I'll be straight with you too. I liked it here. That doesn't mean I'm taking less to stay. My agent's going to listen to everybody.

**STONE:** That's his job, and it's your right.

**STONE:** The offseason. None of it is required. There's no club program before April 21, and when there is one, it's voluntary. I'm not going to call something voluntary and then count it.

**ODEN:** What I owe you: your season and both playoff games, every snap where the ball went to a receiver in your part of the call, off Romeo's sheets. Each one with the call, where the help was, your leverage, the technique and the result. Houston away, Buffalo, Tennessee at home, Indianapolis and Kansas City first, and Saturday's Britt snaps next to Week 16's. Indianapolis at home and the Houston pick go in as keep reps. By the end of the month. It's yours to keep, and nobody's going to ask you about it.

**GRIMES:** And the tackles. I want to know which kind they were.

**ODEN:** They'll be sorted.

**CRENNEL:** From me, Arizona through Saturday: what each week's help plan left on your side, written down from the call sheets. This month.

**STONE:** If the tape comes back as the call, there's nothing for you to fix, and I'll say so. If it comes back as technique, Tony shows you which reps and it's work you can do anywhere. The physical side is yours. You know how you train. If you want the performance staff's input, ask; it's there inside the rules. Anything medical belongs to the medical and training staff, not to me and not to Tony. And the thing I say to everybody: Player Development is here all offseason. Financial education, confidential counseling, help finding the right resource if your family needs something. You don't have to tell me whether you use any of it.

**GRIMES:** One thing. Whatever comes out of the physical today, I hear it from the doctors. Not from a coach, and not from the paper.

**STONE:** That's how it works here. The doctors tell you. We get what football needs for availability, and that's all we get.

**GRIMES:** Then I'm done.

**STONE:** They're ready for you in the training room.

Priority games: Week 12 (Andre Johnson), Week 15 (Manuel and Graham), Week 16 (Britt and the 50-yard Greene touchdown), Week 17 (the late pass defense) and the Wild Card (Avery underneath, second half). The Divisional Britt snaps sit beside Week 16's. Delivered by January 31, 2014. No quiz or follow-up required.
- Classification before any correction: each rep in those games is sorted by the state/05 execution categories (assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing, medical limitation, coaching or teaching failure). The weekly records assign these corrections to the unit; no individual fault is assumed before the tape is classified.
- Tackles, same cutup: his tackles sorted into run support and open-field work versus tackles after a completion in his responsibility, so the 67 is read by kind and not by count.
- Keep reps, same cutup: Week 4 (interception of Luck; the Ballard strip and recovery after Jacksonville's giveaway), Week 12 (interception of Schaub), Week 14 (the late run stops that produced the three-and-out) and Week 6 (Denver: 87 passing yards and 0 of 13 on third down) as a reference for a quiet line on a sound day. Camp evidence to keep: "sound leverage with clear exchanges" (August 3).
- Technique focus, only after the sort: if the classified reps show technique losses, Oden names the specific reps and the technique; the unit items that frame the question are the bye's corrections "explosive-play prevention" and "tackling through the ball" and the Wild Card directive "rally and tackle underneath." If the sort shows the call or the help plan, no individual correction is assigned and Stone says so.
- Physical: his own offseason routine. The performance staff's input is available on request within CBA limits. Nothing is required. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21 and is voluntary; declining it costs nothing (readiness standard section 7). No role is attached to any of this.
- Medical: today's exit physical belongs to the medical staff, and any rehabilitation the medical staff directs is run by the medical and training staff. Findings go to the player from the medical staff; coaches receive only what football availability requires.
- Support: Player Development resources, including financial education, remain available; no disclosure asked or recorded.
