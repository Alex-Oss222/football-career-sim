# Cecil Shorts, WR: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Darryl Drake (wide receivers); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** WR1. A co-starter with Blackmon in Week 1, then first in the receiver order from Week 2 through the Divisional game (order at season end: Shorts, Thielen, Blackmon, Clemons, Brown). Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract; 2011 fourth-round four-year rookie contract, final year 2014 (two accrued seasons through 2012, with 2013 accruing in the branch). Age 26 (born December 22, 1987).

## The season in the record

Shorts dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. In Week 1 he and Blackmon were the starting pair carried from camp ("Shorts/Blackmon lead"); ledger Entry 36 made him WR1 from Week 2, and he stayed first in the receiver order through the Divisional game. Blackmon was on Reserve/Suspended for Weeks 2 to 5 and inactive by Stone's choice in Weeks 6 and 7; from Week 8 the order was Shorts, Thielen (WR2/H), Blackmon (outside Z), Clemons, Brown.

Regular season (game receipts): 157 targets, 104 receptions, 1,158 yards, 6 touchdowns; 11.1 yards a catch and a longest reception of 55 (team statistics view); 4 rushes for 15 yards (long 8); 2 punt returns for 13 yards and 1 kick return for 24; no fumble. Through eight games (bye review): 55 receptions. The ledger's season summary lists his 1,158 receiving yards among the verified statistics.

Postseason: 16 targets, 9 receptions, 148 yards, 2 touchdowns; 2 rushes for 5 yards and a touchdown; 1 punt return for 16 yards.

Recorded moments:
- Spring and camp: at OTAs the most consistent receiver at preserving the taught Stick and Drive landmarks against changed leverage, and he transferred his route job through limited motion ("most dependable receiver landmarks across the taught core"). At mandatory minicamp, "Shorts and Lewis stabilized formation communication"; recorded as "the most dependable current receiver operator." In camp, Shorts and Blackmon formed the leading receiver band, "Shorts was steadier in assignment," and the two separated as the leading pair by cumulative camp operation. The OTA and minicamp records add that no target, workload or roster guarantee followed. The preseason game records do not name him.
- Week 1: 34 yards on the one shot taken; Brandon Flowers intercepted a throw for him on the third drive. The week record says both Week 1 interceptions needed film review "to separate the read, the throw and the route."
- Week 3 at Seattle: Richard Sherman intercepted a throw to him on the second possession; later 18 and 21 yards on the last two scoring drives, the 21 on the drive that began with 58 seconds left and ended in the winning field goal.
- Week 4: seven catches on seven targets. Week 5 at St. Louis: touchdowns of 27 and 1 yards, the second pulling Jacksonville within two with 8:52 left. Week 7: a 24-yard touchdown.
- Week 8 (London): 7 of 13 for 81. Three of Cousins's four interceptions came on throws toward him (Asomugha, Willis, and Aldon Smith at 3:27 of the fourth quarter); the week review listed them first under what must be corrected. At the bye each Week 8 interception was classified separately (read, leverage, protection, route, timing, decision, placement); the records do not keep the classifications.
- Week 10 at Tennessee: 9 of 12 for 116, including a 28-yard Stick and a 35-yarder; the review: "Shorts and Thielen got open." McCourty intercepted a throw to him at the Jacksonville 8, and Tennessee scored on the third snap. Jacksonville had six giveaways.
- Week 12 at Houston: 10 of 17 for 61. The rematch notes: "Shorts drew 17 targets for 61 yards; the passing game averaged 4.2 yards an attempt." The game was 17 runs against 45 dropbacks. In the second half, on the snap after Jones-Drew's 34-yard run to the Houston 22, Brooks Reed intercepted a Snag intended for him and returned it 46 yards.
- Week 13 at Cleveland: 37-yard touchdown on Sprint Flood on the first possession.
- Week 14: Stone's rematch plan said "do not force Shorts." He caught 5 of 6 for 99, including the 36-yard Power Pass Post-Cross touchdown with 3:34 left that won the game 21-20.
- Week 16: 4 of 12 for 104 and a touchdown: 55 on Texas, 36 on Spacing and a 2-yard touchdown on 22 Heavy Snag. That week's quarterback hierarchy was "possession, then correct leverage, then completion, then the explosive."
- Week 17: 18 on Power Pass and 28 on Texas set up two of Scobee's five field goals in the 23-22 loss.
- Wild Card: 27 and 14 on Snag and 22 on Mesh on the five-pass, 76-yard touchdown drive; a 4-yard touchdown run; a 12-yard touchdown on the Post-Cross; 21 on Texas to the 4.
- Divisional: 40-yard touchdown on Mesh at the end of a nine-pass, 80-yard drive for a 10-7 lead. Jacksonville's eight drives after that touchdown gained 62 yards; 1 of 9 on third down.
- Weekly-award shortlists: none. In-season honours: none. Receipt injuries: none; available every week.

Evidence caveat: the Week 7 and Week 8 records state that kernel 2013.6 (Weeks 4 to 8) did not tie call labels to the receiver, and ledger Entry 44 records that its targets followed position-group usage and depth order. Concept names on his catches before Week 10 are therefore not used below as evidence about him, and no evaluation here rests on his target count.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Physical this afternoon?

**SHORTS:** After lunch.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. This is the football part. Darryl coached you every day and Mike ran the offense's week, so they're here. Before I say anything, how do you see your year?

**SHORTS:** Good year. Every game, both playoff games, a hundred and four catches. I'm proud of it. But I keep going back to Saturday. We go eighty yards, I score, it's 10-7, and then eight drives for sixty-two yards. And I still think about London.

**STONE:** We'll get to both. I'll start with what I'd keep and where it comes from. OTAs: you were the most consistent receiver at keeping the Stick and Drive landmarks when the leverage changed, and when we added motion your route job came with you. Minicamp: you and Marcedes steadied the formation communication for everybody else, and the staff wrote you down as the most dependable receiver we had. Camp: you and Justin led the room, and you were the steadier of the two on assignment.

**SHORTS:** Dependable.

**STONE:** You don't like the word.

**SHORTS:** I've heard it since I was a fourth-rounder. The other guy stresses a defense, I line up right. I line up right and I make plays.

**STONE:** The season says you make plays, so I'll say it that way. Seven for seven against Indianapolis. Two touchdowns in St. Louis, the second one to get us within two. Nine for 116 in Nashville on a day we turned it over six times, and the review that week says you and Adam got open. The thirty-seven on the first drive at Cleveland. The thirty-six with three thirty-four left that beat Houston. Fifty-five and thirty-six against Tennessee in December. In Kansas City, three catches on the five-throw touchdown drive, the Post-Cross for a touchdown and the four-yard run. The forty on Saturday. Eleven fifty-eight, and not one fumble.

**SHORTS:** Okay.

**STONE:** On the spring standard you were Better: you kept what was taught and executed it against changing looks. The minicamp communication, steadying the players next to you without losing your own job, is what Best for that phase means. Dependable isn't a ceiling. It's why you had the job in August.

**DRAKE:** And nothing in the season record puts an assignment error on him. I went back through it.

**STONE:** Now the part that isn't finished. Throws toward you ended in interceptions in the opener against Kansas City, at Seattle, three times in London, at our own eight in Nashville, and on the Snag that Reed ran back at Houston. At Houston you also drew seventeen targets for 61 yards. Against Tennessee in December it was twelve targets and four catches, even with 104 yards. I can't tell you today how much of any of that is yours. Some of it may be the quarterback, some protection, some my call, some the route or the timing, and some the other team. After the opener we wrote down that both interceptions needed film to separate the read, the throw and the route. At the bye we classified the London interceptions one at a time. What we kept doesn't carry the answers, so I'm not going to quote them to you from memory.

**SHORTS:** That's what I wanted to talk about. After London what I heard was a number. Three on throws toward me. I've got my own view of some of those, and it isn't the number.

**STONE:** Go ahead.

**SHORTS:** Some of them, Kirk and I weren't seeing the same thing. I'm not saying whose it was. I'm saying when a corner changes what he's doing, I've got an answer, and Kirk has to have the same answer at the same time. In the spring that was the work. Darryl gives you a different leverage, Stick and Drive against it, then again. In the season I don't remember doing that with Kirk against a moving picture. I remember the game plan.

**DRAKE:** He's not wrong. The development time we built into the weeks went to Adam's motion landmarks and Travis's releases. Cecil's week was the plan.

**TICE:** And at the bye protection was the largest block of the practice, and it stayed near the front of most weeks after that. We were giving up sacks. That time came from somewhere.

**STONE:** Then it's a teaching question before it's a Cecil question, and maybe before it's a Kirk question. The spring had a period for the adjustment, and it held. Nothing I can find in the season's weeks gave it a home. Protection getting the time was my choice, and it may have cost us the other thing. I don't know yet. The film tells us.

**SHORTS:** I'm not trying to get out of anything. If some of them are mine, show me.

**STONE:** That's the plan. Jeremy is building a sheet of every interception this year. The ones toward you get watched with Darryl in the room, as a receiver and quarterback question, with the right rep next to each one where we have it. You and Kirk watch it together if you both want to. Nothing gets charged to anybody by default.

**SHORTS:** And the incompletions? Twelve and four, people see that.

**STONE:** London, Houston and December go on the same sheet. And seventeen for 61 at Houston isn't a line I'm putting on you. That game was seventeen runs against forty-five dropbacks, and the call balance was mine. Which brings me to a line you should hear about from me. When I wrote the plan for the Thursday game against Houston, one line said: do not force Shorts.

**SHORTS:** Six targets that night. I figured something had changed.

**STONE:** The rematch notes put your seventeen for 61 under what Houston took away. The line meant the plan shouldn't keep going back to what they'd already taken. It wasn't a finding about you.

**SHORTS:** Five catches, ninety-nine, and the one that won it. I'm not complaining about that game. What I'd say is, if the plan changes where the ball goes, tell me that week. Don't leave me to read it off the targets.

**STONE:** That's fair. Darryl, if we change on purpose where the ball is going, the player hears it in the meeting, not on game day.

**DRAKE:** It goes in the Wednesday install, in plain words.

**STONE:** What else worked or didn't in how we coached you?

**SHORTS:** Spring worked. Darryl shows you the landmark, you walk it, you rep it, then he changes something and you do it again. By minicamp I didn't have to think about where I was, so I could help the young guys line up. In the season it got short. Monday film, a note, next opponent.

**STONE:** What should I know about coaching you that I don't?

**SHORTS:** Show me the snap. Don't tell me about it. If it's mine I'll fix it by Wednesday. If you tell me without the tape, I'm going to argue, and that's a waste of both of us.

**STONE:** Then the sheet is built that way: the snap, and the right rep next to it. Anything else before we get to next year?

**SHORTS:** Saturday. Third time against them, and it felt like they knew everything we had.

**STONE:** The punt with two minutes left is mine. Whether the plan gave you and Kirk enough the third time is Mike's and mine to work out, and I'm not going to answer it two days later in this room.

**TICE:** One of nine on third down. That's where I'm starting, not with what we called on first down.

**SHORTS:** Fair. Next year, then. I'm in the last year of my deal. My agent's going to call Dave. Is something coming?

**STONE:** Contracts are Dave's. If you or your agent have a question, it goes to him, and I'm not going to guess at his answer.

**SHORTS:** Are you going to tell him to pay me?

**STONE:** I'm going to give Dave my football evaluation of every player on this roster, you included. What I tell him stays between him and me. That isn't a hint either way. It's the same for everybody in this building, so nobody hears one thing from me and something else from Dave.

**SHORTS:** That's a coach's answer.

**STONE:** It's the true one.

**SHORTS:** Am I your number one?

**STONE:** Camp evidence put you and Justin at the top, and from Week 2 on you were first in the receiver order every week. That's a fact about 2013. It isn't a promise about September. Football evidence moves roles. Nobody here is owed a target share, and nobody is owed your spot either.

**SHORTS:** And the draft. You're taking a receiver.

**STONE:** Dave runs the board and makes the picks. I'm not going to tell you a receiver won't be on it, because it isn't my decision. What doesn't change is what's written down: talent gets attention, execution earns responsibility, and roles move when the football evidence says they should. That cuts every way. You were a fourth-rounder and you finished this year first in the receiver order.

**SHORTS:** All right.

**STONE:** The offseason. None of this is required. The program can't start before April 21, and it's voluntary until minicamp. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me or with Darryl.

**SHORTS:** I'll be here when it opens.

**STONE:** Then here's what we'd offer.

**DRAKE:** The sheet first, when the rules let us show it to you. Dave's office tells us what we're allowed to send or do before April. Then in Phase Two, when we can coach on the field, you and the quarterbacks on adjustments against leverage, the way we did Stick and Drive last spring. I give you the look, you and the quarterback have to land on the same answer without me saying anything, and I retest it later when you're not expecting it.

**TICE:** Then in OTAs it goes into seven-on-seven against changing looks, where the quarterback has to see it too.

**STONE:** And Mike and I look at whether the season needs a standing period for that next to protection. That's a staff question, and I'll answer it before camp, not today.

**SHORTS:** Anything physical?

**STONE:** Nothing in the record. You were available every week. Anything from today's physical is the doctors' to tell you, not mine. The strength staff will have a plan for anybody who wants one when the program opens.

**SHORTS:** Okay.

**STONE:** Anything you need from us?

**SHORTS:** The London tape before April if the rules let you. And if my role changes, I want to hear it from you first.

**STONE:** The first, if the rules allow it, yes. The second: if the evidence moves your role, you'll hear it from me before you read it anywhere or see it on a sheet. That's how I'd do it for anybody. It isn't a promise about what the evidence will say.

**SHORTS:** Understood.

**STONE:** One more thing, and I say it to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason for anything away from football. You don't go through me and I don't need to know.

**SHORTS:** Got it.

**DRAKE:** I'll call you once we know what we can send.

**SHORTS:** Mornings are better.

**DRAKE:** Mornings.

## Development plan

- Interception review (voluntary to attend): the throws toward him that ended in interceptions (Week 1 Flowers, Week 3 Sherman, Week 8 Asomugha, Willis and Aldon Smith, Week 10 McCourty, Week 12 Reed) go on the quarterback staff's full-season sheet with Drake present, each classified by cause, caller contributions marked, and a correct rep paired where one exists. He and Cousins may watch them together if both want to. Delivered only when and as the CBA permits.
- Incompletion review: Week 8 (13 targets, 7 catches), Week 12 (17 targets, 10 catches, 61 yards) and Week 16 (12 targets, 4 catches) sorted by cause; nothing charged to the receiver by default. Built snap first, with the right rep beside it, which is how he says he learns fastest.
- Receiver and quarterback adjustment against leverage: Phase Two individual work at the permitted speed, then Phase Three seven-on-seven against changing looks, with an unprompted retest. Evidence: the OTA landmark work against changed leverage held through OTAs and minicamp; no in-season period for it is recorded, and he raises it as the gap.
- Keep: landmark and alignment reliability (OTAs, minicamp, camp), formation communication with the players around him (minicamp), ball security (no fumble). No new correction is assigned where the record shows correct work.
- Physical: nothing indicated in the record. Exit physical today with the medical staff; any finding, restriction or clearance is theirs. Strength and conditioning available from April 21, voluntary.
- All of the above is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; declining any of it carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
