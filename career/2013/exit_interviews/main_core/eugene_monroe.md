# Eugene Monroe, OT: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; George Yarno (offensive line); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Starting left tackle; on the game-day active list for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: 2009 first-round (#8) five-year rookie contract, final year 2013; pending unrestricted free agent, four accrued seasons (2009-2012) with 2013 accruing in the branch (contract status register). Age 26 (born April 18, 1987).

## The season in the record

Before the season. In his roster evaluation (written late January to February 2013) Stone listed Monroe in the offensive core and wrote "keep. A rookie tackle (if drafted) doesn't make Monroe expendable immediately; two good tackles is a real asset, not a redundancy." Caldwell agreed "that retaining Eugene Monroe while adding tackle talent is not inherently redundant." Stone's draft board recommended Lane Johnson "with Eugene Monroe staying at left tackle" and recorded "Do not sell Monroe merely because we draft a tackle" and "Drafting Johnson does not authorize a Monroe trade." Johnson was the #2 pick and began at right tackle; "Eugene Monroe remains at left tackle." Monroe's prior record in the initial roster: 2012, 16 games, 16 starts (Jacksonville finished 2012 at 2-14).

Spring and camp. The OTA, mandatory-minicamp and training-camp records do not name Monroe individually. At mandatory minicamp all controlled players attended and completed assigned work, and "the first offensive line completed the final changed-front and twist exchanges without rescue." In camp, "the first offensive line communicated better than it finished every physical rep," and the camp standouts file records the protection unit as: "Assignments were generally identified; leverage and physical finish produced sacks. Players recovered into reps." These are unit findings. The one individual note is Preseason Game 4 at Atlanta (August 29, won 33-27): "Johnson and Monroe identified their protection work but each lost leverage once and recovered into the play."

Regular season and postseason. The line was Monroe, Nwaneri, Meester, Rackley, Johnson in Weeks 1 and 2; Brewster took center from Week 3 (starter confirmed Week 6). At the bye the line was "locked as Monroe, Nwaneri, Brewster, Rackley, Johnson," and it was recorded unchanged through Week 17 and both playoff games. At the bye Monroe was one of eight players on the leadership council (with Cousins, Jones-Drew, Lewis, Posluszny, Daryl Smith, Lowery and Scobee) asked "what wastes practice time, what is unclear and what should not change." Their answers are not recorded.

Statistics. The receipts hold one individual line for him: sacks allowed, 2 in the regular season (Week 10 and Week 12) and 2 in the postseason (Divisional). The 2013 engine charged each sack allowed to a randomly drawn dressed lineman (`runtime/defect_register.md`), so these charges are not used as evidence about him below. Weekly-award shortlists: none. In-season honours: none. Receipt injuries: none.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Physical's today?

**MONROE:** After this.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. George coached you every day and Mike ran the protection week to week, so they're here. I'll give you what the record says about you, and I want your side. Start with yours. How do you see the year?

**MONROE:** Ten and six. A year ago we were two and fourteen. I played every game, we won in Kansas City, and we lost one in Nashville we were winning at halftime. That's the year. And it's the last year of my deal, so I spent a lot of it thinking about two things at once.

**STONE:** That's fair. We'll get to the second thing. I'm not going to dodge it, but I'll answer it inside what I'm allowed to answer. Football first?

**MONROE:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** The written record on you individually is short. I'll tell you why in a minute. Here's what's in it. You were on the active list for all sixteen and both playoff games at left tackle. From Week 3, when Brewster took center, the same five were the starting line every week, and at the bye I locked it. You were one of the eight we asked at the bye what wastes time, what's unclear and what shouldn't change. And there's one individual rep anybody wrote down about you, from Atlanta in August. George.

**YARNO:** [opens the Preseason Game 4 cutup] Here. You identify your work and you set to it. He gets leverage on you once. Then you recover and you're back in the play. Lane had the same kind of rep on the other side that night.

**MONROE:** I remember it. He won the leverage once and I got back into it.

**STONE:** That's the note: identified the work, lost leverage once, recovered into the play. Right assignment, one leverage loss, one rep in a preseason game. I'm not building a season on it.

**MONROE:** So after sixteen games the file on me is one preseason snap.

**STONE:** The written file, yes. Everything else we wrote down is about the five of you together. In camp the note was that the line communicated better than it finished. When the protection gave up sacks in camp, the assignments were generally identified and the losses were leverage and finish. In the season the line rows say "the main correction" in Weeks 1 and 2, "held" at Seattle, "the correction point" against San Diego. That's the unit. I can't hand you your own version of it off paper, and that's on us, not you.

**MONROE:** I noticed. Every Monday it was "protection." I never knew if that meant me.

**TICE:** At the bye we sorted every sack by cause. Physical loss, identification, communication, late pressure, overload, the quarterback holding it, route timing, the call against the look.

**MONROE:** I saw that as a group. I never saw mine.

**TICE:** Then you didn't get what you needed out of it.

**STONE:** Write that down, Mike. If a lineman goes half a season without knowing what the sort said about him, we taught the room and not the player. That's a teaching problem before it's anything else.

**MONROE:** While we're on paper. The sheet's got me for two sacks Saturday.

**STONE:** I've seen the column. I don't evaluate a lineman off it. It charges a name; it doesn't tell you what happened. Zach Brown got to Kirk twice. From the sheet I can't tell you whether that was your pickup, a back's, a tight end's or a call that didn't fit the look.

**MONROE:** I don't think either one was mine.

**STONE:** Then the tape will say so. Mike's going to sort Saturday's three the same way we did at the bye, and you'll get your answer, not a number.

**TICE:** With George. This month.

**STONE:** What I can say with the record behind it. We never had to plan around your availability, and the left side never changed. When I put help on the edge, like Houston against Watt, it was for both tackles by alignment; it wasn't a note about you. What was good you keep. The next step I'd point you to is the same thing the Atlanta rep shows and the same thing we wrote about the whole line in camp: after the assignment's right, does the leverage hold and does the rep finish. You identify. That isn't the question. I'm not going to put a Better or a Best on you that the file can't carry, and I'm not going to invent a flaw to make it sound like coaching.

**MONROE:** Fair enough. Can I say something about the calls?

**STONE:** That's why you're here.

**MONROE:** Houston, the first time. Maurice was 7.2 a carry and we threw it forty-five times.

**STONE:** Seventeen runs against forty-five dropbacks. That's mine. It's the first thing on the list we wrote before the rematch. Once we were behind I got away from the run, and the run was working.

**MONROE:** And Saturday. We had them 10-7 and ran it nineteen times all day.

**STONE:** Nineteen for seventy-two. After the touchdown, eight drives gained sixty-two yards. I'm not handing that to the line. We'll look at the calls, the looks and the execution separately.

**MONROE:** The one I keep seeing is at our own five. Three runs, six yards, punt. That's the five of us.

**STONE:** Maybe. It could also be three runs into a front that was waiting for them. I don't know yet, and I'm not letting you take it before we've looked.

**MONROE:** All right.

**STONE:** The council questions, for the whole season this time. What wasted your time?

**MONROE:** Some weeks we'd rep calls Tuesday and Wednesday that were gone by Friday. The Friday cut was right. Make it Wednesday. Linemen don't get those reps back.

**TICE:** Some of those we could have known early. The week before the first Tennessee game the rule was to cut any call that needed five-man protection when we had a 12 or 13 version. That's a Tuesday decision, not a Friday one.

**STONE:** Agreed. That's practice design, which is mine. What was unclear?

**MONROE:** By December, not much. In May there were a lot of new words between the backs, the tight ends and us. It got better. That same week before Tennessee, when you put a protection on every call before it went on the sheet, was the clearest we were all year. And we still gave up four and lost 41-11.

**STONE:** With six turnovers. That game wasn't the protection alone. What shouldn't change?

**MONROE:** The five. Keep five guys together as long as you can. And the heavy stuff. When we lined up in 12 and the extra-lineman sets and ran it, we were a different team. Kansas City.

**STONE:** A hundred seventy-nine rushing and one sack against a front that had thirty-four. That was the plan working.

**MONROE:** And one more on how you coached us. After Kansas City, Monday was one turnover and nothing made up. I liked that. No speech.

**STONE:** Good. What should I know about coaching you that we got wrong?

**MONROE:** Tell me when it's me. Don't wait for the group meeting to say "protection." And ask the tackles before you put help on us. Sometimes I want the chip. Sometimes the back's worth more in the route.

**STONE:** That's a fair thing for a tackle to want asked. The Houston plan put the help on both of you by alignment, and nothing in it shows we asked either of you first. George, Mike, asking goes into how we build help plans.

**YARNO:** Noted.

**MONROE:** Okay. The other thing, then. When you took Lane at two in the last year of my deal, I did the math like everybody else.

**STONE:** I know. You can have what's on paper from last spring. In the roster evaluation I wrote that a rookie tackle didn't make you expendable. On the draft board my recommendation was Lane with you staying at left tackle, and I wrote that drafting him didn't authorize a trade. That's spring 2013. It tells you nothing about March, and I don't want you to hear it that way.

**MONROE:** Then tell me about March. Do you want me back?

**STONE:** Here's what I can tell you. I'll give Dave my football evaluation of you. The contract, the tag, any of it, is his decision. I'm not going to tell you what's in my evaluation before he has it, and I'm not going to hint at it in here. If I told you something in this room and Dave did something different, I'd have made you a promise I can't keep.

**MONROE:** That's a no-answer.

**STONE:** It's the honest one. The dates are public. The franchise and transition window runs February 17 to March 3. The negotiating window with agents opens March 8, and the league year starts March 11. Anything about a contract is between your agent and Dave.

**MONROE:** He knows the dates. And Lane? Everybody assumes he goes to the left side.

**STONE:** Nobody's 2014 position has been decided, his, yours or anybody's. Lane was drafted to start on the right, won that job in camp and stayed there. What happens next year gets decided by next year's evidence.

**MONROE:** You say that like it's simple.

**STONE:** It isn't simple for you. It's the rule.

**MONROE:** [after a moment] So what do I do between now and March?

**STONE:** Whatever you choose. George has something for you either way.

**YARNO:** I'll cut every pass set of yours the sort touches, and the run reps we pulled for the line, each one tagged by cause, with a corrected rep next to it where we've got one. End of the month. It's yours to keep. Nobody's going to quiz you on it.

**MONROE:** I'd want it either way.

**STONE:** You'll get it either way. The technique piece I'd point you to is leverage and finish through the whole rep, after the identification. George's cutup will show whether it was feet, hands or base on each one. How you work on it is your call. There's no club program before April 21, and when there is one it's voluntary. I'm not going to call something voluntary and then count it. If you want the performance staff's view of what you're doing on your own, ask, and they'll give it inside the rules. If today's physical turns up anything, that belongs to the medical staff and they run it.

**MONROE:** I've got my own spring routine.

**STONE:** Then keep it. One more thing I say to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason: financial education, confidential counseling, family and life support. You don't have to tell me whether you use any of it.

**MONROE:** I know where it is.

**STONE:** Anything you need from us before you go?

**MONROE:** The Saturday sort. That's it.

**STONE:** Mike has it with George, this month. Go get your physical.

Delivered by January 31, 2014. No quiz or follow-up required.
- Divisional classification: the three Divisional sacks sorted by cause and his share, if any, given to him individually, replacing the charged-sack column as the answer to his question.
- Technique focus, his choice of method: leverage and physical finish held through the full rep after a correct identification, with the cutup showing whether footwork, hand placement or base caused each loss. Evidence: the Preseason Game 4 rep at Atlanta and the camp protection-unit finding ("leverage and physical finish produced sacks. Players recovered into reps"). No other individual technique finding exists in the record, and none is assumed.

- Medical: today's exit physical belongs to the medical staff; any diagnosis, restriction or plan is theirs.
- Support: Player Development resources remain available; no disclosure asked or recorded.
