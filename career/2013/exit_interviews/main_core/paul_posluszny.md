# Paul Posluszny, LB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Frank Bush (linebackers); Romeo Crennel (defensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Base linebacker; out since Week 13, with Russell Allen starting beside Daryl Smith from Week 14. Daryl Smith has been the defensive communication lead since Week 4, the game Posluszny missed, and kept it when Posluszny returned in Week 5. On the season-end depth chart the linebackers are listed Daryl Smith, Russell Allen, Julian Stanford, Sio Moore, Paul Posluszny; he was inactive for Weeks 14 to 17 and both playoff games. Availability on the day: out, head/neck, independent medical hold (Week 13), projected return April 5, 2014. The medical staff runs exit physicals today; no finding is recorded here. Contract: under contract; 2011 six-year veteran contract, final year 2016; accrued seasons 4+ (entered 2007), with 2013 accruing in the branch (on the 53 for 16 games; out from Week 13 on independent medical hold, never moved to a reserve list). Age 29 (born October 10, 1984).

## The season in the record

Before 2013 he was listed as Jacksonville's middle linebacker, No. 51, with an NFL experience entry of 5 in the January 2013 snapshot and a 2012 record of 16 games and 16 starts. In 2013 he dressed for 11 regular-season games: Weeks 1 to 3, 5 to 8 and 10 to 13. He missed Week 4 (upper extremity, Week 3 at Seattle; projected return October 2) and, after the Week 13 head/neck injury, Weeks 14 to 17 and both playoff games. Week 9 was the bye.

Regular season (game receipts): 101 tackles (72 solo, 29 assisted), 9 tackles for loss, 3 sacks, 6 pressures, 8 passes defended, 1 interception returned 13 yards; no forced fumble and no recovery. In the team statistics view he is second among Jacksonville linebackers in tackles (Daryl Smith 109 in 16 games) and first among them in passes defended (Smith 3, Allen 2).

Postseason: did not play (inactive, medical hold).

## Conversation

**STONE:** You're with the medical staff after this?

**POSLUSZNY:** After lunch.

**STONE:** Then I'll say this first. I'm not going to ask how you feel or what they tell you. That's yours and theirs. What the staff has is what they've given us: you're on a hold, and their projection is April 5. Whether that date holds or moves, and anything about clearance, is theirs, not mine. This is the football part. Frank coached you every day and Romeo called the defense, so they're here. Before I say anything, how do you see your year?

**POSLUSZNY:** Up to Cleveland I thought I played well, and we were a good defense most weeks. Then I finished the year on the inactive list for a playoff run, and I don't have a good word for that. There's also something from October I want to talk about before I leave.

**STONE:** The communication.

**POSLUSZNY:** Yes.

**STONE:** We'll get there. First what I'd keep, and where it comes from. The first OTA week, the notes say you stabilized the front and fit calls. The next week the other linebackers were finishing the base alignment before you confirmed it. By May 23 the unit was completing its echoes without you having to move anybody. At minicamp Romeo's calls ran with you directing traffic, and the standouts sheet has you and Daryl stabilizing the front, the fits and the second-level communication. In camp the base front and fits held behind you and Daryl, and again in the scrimmage.

**BUSH:** The line I remember from the OTA sheet is "less rescue." That's what you want from the man in the middle. The room works when he isn't fixing it.

**STONE:** On our scale, the Best description for a phase includes helping the man next to you without losing your own job. That is what the spring record shows in your communication: you did your job, and the people next to you became able to do theirs without you. It shows up as less rescue, not more.

**POSLUSZNY:** Okay.

**STONE:** The games. You dressed for eleven. A hundred and one tackles, seventy-two solo, nine for loss, three sacks, and eight passes defended, which is the most of any of our linebackers, plus the interception. Twelve tackles in your first game back at St. Louis. The 12-yard loss on Moreno at Denver. Sacks against San Diego, San Francisco in London, and Tennessee. At Nashville you picked Locker on their fourth snap and sacked him before Roy's safety. Twelve and two for loss at Houston. Thirteen at Cleveland. At the bye, the first-half keep list included the run defense, third-down defense and communication.

**POSLUSZNY:** You're going to get to Tennessee.

**STONE:** I am. First the part that's ours. After camp, nobody wrote an evaluation of you. The weekly sheets read the linebackers as a group: fits held, fits held late, fits held after the first quarter, third-down operation clean. Your name is next to a tackle number, and a tackle number isn't an evaluation, high or low. So I can't tell you today what your fits or your drops looked like snap by snap this year, because we didn't write it down. I can describe your spring from the record. I can't honestly do the same for your season from what we recorded. For the man who ran that room in May, that's thin, and it's on the staff.

**BUSH:** Whatever I told you in season, none of it is written anywhere you can go back to. That's mine.

**POSLUSZNY:** Then here's the one I want written. Tennessee in November. They ran for 210. Johnson had 126 on fourteen carries and went fifty-two on one of them. Locker scrambled for forty-one. I had three tackles. Everybody talked about the pick and the sack, and we lost 41-11. I want to know which of those runs went through me.

**STONE:** So do I, and I'm not going to answer it from memory. Three tackles doesn't tell me you were wrong. It might mean the ball went somewhere else. It might not.

**CRENNEL:** Houston belongs next to it. After that game, under what I'd alter first, I wrote down where the help goes against Andre Johnson and the fit on Foster's long runs, the 30 and the two 14s. The note doesn't say whose fit it was.

**STONE:** Then both go on one review: the long runs at Nashville and at Houston, sorted by cause. Alignment, key, fit, a block that beat us, a call that came in late, a call that was wrong for the look. Romeo's calls are on it and so is the practice week I built. Nothing is charged to you by default, and nothing is cleared by default.

**POSLUSZNY:** Put the Daniels touchdown on it. The plan that week had Daryl and me carrying him.

**STONE:** It goes on. The box has him three of six for 21 and the touchdown. It doesn't say who was on him.

**POSLUSZNY:** Fine.

**STONE:** October. Say it the way you want to.

**POSLUSZNY:** I missed one game. The week I was out, the sheet said Daryl had the communication for my absence only. I came back, and it stayed with Daryl. You told the press he ran it well, so he keeps it. I want to know if that was about Daryl or about me.

**STONE:** It was about the week, and it's the reason I gave the press. Daryl ran the communication well against Indianapolis, the operation held, and they ran for 65. I kept what was working, the same continuity call I'd made at center the week before, when Brewster stayed. Nothing in our record says your communication was a problem. The spring record says the opposite.

**POSLUSZNY:** Then I think it was the wrong call. I didn't lose it for anything I did. I lost it for being out ten days.

**STONE:** You can think that, and I'm not going to talk you out of it. It was my decision, and I'd want to look at it again against the tape before I told you it was right or wrong. What I will own is this. It was a change to a veteran's job, and what I wrote in the spring allows for more direct contact from me before a major role change. That's what this should have had.

**POSLUSZNY:** If something moves again, I want the reason from you, in a room, before anybody else hears it.

**STONE:** You'll get that. If the evidence moves your role, you hear it from me before you see it on a sheet.

**POSLUSZNY:** And next year. Who has it?

**STONE:** As of Saturday, Daryl has it. That's the record, not a decision about September. Next year the communication goes where the 2014 evidence puts it, like every job here, and for you that starts when the doctors clear you. I'm not going to hand it back because you had it in the spring, and I'm not going to keep it from you because you got hurt.

**CRENNEL:** How I teach the calls in the spring, and to whom, I'll bring to Alex before the program starts.

**POSLUSZNY:** And the base spot?

**STONE:** Same answer. When you went out, the line in my plan was: don't replace Paul, play your job. That was about them not trying to be you. Russell started next to Daryl from the second Houston game through Saturday. That's evidence for Russell, and I'll talk to Russell about Russell. Your 2013 is evidence for you. The injury isn't evidence of anything. A medical status isn't a grade. When you're cleared, you compete from what you show us in 2014. That isn't a promise either way.

**POSLUSZNY:** That's about what I expected.

**STONE:** It's what everybody hears, so nobody hears two versions.

**POSLUSZNY:** Then the contract. I'm twenty-nine, I'm signed through 2016, and I'm finishing the year on a hold. I'm not naive about how that looks upstairs. Does this change anything?

**STONE:** Contracts are Dave's. If you or your agent have a question about yours, it goes to him, and I won't guess at his answer. I'll give him my football evaluation of every player on the roster, you included. What I tell him stays between him and me, and that isn't a hint either way.

**POSLUSZNY:** A roster move because of the hold?

**STONE:** None has been made. Any roster transaction is Dave's too.

**POSLUSZNY:** You're going to draft a linebacker.

**STONE:** The draft is Dave's call. I'm not going to discuss the board in here. That isn't a hint either.

**POSLUSZNY:** Understood.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**POSLUSZNY:** Spring worked. Romeo kept it small. When the pressure broke, he took the dressing off and walked it again instead of adding another one. Veterans like that. What didn't: in season, what I saw about myself was a tackle number and a line about the group. I never got my own tape with somebody telling me what was right and what wasn't. And October.

**BUSH:** The tape part is mine.

**STONE:** What should I know about coaching you that I don't?

**POSLUSZNY:** That I don't need to be the one talking. I need to know it's decided on football, and I need to hear it first. And use us. At the bye you asked the council what wastes time, what's unclear and what shouldn't change. I don't remember being asked again.

**STONE:** How often the council gets asked in season is mine to decide, and I'll decide it before camp. Answer them now, for your room.

**POSLUSZNY:** Wastes time: not much. Unclear: who owns the call when there are two of us on the field who can make it. Shouldn't change: keep the menu small.

**CRENNEL:** The second one goes into what I bring to Alex.

**STONE:** The offseason. Your rehab is the medical and training staff's plan. What you do and when is theirs, and they clear you, not us. Everything football from us is voluntary. The program can't start before April 21, and it stays voluntary until minicamp. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me, Romeo or Frank.

**POSLUSZNY:** I'll do whatever the trainers give me. What I don't want is to come back in April a stranger to whatever the defense changed.

**BUSH:** Then here's what I'd offer. A written evaluation of your 2013 from tape, by week: fits, keys, drops, communication. If you got beat physically, that's written separately from an assignment. Correct snaps are written down as correct. The Nashville and Houston runs and the Daniels touchdown go with it. It goes in your hand, when Dave's office tells me what I can send and when.

**CRENNEL:** And the run-defense self-scout, when we're allowed to have you in the room and the medical staff is fine with it. I'd rather have your eyes on it than not, including the weeks you weren't out there.

**POSLUSZNY:** Buffalo. Two thirty-seven. People are going to say that was because I was out.

**STONE:** Our own correction list wrote it as "run defense without Posluszny." That names an absence, not a cause. I don't know that it was because you were out, and leaving it that way is unfair to Russell, to Julian and to you. Buffalo goes on Romeo's review by cause like everything else.

**POSLUSZNY:** Good. I'll sit in on it.

**STONE:** On the physical side, the strength staff builds a plan inside whatever restrictions the medical staff sets, when they allow it. They don't clear you either. The one thing I ask about health is what I ask of everybody: if a plan isn't tolerable or something is getting worse, tell them early.

**POSLUSZNY:** I will.

**STONE:** And what I say to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason for anything away from football. You don't go through me and I don't need to know.

**POSLUSZNY:** Understood.

**STONE:** Anything else you need from us?

**POSLUSZNY:** The tape. The reason first, next time. A seat in the room when I'm allowed one.

**STONE:** The tape and the room when the rules allow. The reason first, yes.

**BUSH:** I'll call you about the tape either way, and send it once Dave's office tells me what I can. Today after your appointment, or tomorrow?

**POSLUSZNY:** Tomorrow.

## Development plan

- Medical first: rehabilitation, restrictions and clearance belong to the medical and athletic-training staff. The April 5, 2014 date is their projection and can move. No football activity beyond what they permit. Stone's only health expectation is the standard one: honest reporting, required treatment, and telling the medical staff early if a plan is not tolerable (readiness standard section 1, "Availability and Medical Honesty").
- Written 2013 evaluation (Bush; voluntary to receive): his fits, keys, coverage drops and communication by week from tape, with assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing and medical limitation kept separate. Correct snaps recorded as correct. Delivered to him individually, only when and as the CBA permits. Evidence: after camp the weekly records hold unit reads ("Fits held," "Third-down operation clean") and his statistical lines only.
- Long-run review by cause (Crennel and Bush): Week 10 at Tennessee (210 rushing; Johnson's 52-yard touchdown run; Locker's 41-yard scramble) and Week 12 at Houston (Foster's 30, 14 and 14 from the rematch notes), plus Owen Daniels's Week 12 touchdown. Staff calls and the practice week are on the review; nothing is charged to him or cleared by default. He asked for the answer.
- Run-defense self-scout (Crennel; voluntary; when the CBA permits and the medical staff agrees): Posluszny invited as a contributor, including Weeks 14 to 17 and the playoffs and Week 15 against Buffalo (237 rushing). This is staff input only. It is not an evaluation of him, Allen or Stanford.
- Staying current during the hold: film and meeting access when the CBA and the medical staff allow, so that any 2014 change to the defense is not new to him in April. Voluntary.
- Spring 2014, if the medical staff clears him (the program opens no earlier than April 21; Phase One is strength, conditioning and physical rehabilitation; Phase Two adds permitted individual and group instruction at walkthrough speed): individual work only on corrections the tape review establishes, retested later without a prompt. If the tape shows correct work, nothing is added. How the defensive communication is taught is Crennel's proposal to Stone, and no role follows from it.
- Keep: front and fit communication that lets the unit operate with less rescue (OTAs, minicamp, camp); ball production (8 passes defended, the Week 10 interception).
- Physical: strength and performance plan inside the medical staff's restrictions, when they allow it. Voluntary.
- Everything above except the medical plan is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; treatment and rehabilitation are the medical and athletic-training staff's. Declining any voluntary item carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
