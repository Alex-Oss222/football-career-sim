# Maurice Jones-Drew, RB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Tim Spencer (running backs); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Lead back, ahead of Jonathan Grimes (RB2) and C.J. Anderson (RB3). Active 53, available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: 2009 veteran extension, final year 2013; pending unrestricted free agent, 7 accrued seasons before 2013 (2006-2012; 2012 on Reserve/Injured, which is full pay), with 2013 accruing in the branch (on the 53 for 16 games). Age 28 (born March 23, 1985).

## The season in the record

Jones-Drew dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both postseason games and was the lead back all season (weekly personnel records, the bye-week second-half plan and the Week 11 decision). Regular season: 282 carries, 1,334 yards, 4.7 a carry, 8 rushing touchdowns, longest run 35; 67 targets, 42 catches, 400 yards, 4 receiving touchdowns, longest catch 37; 2 fumbles, both lost. He also returned 2 punts for 31 yards and 1 kickoff for 18. Postseason: 35 carries, 186 yards, 1 rushing touchdown; 2 targets, 1 catch for 5; 1 fumble, lost. No receipt injury. Weekly-award shortlist: Week 11, AFC Offensive Player, second on the shortlist; Andrew Luck was drawn. Before this staff arrived: 2012, 6 games and 5 starts, on reserve/injured at season close; the pre-hire market file notes the publicly documented 2012 holdout and an injury-shortened season.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Sit down. Medical has your exit physical after this. Whatever they find is between you and them, and we hear what football needs, nothing more. This is the review of your year. I'll go through what our record shows, then I want yours, then we'll talk about what comes next as far as I can talk about it.

**JONES-DREW:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** You dressed for all sixteen and both playoff games, and you were the lead back in every one. 1,334 yards on 282 carries, eight rushing touchdowns, 42 catches for 400 and four more touchdowns. A year after your season ended on reserve, you didn't miss a game.

**JONES-DREW:** Last spring people had me finished. I'd like that written down somewhere.

**STONE:** It's in the receipts. The totals don't coach anybody, though, so here's the detail. The runs that produced were the core ones. Denver, second half: 13, 7, 13, 7 and 10 on Counter, 6OL Power, Split, Inside and 6OL Power again. Arizona, 25 for 191. Houston at home, 11 and 12 on 6OL Power, then 12 on 6OL Counter and the Counter touchdown. The Split for 33 on the second snap in Kansas City. And Cleveland in overtime: 12 on 22 Heavy Power, 8 on Counter, and Josh kicks it.

**JONES-DREW:** Cleveland was fun.

**STONE:** In the pass game you were more than the checkdown. The 37-yard touchdown on Drive in St. Louis. The Stick with the chip release against San Diego. The 17-yarder against Houston. Six catches for 76 and the Texas touchdown against Buffalo.

**JONES-DREW:** Buffalo nobody remembers, because of the score.

**STONE:** I remember it. Now the part nobody sees. June 12 was the day we changed the pictures on you, and you gave us the most dependable veteran run-track and protection communication we had. In camp you were dependable in track, protection and ball security. In our language that's Better for those phases: you held it when the look changed.

**JONES-DREW:** Okay.

**STONE:** Here's what didn't close. Ball security. Seattle and Tennessee in the season, then Kansas City. Camp said dependable. The games didn't hold all the way to that.

**JONES-DREW:** I know. You said it at the podium in Kansas City.

**STONE:** I did.

**JONES-DREW:** You weren't wrong. I'd just rather have heard it in here first. Week 1, they asked you whether Jonathan's yards changed the rotation, and the first thing you said was that I was the lead back. That I appreciated. The fumble I'd have taken in this room.

**STONE:** That's fair. I answered a direct question with the football fact, not a judgment about you. But our standard is that the correction belongs to the team before it belongs to the public, and the first place that one got said was the podium. That one's mine.

**JONES-DREW:** Okay. On the fumbles themselves. Wagner, Verner, DeVito. Those guys take the ball for a living. I'm not making an excuse. I'm telling you they took it. I didn't drop it.

**STONE:** The records have all three as forced, so I'm not arguing that. What they don't say is how the ball came out. Carriage, the contact, a second man, what we called and who we left for you. Each one got a coaching label on the Monday after. Seattle with the other mistakes from that game, Tennessee with the other five turnovers, Kansas City before Nashville. None of those labels got written into the record. So I'm not going to tell you it was technique when I can't show you. Tim, I want the three of them on one tape.

**SPENCER:** I can have it this week. The Nashville ball-security periods had the Kansas City one in them. I want all three next to each other, and I want to go through them with you, not send you a verdict.

**JONES-DREW:** Fine. If it's me, show me and I'll fix it.

**STONE:** And if it's us, we fix that. Second item, and it isn't only yours, so I'm not going to pretend it is. San Diego, 3.3 a carry, and it's on our correction list by your name. Tennessee in November, 15 for 40. Nashville, 13 for 42, and the early-down run game is on the correction list for that game. None of those is classified by cause in writing. Blocking, track, read, the call. George and Tim will go through the runs from San Diego and Nashville and tell both of us what they were.

**JONES-DREW:** Nashville I didn't have it. I'll say that. But I'm going to push on Houston in November. Twelve carries, 86 yards. We threw it all day.

**STONE:** Forty-five dropbacks, sacks included, and seventeen runs. It's on the correction list for that game in those words. The plan that week said make Houston stop the run before we expanded the pass. They didn't stop it, and we expanded anyway. The score explains part of the second half; we were down 21-6 and then 28-6. It doesn't explain all of it, and the correction list doesn't pretend it does. Those calls were mine.

**TICE:** I'll chart the call mix in every game where the run was producing and we moved off it: the score, the clock, and what the caller's rules said at that point. Alex already has his own calls in the self-scout. This goes into that.

**JONES-DREW:** That's all I want. When it's working, let it work.

**STONE:** And when it isn't working, I'll call something else, and that won't be personal either.

**JONES-DREW:** I know.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't? You were on the council at the bye, so you've been asked before. Be as blunt as you want.

**JONES-DREW:** What worked: Friday. Throwing out what we couldn't run. I've been in rooms where Friday was when they added stuff. And the 6OL. When Bradfield came in, they knew what was coming and it still went.

**STONE:** And what didn't?

**JONES-DREW:** The rotation. Not the idea. I'm fine with no quota, and Jonathan earned his. But some weeks I didn't know which calls were his until the call came in. Wham was his, and then Tennessee at home I got one too. I'd like that clear on Wednesday, not on Sunday.

**STONE:** If you found out on Sunday, that's a communication failure, and it's ours before it's yours. Tim?

**SPENCER:** The rotation went by concept from the opener on. Whether you had it clearly by Wednesday is a fair question. I'll go back through what the backs were actually given each week and when, and show you.

**STONE:** What do we get wrong about you?

**JONES-DREW:** That I don't need the individual work because I'm the old guy in the room. I'd have taken those fumbles on one tape in November. Nobody offered, and I didn't ask. That's on both of us.

**STONE:** That's a fair hit. A problem that shows up in September and again in November is exactly what the individual cutup is for, and we didn't build you one. When the same thing keeps coming back, we're supposed to ask whether the teaching is the problem before we ask whether the player is. And one more gap while we're on it: our weekly records say what you gained. They don't carry a written evaluation of your protection during the season. I'm not going to call your protection Better from September on when nobody wrote it down.

**SPENCER:** I've got the tape. I'll write it up.

**JONES-DREW:** Good. Now the real one. My deal's up. Do you want me back?

**STONE:** Here's exactly what I can say. Dave Caldwell makes the contract decisions. I'll give him my football evaluation of you, and it'll be the one you just heard, the Arizona game and the three fumbles both. What I recommend to him stays between him and me, and I'm not going to hint at it in either direction.

**JONES-DREW:** You know what they'll say. Back turns 29, coming off a big year, don't pay him.

**STONE:** I'm not going to talk about the market. That's your agent and Dave. What I can tell you is what the tape says about 2013, and I've told you.

**JONES-DREW:** Last time my contract came up here, it turned into a story I didn't want. I'm not doing that again. My agent will talk to Dave, and I'm going to see what's out there when it opens.

**STONE:** That's between you, your agent and Dave. I'm not going to steer you either way.

**JONES-DREW:** If I'm back, am I still the guy?

**STONE:** Every role here moves on evidence. We don't protect a job because somebody's been here a long time, and we don't give one to somebody because he's young. 2014 roles get decided on 2014 work, and none of it exists yet. That isn't me telling you there's a competition, and it isn't me telling you there isn't one.

**JONES-DREW:** You drafting a back?

**STONE:** The draft is Dave's, and I don't talk about the board with players. It wouldn't tell you anything about you anyway.

**JONES-DREW:** Can you at least tell Dave I'd like to hear something early? One way or the other.

**STONE:** I'll tell him you asked. When he answers is his decision.

**JONES-DREW:** Okay.

**STONE:** Development. None of this is required. Your contract is Dave's side. The league year opens March 11, and our program can't start before April 21. Before it, and in any voluntary part of it, nothing is required, and whatever you decide doesn't cost you anything here. So this is what I'd work on this spring, on your own schedule.

**JONES-DREW:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** One, ball security, but only after the tape. If the three come out as carriage, that's a drill you can do anywhere. If they come out as contact or as something we did, the work is different, and I'm not going to guess which before we see it. Two, keep what's already Better: the run track and the protection communication from June and camp. Three, the body. Your own program. If you want an individual recommendation from the performance staff, ask, and they'll put one together. Four, anything medical belongs to the medical staff, starting with today.

**SPENCER:** You'll get the fumble tape and the protection write-up from me by the end of the week, and the rotation answer with them.

**STONE:** What else do you need from us?

**JONES-DREW:** Those three things. And send them to me directly. I won't be in the building much.

**SPENCER:** I'll send them.

**STONE:** One more thing, and it's not a question. Player Development is there for anything off the field. It's confidential and it doesn't go on any report. I'm not asking whether you use it.

**JONES-DREW:** Understood.

**SPENCER:** Medical's expecting you.

## Development plan

All of it is voluntary. Jones-Drew's register status is pending unrestricted free agent (final contract year 2013); the 2014 league year opens on March 11; Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and the club cannot require voluntary offseason work before the program or in its voluntary phases. Nothing here assumes where he plays in 2014.

- Ball security (the open item): review the three lost fumbles on one cutup with Spencer (Week 3 at Seattle, Power R, Wagner; Week 10 at Tennessee, Verner, recovered at the Jacksonville 14; Wild Card at Kansas City, the Access call, DeVito). Classify each by cause before choosing any work. Carriage work is self-directed and can be done anywhere; a contact or call cause changes the work. Evidence: camp record "dependable in track, protection and ball security" against the game record of two lost in the regular season and one in the postseason.
- Retain what is already Better: run-track and protection communication against changed pictures (minicamp June 12; camp track, protection and ball-security periods).
- Receiving work to retain: Drive, the chip-release Stick, Texas and the Sprint Flood checkdown (Weeks 5, 7, 15 and the Wild Card).
- Run efficiency: no individual work assigned until Spencer and Yarno classify the runs from Week 7 (3.3 a carry) and the Divisional (3.2 a carry) by cause. Both correction items carry his average, but neither record classifies the cause.
- Physical: his own offseason program; an individualized recommendation from the performance staff is available on request.
- Medical: exit physical with the medical staff on January 13. Any finding, restriction or clearance belongs to the medical staff.
