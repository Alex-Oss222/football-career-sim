# Sen'Derrick Marks, DT: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Anthony Pleasant (defensive line); Romeo Crennel (defensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** Starting defensive tackle (Miller/Marks inside); dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today, and no finding from it is recorded here. Contract: branch free-agent signing, one year ($1.50M), final year 2013; pending unrestricted free agent, 4+ accrued seasons (entered 2009; his 2009 Tennessee four-year rookie contract expired after 2012 and he signed with Jacksonville from outside), 2013 accruing in the branch (contract status register). Age 26 (born February 23, 1987).

## The season in the record

Before the season. The free-agency board listed Marks as a value pursuit: "The attraction is age, movement and a chance to buy a developmental interior player without paying established-star money." Preferred structure: "one-year prove-it contract with limited guaranteed exposure," with the note "This is not a prediction of Marks' future performance." He signed for one year and $1.5M; "Jacksonville offered a clear interior-rotation opportunity without a starting guarantee, while the short structure preserved Marks's chance to return to market."

Spring and camp. The OTA and mandatory-minicamp records do not name him individually; at mandatory minicamp "All controlled players attended and completed assigned work." In the first physical camp work (July 26-29), "the defense's physical work showed Miller and Marks as the most stable interior run combination, with Alualu rotating across the front." The camp pass-rush note was Babin's ("the leading edge-rush pressure"). At the August 3 stadium scrimmage, "Crennel's first defense was sound against the run when the front call arrived early. Posluszny and Smith stabilized fits; Miller/Marks held the interior." Miller/Marks then led the interior rotation, and at camp close "Crennel retained Miller/Marks inside" (position battles: "Miller/Marks and Posluszny/Smith stabilize"). The four preseason game records do not name him.

Regular season and postseason. Miller and Marks started inside all year with Alualu rotating; Mosley, unavailable before the bye, "dressed and joined the interior rotation with Marks, Miller and Alualu" in Week 11. Crennel called the defense. From Week 8 on, Stone's recorded directives to Crennel put the run fit or quarterback containment ahead of the rush in most weeks: Week 8 "fit Gore before chasing Kaepernick"; Week 12 "Foster first"; Week 13 "fit Richardson before rushing past the run"; Week 15 "rush lanes against a mobile Manuel"; Week 16 "Johnson's run fit before the rush plan; contain Locker"; Week 17 "fit the back before chasing Luck; keep Luck inside the rush" and "finish rushes"; Wild Card "keep Smith inside the rush"; Divisional "keep Locker inside the rush." Week 10's directive included "keep finishing the rush." The bye review's defensive correction was "More disruption without losing structure: rush finish, tackling through the ball," and the second-half line was "keep the structure, increase the finish." Posluszny was injured in Week 13; Russell Allen started beside Daryl Smith from Week 14, with Smith the communication lead, as he had been since Week 4.

- Postseason, 2 games: 13 tackles (9 solo, 4 assisted), 1 sack, 2 tackles for loss, 3 pressures.

## Conversation

**STONE:** You've got the physical after this?

**MARKS:** Right after.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. Anthony coached you every day and Romeo called every snap you played, so they're both here. I'll give you what the record says, and I want yours.

**MARKS:** Can I ask the one thing first, so I'm not sitting here waiting on it? I came here on one year. It's up. Are you bringing me back?

**STONE:** I'll answer it, straight, inside what I'm allowed to answer. The answer's short, and if I give it to you first it eats everything else. Football first, then that. Deal?

**MARKS:** Go.

**STONE:** Before free agency we wrote you down as a value pursuit. One year, prove-it, a rotation opportunity and no starting guarantee, and a note that it wasn't a prediction about you. Then camp. First days of physical work, the note is that you and Roy were the most stable interior run combination we had. August 3, the scrimmage: the defense was sound against the run when the front call got there early, and you and Roy held the interior. Romeo kept you inside at the end of camp. You started every game from Kansas City in Week 1 to Saturday in Nashville. Nothing on any injury sheet.

**MARKS:** That was the plan. Show up and play.

**STONE:** In our language, camp in the run fit was Good without any question. "Stable" through the physical weeks is part of Better, the part where the technique holds rep after rep. The rest of Better, holding a correction, handling a changed look, I can't give you off paper, because nobody wrote your corrections down. And I'm not putting a word on your rush for camp. The camp rush note was Babin's.

**MARKS:** Maybe nobody wrote them down because there weren't many.

**PLEASANT:** Maybe. Or because we wrote about the room and not about you. I can't show you which, and that's mine.

**STONE:** Now the season, and the sacks first, because they mattered. Oakland, fourth quarter, they punt three straight times and one of them comes after you get Pryor. Seattle had just hit Lynch for forty-six, you sacked Wilson on the same drive, and they came away with nothing. Denver, three, and the second and third forced the punt before our second touchdown drive of the fourth quarter. They went 0 for 13 on third down that day.

**MARKS:** Denver's the best game I've played.

**STONE:** Houston away, their first possession, you sacked Schaub and they punted from their 16. The rematch notes say that possession and Grimes's interception were the only two times we made Houston change anything. Indianapolis, their first snap after our field goal, you sacked Luck and knocked it loose at their 8. Kansas City, the sack on Smith before the half and two tackles for loss. Eight in the regular season. The whole defense had twenty-three.

**MARKS:** Eight of twenty-three from a tackle on a one-year deal.

**STONE:** I've read the same sheet. Here's the rest of it. Before the bye you've got pressures in every game except Indianapolis and St. Louis. After the bye, one at Tennessee, one at Houston, one at Indianapolis, and none in the other five. Then three in the two playoff games. I'm not telling you what that means, because I don't know yet. It's the one thing on your sheet I want answered before anybody treats it as a verdict.

**MARKS:** I'll tell you what it means. Read what you asked us to do. From London on it was fit Gore before you chase Kaepernick. Foster first. Fit Richardson before you rush past the run. Johnson's fit before the rush plan. Fit the back before chasing Luck, keep him inside the rush. Keep Locker inside the rush. Every week the rush came second. I played it the way it was called.

**CRENNEL:** The directives were his. The calls under them were mine. Against Johnson and Foster I'd call most of them again. The fit comes first against those backs.

**STONE:** They were my directives and I think the order was right for the defense. It still means your pressures after the bye can't be read without them. Tennessee here, twenty runs for seventy-seven. Indianapolis, forty-nine. Kansas City, forty-seven. You were in the middle of all three. If the fit working cost you pressures, that's a price we chose. It isn't a hole in your rush.

**MARKS:** That's all I'm saying.

**STONE:** I'm not going to give you the whole thing either. The Week 17 directive also said finish rushes, and that's the week you got the strip. Week 1 you had two pressures and we had no sack. San Diego, two pressures and no sack from you. At the bye we put rush finish on the whole defense. So there are two questions, and they're different. How many of the rushes after the bye were lanes you held because we asked, and how many were rushes that didn't win or didn't finish. I don't know the split. Neither do you.

**MARKS:** I know how many won.

**PLEASANT:** Then it'll be quick to show. I'll pull every rush rep you had and sort them: lane held on the call, won and finished, won and didn't finish, lost, call against the look. That week's directive goes on top of each game.

**MARKS:** Before the bye too. Same sort. Otherwise you're comparing two different things.

**PLEASANT:** Before the bye too.

**STONE:** That's the right way to do it. Now the run. Five games have a run note in the file. Tennessee away, the fourth quarter: 210 for the game and Johnson's fifty-two-yard touchdown. Houston away, Foster twenty-nine carries for 133, with runs of thirty, fourteen and fourteen. Cleveland, Richardson's 129. Buffalo, 237 at 7.2 a carry without Paul. Saturday, Johnson twenty-four for 103, and they had the ball 37:06. None of those notes has your name on it, and I'm not going to write it in today when the file doesn't. They belong to the defense, and you're in the middle of the defense. On the other side of it, Arizona: three tackles for loss and they ran for seventy-six.

**MARKS:** Tennessee away, fourth quarter, I'm not going to argue. That was bad and I was out there for it. Buffalo I want to say something about.

**STONE:** Say it.

**MARKS:** After Poz went down, the call got to us later. Not every snap. Enough of them. Daryl's doing his job and half of Poz's, and when the front call's late the tackles pay first, because we're closest to the ball. You had that in camp. You wrote it down in camp.

**CRENNEL:** We did. Sound against the run when the front call arrived early. That was August.

**MARKS:** And it came back in December.

**CRENNEL:** Maybe. Daryl had the communication from Week 4 on, with Paul beside him and after Paul went down. Whether the call got to the line later once Allen was in there, I can check. The snap clock's on the tape.

**STONE:** Check it. If the call was late, that's on the staff, not on Daryl and not on the tackles. We reshaped the base defense on a short week for Houston. Houston says it held that night: Foster at 2.4 a carry. Buffalo says something else. I'd rather find out we asked for the change too fast than decide a lineman missed a fit.

**MARKS:** And Saturday. We were out there thirty-seven minutes. Johnson was still getting nine and ten on the last drive.

**STONE:** He was. And on offense we went one for nine on third down. A defense doesn't face seventy-seven plays without the offense giving the ball back that fast. That part's mine before it's yours.

**MARKS:** I'm not putting it on Kirk.

**STONE:** I didn't say Kirk. I call it. What worked in how we coached you?

**MARKS:** I knew what we were taking away every week. Romeo said it early in the week and it was the same thing on game day. And Anthony didn't try to rebuild me. I've been doing this five years.

**STONE:** What didn't?

**MARKS:** "Finish." After the bye it was finish, finish the rush, every day. It's a word. Show me mine. Show me the ones I should've had.

**PLEASANT:** That's fair. The bye cutup was every twenty-yard play we gave up. It wasn't a rush cutup, and I didn't build you one.

**STONE:** Then write that one down as ours. If we put "finish" on the board for the whole defense and never hand a rusher his own reps, that's a teaching failure before it's a rush problem. What should I know about coaching you that we got wrong?

**MARKS:** Tell me what the rush is for that week. Not only the fit. If I'm holding a lane so Locker can't step up, say so. And when we look at the rush on Monday, look at it against that.

**CRENNEL:** That's reasonable. We wrote down what we were stopping every week. We never wrote down what we were asking the rush to give up to do it.

**STONE:** Then it goes on the sheet. Now your question.

**MARKS:** Same question. Are you bringing me back?

**STONE:** I'll give Dave my football evaluation of you, built on the same file you've heard in here: Denver, Indianapolis, the pressures after the bye, the run games. Whether there's a contract and what it looks like is Dave's decision. I'm not going to tell you what I recommend, and I'm not going to hint at it. If I said something in this room and Dave did something else, I'd have made you a promise I can't keep.

**MARKS:** You signed me to prove something. Did I?

**STONE:** You started every game on a deal that offered you a rotation. That's in the record and nobody takes it out. Whether it proves what a contract needs it to prove is a contract question, and that one isn't mine.

**MARKS:** That's a no-answer.

**STONE:** It's the true one. I'd rather give you that than a nice one that doesn't hold up in March.

**MARKS:** My agent's going to talk to Caldwell. And when the window opens he's going to talk to everybody else.

**STONE:** That's his job, and it's your right. The dates are public. The window with agents opens March 8 at noon, and the league year starts March 11. Until then your agent talks to Dave.

**MARKS:** And if I'm back, am I starting?

**STONE:** Nobody's 2014 job is set. Not yours, not Roy's, not Mosley's. You started this year because camp put you there and the games kept you there. Next year it's the same process with new evidence.

**MARKS:** Even after this year.

**STONE:** This year counts as evidence. It doesn't count as a guarantee. Nobody in the building has one, and I'd be lying to you if I said otherwise.

**MARKS:** [after a moment] I figured. I wanted to hear you say it.

**STONE:** Between now and March, what you do is your business. Anthony's cutup comes to you either way.

**PLEASANT:** End of the month. Every rush rep of the year, before the bye and after, sorted the way I said, with the week's directive on top. Then the run reps from Tennessee away, Houston away, Cleveland, Buffalo and Saturday where you were in the fit, tagged by what went wrong, whether it was the call, the communication, the technique or a physical loss, with a clean rep beside it where we have one. It's yours to keep. Nobody's going to ask you about it.

**MARKS:** Put Denver in it.

**PLEASANT:** It's in there.

**STONE:** The technique I'd point you to is the finish, but only after the sort. If it comes back that the lanes were the call, there's nothing to fix in your rush, and I'll tell you that. If it comes back as rushes that got there and didn't finish, that's work you can do anywhere, and the cutup will show you which ones. How you do it is up to you. There's no club program before April 21, and when there is one, it's voluntary. I'm not going to call something voluntary and then count it.

**MARKS:** I train with my own people in the spring.

**STONE:** Then keep doing it. If you want the performance staff's read on anything, ask; it's there inside the rules. If the physical turns anything up, the medical staff runs it, not me. And one thing I say to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason: financial education, confidential counseling, family support. You don't have to tell me whether you use any of it. Anything else you need from us before you go?

**MARKS:** The cutup. And Romeo's answer on the calls after Poz. I want to know if I'm right.

**CRENNEL:** You'll have it this month, with Frank's help on the linebacker side.

**STONE:** Go get your physical.

## Development plan

- Film, voluntary and his to keep: Pleasant's individual cutup of every rush rep of the 2013 season and both playoff games, before and after the bye, sorted as lane held on the call, won and finished, won and not finished, lost, or call against the look, with that week's recorded directive to Crennel at the top of each game. Denver (Week 6) included. Delivered by January 31, 2014. No quiz or follow-up required.
- Run-fit reps, same cutup: his reps in the fit from Week 10 (fourth quarter), Week 12, Week 13, Week 15 and the Divisional game, each tagged by the state/05 execution categories (assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing, medical limitation, coaching or teaching failure), with a corrected rep beside it where one exists. No individual fault is assumed before the tape is classified; the weekly records assign these corrections to the unit.
- Technique focus, only after the sort: rush finish, if the sort shows rushes that won and did not finish. Evidence behind the question: Week 1 (two pressures, no team sack), Week 7 (two pressures, no sack of his), the bye correction "rush finish," and the post-bye pressure lines. If the sort shows lanes held on the call, no rush correction is assigned and Stone says so.
- Keep: the interior run fit. Evidence naming him: camp "most stable interior run combination" and "Miller/Marks held the interior" at the August 3 scrimmage; his three tackles for loss in Week 11. Unit results, which like the run corrections belong to the whole defense: 76 rushing yards allowed in Week 11, and 77, 49 and 47 in Weeks 16 and 17 and the Wild Card.
- Physical: his own spring routine. The performance staff's input is available on request within CBA limits. Nothing is required. Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21 and is voluntary; declining it costs nothing (readiness standard section 7). No role is attached to any of this.
- Medical: today's exit physical belongs to the medical staff; any diagnosis, restriction or plan is theirs.
- Support: Player Development resources (financial education, confidential counseling, family support) remain available; no disclosure asked or recorded.
