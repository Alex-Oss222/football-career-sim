# Adam Thielen, WR: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Darryl Drake (wide receivers). Alan Lowry, the special teams coordinator who directed the kicking game in 2013, left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach and was not present (ledger Entry 75); the coordinator job is vacant, and Stone directs the kicking game until it is filled.
**Status going in:** WR2 and the H (movable receiver), with coverage-unit jobs; second on the receiver depth chart at season end (Shorts, Thielen, Blackmon, Clemons, Brown). Available, no communicated restriction: cleared from his Wild Card minor injury when his projected return of January 6 was reached; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract; branch undrafted-free-agent minimum contract, three years, final year 2015; no accrued seasons through 2012, with 2013 accruing in the branch (on the 53 for 16 games). Age 23 (born August 22, 1990). Signed after the 2013 draft from Minnesota State.

## The season in the record

Before the draft Jacksonville's board carried him as a "low-confidence regional follow" with "no actionable draft grade." The signing record says he was added for receiver and special-teams competition, with "no roster role, target share, or return job promised." Camp made him WR3 (the August 24 roster decision) and a core special-teams player. When Blackmon's four-game suspension began after Week 1 he moved to WR2 (Shorts, Thielen, Clemons, Brown); from Week 8 the order was Shorts, Thielen (WR2/H), Blackmon (outside Z), Clemons, Brown. He dressed for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games.

Regular season (game receipts): 84 targets, 52 receptions, 616 yards, 3 touchdowns; 11.8 yards a catch and a longest reception of 50 (team statistics view); 1 rush for 3 yards; 1 punt return for 3 yards and 1 kick return for 22; no fumble.

Postseason: 11 targets, 6 receptions, 40 yards, no touchdown; 1 punt return for 9 yards and 1 kick return for 24; 1 fumble, lost.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Physical today?

**THIELEN:** After lunch.

**STONE:** Whatever comes out of it is between you and the doctors. This is the football. Darryl had your routes all year, so he's here. Alan had you on his units, and he isn't. He left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach, and the special teams coordinator job is open. Before I say anything, how do you see your year?

**THIELEN:** Honestly? Saturday. Fourth quarter, we're down four, I catch a six-yard ball and it's gone, and they kick a field goal off it. That's what I see.

**STONE:** We'll do Saturday properly. I'm not starting there, because it isn't the year. Last April you were a regional name on our board with no draft grade. When Dave signed you the note said no roster role, no target share, no return job promised. At rookie minicamp you lined up right on every split we'd taught and could tell us the Stick job. One controlled press look disrupted your release; you changed the release plan on the next rep and kept the landmark. That's Good, and Good is what a rookie minicamp asks for.

**THIELEN:** I remember that rep.

**STONE:** OTAs is where your name started showing up on the teams side. Alan put you on more than one unit and your communication held without a coach placing you from the sideline every snap. There are two mistakes on record. May 23 you waited for a confirmation on an alignment you didn't need, and you corrected it on the repeat. In June, when Alan threw a surprise substitute in, you called the landmark of the guy who came out instead of your own; you took the correction from Darryl and Alan and handled the return. Nothing later in the spring shows either one coming back. At minicamp the record says your receiver and coverage communication held. In camp it was route depth, repeated assignment-correct routes, lane responsibility and pursuit on teams. That's why you were the third receiver on August 24. And it carried into the preseason games. Punt coverage at the Jets and in Atlanta: the calls came out right both times, you kept your lane at the Jets, and you pursued in Atlanta. On the teams side, keeping corrections and talking without being prompted is Better for the spring. Best would have been the surprise substitution right the first time, and helping the man next to you while you did it. On the receiver side you were Good with parts of Better by the preseason: your route jobs held against Philadelphia and Atlanta, when nobody was scripting the defense.

**THIELEN:** Okay.

**STONE:** Then the season gave you more than we planned. Justin's suspension started after the opener and you were the second receiver in Oakland, with one week to rep the new alignments. Seattle, the fifty-eight seconds, you had the twelve in the middle of that drive. You got hurt in St. Louis and were back for Denver. Denver through London we put development work on your name, and in London you were the H: seven catches, a hundred and nine, the touchdown. In Nashville in November, eighteen, twenty-five and thirty-seven on a day we gave the ball away six times; the review says you and Cecil got open. Against Tennessee in December, two catches, seventy-five yards, the touchdown and the fifty. Every game and both playoff games dressed, and no fumble in the regular season.

**THIELEN:** Until the one that counted.

**STONE:** Until Saturday. Here's what I can't do. I can put a teaching state on your spring because the practice records describe the reps. For the season I can tell you what we planned to teach you on Wednesdays and Thursdays and what happened on game day. The records don't describe how the movable-receiver reps in between went. So I'm not putting Good, Better or Best on your season receiver play. I'd be guessing, and you'd know it.

**THIELEN:** I'd rather see it on film anyway.

**STONE:** Then Saturday. One fumble. Fifty-two catches in the season and six in the playoffs, and it's the only time the ball came out. That doesn't make it small. That week's plan had ball-security periods Tuesday and Thursday, and it was still the turnover the review named. What I don't know yet is why: how you were carrying it, where the hit came from, what you were doing with the ball after contact. I'm also not connecting it to the Kansas City injury. The doctors cleared you, and that's their field, not mine.

**THIELEN:** I'm not using that. I had it and I lost it.

**STONE:** Then we find out how. Darryl owns ball security after the catch.

**DRAKE:** We'll watch it frame by frame, next to your other catches this year where somebody got a hand on the ball, if the tape has them. You see what you did when it stayed in and what was different Saturday. And one thing I'll check before we sit down. The Tuesday and Thursday periods that week included Maurice's fumble in Kansas City. What we wrote down doesn't say how much of them was receivers carrying it after the catch. If it wasn't much, that's the plan, not you.

**STONE:** Two more plays go on a sheet. In London, after we took over with two and a half minutes left, Tarell Brown intercepted a throw to you. In Nashville in the third quarter, Zach Brown did. Jeremy's building a sheet of every interception this year with the quarterbacks. The throws toward you get watched with Darryl in the room. Nothing's charged to the receiver by default.

**THIELEN:** The London one I think about. We're up seven, late. I thought I was where I was supposed to be. If I wasn't, I want to know.

**STONE:** You'll know. What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**THIELEN:** Oakland is what worked, if that makes sense. Justin's out, I'm the two, one week to get it. But it was the same plays. Stick is Stick wherever I line up; somebody else is doing a different job in it. I didn't have to learn a new offense. I had to learn a new spot.

**STONE:** That's how the book is built: the concept stays, the jobs move. Good to hear it held when the jobs moved.

**THIELEN:** And Denver to London. Wednesday I had my own period: motion, where I stop, how deep. Thursday the scout team gave me different looks and I had to find the answer myself. By London I wasn't thinking about where to line up.

**DRAKE:** That's what those Thursdays were for.

**THIELEN:** Then after the bye it just stopped. I didn't know if that meant I'd passed or there wasn't time.

**DRAKE:** Fair question. The bye practice gave protection the biggest block, with ball security and third down beside it, and your Wednesday period isn't in any week's plan after London. Nobody told you whether it was closed or parked. That's mine.

**STONE:** A development item that stops without anybody telling the player why is a teaching problem before it's a player problem. It goes down that way. What should I know about coaching you that I don't?

**THIELEN:** One voice on a rep. In June, the substitution drill, I had Darryl and Alan both on me about the same landmark. I fixed it. But when I'm on two units I want to know who owns which correction.

**STONE:** That's fair. The route is Darryl's. The landmark on a kicking-game unit belongs to special teams, and until the coordinator job is filled, that's me. If two of us see it, one of us says it.

**DRAKE:** Agreed.

**THIELEN:** Can I ask where I stand? Justin's back. I'm undrafted, on the minimum. I know how that usually goes.

**STONE:** You were second on the depth chart from Oakland on. When Justin came back you stayed second, and we made you the H in London. Before London I told the press you'd earned more work, and it was true in October. That's a fact about 2013. It isn't a promise about September. Roles move when the football evidence says they should. That cuts both ways: nobody waits because somebody else was drafted higher, and nobody keeps a spot because he had it last year.

**THIELEN:** So the two's open.

**STONE:** It gets decided on 2014 football evidence, the same way it got decided in 2013. I'm not going to tell you it's yours, and I'm not going to tell you it isn't.

**THIELEN:** And the contract side? I don't know if I'm even supposed to ask you that.

**STONE:** You can ask me anything. That one is Dave's. Your deal runs through 2015. Anything beyond what it says is his decision, and if you or anybody who represents you has a question, it goes to him. I'll give Dave my football evaluation of every player on this roster, you included. What I tell him stays between him and me, and that isn't a hint either way.

**THIELEN:** Okay. One more. If I'm playing a lot on offense, do I come off teams? Because I don't want to. That's how I made this team.

**STONE:** Nobody comes off a unit because of a title, and nobody stays on one because he asked. It's the same as August: communication, lane and pursuit. Unit decisions get made off minicamp, camp and the preseason.

**THIELEN:** Who's making them, with Alan gone?

**STONE:** Who coaches special teams isn't decided. Until the coordinator job is filled, the kicking game is mine.

**THIELEN:** Returns too? I had a couple.

**STONE:** A punt in St. Louis, a kickoff against Buffalo, a punt in Kansas City and the kickoff Saturday. Punt return went by situation. None of that was a return job, and none of it tells me whether you should have one. Returner evaluation belongs to special teams, and it runs through camp. If you want to be looked at, you will be, same as anybody. The offseason. None of this is required. The program can't start before April 21, and it's voluntary until minicamp. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me, with Darryl, or with whoever coaches special teams.

**THIELEN:** I'll be here.

**STONE:** Then here's what we'd offer.

**DRAKE:** The film first, when the rules let us show it to you. Dave's office tells us what we can send before April. Saturday's fumble. The two interceptions, with the quarterbacks. And a cut-up of the movable jobs from Denver to London, so we can see together what held and what didn't. Then in Phase Two: ball security after the catch, every individual period. Releases against press: you fixed the one at rookie minicamp on the repeat, and we worked release tempo the San Diego week, but nothing we kept says how either held up after that, so we look again. And the motion landmarks retested cold, when you're not expecting it.

**STONE:** On teams, the substitution work, whatever your offensive load turns out to be. You fixed the June one on the repeat. Best is the surprise call right the first time, including when you've already played a lot of snaps on offense. Physically, nothing in our record says you need anything specific. Whatever the exam says today is between you and the medical staff, and if they give you a plan, they run it, not us. The strength staff will have a program for anybody who wants one when the program opens. Last year you came in after the draft. This is the first spring you can have from the start, if you want it.

**THIELEN:** I want it.

**STONE:** One more thing, and I say it to everybody. Player Development is here all offseason for anything away from football. You don't go through me, and I don't need to know.

**THIELEN:** Got it.

**STONE:** Anything you need from us?

**THIELEN:** The fumble tape as soon as the rules let you. And a list of what's open for me and what's closed, written down. I don't want to guess again like after the bye.

**STONE:** Darryl writes it. I'll add the teams side until the coordinator job is filled.

**DRAKE:** You'll have it before I call you. Call or text?

**THIELEN:** Text first.

## Development plan

- Ball security after the catch (Drake): the Divisional fourth-quarter catch-and-fumble reviewed frame by frame beside any other contested catches the 2013 tape holds; ball-carriage and ball-security drill work in Phase Two individual periods, at walkthrough speed and without live contact (readiness standard section 7). One fumble in the record; it is treated as one rep to understand, not a pattern.
- Interception review: the Week 8 (Tarell Brown, after Jacksonville took over with 2:30 left) and Week 10 (Zach Brown, third quarter) interceptions on throws to him go on the quarterback staff's full-season sheet with Drake present, classified by cause with caller contributions marked and a correct rep paired where one exists. Nothing charged to the receiver by default.
- Movable-role retention: a Weeks 6 to 8 cut-up of the taught jobs (motion landmarks, route depth, Whip, Mesh, Sprint Flood) reviewed with Drake, then an unprompted retest in Phase Two. The week records show what was planned, not how each rep went, so the review sets the baseline.
- Release against press: he corrected the rookie-minicamp press rep on the repeat, and the Week 7 plan scheduled release tempo; no later record shows how either held, so it is retested, not treated as an open flaw. Phase Two release work against press, at walkthrough speed and without live contact.
- Special teams (Stone until the coordinator job is filled): emergency and unexpected-substitution communication under an offensive workload; returner evaluation open to him on the same basis as anyone, with no job implied.
- Keep: alignment and assignment reliability, correction retention (each recorded spring correction was taken and none is recorded as recurring), cross-unit communication. No new correction is assigned where the record shows correct work.
- Physical: nothing indicated in the record. The exit physical today is the medical staff's; any finding, restriction, rehabilitation or clearance is theirs. Strength and conditioning available from April 21, voluntary; his first full offseason program with the club.
- All of the above is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; declining any of it carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
