# Justin Blackmon, WR: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Darryl Drake (wide receivers); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** WR3 and the outside Z receiver since Week 8, behind Cecil Shorts (WR1) and Adam Thielen (WR2/H), ahead of Toney Clemons (WR4) and Mike Brown (WR5). Active 53, available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: 2012 first-round (#5) four-year rookie contract, final year 2015; under contract. One accrued season before 2013, with 2013 accruing in the branch (on the 53 for 12 games: Week 1 and Weeks 6-17; Weeks 2-5 on Reserve/Suspended). 4/17 of his 2013 base salary was forfeited for Weeks 2-5; the amount is unresolved, and no contract tolling is recorded or assumed. Age 24 (born January 9, 1990).

## The season in the record

Blackmon dressed for 10 regular-season games (Week 1, Week 8 and Weeks 10-17; Week 9 was the bye) and both postseason games. He served the league's four-game suspension on Reserve/Suspended in Weeks 2-5 (ledger Entry 36), was reinstated and activated to the 53 on October 7 (Entry 42), and was Stone's game-day inactive in Weeks 6 and 7 while he practiced on the active roster, a decision recorded in September as "Stone's availability decision, not a second suspension." Regular season: 53 targets, 32 catches, 271 yards, 8.5 a catch, no touchdown, longest catch 29; 1 run for 2 yards; 2 kickoff returns for 59 and 1 punt return for 4; no fumble. Postseason: 5 targets, 4 catches, 28 yards; 1 punt return for 20. No receipt injury. No weekly-award shortlist. Before this staff: 2012, 16 games and 14 starts.

## Conversation

**STONE:** Sit down. Medical has your exit physical after this, and what they find is between you and them. This is your year the way our record has it. I'll go first, then I want yours, then the offseason.

**BLACKMON:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** Ten regular-season games and both playoff games. You were targeted 53 times, caught 32 for 271, no touchdowns. The long was 29 in Indianapolis.

**BLACKMON:** You can skip the no touchdowns. I know.

**STONE:** I'm not skipping anything, either direction. Start with the spring, because that's where the record on you is thickest. June 12, the day we changed the pictures, your route work was productive when your pre-snap split was clean, and Darryl had to re-test a motion-alignment correction. He prepared your motion and split correction over the break. In camp, you and Cecil came out as the leading receiver pair on the whole body of work, and the note on you was that you created difficult coverage stress when you were aligned correctly.

**BLACKMON:** There's always a "when" with me.

**STONE:** In that sentence it's the right word. Nothing in the camp record questions whether you could stress coverage. The open item was getting to the right spot before the snap, every time. That's alignment and communication. It isn't talent and it isn't effort.

**BLACKMON:** Then say that. People hear "when" and think I don't know the plays.

**STONE:** You knew the jobs. In our language the camp record supports Good. It doesn't support Better yet, because Better means the correction holds without anybody prompting you, and nobody wrote down that it held.

**DRAKE:** That part's mine. I re-tested it in June and built the correction over the break. After that I never put on paper whether it closed. So I can't show you it didn't, and I can't show you it did.

**BLACKMON:** It closed. I wasn't lining up wrong on Sundays.

**STONE:** Maybe you weren't. The weekly records don't say either way, so I'm not giving you the credit and I'm not taking it away. Darryl answers it from the tape.

**BLACKMON:** Fine.

**STONE:** Week 1 you were our second receiver. Thirteen targets, the most on the team that day, six catches for 39, and a 32-yard kickoff return.

**BLACKMON:** Thirteen and 39. That's a bad day.

**STONE:** It might be. Six on thirteen could be the route, the throw, the coverage or the call, and nobody classified them. I'm not going to do it from a box score, for you or for Kirk. Then the four games. I'll say the football part and stop there. The league suspended you for four games, you were on Reserve/Suspended for Weeks 2 through 5, and you were reinstated October 7. I'm not asking you about anything beyond that.

**BLACKMON:** Good. That's mine. I served it.

**STONE:** You did. The next two weeks were mine.

**BLACKMON:** Yeah. Those I want to talk about.

**STONE:** Go ahead.

**BLACKMON:** The league was done with me October 7. I practiced two full weeks. You told the reporters I worked hard. I still didn't dress for Denver or San Diego. From where I sat, that was six games, not four.

**STONE:** What I decided in September was two weeks of practice on the 53 before you dressed, after four weeks when you weren't allowed to practice. It was an availability decision. It wasn't a second suspension, and I never called it one.

**BLACKMON:** You decided it before you saw me take a rep.

**STONE:** That's true.

**BLACKMON:** So practicing well didn't matter.

**STONE:** It mattered for Week 8. Darryl.

**DRAKE:** Your Week 8 Wednesday was a role install, not a re-install, because you'd been out there two weeks. We started at the Z jobs, not at the beginning.

**BLACKMON:** Fine. The two games were already gone before any of that.

**STONE:** They were. Here's the honest version. We tell everybody in this building that football evidence moves roles. A date I set in September isn't evidence about October. I'm not going to tell you it was wrong, and I'm not going to tell you it was right because I'm the one who made it. Whether a player coming back from a long absence gets a set period or is judged on his practice work is a question I have to answer deliberately, and I'm not answering it in this room. Your point goes on my list.

**BLACKMON:** Okay.

**STONE:** Week 8 you came back as the third receiver and the outside Z. Run blocking, Smoke against cushion, Slant-Flat, backside access, and the vertical alert or the Post in Mills only if San Francisco earned it. No target quota, and I didn't script you a first call to make a moment out of it. One catch for a yard, a 4-yard punt return. I said afterward that nothing about your day needed to be special. That was the plan.

**BLACKMON:** For one game, fine. Then it stayed that way. Week 1 I'm the two. I come back, I'm the three, and the three stays the three. Adam got the H, all the motion. I'm not mad at Adam; he was here and I wasn't. I'm asking what I have to do to stop being the guy blocking on the backside.

**STONE:** Two answers. First, I call the offense, so how often the ball came your way is partly me. From Tennessee in November we built on 12 personnel, with a protection attached to every call before it went on the sheet. That was a decision about the whole offense, not about you. There was no quota for you or anybody else, so the ball went where the call and the coverage sent it.

**BLACKMON:** Which mostly wasn't me.

**STONE:** Sometimes it was. Arizona, the 18 on the first snap of the touchdown drive in the third quarter. Cleveland, tied in the last two minutes, 23 and 18 to get us to their 28. Indianapolis, the 29 on Spacing in the 83-yard drive. In Kansas City the plan had the Power Pass shot coming off the run, and it came to you. It was incomplete, and Cecil scored on the Post-Cross the next snap.

**BLACKMON:** And that drive ended with Haden.

**STONE:** On the next throw. Your two got us to the 28. Second answer: what moves anybody in that room is 2014 work. That isn't a dodge, and here's what makes it harder for me to hold you to it. After Week 8, our weekly records have your targets and your catches and nothing else. We told you the Week 8 install was graded on blocking as much as routes. I can't find the grade. Houston away and Houston at home, seven targets each, four catches and then three. I can't tell you why from anything written down.

**BLACKMON:** I never saw a blocking grade. You put me out there to block and nobody told me if I was any good at it.

**DRAKE:** If it isn't written down, you didn't get it in a form you could use. That's mine too.

**STONE:** When the same gap runs through a whole season, the first question is whether the teaching failed, not the player. Here it's ours. So before anybody assigns you route work or blocking work, Darryl puts the tape together. Every snap you blocked on the Z from Week 8 on, and every target, starting with Week 1, both Houston games and the Divisional, each one sorted: alignment, release, route, leverage, the throw, the coverage, the call. Jeremy sits in on the throws.

**TICE:** And I'll chart the other side of it. Week by week, which calls had the Z as a primary read or an alert, and how many of them we actually called. If the sheet didn't give you chances, that shows up as the sheet, not as you. It goes in with Alex's self-scout.

**BLACKMON:** That's all I'm asking. Show me.

**STONE:** What worked in how we coached you, what didn't, and what do we get wrong about you? Blunt is fine.

**BLACKMON:** What worked was the split work Darryl put together in the summer. It's boring, it's feet and landmarks, and it's why camp went how it went. And Friday. If Kirk and I weren't seeing the same thing, it came off. I'd rather it come off Friday than I wear it Sunday.

**STONE:** And what didn't?

**BLACKMON:** After I came back I never knew what I had to show you to get more. You told the reporters I'd be evaluated like everybody else. Evaluated on what? Give me the list and I'll go get it.

**STONE:** That's a fair hit. You got the jobs at the install. Nothing we wrote down gave you what we'd need to see before the job got bigger. That should have come with it.

**BLACKMON:** And what you get wrong about me: you think I need the ball to stay in the game. I'll block. I blocked every week I dressed. But I'm not a blocker who runs a route sometimes. When the corner's pressed and there's nobody over the top, that's my ball.

**STONE:** That's worth knowing, and I'm not going to argue it with you from memory. The record doesn't tell me whether those looks were there and we passed on them or whether they weren't there. Mike's chart and Darryl's tape will. If they were there and we passed, that's on the caller.

**BLACKMON:** Straight question. Am I getting traded?

**STONE:** Trades, releases and contracts are Dave Caldwell's. I'll give him my football evaluation of you, built on the same record we just went through. What I recommend to him stays between him and me, and I'm not going to hint at it either way.

**BLACKMON:** Would you tell me if it was coming?

**STONE:** I'm not going to promise you anything about a decision that isn't mine to make.

**BLACKMON:** Next year, then. Am I the three?

**STONE:** Every 2014 role gets earned on 2014 work. Cecil's, Adam's, yours, and anybody who gets added. That isn't me telling you you're moving up, and it isn't me telling you you're moving down.

**BLACKMON:** I was the fifth pick.

**STONE:** I know. Talent got you the attention in camp. What got you responsibility was how you operated, and that rule is the same for everybody in the room.

**BLACKMON:** One more. They took four checks. Nobody's told me what that does to my deal going forward, and I want it in writing.

**STONE:** I don't know, and I'm the wrong person to answer it. That's Dave's office, your agent and the union. I'll tell Dave you asked and that you want the answer in writing. When he answers is his call.

**BLACKMON:** Fine.

**STONE:** The offseason. You're under contract, and that doesn't change what's voluntary. Our program can't start before April 21. Before it, and in every voluntary part of it, nothing is required, and choosing not to come doesn't cost you anything here. So these are suggestions for wherever you're working.

**BLACKMON:** Go ahead.

**STONE:** One, the alignment. The split rules and motion landmarks are in the book you already have; that's self-study. When the program reaches the phase where Darryl is allowed to coach you on the field, he'll walk it and re-test it with you, if you're there. Two, route work waits for Darryl's tape. If the targets come out as release, route or leverage, that's work you can do anywhere. If they come out as the throw or the call, the work is ours. I'm not guessing which before we see it. Three, the blocking write-up, so you know what to keep and what to fix. Four, the body is your program. If you want an individual plan from the performance staff, ask and they'll build one. Five, anything medical belongs to the medical staff, starting today.

**BLACKMON:** What about returns? I had the 20 in Nashville.

**STONE:** Returner evaluation belongs to the special teams coordinator. Alan left on January 12 to become Atlanta's head coach, and that job is open, so until it's filled it comes to me. I'm not handing out a return job in here.

**BLACKMON:** Okay.

**STONE:** What else do you need from us?

**BLACKMON:** The tape, the blocking thing and the list. Send them to me. I won't be in the building much.

**DRAKE:** You'll have the tape and the blocking write-up by the end of the week, with the list.

**STONE:** So it's clear what the list is: it's what we'll evaluate. It isn't a deal where you check the boxes and get a job.

**BLACKMON:** I know what it is.

**STONE:** Last thing, and it isn't a question. Player Development is there for anything off the field. It's confidential, and it doesn't go on any report. I'm not asking whether you use it.

**BLACKMON:** Okay.

**DRAKE:** Medical's expecting you down the hall.

## Development plan

All of it is voluntary. Blackmon is under contract through 2015; Jacksonville's 2014 offseason program may begin no earlier than April 21, and the club cannot require offseason work before the program or in its voluntary phases, or imply that declining costs him anything. Nothing here assumes a 2014 role.

- Pre-snap alignment and motion (the open spring item): self-study of the split rules and motion landmarks in his playbook; Drake's written status of the June 12 motion-alignment correction from spring and camp film; an on-field walk and re-test with Drake once the program reaches the phase that permits individual football instruction, if Blackmon attends. Evidence: minicamp June 12 and camp entry ("communication correction open"); camp ("when aligned correctly"); no in-season record either way.
- Targets and routes: no route work assigned until Drake's classified cutup is done (every target in Week 1 and from Week 8 onward, starting with both Houston games and the Divisional, sorted by alignment, release, route, leverage, throw, coverage and call, with Bates on the throw side). Release, route or leverage causes point to work he can do anywhere; throw or call causes point to staff work.
- Blocking in the Z role: Drake's written evaluation from Week 8 onward, since the Week 8 install grade was never recorded. No finding is presumed.
- Keep: the camp standard (the leading receiver pair "by cumulative camp operation") and the catches that moved drives when it counted (Week 11 and Week 17 on touchdown drives; Week 13 in the final two minutes of regulation).
- Special teams: no assignment made here. The special teams coordinator evaluates his returns (2 kickoff returns and 1 punt return in the regular season; 1 punt return in the postseason); the job has been vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12, and Stone holds it until it is filled.
- Physical: his own program; an individualized recommendation from the performance staff is available on request. No communicated restriction.
- Medical: exit physical with the medical staff on January 13. Any finding, restriction or clearance belongs to the medical staff.

Delivered by the end of the week.

- Tice: chart, by week from Week 8, the calls with the Z as a primary read or alert and how many were called, with the looks available, as part of Stone's self-scout.
- Stone: tell Caldwell that Blackmon asked for a written answer on what the four-game forfeiture means for his contract going forward. The answer and its timing are Caldwell's.
- Stone: give Caldwell his football evaluation of Blackmon. The recommendation is not recorded here.
- Special teams coordinator (vacant since Lowry left for Atlanta on January 12; Stone until the job is filled): evaluate Blackmon's return tape; no return role was discussed as a commitment.
- Medical staff: exit physical, January 13. No finding is recorded in this interview.
- Teaching note: a spring correction (June 12 motion/split alignment) was re-tested at minicamp and prepared over the break, and it was still open at camp entry, but no later record says whether it closed, so it could not be confirmed closed or re-tested in season. At each phase close, record whether an open correction closed or carries forward.
- Teaching note: the Week 8 role install was "graded on blocking as much as routes," but no grade was recorded, and Blackmon says he never saw one. When a role is defined largely by blocking, the player is owed a recorded blocking evaluation, as he is for routes.
- Teaching note: a returning player was told publicly he would be "evaluated like everybody else" but was not given, in writing, what the staff needed to see before his role could grow. Give role criteria in writing at a role install.
- Teaching note: the weekly records carry receiver targets and catches but no classification of incompletions (alignment, route, leverage, throw, coverage, call), so low-yield games such as Weeks 12 and 14 cannot be diagnosed from the record.
- User decision: how Stone handles a player's return after a suspension or other long absence: a game-day inactive period set in advance (as for Weeks 6 and 7, decided in September) or a return decided on practice evidence. Stone took Blackmon's point without committing to a policy.
- User decision: the football evaluation and any recommendation Stone gives Caldwell on Blackmon, who is under contract through 2015.
