# Kirk Cousins, QB: exit interview

**Date:** Monday January 13, 2014. **Present:** Alex Stone; Jeremy Bates (quarterbacks); Mike Tice (offensive coordinator).
**Status going in:** QB1 (named August 24, 2013, after the third preseason game), Henne QB2 and Wilson QB3. Available, no communicated restriction; his exit physical is with the medical staff today. Contract: under contract; inherited 2012 Washington four-year rookie contract, acquired by branch trade, final year 2015 (one accrued season through 2012, with 2013 accruing in the branch). Age 25 (born August 19, 1988).

## The season in the record

Cousins was QB1 for all 16 regular-season games and both playoff games. Acquired from Washington before the draft for Jacksonville's 2014 second-round selection; the pre-trade board recorded his 2012 fourth-round entry and limited rookie work, including a start against Cleveland.

Regular season (game receipts): 357 of 564 for 3,981 yards, 23 touchdowns and 18 interceptions; 602 dropbacks; sacked 38 times for 247 yards; 37 rushes for 106 yards; 2 fumbles, both lost. Through eight games (bye review): 1,871 passing yards and 14 touchdowns.

Postseason: 38 of 59 for 480 yards and 4 touchdowns, no interception; 63 dropbacks; sacked 4 times for 24 yards; 7 rushes for 12 yards.

Recorded moments:
- Spring and camp: the cleanest huddle, cadence, protection-point and two-minute operation in the room at OTAs, mandatory minicamp and camp. Recorded mistakes: a forced tight-window throw instead of the outlet (minicamp; corrected in film and in the later field work); late once moving to the underneath Mesh window (OTAs; delivered on the repeat); holding the ball when a changed picture moved the Mesh outlet (OTAs; corrected on its return) and one placement outside the receiver's frame (OTAs; no retest recorded); a third-down protection error (camp; corrected within the period) and a late red-zone decision (camp; corrected in the next situational possession). Threw Jacksonville's only interception in the 33-17 preseason win over Miami; clean operation in preseason Games 2 and 3; named QB1 August 24.
- Week 3 at Seattle: took over with 58 seconds left; 21 yards to Shorts on Y-Cross, 12 to Thielen on the same call and a Spacing throw to Lewis set up the winning field goal on the final snap.
- Weeks 4, 5, 6 and 7: no interception; Week 7 was recorded as the third straight turnover-free game, with four sacks.
- Week 8 (London): four interceptions, three on throws toward Shorts. Week 10 at Tennessee: four interceptions and a lost fumble on Pollard's strip-sack; "eight interceptions in two games." Stone publicly: "Kirk is our quarterback" and, before Week 11, "The job's his."
- Coaching points given to him: Week 10, "the defense does not owe us the route we called"; Week 11, "When the trigger dies, the shot dies"; Week 16, possession, then correct leverage, then completion, then the explosive, and "Do not throw a defender's reputation."
- Week 11: 19 of 24, no turnover; Stone: "when the answer wasn't there Kirk left it."
- Week 12 at Houston: 17 runs against 45 dropbacks; Stone, before Week 13: "We stopped calling what was working. That was mine." Week 13: Haden's second interception came at the Cleveland 28 with 20 seconds left in a tied game.
- Weeks 16 and 17, Wild Card and Divisional: no interception. Wild Card: threw on all five snaps of a 76-yard touchdown drive; 53 yards to Kelce on Mesh. Divisional: an 80-yard touchdown drive on nine straight passes, then eight drives for 62 yards; Jacksonville 1 of 9 on third down.
- Boot Flood: Berry's Week 1 interception came on a Boot Flood throw, and both Week 2 Boot Flood sacks went to film review; from Week 3 the call stayed off the sheet for the rest of the season. After Week 1 Stone told reporters the interception "on the boot" needed the coverage in front of him "before I say whose they are."
- Bye week: member of the leadership council.
- Weekly-award shortlists: none. In-season honours: none. Receipt injuries: none; available every week.

## Conversation

**STONE:** You've got the physical after this?

**COUSINS:** This afternoon.

**STONE:** That's between you and the doctors. This is the football part. Before I say anything, how do you see your year?

**COUSINS:** Ten and six, a playoff win, and eighteen interceptions. All three are true. I think I finished better than I was in the middle of the year, and the last four games I didn't throw one. Then Saturday we had one drive. That's what I keep watching.

**STONE:** Then I'll start with what I'd keep. From May to the third preseason game you had the cleanest operation in the room: huddle, cadence, protection point, two minute. That's how you got the job in August. Not the trade. The camp record.

**COUSINS:** I know how I got it.

**STONE:** The season has a lot in it. Seattle, fifty-eight seconds: Shorts on Y-Cross, Thielen on the same call, Spacing to Marcedes, the kick on the last snap. No interception from Week 4 through Week 7. Nineteen of twenty-four against Arizona the week after Nashville. Kansas City in the playoffs you threw on all five snaps of a touchdown drive and had three touchdowns. On operation, you're Better, and the two minute in camp was the best version we had.

**COUSINS:** And the rest.

**STONE:** The rest is eighteen interceptions and two lost fumbles. Four in London, four in Nashville, two at Houston, two at Cleveland, three against Buffalo. Oakland was the last possession of a four-point game. Throwing to the right place when the first picture dies is Good. Better means the correction holds when the picture changes. Against Arizona and in the last four games there was no interception. At Houston, at Cleveland and against Buffalo they came back, and whether those were the decision is what the film has to tell us. Until it does, I can't call it Better.

**COUSINS:** That's fair.

**STONE:** Here's what I can say and what I can't. Most of what the spring record has on you is decisions. The tight window at minicamp when the outlet was there. The Mesh outlet in OTAs when the picture changed. The late red-zone decision in the stadium scrimmage. Each of those you fixed the next time it came up. There was a placement miss and a third-down protection error too; the protection one you fixed inside the period. In the season I put the decision in your week three times. What I can't tell you today is how many of the eighteen are that kind and how many are a route, a protection, a placement or my call. We labelled London and Nashville at the time. I want all eighteen on one sheet, with the fumbles and the sacks.

**BATES:** One cause or more than one per throw, and the right rep next to it where we have one.

**COUSINS:** Can I say something about the eighteen?

**STONE:** That's why you're here.

**COUSINS:** Some of them are mine and I know which ones. Some are Cecil and me not seeing the same thing, and I don't think that's all on either of us. And a couple I'd throw again. I'm not saying it to get out of anything. I'm saying if the sheet puts every one in the decision column, I'm going to argue with it.

**STONE:** Good. If the sheet's wrong it's no use to you. Three of the four in London were throws toward Cecil. Jeremy, that's a receiver and quarterback question before it's a quarterback question.

**BATES:** Then Darryl sits in on those.

**STONE:** Which ones would you throw again?

**COUSINS:** Cleveland, the second one.

**STONE:** That's the one I want to watch with you first. Their 28, twenty seconds, tied game. A ball in the stands still gives us a kick.

**COUSINS:** A completion gives us a shorter one. I'll watch it. I'm not conceding it yet.

**STONE:** You don't have to. That's what the film is for.

**STONE:** The sacks. Thirty-eight. I'm not handing that number to you or to the line. Some of it is identification, some is holding it, some is protection, and some may be me calling a five-man protection when we had a better answer. Mike's staff sorted the first half at the bye. The review does the whole year.

**TICE:** I'd start with the blocked, hot or kill period you and Brewster ran the Thursday before Nashville. That period practised identification as its own job. I'd make it a standing period in the spring.

**STONE:** And my part, before you have to say it. The first Houston game was seventeen runs and forty-five dropbacks. At Cleveland I wrote down a three-pass rule and we threw six straight to open the second half; that drive ended in Haden's first one. The punt with two minutes left on Saturday is mine. Those go on the sheet under my name.

**COUSINS:** I wasn't going to bring up Houston.

**STONE:** I know. It still goes on the sheet. Your turn. What worked in how we coached you, and what didn't?

**COUSINS:** Camp worked. The same picture three or four times, the fix, then the same picture with something changed. By Miami I knew what you wanted. In the season it got shorter. Monday I'd get a line. "The trigger dies, the shot dies." "Don't throw a defender's reputation." I understood the line. What I needed was the picture next to it: two or three clips of the same coverage, one I got right and one I didn't.

**BATES:** That's fair. In the spring the correction had the room to itself. In the season it was fighting the next opponent for time.

**STONE:** Then that's a teaching problem before it's yours. We build it the way you just described. What didn't work?

**COUSINS:** Honestly, the menu. I understood it in September. By January we were playing Tennessee a third time with no new family on the sheet, and they knew what was coming. We went eighty yards on nine throws and then we had nothing. Boot came off after Oakland and never came back. Boot and play-action are part of what I came here with. I'm not asking for the whole book. I'm asking whether some of what we cut was cut because it was wrong or because it was easier to cut.

**TICE:** Part of that runs the other way. We were one of nine on third down. A new concept family doesn't fix that by itself.

**STONE:** He's right, and so are you. Boot Flood had an interception in Week 1 and two sacks at Oakland. We watched them, I took it off, and we never wrote down whether it was the read, the protection or the call. That's a staff question first. Whether the Tennessee plan gave you enough answers the third time is Mike's and mine to answer, and I'm not answering it in this room two days later.

**COUSINS:** Okay.

**STONE:** What do you think we misunderstand about you?

**COUSINS:** That I need to be protected from the offense. After London it felt like the sheet got smaller around me every week. I get why. I'd rather be coached out of a bad throw than have the throw taken away.

**STONE:** Some weeks the sheet got smaller because of the rush, not because of you. Before Nashville in November every pass concept had its protection attached before it went on. That was the line and the rush. But I hear you. The rule's the same for you as for everybody: a smaller menu run right beats a bigger one that makes you slow. And the way it grows is the same too. Clean in practice, then it competes for a game sheet.

**COUSINS:** Can I ask about next year?

**STONE:** Ask.

**COUSINS:** Am I your quarterback?

**STONE:** Today you're the quarterback on our depth chart, because the evidence put you there in August and kept you there. That's a fact about 2013. It isn't a promise about September. Football evidence moves roles. It moved them for you last year.

**COUSINS:** After Nashville you said the job was mine. In front of everybody.

**STONE:** I said it because that's what the film said that week, and that Sunday you went nineteen of twenty-four without a turnover. I'd say it again in the same spot. I'm not going to say it in January about a season that hasn't happened.

**COUSINS:** Are you drafting a quarterback?

**STONE:** Dave runs the board and makes the picks. I'll give him my evaluation of this room and of the class, and that stays between him and me. I'm not going to tell you yes or no, because it isn't my decision. What I can tell you is what's written down: draft position, trade cost and who the staff likes don't decide who plays. It's in the quarterback plan. It's why last spring was a real competition.

**COUSINS:** And the contract. I'm signed through 2015. Is there anything I should be hearing?

**STONE:** Contracts are Dave's. If you or your agent have a question, it goes to him. I'm not going to guess at his answer, and I'm not going to tell you what I tell him.

**COUSINS:** Understood.

**STONE:** The offseason. None of this is required. The program can't start before April 21, and it's voluntary until minicamp. If you do none of it, it costs you nothing with me.

**COUSINS:** I'll be here.

**STONE:** Then here's what we'd offer. The sheet: eighteen interceptions, two fumbles, thirty-eight sacks, by cause, with my calls marked and the right rep next to each one where we have it. Jeremy builds it, Charlie helps, Mike does the protection side. When you can have it depends on what the rules let us hand you before the program opens. Dave's office tells us that. I'm not guessing at it.

**BATES:** Then, when the rules let us teach it, the pairs you asked for. Same coverage, changed picture, your right answer next to the wrong one. In OTAs, when we can put a defense across from you, a retest without me telling you what's coming.

**TICE:** Blocked, hot or kill with the center as a standing period in OTAs. And ball carriage. Oakland was a run and Nashville was a strip on a sack. Both go in the carriage work.

**STONE:** And Boot Flood. Mike and I go through the Week 1 interception and the Oakland sacks first. If it was our call or our protection, we fix ours. If it's clean in the spring it can compete for camp, the way everything else got on the sheet. No promise it makes it.

**COUSINS:** That's all I'm asking.

**STONE:** Physically I've got nothing for you. No restriction all year. Anything from today's physical is the doctors' to tell you, not mine. Strength staff will have a plan for anyone who wants one when the program opens. Anything you need from us?

**COUSINS:** Two things. The sheet before April if the rules allow it. And on Thursdays I want to be able to say which calls I trust before Friday takes them off.

**STONE:** The first, if the rules allow it, yes. The second you can already do. That's what the council question was at the bye: what's unclear, what shouldn't change. Say it Thursday. Friday is still my call.

**COUSINS:** Fair.

**STONE:** If you want anything set up away from football, Player Development is here all offseason. You don't go through me and I don't need to know.

**COUSINS:** Got it.

**STONE:** Jeremy will call you once Dave's office tells us what we can send.

## Development plan

- Full-season review sheet (voluntary to receive and to discuss): all 18 interceptions, both lost fumbles (Week 2 run, Week 10 strip-sack) and all 38 sacks, each classified by the quarterback plan's section 8 causes (identification, progression, late decision, unseen defender, placement, pressure or mechanics, receiver error, deflection, justified end-game risk), with staff and caller contributions marked and a correct rep paired where one exists. Delivered only when and as the CBA permits.
- Decision under a changed picture: paired-picture teaching (same coverage, correct and incorrect rep side by side) when the offseason rules permit it, then an unprompted retest against a changed look in OTAs (Phase Two allows no offense against defense). Evidence: the spring decision corrections held the next time the picture came up; in-season he threw no interception in Weeks 11, 14, 16 and 17 or either playoff game, and threw interceptions in Weeks 12, 13 and 15 whose causes the week records do not classify.
- Shorts timing: the London throws toward Shorts reviewed as a receiver and quarterback question with Drake present, before any quarterback-only conclusion.
- End-game and fringe decisions: the Week 13 interception at the Cleveland 28 reviewed first, against the quarterback plan's two-minute and broken-play standards (when the best result is a legal throwaway) and its "justified end-game risk" cause. Cousins disputes that it was an error; the film review settles it.
- Protection identification: the blocked, hot or kill period with the center (run the Thursday before Week 10) as a standing OTA period, with film identification work before that where the rules permit; sacks sorted by cause, not charged to him by default.
- Ball carriage in the pocket and on runs, from the Week 2 and Week 10 fumbles.
- Boot Flood: staff review first; it may compete for the camp menu only if it is clean in spring work. No commitment that it returns.
- Physical: nothing indicated in the record. Exit physical today with the medical staff; any finding, restriction or clearance is theirs. Strength and conditioning available in Phase One from April 21, voluntary.
- All of the above is voluntary until mandatory minicamp; declining any of it carries no role, roster or standing consequence (readiness standard section 7).
