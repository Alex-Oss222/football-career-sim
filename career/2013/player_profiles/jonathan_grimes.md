## Best traits

- Assigned receiving work held in camp and preseason; the season records finishes on Wham and Counter.
- He produced when his calls came: Wham touchdowns against San Diego, Arizona and Tennessee, the Counter touchdown against Indianapolis, Draw at Kansas City, and the 28- and 39-yard catches. Four rushing touchdowns and a receiving touchdown came out of an RB2 workload.
- One lost fumble all season. His assigned route work held from camp into the preseason games.

## Main weaknesses

- No phase supplies an individual pass-protection evaluation. The Week 14 fumble and missed targets have no written cause.
- Ball security. The Week 14 fumble came at the Houston 18 in a game Jacksonville won by one point. The correction list names it; nothing written classifies the cause (carriage, contact, a second defender, the call). Stone would not guess.
- Low-yield games. Denver, 10 carries for 12; Houston in Week 12, 2 for 0. The records do not say whether those runs were the blocking, the track, the read or the call, so Stone did not put them on Grimes.
- Protection. No camp, preseason or in-season record contains a written evaluation of his pass protection. Stone told him he could not evaluate what nobody wrote down, and that protection and ball security are inputs the framework uses to decide how a back is used.
- Receiving. Eight catches on 15 targets; the targets that were not caught are not classified, and none is charged to him. Teaching state: the preseason record supports Good on his assigned route jobs. Nothing recorded establishes Better (a correction retained against changing looks, unprompted communication), and no state was assigned for run track or protection because none was written.
