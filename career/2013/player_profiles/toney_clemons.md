## Best traits

- The season records receiving contributions during the expanded role in Blackmon’s absence.
- He answered when the role grew. He moved into the WR3 jobs in Week 2 with the concepts unchanged and scored in three of the next five games.
- Late in the year Mesh found him, and each of those four catches came on a scoring drive, two of them in the playoffs. The Cleveland touchdown put Jacksonville ahead in a game it won in overtime.
- Returns: eight kickoffs by situation. Stone did not grade them in the meeting; the return evaluation is a special-teams follow-up (Stone until the coordinator job is filled).

## Main weaknesses

- Individual route work is thin. The Week 4 fumble was coached but its cause is unrecorded; randomly attributed returns do not certify return skill.
- Ball security after the catch. The Indianapolis fumble is the only one in his record; it was coached on the Monday film and did not recur. Stone classified it as a football mistake: coached and re-checked, not punished.
- The practice record is thin. No spring phase record names him, and camp recorded him as depth. Stone told him the staff cannot state a Good, Better or Best reading for him from the written record, and would not invent one. His 2014 evaluation needs practice evidence, not only game results.
- Two short catches lost yardage (Weeks 5 and 10). Nothing written classifies them, and Stone drew no conclusion from them.
