## Best traits

- Coverage-lane communication retained teaching in camp; the record documents preparation and retention when his defensive role expanded.
- Ready when the job moved. His role changed on a week's notice twice, in Week 4 and Week 14. Week 4's record says the linebacker operation held, and Week 14's lists "Smith and Allen in the reshaped base defense" as held up. Stone repeated his own Week 15 note to him: correctly done.
- Finishing the plays that came to him: the Arizona interception that became three points before the half, the Wild Card interception that set up the first touchdown, and the Week 13 sack on Cleveland's first series.
- Coverage units. Camp recorded repeated, communicated lane execution and finish. By the framework's definitions, reproducing a communicated job through repeated reps is Better. Stone said that is the one teaching state the record supports for him, and only for that job in that phase.

## Main weaknesses

- Special-teams learning cannot automatically grade base defensive fits. Individual in-game run and coverage responsibilities are not classified.
- Run fits in his starts. By unit result they held in Weeks 14, 16 and 17 and the Wild Card, and were corrected in Week 15 and the Divisional. Neither correction has been classified by cause, so nothing in them is assigned to him until the review says whose fit broke and why (assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing, or the call).
- Linebacker evidence is otherwise unit reads and tackle totals. Stone did not build a linebacker teaching state from box-score lines and said so.
