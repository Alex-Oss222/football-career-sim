"When did you first know?"

"I want to answer that precisely, because there are two different questions there.

I first became aware in early 2010 that the league was asking questions about whether there were improper incentives on the defensive side of our team. So I am not going to sit here and tell you I had never heard anything about it until 2012. That wouldn't be an honest answer.

What I knew in 2010 was that there was an allegation and a league inquiry. I did not know that there was a program paying players for knocking opponents out of games. I did not know that specific opposing players were being targeted for money. I was not in those defensive meetings, I did not contribute to a pool, and I did not administer one.

At that point I was the offensive coordinator. I treated it as an issue being handled by the head coach, the general manager, the defensive staff and the league. I did not independently investigate the defensive room.

In hindsight, that distinction matters to me. As an offensive coordinator, there were parts of the organization that were outside my authority. As a head coach, there is no 'other side of the building.' If something serious enough reaches me that the league is asking questions about it, I have a responsibility to find out what is happening and make sure it stops if it violates league rules.

When the league announced its findings in March of 2012, that is when I understood that this was not simply an allegation. I learned that there had been a prohibited pay-for-performance program and that injury-related payments were part of it.

When I became the interim head coach after that, there was no ambiguity about the standard. No payments for injuring opponents. No unofficial cash pool tied to hits or injuries. No coach or player gets to operate something like that because it happens inside his position room or on his side of the football team.

So if you're asking me whether I knew in 2009 or 2010 that Gregg Williams was operating the bounty program that the league ultimately described, no. I did not.

If you're asking whether I knew in 2010 that the league had raised an allegation about improper defensive incentives, yes. I did.

And if you're hiring me as your head coach, the lesson I took from it is that I don't get to stop at knowing there is an allegation. I have to find out what is happening in my building."

If they ask, "Did you participate in it?"

"No."

"Did you contribute money?"

"No."

"Did you know players were being paid for knockouts or cart-offs before the league findings became known?"

"No."

"Did you know specific opponents had bounties placed on them?"

"No."

"Why didn't you do more in 2010?"

"Because at the time I was the offensive coordinator, I knew there was a league inquiry, and I treated the matter as something being handled above me and within the defensive operation. I'm not going to rewrite that now and claim I conducted an investigation that I didn't conduct. What changed for me is becoming responsible for the whole football team. If I'm the head coach and the league is asking whether something improper is happening inside my building, 'that's the defense' is not an acceptable answer."

"Are you blaming Sean Payton, Mickey Loomis or Gregg Williams?"

"No. I'm telling you what I personally knew and what I personally did. They can answer for themselves. You're interviewing me."

"Could something like this happen under you?"

"I can't promise you that fifty-three players and an entire coaching staff will never make a bad decision. I can tell you what happens when I learn about one. We stop it. We establish the facts. We involve the general manager and ownership when appropriate. If it involves league rules, we deal with the league honestly. Nobody gets protected because he's a good player or an important assistant."
