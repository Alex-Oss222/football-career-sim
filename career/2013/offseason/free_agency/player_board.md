# 2013 free-agency player board — Stone's decision view

**Board purpose:** the free-agent market as Alex Stone can legitimately see it through Jacksonville's coaching staff, pro-personnel/scouting operation, public information and his own football judgment.

**Decision authority:** Stone recommends football priorities, roles and acceptable football value. General manager David Caldwell controls negotiation, cap structure and final contract execution.

**Primary snapshot:** **March 8, 2013**, immediately before the new March 9–11 agent negotiating window. This file does not advance the simulation clock by itself. When the simulation reaches a later date, later agent feedback and signings may be appended as branch events.

**Critical rule:** projected price is Jacksonville's estimate, not hidden knowledge of the player's eventual contract. Other clubs' interest, agent demands and the player's willingness to join Jacksonville remain unknown until communicated.

## Information labels

- **PUBLIC FACT** — contract status, 2012 role, public injury information and other ordinary league information.
- **PRO PERSONNEL** — Jacksonville pro-scout evaluation communicated to Stone.
- **COACHING STAFF** — scheme/role evaluation from Stone, Crennel, Tice, Lowry or a position coach.
- **STONE VIEW** — Stone's own priority or walk-away recommendation.
- **MARKET ESTIMATE** — Jacksonville's expected range; not a real offer.
- **MARKET UNKNOWN** — player/agent/other-club information Jacksonville does not possess yet.

## Market context Stone sees

The 2013 market is not a seven-player list.

Public pre-opening rankings showed real depth at pass rush and defensive back. NFL.com placed Michael Bennett among the stronger available prime-age pass rushers and ranked Brent Grimes inside its broader top free-agent group despite his Achilles recovery. That gives Jacksonville alternatives and price pressure rather than a single predetermined path.

# 1. Priority pursuit — Michael Bennett, DE

### PUBLIC FACT

- Tampa Bay free agent.
- Coming off consecutive productive seasons as a complete edge defender.
- Publicly regarded as one of the better prime-age pass rushers available.
- Can contribute against both run and pass rather than functioning only as a designated rusher.

### COACHING STAFF

Crennel and the defensive staff see potential value because Bennett can give Jacksonville:

- edge snaps on early downs;
- four-man pass-rush value;
- movement inside on passing downs;
- flexibility without forcing the defense into one front.

The fit is attractive because The Prowl does not require pressure to come from blitz volume.

### STONE VIEW

**Priority:** first premium external call.

Stone is willing to spend materially here because Jacksonville lacks dependable pressure.

He is **not** treating Bennett as a guaranteed sack total or future star.

### MARKET ESTIMATE

**Jacksonville projection:** roughly **1 year / $5.5M–$6.0M**, with an internal walk-away around **$6.5M** for a one-year structure unless new market information changes the value equation.

This is Jacksonville's estimate before agent negotiations.

### MARKET UNKNOWN

- Bennett's actual asking price;
- whether he wants a one-year prove-it deal;
- whether he prefers another club/scheme/location;
- medical information not legitimately available to Jacksonville;
- competing offers.

If Jacksonville's medical process identifies a material issue, that new information enters the branch when communicated. Stone does not know later medical reporting in advance.

# 3. Interior defensive-line value board

Jacksonville needs enough interior depth to let Crennel build the front he actually wants.

## Sen'Derrick Marks — DT

### PRO PERSONNEL / COACHING STAFF

The attraction is age, movement and a chance to buy a developmental interior player without paying established-star money.

### STONE VIEW

**Tier:** value pursuit.

**Preferred structure:** one-year prove-it contract with limited guaranteed exposure.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** approximately **$1.0M–$2.0M** total depending on guarantees and competing interest.

This is not a prediction of Marks' future performance.

## Roy Miller — DT

### COACHING STAFF

The staff views Miller primarily as a run-defense / interior-rotation option rather than a pass-rush solution.

### STONE VIEW

**Tier:** value pursuit / role player.

**Preferred structure:** modest two-year agreement if the guarantee remains controlled.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** roughly **$2M–$3M per year** only if the role and guarantee make sense; otherwise use cheaper rotation alternatives.

## Other interior market context

| Player | Football context | Stone posture |
|---|---|---|
| Desmond Bryant | Younger interior player with stronger public market | Monitor; likely costs more than Jacksonville needs to pay |
| Mike DeVito | Dependable veteran front player | Scheme/price contingency |
| Terrance Knighton | Jacksonville's own expiring player | Evaluate independently; prior club tenure does not create automatic retention |

Marks and Miller are not automatically superior to these alternatives. They are the current value targets Stone wants Caldwell to price.

# 4. Cornerback board

## Brent Grimes — CB

### PUBLIC FACT

- High-level prior starting tape.
- Entering market after a torn Achilles cost him most of 2012.
- Public rankings still place him among the stronger available corners, with injury creating uncertainty.

### STONE VIEW

Grimes is **not** the default second premium signing.

He is a contingency if Jacksonville does not spend its major external money on Bennett or if the market becomes unexpectedly favorable.

### MARKET ESTIMATE

Jacksonville's pre-market projection is a short-term deal in roughly the **$4M–$6.5M** range depending on medical comfort and guarantees.

This is an estimate, not knowledge of his eventual contract.

## Alan Ball — CB

### PRO PERSONNEL

Veteran depth candidate with experience across secondary roles and special teams.

### STONE VIEW

**Tier:** value / competition signing.

Jacksonville should not sell Ball as a solved CB1 problem.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** low-cost veteran range; roughly **$0.8M–$1.5M** depending on guarantees and role.

## Broader corner market

# 5. Running-back depth

## Justin Forsett — RB

### COACHING STAFF

Tice/Spencer see potential value as:

- experienced reserve back;
- passing-game contributor;
- change-of-pace option;
- insurance while Maurice Jones-Drew's health/contract situation is evaluated.

### STONE VIEW

**Tier:** value depth.

Do not spend meaningful starter money.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** roughly veteran-minimum to **$1.25M** range depending on guarantees and competing role.

Signing Forsett would reduce the urgency to draft a reserve running back, but it would **not** prohibit Jacksonville from drafting one if value is compelling.

## Market alternatives

Jacksonville does not need to chase the top running-back market simply because players are available.

Higher-profile backs should be evaluated against:

- Jones-Drew's current contract;
- positional value;
- draft alternatives;
- whether the player materially improves the offense rather than merely creating name value.

# 6. Own free agents

Own-player familiarity is information, not an automatic retention bonus.

## Brad Meester — C

### COACHING STAFF

Tice/Yarno value:

- familiarity with Jacksonville protections/personnel;
- veteran line communication;
- potential transition value while a younger center develops.

### STONE VIEW

**Preferred structure:** one year.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** approximately **$1M–$2M** depending on guarantee and role.

No starting job is contractually promised.

## Daryl Smith — LB

### PUBLIC / CLUB FACT

- Long Jacksonville tenure.
- Limited 2012 availability because of injury.
- Substantial prior starting tape.

### COACHING STAFF

Crennel/Bush should evaluate him as a football player in the new defense rather than retain him for institutional familiarity.

### STONE VIEW

Offer a short deal only if:

- medical evaluation is satisfactory;
- football role is clear;
- guarantee remains controlled.

**MARKET ESTIMATE:** roughly **$2M–$4M per year** on a short structure, highly sensitive to medical/market feedback.

If another club offers materially more security or money, Stone is willing to let him leave.

# 8. What Stone receives when the market opens

Once the March 9 negotiating window begins, Caldwell / football administration may report actual branch information such as:

- agent asking price;
- preferred term length;
- guarantee demand;
- whether Jacksonville is in the player's preferred group;
- whether another club has made a communicated offer;
- medical visit status where legally/appropriately shared;
- requested role;
- deadline to respond.

Only then may this file replace **MARKET ESTIMATE** with **BRANCH MARKET INFORMATION**.

Stone does not get:

- another club's private cap sheet;
- an agent's uncommunicated bottom line;
- the player's private preference before it is communicated;
- knowledge of the contract the player signed in real history.

---

# 9. Decision rule

For every free-agent decision, Stone should see:

1. the player's football role and uncertainty;
2. Jacksonville's pro-scout/coaching evaluation;
3. the current cap impact from football administration;
4. the actual communicated market price;
5. credible alternatives at the position;
6. what opportunity cost the deal creates elsewhere.

The question is never merely, "Is this player good?"

It is:

> Is this player worth this role, this guarantee, this cap/cash commitment and the alternatives Jacksonville gives up to sign him?

That is the board Stone sees.
