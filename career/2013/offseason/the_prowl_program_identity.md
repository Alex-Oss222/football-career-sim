# THE PROWL

**Status:** User-established canon, 2026-09-18. This is Stone's actual adopted program identity for Jacksonville, entered into Document 3 §2.1 per §2.2's instruction that coaching identity is resolved directly into canon once the user decides it — not a discarded or legacy candidate. Full text preserved verbatim below; Document 3 §2.1 carries the summary and points here.

---

The Prowl is Jacksonville's operating standard under Alex Stone.

It is not a drill.

It is not punishment.

It is not the name of the conditioning program.

It is not something players yell before breaking a huddle.

It is the way Stone wants the organization to work.

Players. Coaches. Performance staff. Support staff. Head coach.

The same standard moves upward.

## The Year One Idea

Stone introduces the new program with one passage:

> And the day came
> when the risk
> to remain tight
> in a bud
> was more painful
> than the risk
> it took
> to blossom.

He does not turn it into a weekly quotation.

It establishes what the first year is about.

Jacksonville cannot become something different while protecting everything it used to be.

That does not mean changing things simply because they are old.

If something works, keep it.

If a player can play, use him.

If a teaching method works, teach it.

If a system fits the roster, there is no prize for replacing it.

But reputation, habit, comfort and history do not receive veto power over improvement.

That is where The Prowl begins.

# GOOD. BETTER. BEST.

This is the line Stone repeats.

Good is real.

Good matters.

Good also does not finish the conversation.

A player can play a good game and have four snaps he needs corrected.

A defense can allow seventeen points and find a coverage communication problem that will eventually cost it a game.

A coach can build a good plan and discover that one portion asked too much of the players.

A win can contain bad football.

A loss can contain things worth keeping.

The standard is:

**Good, Better, Best.**

Take what was good.

Find what can become better.

Work until better becomes the best version presently available.

Then start again.

Stone does not manufacture criticism merely to prove that the standard is demanding.

Sometimes the answer after film is: that was done correctly.

Keep it.

The purpose is improvement, not permanent dissatisfaction.

# WHAT THE PROWL EXPECTS

## Know Your Job

Every player should know what belongs to him.

Assignment.

Technique.

Adjustment.

Communication.

Situation.

A player who does not understand something is expected to ask.

A coach who cannot explain something clearly is expected to teach it differently.

Confusion is not toughness.

Complexity is not intelligence.

The Jaguars would rather execute six answers correctly than carry twelve answers they cannot use under pressure.

## Be Ready Before You Are Needed

Preparation happens before the opportunity.

Backups prepare as if their role can change on the next snap.

Young players do not wait until they are promoted to learn the job.

Veterans do not receive permission to stop studying because they have seen the coverage before.

Coaches do not install a change Friday night and blame the player Sunday when it is not clean.

Everybody is responsible for arriving ready for the work assigned to him.

## Fundamentals Travel

Scheme changes.

Opponents change.

Personnel changes.

Footwork, leverage, tackling, ball security, route depth, hand placement, pursuit angles, protection rules and communication still matter.

Jacksonville does not become so interested in being multiple that it becomes technically unreliable.

The better the player, the greater the expectation that his fundamentals survive pressure.

## Tell the Truth Quickly

Stone does not want delayed honesty.

If the call was bad, the coach says it.

If the technique was bad, the player owns it.

If the teaching was unclear, the staff fixes it.

If an opponent presented something Jacksonville was not prepared to handle, the staff identifies why.

Nobody protects a meeting-room idea after the field has disproved it.

Nobody needs to invent blame when the answer is already visible on film.

## Practice the Problem

Difficult football should not arrive for the first time on game day.

Third-and-long.

Backed up.

Low red zone.

Four minute.

Two minute.

Sudden change.

Noise.

Pressure.

Personnel changes.

A starter leaving the game.

A communication failure.

The point is not to make practice miserable.

The point is to make Sunday's problems recognizable.

## Earn Responsibility

Talent gets attention.

Execution earns responsibility.

A starting job is not lifetime tenure.

A veteran does not lose his place because a coach wants to prove a cultural point.

A young player does not wait simply because somebody older arrived first.

Roles move when the football evidence says they should move.

Competition is supposed to improve the team, not divide it.

Players are expected to help teammates learn even when those teammates compete for their snaps.

## The Standard Goes Upward

Stone is inside The Prowl.

So are his coordinators.

If the staff expects players to be prepared, the plan has to be prepared.

If the staff expects players to accept correction, coaches accept correction.

If Jacksonville demands physical readiness, the club has to provide competent strength work, nutrition, recovery and medical support.

If Stone makes the wrong fourth-down decision, it does not become somebody else's failure Monday morning.

Authority increases accountability.

It does not remove it.

# DEFENSIVE PHILOSOPHY

Stone does not hire a defensive coordinator to operate Alex Stone's defense.

He hires one to build a Jacksonville defense within several non-negotiable team principles.

## Make Offenses Earn the Field

Jacksonville's first defensive objective is eliminating free offense.

That means:

* explosive plays caused by busted assignments;
* uncovered receivers;
* missed tackles that turn ordinary gains into explosives;
* avoidable pre-snap penalties;
* broken contain;
* poor communication against shifts and motion;
* uncontrolled quarterback scrambles;
* cheap red-zone touchdowns.

The opponent is allowed to make a good play.

Jacksonville is not allowed to repeatedly make the opponent's job easier.

## Stop the Run Without Becoming Desperate to Stop the Run

Stone wants a defense capable of fitting the run correctly without committing so many resources that the passing game becomes simple.

Defensive linemen understand their gaps and combinations.

Linebackers fit with disciplined leverage.

Edges know when they are responsible for the quarterback, the back, boot action or contain.

Safeties know when they are entering the fit and what coverage consequence accompanies it.

The defense does not receive credit for putting nine defenders near the line of scrimmage and calling itself physical.

The objective is sound run defense that allows the rest of the defense to remain functional.

## Make the Quarterback Confirm the Picture

Jacksonville does not need to become an exotic defense on every snap.

It does want the quarterback to work after the ball is snapped.

The coordinator can use:

* late safety movement;
* disguise;
* simulated pressure;
* creepers;
* overloaded presentations;
* four-man rushes from changing locations;
* man coverage;
* zone;
* match principles;
* split-safety structures;
* single-high structures.

The presentation can change more often than the underlying rules.

The objective is not confusion for its own sake.

Jacksonville wants its own defenders playing faster than the opposing quarterback.

## Pressure With a Reason

There is no blitz quota.

Pressure can come from four.

It can come from five.

It can come from showing six and bringing four.

It can come from personnel.

It can come from making the offensive line identify the front twice.

Stone's question is not, "How aggressive are we?"

It is:

**What problem are we creating for the protection and what are we giving up to create it?**

If Jacksonville's front four is winning, the defense does not need to send extra rushers merely to establish an identity.

If pressure is necessary, it should attack something the staff has identified.

## Tackling Is Part of the Scheme

The call is not successful because the whiteboard says somebody should be tackled.

Defenders close under control.

They maintain leverage.

They bring help toward the football.

The first defender has a job.

The second defender has a job.

Strip attempts come after the tackle has been secured unless the situation clearly allows otherwise.

Creating turnovers matters.

Gambling away sound defense in search of turnovers is different.

## Speed Comes From Recognition

Stone does not want defenders running faster in the wrong direction.

Young defenders receive enough information to play.

As their recognition improves, the staff can expand their responsibility.

The defense should eventually be able to change presentation without forcing eleven players to learn a different defense every week.

## Situational Defense Is Its Own Skill

Jacksonville explicitly practices:

third down;

red zone;

low red zone;

goal line;

two minute;

four minute;

backed-up offense;

sudden change;

end of half;

end of game.

Stone expects the defensive coordinator to know which calls the unit trusts in those situations.

A defense that plays well for fifty yards and repeatedly fails on third down is not playing complete defensive football.

A defense that allows yards and repeatedly forces field goals may be doing something valuable.

The situation matters.

# THE DEFENSIVE PERSONALITY

Jacksonville should look difficult to play against without trying to look angry.

The desired defense is:

disciplined before the snap;

fast after recognition;

physical without losing leverage;

multiple without becoming confused;

aggressive when the situation rewards aggression;

patient enough to make the offense execute another snap;

capable of changing the quarterback's picture without changing its own rules every five minutes.

The best version of the defense is not the defense with the most blitzes.

It is the defense that forces an offense to keep solving problems.

# DISCIPLINE APPROACH

Stone does not use The Prowl as a punishment system.

Nobody "runs The Prowl."

Nobody performs a ceremonial conditioning punishment because he dropped a pass or threw an interception.

Mistakes are classified before they are punished.

## Football Mistake

A missed technique, incorrect read or physical loss is coached.

Identify it.

Correct it.

Rep it.

Check whether the correction held.

A player does not run because he used the wrong footwork.

His footwork gets corrected.

## Preparation Failure

Not knowing an assignment that should have been known is different.

The immediate consequence is football-related.

A player may lose the rep.

A backup may receive the rep.

The player may be required to complete additional meeting or walkthrough work.

Repeated preparation failures can reduce a package or role because the staff cannot call football the player cannot reliably execute.

## Effort Failure

A genuine effort failure is addressed directly.

Stone first wants the film and the player's explanation.

If there is no physical or assignment reason for it, the issue becomes playing responsibility.

One bad snap does not automatically define the player.

Repeated loafs can change playing time.

## Conduct Failure

Late meetings, missed responsibilities, disruptive behavior and violations of team rules are handled according to severity and club policy.

Stone controls football consequences within his authority.

Formal fines, suspensions or employment matters go through the proper organizational process.

He does not improvise financial punishment because he is angry.

## Repeat Failure

This is where consequences increase.

Stone is patient with correction.

He is much less patient with the same correctable problem appearing after the player has been given the answer, the repetitions and enough time to fix it.

At that point the question becomes whether the team can continue assigning that responsibility to him.

## Dishonesty

Stone can tolerate a player being wrong.

He has far less tolerance for a player or coach hiding information that the team needs.

A missed assignment can be corrected.

A coach disguising an installation problem or a player knowingly giving a false explanation creates a second problem.

The response becomes more serious because the staff cannot correct information it cannot trust.

## Stars and Veterans

The standard does not change.

The conversation might.

A veteran may receive a longer private discussion because his experience provides information the staff needs.

A star may receive more direct contact from Stone before a major role change.

Neither receives immunity.

## Public Discipline

Stone does not perform discipline for reporters.

If a matter can remain private, it remains private.

If playing status changes, he states the football reality without inventing a character judgment.

If league or club rules require disclosure, the club discloses what is required.

The correction belongs to the team before it belongs to the public.

# MEDIA VOICE

Stone does not sell The Prowl every Wednesday.

That is important.

If reporters hear the phrase fifteen times a week, it becomes branding instead of culture.

His normal media voice remains understated.

Specific.

Dry when appropriate.

Football first.

He answers what he can answer.

He protects what should remain private.

He does not pretend every question is disrespectful.

He does not use the podium to coach through a player.

## Opening Introduction

"The Prowl isn't a slogan we're asking people to believe today. It's a standard we're going to have to demonstrate. There are good players here. There are good people here. Our job is to make the football better. Good, better, best. That's going to take some time and it's going to take work."

## Asked What "Good, Better, Best" Means

"It means good counts. We're not going to manufacture problems because somebody played well. But good doesn't mean finished. We grade it, we find the next thing available to us and we work on that."

## After a Win

"We won the game. That's the first thing. There are also things on that tape that will get us beat if we leave them there. Both can be true."

## After a Loss

"We weren't good enough. Start with me. I approved the plan and I made the game-management decisions. We'll find the football reasons, correct them and make the changes we need to make."

## After a Player Makes a Costly Mistake

"He made a mistake on the play. There were other things on that play that we have to coach too. We'll correct it inside the building. I'm not turning one snap into a judgment about the player."

## Asked About Internal Discipline

"He didn't meet the standard on that issue. We've addressed it. If it changes his availability, I'll tell you. The rest stays in the building."

## Asked About a Defensive Coordinator Blitzing More

"I don't care whether we look aggressive. I care whether we're putting the offense in difficult situations. Sometimes that's five rushers. Sometimes four does the job better."

## Asked Whether The Prowl Means Jacksonville Will Be More Physical

"It should mean we're more reliable. Physical football is part of that. So is communication. So is tackling. So is knowing where to line up. I'm not interested in looking tough before the snap and being wrong after it."

## Asked About a Young Player Taking a Veteran's Job

"We don't give somebody a job because he's young and we don't protect a job because somebody's been here a long time. We play the people who give us the best chance to execute what we're asking them to do."

## Asked About High Expectations

"Expectations don't move the ball. Work does. We know what we're trying to build. Today we're responsible for today's part of it."

# HOW STONE USES THE BLOSSOM LINE

The quotation belongs primarily to the beginning of the program.

Stone uses it when he first explains why Jacksonville is changing.

He does not put flowers on the wall.

He does not explain Jaguars football through a botanical metaphor for the next four years.

Its purpose is more specific:

there comes a point when preserving the familiar costs more than changing it.

That applies to an organization entering a new era.

After that message has been delivered, Stone moves back to football.

The permanent language is simpler:

**Good. Better. Best.**

# WHAT THE PROWL SHOULD FEEL LIKE AFTER A YEAR

Not louder.

Not meaner.

Not covered in slogans.

More dependable.

Players know their jobs.

Coaches teach the same answer.

Young players understand how to earn larger roles.

Veterans understand that experience gives them responsibility rather than immunity.

The defense communicates before the snap and tackles after it.

The offense can change presentation without losing execution.

Special teams receive real practice time.

The staff tells Stone when something is wrong before it becomes visible on Sunday.

Stone admits when one of his decisions was wrong.

Players receive specific corrections rather than general disappointment.

Winning does not suspend coaching.

Losing does not send the organization searching for a new identity every Monday.

That is when The Prowl has become real.
