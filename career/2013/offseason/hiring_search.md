## Turn 1 result — user's priority order and terms (recorded 2013-01-14)

San Diego dropped (real hire already agreed the day before Stone's candidacy opened; treated as closed). Priority order and conditions for the remaining four:

1. **Jacksonville** — sells a 4-year rebuild. Non-negotiables: he calls offensive plays, picks his own staff, no Gabbert guarantee, no forced QB at #2, controls the depth chart, minimum 4 guaranteed years, ownership funds a strong staff (he'll take less personal salary to fund it). Caldwell keeps contracts/cap/scouting/draft administration; QB decisions collaborative with meaningful HC veto. Walks if: Gabbert mandated as starter, QB forced at #2, GM picks coordinators, staff underfunded, play-calling taken away, contract under 3 years without real guarantees, or ownership expects an immediate playoff push.
2. **Arizona** — sells a QB reset built on offensive-line investment first. Same play-calling/staff/contract asks. No Kolb guarantee, no forced QB at #7; OL investment must be an organizational priority. Walks if: Kolb mandated as starter, Keim forces a QB at #7 regardless of grade, no real OL investment, or play-calling/staff control denied.
3. **Philadelphia** — sells the fastest offensive rebuild (existing pieces, less demolition). Vick's existing contract is not assumed guaranteed nor is a starting job; Foles gets a real evaluation, kept unless there's a real reason not to. No forced QB at #4. Prefers a 5-year deal, will accept 4. Roseman keeps contracts/cap/scouting/trades; QB and top personnel decisions are joint. Walks if: Vick's deal/starting job is mandated as-is, play-calling is taken away, or Roseman picks coordinators.
4. **Chicago** — sells an offensive repair job on a 10-6 roster, not a rebuild. 2013 is explicitly Cutler's evaluation year — no extension before Stone coaches him a season. OL is the top investment priority; more conservative on defense (don't change what's working). Same play-calling/staff/4-year asks. Walks if: Cutler gets an extension before evaluation, Emery picks the offensive staff, or the organization expects only "beat 10 wins" rather than genuine offensive improvement.

**Universal terms across all four:** play-calling, staff selection, staff funding (traded against his own salary), no forced/guaranteed starting QB, no mandated QB at a specific draft slot before evaluation, control of the depth chart and game-day decisions, GM retains contracts/cap/scouting while QB/top personnel calls are collaborative, minimum 4 guaranteed years, and Year 1 expectations agreed before signing, not redefined after.

## Turn 2 — Market-update turn (January 15, 2013)

**Jacksonville — substantive, fast, mostly receptive.** Khan and Caldwell moved within a day; both are motivated (Khan's own words, publicly: "we simply must do better for our fans," and Caldwell — 9 days into his own job — wants a program-building HC partner, not a subordinate). Real, checkable point in Stone's favor: Jacksonville's actual draft board at #2 was never QB-driven (the real 2013 class was QB-thin, and the real Jaguars used that exact pick on an offensive tackle) — his "no forced QB at #2" ask costs them nothing they wanted anyway. Caldwell agrees to the full division of labor as proposed (he keeps contracts/cap/scouting/draft administration, Stone gets scheme/staff/depth chart/play-calling, QB decisions collaborative with real HC veto) and to the staff-funding trade (Stone's salary concession redirected into assistant pay). One real sticking point: **the contract term.** Khan is willing to do 4 years but is not willing to fully guarantee all 4 up front for a coach whose only head-coaching experience is one interim half-season, however successful. Caldwell's actual offer on the table: 4 years, with years 1-2 fully guaranteed and years 3-4 vesting (converting to fully guaranteed if specific, negotiable performance/organizational-progress benchmarks are met after year 2) — not the "3 years, no real guarantees" scenario that was an explicit walk-away condition, but not full 4-year guarantees either.

**Arizona, Philadelphia, Chicago — real clock, no substantive engagement yet.** All three have acknowledged interest in principle but nothing concrete has been offered. This matters because, in the history this simulation diverges from, all three real jobs closed within the next one to two days (Chicago and Philadelphia's real hires landed January 16, Arizona's January 17). Nothing has yet happened in this simulation to say those doors are still open by the time a Jacksonville decision is made — they simply haven't reported back yet.

### Your call

Two live questions:
1. **Jacksonville's contract counter** — take the 4-year deal with years 1-2 guaranteed and years 3-4 vesting on stated benchmarks (and if so, what benchmarks would you actually accept), push back and hold out for a fuller guarantee, or treat this as a dealbreaker under your own walk-away terms?
2. **The other three** — given the real clock, do you want to press Arizona/Philadelphia/Chicago for a faster answer now (risking looking like you're using them as leverage against Jacksonville), or let Jacksonville's answer resolve first even if it costs you a shot at the other three?
