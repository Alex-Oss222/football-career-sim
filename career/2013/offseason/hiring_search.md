## Entry 1 — Criteria freeze

**Recorded:** Opening of January 14, 2013, before review of the hiring-search brief
**Scope:** Jacksonville, Arizona, Chicago, Philadelphia, and San Diego
**Freeze state:** CLOSED

### Jacksonville Jaguars

**Decision structure and situation**

- Owner Shad Khan had hired David Caldwell as general manager, and Caldwell was the primary football counterpart for the new head coach.
- The club was beginning a new GM–head coach partnership after a 2-14 season, with an unsettled quarterback position and a broad roster reconstruction ahead.
- Jacksonville held the second selection position in the coming draft based on the completed standings.

**Documented criteria and constraints**

- Caldwell publicly said prior head-coaching experience was not required and that he sought the right person rather than a predetermined experience category.
- The visible job demands were a functional partnership with the new GM, whole-program leadership, credible quarterback evaluation and development, staff construction, and roster-development judgment.
- The public field was broad and coordinator-heavy. No completed Jacksonville interview had been reported by the cutoff; several candidates were subjects of planned or reported interest, and candidates attached to active playoff teams remained schedule-restricted.

**Labeled inference**

- **Inference from the documented organizational reset:** the successful candidate would need to articulate an operating structure that Caldwell could evaluate as workable. This does not imply a preferred scheme or a grant of personnel control.

**Unknown private weighting**

- Candidate order, offensive-versus-defensive preference, contract budget and term, personnel authority, staff-retention preference, and the weight Caldwell placed on prior relationships all remain unknown.

### Arizona Cardinals

**Decision structure and situation**

- Team president Michael Bidwill and newly promoted general manager Steve Keim were leading a search after the dismissal of Ken Whisenhunt and Rod Graves.
- The club had finished 5-11 after a severe second-half collapse. Quarterback instability and offensive-line/run-game functionality were visible football problems.
- Coach–GM fit was publicly emphasized. Authority structure was legitimately negotiable, but no candidate was promised personnel control.

**Documented criteria and constraints**

- The supported needs were coach–GM compatibility, quarterback evaluation and development, organizational stabilization, and improved offensive operation.
- Keim publicly framed the roster task as a retool rather than an assumed total teardown.
- Ray Horton, Mike McCoy, and Jay Gruden had documented places in the pre-cutoff process; a reported strong push for McCoy remained attributed reporting rather than a fixed private ranking.

**Labeled inference**

- **Inference from the public football problems:** a candidate's quarterback, protection, and offensive-operation plan would be material to the evaluation. It does not establish that only an offensive coach could satisfy the search.

**Unknown private weighting**

- Final candidate ranking, mandatory scheme, Horton's retention, exact authority, contract terms, later additions to the search, and the relative weight of immediate repair versus longer-term program design remain unknown.

### Chicago Bears

**Decision structure and situation**

- General manager Phil Emery held final selection responsibility, with chairman George McCaskey and president/CEO Ted Phillips expected to meet a small finalist group.
- Chicago had finished 10-6 but missed the playoffs. The organization sought a changed operating trajectory while remaining competitive, with protection and offensive consistency among the visible football issues.

**Documented criteria and constraints**

- Emery publicly sought demonstrated excellence in the candidate's current role without limiting the search by side of the ball or level.
- He identified organization, leadership, administration, command of operational detail, energy, building-wide unity, credible media representation, consistency under adversity, and a strong proposed staff package.
- The stated end was consistent playoff and championship contention. Chicago maintained a wide field and was willing to wait through playoff scheduling restrictions.

**Labeled inference**

- **Inference from the published process:** staff construction and operational detail would carry unusual interview importance because Emery expressly named them, not because of anything in Stone's dossier.

**Unknown private weighting**

- The weighting among the public criteria, any preferred scheme, candidate order and finalist list, the exact role of the quarterback in the decision, staff-retention expectations, authority, and contract terms remain unknown.

### Philadelphia Eagles

**Decision structure and situation**

- Chairman/CEO Jeffrey Lurie led the process with general manager Howie Roseman and team president Don Smolenski after a 4-12 season and the end of Andy Reid's tenure.
- The roster included both an established veteran quarterback under a major existing obligation and a young quarterback who had completed his rookie season. Later contract and roster decisions were not yet known.
- Philadelphia publicly characterized the search as exhaustive and already had the deepest documented interview activity among the five clubs.

**Documented criteria and constraints**

- Lurie's published framework emphasized high-level leadership; short-, medium-, and long-term strategy; forward thinking and innovation; attention to detail; a high-caliber staff and colleagues; toughness and discipline; strategic personnel understanding; and an ability to represent the Philadelphia football environment.
- Several interviews had been completed or reported by the cutoff. Other candidates had become unavailable after publicly remaining in college jobs.
- The broad search did not establish a single required scheme or archetype.

**Labeled inference**

- **Inference from the published criteria and broad field:** Philadelphia could test program design, adaptability, and staff quality across candidates with very different backgrounds. Breadth alone reveals no favorite.

**Unknown private weighting**

- Candidate ranking, preferred offensive system, quarterback plan, personnel authority, staff-retention expectations, contract terms, and the relative weight assigned to innovation versus prior head-coaching evidence remain unknown.

### San Diego Chargers

**Decision structure and situation**

- The organization hired Tom Telesco as general manager before completing the head-coaching search. Telesco joined a group operating under Dean Spanos with John Spanos, Ed McGuire, and consultant Ron Wolf involved.
- San Diego had finished 7-9 and missed the playoffs for a third consecutive season. Quarterback protection and offensive operation around Philip Rivers were visible issues.
- Stone's long Chargers playing history is an established résumé fact, but the decision-makers' view of that connection is not established.

**Documented criteria and constraints**

- The supported needs were a functional partnership with the new GM, reversal of a multi-year decline, and a credible quarterback/offensive-line plan.
- Leadership and staff construction were necessarily relevant head-coach functions, but the club did not publish a detailed weighting comparable to Chicago's or Philadelphia's.
- The public field included candidates from several backgrounds. Its offensive tilt did not prove an offense-only mandate.

**Labeled inference**

- **Inference from the GM-first process:** Telesco's confidence in a workable football partnership would be material. This does not establish favoritism toward a former colleague or any particular candidate type.

**Unknown private weighting**

- Candidate ordering, Telesco's private preferences, the value or cost assigned to Stone's franchise history, exact scheme, personnel authority, staff expectations, salary, and contract term remain unknown.

### Freeze-wide controls

- Stone's résumé may be assessed only as demonstrated evidence: his interim season, coordinator and play-calling work, team-level supervision, staff leadership, personnel participation, and experience on both sides of the ball.
- His untested permanent-head-coach program construction, full staff assembly, long-term young-quarterback development, and final personnel authority remain legitimate uncertainties rather than proof of inability.
- No interview answer, scheme proposal, coordinator target, authority demand, contract term, promise, motive, or preference is inferred from the dossier.
- Biography length, confidence, persuasive language, academic credentials, playing honors, championships, and famous professional overlap receive no hidden bonus. Equivalent evidence receives equivalent treatment for every candidate.
- Every unsupported private criterion remains unknown. No eventual real hire, later staff, later roster decision, or later coaching outcome may fill the gap.

**Freeze closure:** The criteria above were fixed before opening `career/2013/offseason/hiring_search_brief/`. Later simulation-side developments may change an organization's position only through a dated interaction grounded in this record.

### Universal proposed terms

- Stone retains offensive play-calling, coaching-staff selection, depth-chart control, and game-day decision authority.
- Stone is willing to reduce his own compensation in exchange for a stronger assistant-coach budget.
- No incumbent quarterback may be guaranteed the starting job, and no quarterback selection at a specified draft slot may be mandated before Stone evaluates the position.
- The general manager retains contract, salary-cap, scouting, and draft-administration authority. Quarterback and other leading personnel decisions are collaborative, with Stone holding a substantive rather than symbolic voice.
- Stone seeks at least four fully guaranteed contract years.
- First-season expectations must be agreed before signing rather than redefined afterward.
- A partial or performance-vesting guarantee does not satisfy the requested full guarantee. The brief expressly withholds authority to accept, counter, or leave over such a proposal.
- There are no automatic acceptance or rejection instructions. Every offer must return to the user.

### Team-specific substance

**Jacksonville**

- Present the assignment as a four-year rebuild.
- Do not guarantee Blaine Gabbert the starting job or mandate a quarterback with the second selection.
- Caldwell retains the GM functions listed above; quarterback decisions are collaborative, with Stone holding a substantive head-coach veto.
- Walk away if the club mandates Gabbert, forces the named draft outcome, lets the GM choose coordinators, underfunds the staff, removes play-calling, offers fewer than three years or no real guarantees, or demands an immediate first-season playoff push.
- A partial/vesting guarantee between the requested term and the express walk-away floor is unresolved and must come back to the user.

**Arizona**

- Present a quarterback reset supported by offensive-line investment first.
- Do not guarantee Kevin Kolb the starting job or mandate a quarterback with the seventh selection.
- Offensive-line investment must be a genuine organizational priority.
- Keim retains the GM functions listed above; quarterback decisions are collaborative, with Stone holding a substantive head-coach veto.
- Walk away if the club mandates Kolb, forces the named draft outcome, refuses genuine line investment, removes play-calling, or denies staff-selection authority.

**Philadelphia**

- Present the job as a faster offensive rebuild because useful pieces are already present and less demolition is required.
- Michael Vick's existing contract and starting role are not presumed fixed. Nick Foles receives a genuine evaluation and remains unless a football evaluation supports a change.
- Do not mandate a quarterback with the fourth selection.
- Stone prefers five years but is willing to accept a four-year term, subject to the universal full-guarantee request.
- Roseman retains contracts, cap, scouting, and trades; quarterback and other leading personnel decisions are joint.
- Walk away if Vick's contract or starting job is mandated without evaluation, play-calling is removed, or Roseman selects the coordinators.

**Chicago**

- Present the job as an offensive repair of an already competitive roster, not a rebuild.
- Treat 2013 as Jay Cutler's evaluation season; do not extend him before Stone has coached him for that season.
- Make the offensive line the leading investment priority and preserve rather than dismantle the functioning defensive foundation.
- Emery retains the GM functions listed above; leading personnel decisions are collaborative with a substantive head-coach voice.
- Walk away if Cutler is extended before the evaluation season, Emery selects the offensive staff, or expectations are reduced to exceeding the prior win total rather than achieving genuine offensive improvement.

### Matters the brief does not authorize

- It supplies no user statement addressing Stone's knowledge and conduct concerning the Saints bounty matter.
- It supplies no new promises, no permission to modify a non-negotiable, no authority to accept a counter outside the requested terms, and no automatic offer disposition.

### Batched interview agenda and unresolved Stone decision

Routine outreach has reached the interview stage, but the brief expressly says there is no standing interview script. Each active club requires material candidate-controlled answers before the organization can resolve finalists, negotiations, or offers. The overlapping agenda is:

- the offensive system Stone proposes and how it adapts to the club's current quarterbacks and line;
- defensive and special-teams structure, including what Stone would preserve and what authority coordinators would hold;
- credible coordinator and core-assistant targets, plus alternatives if preferred candidates are unavailable;
- practice, player-development, analytics, discipline, media, and game-management methods;
- how Stone will combine offensive play-calling with permanent whole-team oversight;
- how the proposed collaborative personnel process and requested head-coach veto work when Stone and the GM disagree; and
- what Stone knew about the Saints bounty program, when he knew it, and what he did.

Team-specific emphasis follows the frozen criteria: Jacksonville will press on building a program and staff from a broad reset; Arizona on quarterback/protection repair and coach–GM fit; Philadelphia on multi-horizon strategy, innovation, personnel adaptation, and staff quality; Chicago on detailed administration, the proposed staff package, offensive repair, and continuity under adversity.
