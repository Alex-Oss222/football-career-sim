# Player Onboarding and Offseason Development Framework — Jacksonville Jaguars

**Status:** Permanent organizational framework, established 2026-09-19. Separate from the event ledger — this file owns durable philosophy, process, and standards; it does not record what actually happened on any given day. Actual events (rookie minicamp, OTAs, mandatory minicamp, training camp, and any later phase) are recorded in their own dated output files and in `career/2013/ledger.md`, never by rewriting this document.

**Authority and scope:** Governs how Jacksonville onboards and develops every player it controls, from first contact through training camp. Connects two existing documents without rewriting either: `the_prowl_player_readiness_standard.md` remains the medical, physical, support, and CBA authority; `career/playbook/alex_stone_2013_offensive_playbook_iteration_i.md` remains the scheme authority. This file supplies the process and teaching philosophy that sits between them.

**Playbook rule, restated from `career/playbook/README.md`:** the only book issued in 2013 is Iteration I. The 2016-and-later evolution files stay locked — no later terminology, motion system, or answer leaks backward into 2013 teaching. The full Iteration I book is a reference manual handed to every player; it is not a demand that a player memorize it before his first practice, and possession of it does not mean the whole book is installed or testable.

**Who this applies to:** every player Jacksonville actually controls at the time a phase runs. The current working inventory is 75, but the inherited roster still has unresolved control status on many names — reconciling that control status determines the actual mailing/call list for each step below, not the raw inventory count.

## 1. Player welcome process

1. **Day 0 — send the welcome package.** Every current veteran, new veteran, draft pick, and UDFA signee receives the same organizational package at the same standard. The personalized cover letter changes by player; the standards inside do not. Package contents: Stone's welcome letter, a short Prowl handbook, physical-readiness expectations, player/coaching responsibilities, support-resource information, the active 2013 playbook (Iteration I), a phase-by-phase offseason roadmap, and position-specific material where available. State explicitly that having the full playbook does not mean the whole book is installed or immediately testable.

2. **Day 2 — Stone calls every player individually.** Purpose is not to quiz him. Confirm he received the package and playbook, then ask: what made sense, what didn't, what he likes, what concerns him, what he needs from the staff, how he learns best, what he thinks coaches frequently misunderstand about his game, what questions he has, and "What should I know about how to coach you?" Football-relevant feedback is recorded. Private financial, medical, counseling, or family details are not turned into personnel notes.

3. **Day 2-4 — position coaches call their own room.** Each position coach (and coordinator, for staff-level items) makes the same kind of individual call to every player in his own room — same spirit as Stone's call: confirm receipt, ask what's unclear about the position-specific material, ask how the player learns best, ask what the coach should know about coaching him. This is not a duplicate of Stone's call and does not replace it; it is the position coach building his own individual relationship and communication line with each player he will actually teach day to day. The same confidentiality rule applies: football-relevant feedback is recorded, private information is not.

4. **After the calls — staff closes the loop.** Every legitimate football question gets answered. If several players are confused by the same thing, the first assumption is that the teaching needs improvement, not that the players are slow. Position coaches receive relevant teaching notes surfaced by either their own calls or Stone's. Player Development receives logistical requests. Medical questions go to medical staff. Strength questions go to performance staff. Neither Stone nor a position coach becomes the clearinghouse for confidential information.

5. **Any later acquisition gets the same process.** A player joining in June, August, or during the season receives his package on arrival, gets Stone's follow-up call approximately two days later, and gets his position coach's call in the same window. Nobody skips onboarding because he wasn't part of the original roster.

The Day 2 calls (Stone's and each position coach's) are a relationship and communication tool. During any portion of the offseason where participation is voluntary, declining an optional coaching call cannot secretly become a roster or depth-chart penalty.

### What is in the pamphlet

| Section | Player is told |
|---|---|
| What Jacksonville expects | Know your job. Ask when you do not know. Communicate. Tell the truth quickly. Prepare for the role you have and the role you may inherit. Protect the football. Practice fundamentals. Help teammates learn even when they compete with you. |
| What Stone expects physically | Arrive prepared for your football job. Maintain your individualized playing-weight range. Build functional strength, movement, repeated-effort capacity and recovery habits. Report medical issues honestly. |
| What Jacksonville does **not** expect | One universal body type, one body-fat number, random punishment conditioning, pretending an injury is weakness, or instant mastery of an entire playbook. |
| What players should expect from coaches | Clear instruction, consistent terminology, specific correction, prepared practice plans, honest competition, direct answers, no public humiliation, and acknowledgment when the coaching was wrong or unclear. |
| Medical promise | Coaches determine football roles. Qualified medical staff determine diagnosis, restrictions and clearance. Stone does not overrule them. |
| Competition promise | Veteran status does not guarantee a job. Youth does not guarantee opportunity. Football evidence moves roles. |
| Support promise | Nutrition, strength, rehabilitation, mental-performance resources, confidential behavioral-health resources, financial education and Player Development support are available. Private use of those resources is not a depth-chart grade. |
| Learning philosophy | "Know the job, understand why, execute it, correct it, execute it again." Confusion is something the staff solves, not something the staff mocks. |
| Team standard | Good. Better. Best. Good is acceptable progress. Better means correction is taking hold. Best means the best version currently available, not perfection forever. |

### The Day 2 call (Stone, and its position-coach equivalent)

| Part | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Receipt check | "Did you get everything? Letter, standards packet and playbook?" |
| First reaction | "What did you think?" |
| Clarity | "What wasn't clear?" |
| Football input | "What do you think helps you play your best football?" |
| Learning | "How do you learn something fastest: meeting, film, walkthrough, individual rep, written notes, something else?" |
| Coaching relationship | "What should I know about coaching you?" |
| Role discussion | Explain that roles begin open unless already established, and opportunities will follow demonstrated execution. |
| Staff accountability | "What do you need from us to give you a fair chance to do your job?" |
| Support | Ask whether there is any football/logistical resource the organization can help arrange, without demanding personal disclosure. |
| Close | Clarify the next reporting/work phase and what the player is actually responsible for knowing at that point. |

A position coach's version of this call covers the same ground scoped to his own room. The important output is aggregate, not individual: if several players in a room (or across rooms, from Stone's calls) say the same term or teaching point is confusing, the assumption is that the teaching needs improvement, not that the players are slow. Position coaches get that finding as a teaching note, not as a grade on any one player.

## 2. The learning system

Every phase uses the same basic teaching cycle:

**Explain → Show → Walk → Rep → Correct → Rep again → Retain → Add complexity.**

Stone's playbook already supports this. Calls are modular. Concepts are taught as jobs rather than making players memorize isolated routes — the quarterback sees a stable concept even when a TE, WR, RB, or FB fills a different job within it.

One rule applies across the whole building: **do not install the next layer because the calendar says you can; install it because the previous layer is usable.** More plays installed is not automatically progress.

### Good, Better, Best as an evaluation scale

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Good** | Player knows where to line up, knows his assignment, communicates correctly and can execute the basic technique at the speed/contact level permitted in that phase. A mistake can happen without the entire concept collapsing. |
| **Better** | Player retains corrections, executes against changing looks, understands common adjustments, communicates without prompting and can reproduce his technique through repeated reps. |
| **Best for this phase** | Player plays fast because he understands the job, handles situational and personnel variation, self-corrects routine problems and can help the player next to him without losing his own assignment. |

Rookie minicamp expects **Good**, not Best. A rookie can make a physical mistake and still have a good practice. What should not be repeatedly accepted is not knowing where to line up after it has been taught clearly and corrected.

## 3. Phase-by-phase development framework

| Phase | Primary objective | What they learn | How they train | Evaluation standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial onboarding** | Establish common language and expectations | Prowl standards, organizational responsibilities, playbook navigation, terminology, position responsibilities, support resources | Reading, introductory communication, permitted orientation | Understand what the organization is asking and know where to get answers |
| **Rookie minicamp** | Give rookies a manageable NFL foundation and establish a baseline | Huddle/call mechanics, formation language, position labels, basic alignments, core technique, small portion of core scheme, special-teams fundamentals, professional routine | Meeting → walkthrough → individual → controlled team work as permitted → immediate correction | **Good.** Line up, communicate, know the assignment, show coachable technique, retain corrections |
| **Phase One offseason work** | Build the body that will execute the system | No artificial football-install burden beyond what rules permit | Strength, conditioning, rehabilitation and individualized physical work within CBA limits | Consistency, movement quality, strength development, recovery habits, medical honesty |
| **Phase Two** | Connect individual technique to football jobs | Position technique, individual assignments, formation recognition, limited group concepts and walkthrough-speed understanding permitted by the CBA | Individual/group teaching and permitted walkthrough work, no pretending this is full team competition | Correct technique and assignment understanding without needing full-speed offense-versus-defense reps |
| **OTAs / Phase Three** | Begin operating as units | Broader core offense/defense, personnel packages, shifts/motion, protection communication, situational introductions, special-teams units | Meetings, individual periods, group periods, team periods without live contact, film correction | Assignment reliability, communication, retention between practices and execution against changing pictures |
| **New-HC voluntary veteran minicamp, if separately used** | Give veterans an early common operating language | Base system, terminology, communication standards and core concepts | Only within the special new-head-coach CBA allowance and with voluntariness preserved | Teaching and alignment checkpoint, not a punishment or roster loyalty test |
| **Mandatory veteran minicamp** | Verify that the team can operate the system together | Core menu, major personnel families, base situational football, protections, checks, defensive adjustments and ST responsibilities | Higher-volume team installation within applicable minicamp limits, still observing contact rules | Move from knowing individual jobs toward coordinated unit execution |
| **Training camp** | Convert knowledge into competitive NFL football | Full core menu, selected weekly concepts, change-ups, situational football, pressure answers, tempo, substitution, red zone, third down, two minute, four minute, backed up, goal line, sudden change | Pads/contact as allowed, competitive periods, team periods, situational work, individual fundamentals, film, recovery and repeated-effort conditioning | Roles are now earned through football evidence. Assignment, technique, physical execution, communication, consistency, special teams and response to correction all matter |
| **Preseason** | Test whether camp learning transfers to games | Game operation, substitutions, communication, sideline adjustments and reduced game-plan packages | Actual game reps plus correction cycle | Can the player execute when the opponent is unscripted and consequences are real? |

### Offensive install progression

Rookie minicamp deliberately does not dump the entire Iteration I menu onto the field. Players first need call order, formations, strength, position labels, splits, cadence, core communication, and the idea that concepts consist of jobs — the **language before the library.** Then they get a narrow core package.

The playbook's long-term 2013 core is Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Outside Zone, Stick, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Four Verticals, Boot Flood, and the RB slow screen. That is the backbone; it does not mean every rookie must execute every variant immediately.

OTAs expand the same families instead of continuously introducing unrelated plays — personnel portability, shifts, motion, HALF/SCAT/SOLID protection concepts, and the major formation families become operational.

Veteran minicamp asks whether the offense can **communicate and transform**: same concept from different personnel, same personnel from different formations, basic pressure answers, and early situational work.

Training camp opens the real toolbox. CORE concepts must become dependable, WEEKLY concepts can compete for game-plan space, and CHANGE-UP calls earn their place only if the roster can execute them.

The future Stone offense does not leak backward. No later terminology, motion system, or answer from Iterations II through the 2025-2026 Mature System enters 2013 teaching (`career/playbook/README.md`'s active-iteration lock).

## 4. Physical-development progression

| Phase | Physical emphasis |
|---|---|
| Rookie minicamp | Establish baseline movement and football conditioning observations. Do not turn the weekend into a punishment test. |
| Offseason program | Individualized playing weight, functional strength, acceleration/deceleration, position movement, rehabilitation where required and sustainable recovery habits. |
| OTAs | Maintain physical development while introducing repeated football movement. Coaches and performance staff begin seeing whether technique survives accumulating reps. |
| Veteran minicamp | More repeated unit work. Communication and technique should remain intact as mental load rises. |
| Pre-camp break | Player receives an individualized camp-entry plan. The club tells him exactly what "arrive ready" means for his position. |
| Training camp reporting | Medically cleared, position-adjusted conditioning test with the standard written beforehand. Passing means ready to begin the expected camp workload. Failure creates an individualized plan and retest, not random punishment running. |
| Training camp | Football conditioning becomes the priority: repeated technique, recovery between periods, sustaining leverage/footwork/communication late in practice and recovering for the next workday. |

## 5. What changes as the calendar advances

At **rookie minicamp**, coaches are asking: "Can we teach this player?"

At **OTAs**, the question becomes: "Can he retain it and execute it with ten teammates around him?"

At **veteran minicamp**, it becomes: "Can the unit communicate and solve ordinary football problems without the coaches rescuing every rep?"

At **training camp**, it becomes: "Can we trust this player with responsibility when the opponent, fatigue, contact, substitutions and consequences are real?"

This creates a legitimate progression rather than using every camp as a smaller version of training camp.

## 6. Team culture: family dinners

Family inclusion is a recurring part of the offseason program, not a one-off event.

**Default cadence:** schedule one family-inclusive team dinner in each major football-development phase — rookie-minicamp window, offseason/OTA window, mandatory veteran-minicamp window, and training camp. Because training camp is longer, schedule an opening dinner and a mid-camp family night/dinner when the verified calendar and logistics permit.

The invitation standard is broad:

- every current Jacksonville player under verified club control is invited, even if the football work in that particular window is limited to a subgroup such as rookies;
- coaches and appropriate football/support staff are invited;
- each player or staff member may bring family or another meaningful guest;
- the club does not require one definition of "family";
- family/guest attendance is always voluntary;
- a voluntary football window does not become mandatory because a dinner is scheduled inside it;
- an excused football absence does not create a separate punitive dinner-attendance issue.

The dinners serve four purposes:

1. give families a real relationship with the organization rather than meeting it only during a problem;
2. let players, coaches, staff, partners, children, parents, and other guests know one another as people;
3. make Player Development and support resources easy to find;
4. give the team normal shared time that is not another meeting.

The dinners are deliberately **not** football evaluation events:

- no install presentation;
- no playbook quiz;
- no depth-chart announcement;
- no personnel meeting;
- no attendance grade;
- no conclusion drawn from a player's guest choice or family circumstance;
- no private family, financial, medical, or counseling information turned into a personnel note.

Stone may give a short welcome or thank-you. Coordinators and position coaches may introduce themselves. Player Development may provide contact information and quietly arrange follow-up support. The social event must stay social.

The phase-specific plans own the practical execution:

- `rookie_minicamp/plan.md` — Rookie Welcome Family Dinner, open to the full current team and families while keeping veteran involvement social/voluntary.
- `otas/plan.md` — full-team offseason/OTA family dinner.
- `mandatory_minicamp/plan.md` — Veteran Minicamp Family Dinner.
- `training_camp/plan.md` — Camp Opening Family Dinner plus a Mid-Camp Family Night/Dinner when logistics permit.

When any dinner actually occurs, record it in that phase's output file and, if materially relevant to the season chronology, the season ledger. Do not rewrite this durable framework to record attendance or anecdotes.
