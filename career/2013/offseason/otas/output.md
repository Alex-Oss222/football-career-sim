## May 13-15 — OTA block 1

**Event status:** COMPLETE through the end of May 15, 2013.
**Event boundary:** This record closes only the May 13-15 voluntary OTA block. The May 20-21 block has **not** been run. The missed April 16-18 voluntary veteran minicamp remains a continuity gap and was not backfilled.

### Control, onboarding, participation and medical gate

Jacksonville opened the block with the reconciled **64-player controlled roster**. Before football participation, the staff completed the established welcome process for every controlled veteran who did not already have a closed onboarding record: each received the organizational standards package, support information and complete active 2013 Iteration I playbook; Stone and the applicable position coach completed the individual follow-up contacts. Repeated requests for simpler cross-room vocabulary were routed back to the coordinators. The outreach created no role, attendance or character grade.

Qualified medical personnel supplied a current football-use status before work began. Daryl Smith was cleared for the permitted OTA work and participated without a communicated restriction. Brent Grimes participated in meetings and medically directed rehabilitation/individual work, but was withheld from offense-versus-defense team periods throughout this block; the staff received no new diagnosis or return date. Every other controlled player was cleared for the work assigned during the block and participated. Rookie-minicamp clearance was not treated as automatic May clearance. No new injury or medical restriction was produced or communicated during May 13-15.

Participation was voluntary. No absence, rehabilitation assignment or declined optional activity was used as commitment evidence or a hidden personnel grade.

### Work performed and teaching progression

Each day followed **Explain -> Show -> Walk -> Rep -> Correct -> Rep again -> Retain -> Add complexity**. Meetings and walkthroughs established common personnel, formation, strength, point and coverage language before non-contact individual, group, team and special-teams periods. The staff evaluated only this taught menu:

- offense: huddle/cadence, basic formation portability, Power and Inside Zone rules, Stick and Drive jobs, the protection point and back responsibility, and a limited purposeful motion presentation;
- defense: base front/alignment, run-fit and leverage communication, core coverage distribution and a limited pressure presentation;
- special teams: substitution language, punt/kickoff lane and leverage rules, protection/release fundamentals and specialist operation.

The team did not reach a basis for broad situational installation or PRESS tempo. No preset snap share, touch allocation, personnel percentage or depth-chart probability controlled the work.

### Unit and player evidence

**Quarterbacks and offensive operation.** Kirk Cousins supplied the cleanest block of huddle operation and protection-point communication. His first-day timing in Drive improved after the receivers and quarterbacks re-walked the landmark together and the correction carried into May 15. Chad Henne was generally assignment-sound and accurate in the short taught menu; on changing pictures he was slower to reset the protection answer, then retained the simplified communication on the final day. Tyler Bray needed repeated help marrying cadence, footwork and progression timing; the issue was processing within the taught picture rather than unwillingness or a medical limit. John Parker Wilson remained steady without creating a role change. No quarterback order was awarded.

**Offensive line and skill groups.** Brad Meester gave the line its most consistent point and combination communication. Lane Johnson carried his rookie-minicamp assignment reliability into veteran work and corrected his spacing on a non-contact twist exchange, but the evidence remains an opening at right tackle, not a starting decision. The interior group did not consistently transfer Power combination timing when the defensive presentation changed. Tice and the line staff reduced the next installation step and returned to one point declaration and one shared combination vocabulary rather than blaming physical execution for a teaching/communication problem.

Cecil Shorts was the most consistent receiver at preserving the taught Stick and Drive landmarks against changed leverage. Travis Kelce handled attached-to-flex alignment changes correctly, while his hand placement and base on the taught run-block fit still needed correction; the correction improved in individual work but was not yet stable across the block. C.J. Anderson showed assignment reliability in the installed back tracks and check responsibility. None of that created a target, workload or roster guarantee.

**Defense.** Paul Posluszny stabilized front and fit calls, and Daryl Smith executed his assigned non-contact work without a communicated limitation. Sio Moore initially chased backfield action past his coverage landmark, accepted the correction and retained it when the same picture returned May 15. Jordan Poyer carried forward his leverage discipline and communicated cleanly through a changed receiver distribution. With Grimes withheld from team periods, the secondary rotated the available corners through outside and inside teaching positions; that was a temporary evaluation experiment, not a depth-chart move.

The defense became less reliable when disguise and pressure presentation were added before the base coverage exchange was settled. Crennel removed the extra presentation, re-taught the underlying coverage ownership, and the unit finished the block with the base communication intact. The postponed pressure variation is not installed team football yet.

**Special teams.** Brynden Trawick and Adam Thielen provided useful evidence across more than one taught coverage/return responsibility, particularly in alignment and lane communication. Bacarri Rambo needed an initial substitution reminder and retained the corrected unit call on the following days. These are provisional special-teams evaluations, not final-unit assignments or roster decisions.

### Coaching and install decisions at block close

- Retain the common huddle, cadence, point, formation and base coverage language.
- Carry Power, Inside Zone, Stick and Drive forward for recall rather than expanding every available family.
- Re-teach interior combination timing and quarterback/line protection communication before adding presentation.
- Keep motion limited to a football purpose; do not add variation merely because another OTA block is scheduled.
- Postpone the added defensive pressure disguise until base coverage exchange is reliable without coach rescue.
- Continue Grimes only within the next qualified medical instruction; no coach-created timetable applies.

All role movement remains provisional. Cousins, Meester, Shorts, Posluszny, Johnson, Poyer, Moore, Trawick and Thielen produced meaningful positive evidence in the work described above; Kelce, Bray, Henne, Rambo and the interior offensive unit leave with specific retained or still-open corrections. A clean rep was recorded as clean rather than given a manufactured flaw.

### Stone's practice structure and install decisions

Stone made retention under changed presentation the block's first test. Both days followed **Explain -> Show -> Walk -> Rep -> Correct -> Rep again -> Retain -> Add complexity**. May 20 began with unprompted recall of huddle, cadence, formation, protection point, run-combination, coverage-exchange and special-teams substitution language. Only after the applicable unit carried its base job did the staff change alignment, motion, front or distribution.

Offensively, Stone kept Power, Inside Zone, Stick and Drive at the center. He accepted Tice's simplified one-point/one-combination presentation for the interior line, then changed the defensive front without changing the offensive job. The line handled the base declaration more independently, but interior combinations again became late when the front moved after the initial picture. Stone stopped the presentation expansion, returned to the common call and assigned another walk-through/rep-again cycle rather than installing another run family.

The huddle, skill group and protection operation retained enough of Stick and Drive for Stone to introduce **Mesh only as a narrow concept progression**: route-job explanation and walk-through on May 20, then group work and selected team repetitions on May 21. Mesh remained an early install, not a game-ready package. Y-Cross, Counter, Outside Zone, broader protection variation, PRESS tempo and a larger motion menu were deferred. Previously taught purposeful motion remained available only where the underlying job stayed unchanged.

Defensively, Crennel kept the opening emphasis on base alignment, fit, coverage distribution and exchange. The front and linebackers carried the base fit calls without Posluszny having to rescue every alignment, and the secondary handled the original coverage exchange through changed receiver distribution. That earned one return to the previously postponed pressure presentation late May 21. The first look produced a late secondary exchange; Crennel removed the disguise, re-walked rush and coverage ownership, and the group executed the same presentation cleanly when it returned. Stone and Crennel retained that single presentation for recall but did not expand the pressure menu.

Lowry removed some coach placement from core substitution and alignment periods. The units operated the taught punt/kickoff lane and leverage language with greater independence, but hands, onside and broader situational material remained deferred.

## May 23 — OTA day

**Event status:** COMPLETE through the end of May 23, 2013 (OTA Day 6).

### Fresh medical and participation communication

Qualified medical personnel issued new May 23 football-use instructions before Stone and the coordinators set the field sequence. Grimes remained in meetings, rehabilitation and individual work and was again permitted a medically controlled set of non-contact group and base team repetitions. ### Stone's plan, meetings and walkthrough

Stone set one governing question in the coordinator meeting: could Jacksonville operate the May 21 menu after recall and a changed picture without a coach supplying the answer? He declined Tice's option to preview another run family and agreed with Crennel that only the already-corrected pressure presentation would be tested. Lowry was directed to remove coach placement from taught substitution, alignment and lane jobs where prior evidence supported doing so. Cousins received the first changed-presentation quarterback sequence he had earned; Henne was scheduled immediately behind him against comparable football problems, Wilson remained in the working rotation, and Bray's menu stayed narrower around cadence, protection point and core progression timing.

In meetings the offense recalled huddle operation, cadence, formations, protection point, Power, Inside Zone, Stick, Drive, limited purposeful motion and only the narrow Mesh rules already taught. The defense recalled its base front, fits, coverage distribution and the single pressure presentation. Walkthrough exposed the offense to a late front reset and the defense to a changed receiver distribution. The interior line's first changed-front declaration was late between the guards even though Meester's point was correct. Stone stopped the group, had Tice and the line coach separate point identification from the combination echo, and ordered the same picture walked again before either side added speed. The repeated declaration was clean. On defense, Crennel required the secondary to state the exchange before the pressure shell moved; Rambo delivered the echo on time in the return walkthrough.

### Individual, group and special-teams work

Position coaches used individual work for the open corrections rather than creating new assignments. The quarterbacks tied feet to the retained progression and practiced the protection reset; the line repped the common combination language against static and late-changing fronts. Kelce maintained the correct assignment and improved his hand placement and base from attached and flex alignments in individual work. In the later group run period his base widened once after the alignment changed, so the technique traveled farther than May 21 but was not closed. Johnson retained his tackle assignment and pass-set landmark through the harder presentation. Shorts preserved his Stick and Drive landmarks through the limited motion already installed, and Anderson retained the taught track and check responsibility.

Lowry ran core punt and kickoff substitution, alignment and lane/leverage work with less sideline placement. Trawick operated his previously taught jobs without a placement correction and earned first exposure to another taught cross-unit sequence on June 4. Thielen initially waited for an unnecessary confirmation on one alignment, corrected the communication on the repeat and retained his cross-unit work. Rambo's substitution language remained clean. These were responsibility adjustments for the next practice, not final-unit or roster awards.

### Team work, corrections and coordinator decisions

The non-contact competitive work included legal 9-on-7 run fits, 7-on-7 distribution and selected 11-on-11 team periods. Stone observed the entire operation, moved between units rather than replacing position coaches, and stopped complexity when the day's known problems required another teaching cycle.

**Run game and protection.** On the first faster changed-front Power sequence, the interior combination echo again arrived late after the defense moved from its original picture. The assignment failure was communication/processing, not a physical loss. Stone sent the offense back to the walk-through landmark; Tice and Meester reduced the call to point, echo and confirmation, then repeated Power and Inside Zone against the same change. The return work was correct, and a later different front was handled without coach rescue. Because the correction required an in-practice reset, Stone classified the rule as improved but not stable enough to add Counter, Outside Zone or broader protection variation. June 4 will open by recalling the same changed-front rule.

**Pass concepts and quarterbacks.** Cousins opened the harder changed-presentation sequence with clean huddle, cadence and protection communication. He was late once moving from the initial shell to the underneath Mesh window, diagnosed it correctly with Stone and Tice, and delivered the progression on the repeat without changing the protection answer. Henne handled the comparable reset promptly and kept Stick and Drive on schedule; his clean May 23 transfer materially narrowed the operational gap but did not erase the larger body of prior work. Wilson remained assignment-sound in the retained menu and did not separate the competition. Bray's cadence-to-footwork link held in core work, but when the coverage distribution changed his progression again arrived late; Stone removed Mesh from Bray's remaining team segment and gave him a correct repeat of Stick/Drive timing rather than burying the issue under more volume.

The first narrow Mesh team sequence produced compressed spacing between the shallow jobs. Film-side observation and the position coaches identified shared landmark timing rather than a quarterback-only error. Stone stopped the period, Tice re-showed the stagger and receivers walked it at the prescribed depth. The repeated 7-on-7 and selected team work held for Cousins, Henne and Wilson. Stone retained **only that narrow Mesh teaching version** for June 4. No broader Mesh menu, Y-Cross, Counter, Outside Zone, expanded protection, PRESS tempo or large motion package was added.

**Defense.** Crennel called the defense and sequenced base fit and coverage recall before pressure. Posluszny made the opening declaration, but the unit completed its echoes without requiring him to relocate a teammate. Moore delivered the changed-distribution handoff on time in group work and again during 7-on-7; the correction advanced, though June 4 must test it after the break. Poyer maintained leverage and made the base exchange call through the changed distribution. Rambo communicated the defensive exchange on time in both walkthrough and team recall.

Crennel then called the one retained pressure presentation. Rush and replacement ownership were correct on the first team repetition, and the secondary exchange remained intact when the offense used its permitted motion on the return. Crennel retained that one presentation for June 4 and refused broader pressure or disguise expansion. Stone agreed: the defense had become more multiple in its picture without changing its base rules, but one successful recall did not authorize the rest of the menu. Grimes did not participate in this pressure work under the medical instruction.
