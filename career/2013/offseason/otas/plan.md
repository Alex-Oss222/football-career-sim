## Player playbook access and evidence rule

Every participant may possess and study the **complete active 2013 Iteration I playbook**. The phase plan controls what the staff formally installs, practices, and evaluates together; it does not ration access to pages. A player may read ahead and ask about later material. He is evaluated on what has actually been assigned and taught for the phase.

No preset personnel percentage, snap share, rep quota, touch quota, or depth-chart probability determines the result of this phase. Reps and roles are adjusted from football evidence: assignment, communication, technique, physical execution, health, correction retention, matchup, special teams, and what the player demonstrates in practices and games. The plan creates opportunities to evaluate; it does not manufacture the answer.


This plan covers the progression from the official offseason program into OTAs. If Jacksonville separately uses the additional veteran minicamp available to a new-head-coach club, its work is governed by the same teaching framework and the applicable CBA restrictions. Do not invent a date or treat an optional window as mandatory.

## 1. Purpose of the offseason program

The offseason is not a miniature training camp.

Its job is to build the body, language, technique, communication, and shared football understanding that training camp will later stress under greater speed and competition.

The sequence is:

**Build the player -> teach the job -> connect the unit -> test communication -> carry the learning forward.**

The target moves from **Good toward Better**. The staff should expect players to retain corrections across days and weeks, not merely perform one clean scripted rep.

## 2. Participation and voluntariness

The Prowl standard does not create attendance authority that the CBA does not give the club.

During voluntary periods:

- no player loses a roster spot, role, rep, or standing because he lawfully declines voluntary participation;
- coaches do not use attendance as a hidden commitment grade;
- workout bonuses or other lawful contract mechanisms are handled through proper club/contract channels, not informal threats;
- football evaluation is based on work the staff is actually entitled to evaluate;
- a player who is absent from voluntary work receives the necessary re-entry teaching when he lawfully reports later.

The plan describes what Jacksonville teaches **when lawful participation occurs**. It does not convert the plan into a mandatory summons.

## 3. Phase One: build the physical platform

Follow the exact CBA restrictions established by the governing source. Do not add football work that the phase does not permit.

Primary objectives:

- individualized playing-weight direction;
- functional strength;
- acceleration and deceleration;
- change of direction;
- position movement;
- mobility;
- rehabilitation;
- conditioning base;
- recovery habits;
- nutrition/hydration education and support.

Performance staff should give each player a clear answer to:

**What physical qualities does your football job require, and what are we building now to support them?**

No player receives a universal body target simply because another player at the position has a different frame.

Medical restrictions control all work.

### Phase One coaching restraint

Football coaches may prepare teaching material and coordinate with the appropriate staff, but they may not use The Prowl as a reason to exceed the phase's permitted football-contact or field-work boundaries.

If the rule says the football answer must wait, it waits.

## 4. Phase Two: technique and job connection

As the calendar permits individual/group football teaching and walkthrough-speed work, connect physical training to football responsibilities.

### Offensive emphasis

- stance/alignment;
- formation recognition;
- personnel and role labels;
- run-footwork fundamentals;
- route landmarks;
- ball-security technique;
- protection technique;
- center/QB/back communication;
- shift/motion mechanics where permitted;
- concept teaching without pretending walkthrough success equals team-execution mastery.

### Defensive emphasis

- stance/alignment;
- front and leverage rules;
- pursuit landmarks;
- run-fit responsibility;
- coverage landmarks;
- communication;
- rush-lane responsibility;
- tackling approach/positioning taught without creating prohibited live-contact work.

### Special-teams emphasis

- stance/start mechanics;
- protection/release technique;
- lane and leverage rules;
- return-path landmarks;
- ball handling;
- specialist operation;
- substitution responsibilities.

Phase Two is where the staff should find vocabulary problems before eleven-man work exposes them.

## 5. OTA / Phase Three teaching gates

The team does not install by calendar volume. It advances through gates.

### Gate 1 — common language

Before expanding, the unit can:

- get personnel on and off;
- break the huddle;
- align;
- communicate strength/point/coverage information;
- use the same terminology across position rooms;
- execute the cadence;
- identify who owns a correction.

### Gate 2 — core concept execution

The offense runs the core families being emphasized with correct alignment, assignment, and basic technique.

The defense plays its base front/coverage responsibilities with correct fit, leverage, and communication.

Special teams can align and operate the core units without coaches placing every player by hand.

### Gate 3 — presentation and portability

Only after the job is stable does the staff vary presentation:

- formation;
- personnel;
- shift;
- motion;
- eligible location;
- defensive front/presentation;
- coverage shell;
- pressure look.

The job should survive the presentation change.

### Gate 4 — situation

Introduce situational context without turning every period into a game-plan exam:

- third down;
- red zone;
- backed up;
- two minute;
- four minute;
- sudden change;
- end-of-half communication;
- field-position awareness.

### Gate 5 — retention

The true OTA test is whether yesterday's correction survives today's practice without a coach standing next to the player.

## 6. Offensive OTA progression

The offense expands Iteration I deliberately.

### Formation and personnel

Build operational familiarity with the formation families and personnel groups the current roster can actually support. Do not force a grouping just because it exists in the book.

Players should learn that the job travels:

- a Y may be attached, winged, flexed, or moved;
- H/F jobs can be filled by different body types;
- a receiver can move between outside, slot, reduced, or motion alignment;
- backs must understand both run and protection responsibilities;
- 11/12/21 or other groupings should carry enough shared core that the defense cannot identify the call from personnel alone.

### Run game

Develop the core families in layers:

1. alignment/point/strength;
2. footwork and combination rules;
3. back track;
4. perimeter responsibility;
5. front adjustment;
6. change of presentation.

Power, Counter, Inside Zone, and Outside Zone are long-term core families, but the staff may sequence them according to roster comprehension rather than install all variants at once.

### Pass game

Build concept understanding as jobs:

- Stick;
- Mesh;
- Drive;
- Y-Cross;
- other core/weekly concepts only as readiness permits.

Receivers learn the job and landmark. Quarterbacks learn the picture/progression. Protection and route timing are taught together.

### Protection

Expand common use of the protection families appropriate to the installed pass concepts, including SCAT, HALF, SOLID, or other Iteration I families as justified.

Priority:

- one protection point;
- one line language;
- clear back responsibility;
- clear quarterback hot/weak-point understanding;
- no illusion that every pressure must be blocked.

### Motion and shifts

Motion has a job. It should:

- identify coverage;
- change leverage;
- change strength;
- create a blocking angle;
- move a role without changing the concept.

Do not add motion merely to make practice look multiple.

### Tempo

Introduce NORMAL/HOLD/ROTATE concepts before expecting PRESS operation. PRESS is earned through mastery and uses a narrower menu.

## 7. Quarterback-room OTA plan

Quarterbacks develop:

- huddle operation;
- cadence;
- Pre-Snap Five discipline;
- protection-point communication;
- progression speed;
- footwork tied to concept timing;
- checkdown discipline;
- movement/launch-point mechanics where installed;
- situational clock/field awareness;
- post-rep self-diagnosis.

The staff should distinguish:

- a quarterback who knew the answer and missed the throw;
- a quarterback who had the wrong answer;
- a protection/receiver mistake that changed the picture;
- a coaching design problem.

Not every incompletion is a quarterback processing failure.

## 8. Offensive-line OTA plan

The line should leave OTAs with one common communication system.

Emphasis:

- stance and first step;
- combination timing;
- point declaration;
- ACE/DEUCE/TREY and other installed combination words;
- FAN/PINCH/LOCK/ME-YOU communication where relevant;
- hand placement and leverage;
- pass-set landmarks;
- twist/game exchange in non-live work;
- screen and movement rules;
- correction continuity from center through tackles.

The center has a communication leadership role, but the quarterback remains responsible for his protection picture.

## 9. Skill-position OTA plan

**Backs**
- run tracks;
- ball security;
- protection;
- scan/check;
- screen mechanics;
- route jobs;
- motion/flex responsibilities.

**Tight ends / H**
- attached and detached execution;
- run blocking;
- protection;
- release and route jobs;
- formation transformation.

**Receivers**
- split families;
- releases;
- route depths;
- leverage;
- blocking responsibility;
- motion;
- concept-job portability.

Cross-training expands only after primary jobs are usable.

## 10. Defensive OTA progression

The defensive objective is to become multiple in presentation without becoming multiple in confusion.

Install/teach in this order:

1. alignment;
2. assignment;
3. run fit;
4. coverage rule;
5. communication;
6. pursuit;
7. presentation/disguise;
8. pressure answer;
9. situation.

Key team principles:

- eliminate free offense;
- fit the run without surrendering the whole coverage;
- make the quarterback confirm the picture;
- pressure with a reason;
- tackle as a scheme requirement even when live tackling is not the period's work;
- create speed through recognition.

### Defensive-line work

- front alignment;
- gap and movement responsibility;
- strike/separate;
- edge/contain;
- pass-rush lane discipline;
- games/stunts only after base communication is stable.

### Linebacker work

- front recognition;
- run fits;
- leverage;
- coverage landmarks;
- backfield keys;
- pressure communication;
- pursuit.

### Secondary work

- leverage;
- coverage communication;
- transition;
- route distribution;
- safety/corner communication;
- force/support;
- disguise without sacrificing assignment certainty.

## 11. Special-teams OTA plan

Build the full operating language:

- punt;
- punt return;
- kickoff;
- kickoff return;
- field goal/PAT;
- field-goal block;
- hands/on-side/emergency concepts only as permitted and useful.

Identify primary and backup jobs without declaring the final 53.

A player can increase his usefulness by learning multiple special-teams jobs, but voluntary attendance itself is not a grade.

## 12. Practice teaching rhythm

A normal OTA teaching cycle should include, within the verified legal work inventory:

- meeting install;
- walkthrough;
- individual technique;
- group work;
- team work;
- special teams;
- correction;
- film/review;
- next-practice recall.

The exact clock is not written here because the 2013 Jacksonville calendar/hours must be verified. The principle is fixed: **teach before testing, correct before expanding.**

## 13. Staff review after each OTA block

Position coaches report:

- concepts that are Good;
- concepts moving toward Better;
- concepts that are not ready to expand;
- recurring communication errors;
- recurring teaching confusion;
- individual corrections;
- medical/performance limits through proper channels;
- special-teams development;
- no final roster verdict from voluntary attendance.

Coordinators then decide what to retain, simplify, or postpone.

Stone should remove an install if the team cannot execute it rather than protect it because it looked good on paper.

## 14. Family-inclusive team dinner

Schedule one **full-team family dinner during the offseason/OTA window**.

Invitation extends to:

- every current Jacksonville player under verified control;
- coaches;
- relevant football, performance, medical-support, and Player Development staff where appropriate;
- each person's invited family or meaningful guest.

Rules:

- family/guest attendance is voluntary;
- during a voluntary offseason period, the social event does not become a hidden football-attendance requirement;
- there is no install presentation or personnel evaluation;
- Stone keeps remarks short and centered on welcome, appreciation, and the club's responsibility to families;
- Player Development can provide a resource table/contact point without asking families to disclose private issues publicly;
- coaches are encouraged to know families as people, not collect information about them;
- any private family, financial, medical, or counseling detail remains outside personnel evaluation.

When it happens, record the event in `otas/output.md`.

## 15. End-of-OTA objective

By the end of the OTA program, Jacksonville should know:

- which core concepts are genuinely usable;
- which concepts need simplification;
- which communication rules are stable;
- which players need narrower teaching;
- which players are ready for expanded responsibility;
- which position-room teaching methods are working;
- what must be emphasized in veteran minicamp;
- what physical preparation remains before camp.

The staff is not required to know the final roster.

The question is:

**Can the team take the same language into veteran minicamp and operate it with less coaching intervention?**
