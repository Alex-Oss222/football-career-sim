### Market calibration

NFL assistant salaries were generally private in 2013, so the contracts use public market anchors rather than fake historical precision.

A February 2013 Dallas Morning News report described the Cowboys' veteran assistant overhaul as expensive and estimated that Monte Kiffin, Rod Marinelli, Rich Bisaccia and Derek Dooley together would likely be in roughly the **$5 million annual range**. The same report noted:

- Kiffin had made more than $1.5 million at USC;
- Dooley had made $2 million at Tennessee;
- Bisaccia had accepted a two-year Auburn deal at $500,000 per year before leaving for Dallas;
- clubs were still financially responsible for prior coaching commitments after staff changes.

### Common contract mechanics

Every successful assistant accepted the same basic legal/economic framework unless a future amendment states otherwise:

- salary guaranteed for the stated term if terminated without cause;
- dollar-for-dollar offset for salary actually earned in a later NFL or major-college football coaching job during the guaranteed period;
- no guaranteed continued employment if Stone ceases to be Jacksonville head coach, but a without-cause release does not erase the salary obligation;
- no unrestricted right to leave for a lateral outside job while under contract;
- outside interviews/releases follow NFL permission and anti-tampering rules;
- no assistant contract guarantees a starter, roster spot, acquisition, draft pick, player contract or personnel veto;
- cause is limited to a real material employment breach, not ordinary poor football performance or disagreement;
- Stone may make reasonable duty adjustments inside the coach's role, but may not use a sham demotion simply to evade the guarantee.

## Contract-close reconciliation

The hiring process is complete only because both football role and economic terms are now closed.

### Offensive structure

- Stone remains the offensive play-caller.
- Tice accepted the coordinator role with that limitation explicitly priced into a $1.10M first-year salary rather than being promised later play-calling.
- Bates, Yarno and the position coaches accepted defined teaching/planning domains rather than vague titles.
- Skalaski's compensation reflects an assistant/QC role rather than pretending he is a second quarterbacks coach with equal authority.

### Defensive structure

- Crennel's $1.60M salary and three-year term buy actual coordinator autonomy, including the defensive call sheet.
- Pleasant's hire after Crennel preserves the intended coordinator/DL pairing.
- Bush's pay reflects former-DC experience even though his title is linebackers coach.
- Oden is paid as an experienced position coach, not as a hidden co-coordinator.

### Special teams structure

- Lowry is paid as a full coordinator and owns the kicking-game operation.
- His role is not folded into another position coach merely to save payroll.

### No outcome promises

Compensation did not buy any coach:

- a guaranteed player;
- a guaranteed scheme fit;
- a promised draft pick;
- a promised starter;
- protection from ordinary football evaluation;
- personnel authority belonging to Caldwell or Stone.

The contracts buy coaching labor and defined responsibility, not predetermined success.

### Future contract events

Any extension, raise, release, resignation, outside-interview permission, title change, play-calling change or settlement must be recorded as a new dated staff transaction. The original hiring outcome is not rewritten after the fact.
