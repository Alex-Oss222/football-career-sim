# 2013 draft board — Stone's plan

**Status:** Stone's stated priorities and football reasoning for each of Jacksonville's picks, dated ahead of the April 25-27 draft. **This is a recommendation to Caldwell, not a guaranteed selection.** Caldwell holds final draft authority (Authority Map row 4). More importantly: whether any named player is actually still available when Jacksonville is actually on the clock is not something this board gets to assume — that has to come from the real, date-gated pre-selection data in `library/2013_draft_pool_registry.md` and `library/2013_draft_information_gates.md`, evaluated fresh against the simulation's own draft-day board, never from knowing where a player really ended up being drafted. Do not treat any entry below as locked in advance of the actual pick.

Where Stone's reasoning below references a player's real pre-draft evaluation (a mock-draft consensus, a measured athletic score, a described skill set) as of the cutoff, that's legitimate pre-selection evidence under Document 2 §12's own rule. Where it would otherwise reference where a player actually ended up being drafted, that fact is intentionally omitted here — it belongs only in `archive/`, never in a working draft board.

## Round 1, pick #2 — Lane Johnson, OT

Stone and Caldwell's stated preference, on the football case that Johnson's movement skills fit the offense better than defaulting to whichever tackle is ranked first by outside consensus.

**If selected:** plan is LT Eugene Monroe / RT Lane Johnson — do not immediately move Johnson to left tackle; let him acclimate on the right with Monroe protecting the blind side. Long term, this also gives the club leverage in Monroe's next contract conversation.

## Round 2, pick #33 — Travis Kelce, TE

Stone's stated case for prioritizing Kelce here, ahead of where public big boards had him, is that Stone evaluates him as a central offensive piece — a mismatch-creating move tight end — rather than a conventional TE2. This is Stone's own scouting conviction, not a guarantee of the pick's value or that Kelce is actually still on the board at #33.

**If selected:** does not replace Marcedes Lewis. Lewis stays the inline/blocking/matchup piece; Kelce is used in the slot, in motion, and on seam/underneath routes — the plan is 12-personnel that can actually threaten defenses through the air.

## Round 3, pick #64 — Keenan Allen, WR

Stone's evaluation has Allen as good value at this slot, not a reach, behind Justin Blackmon and Cecil Shorts in the receiver pecking order. He would not need to immediately carry the passing offense.

## Round 4, pick #98 — David Bakhtiari, OL

Cross-trained rather than locked into one position — reps at left guard, left tackle, and right tackle. With Monroe and Johnson penciled in outside, his clearest immediate path is left guard, which would give the line Monroe-Bakhtiari-Meester-Nwaneri-Johnson if he wins the job.

## Round 5, pick #135 — Lavar Edwards, DE

Not expected to produce double-digit sacks immediately — rotational end, base-end snaps, inside/outside pass-rush experimentation, special teams. Adds real competition to an edge room that includes Bennett (if signed), Jason Babin, Jeremy Mincey, and Andre Branch.

## Round 6, pick #169 — Jordan Poyer, CB

Cross-trained rather than declared an outside-only corner — reps at outside corner, nickel, free-safety packages, and returner. The secondary is described as the roster's weakest unit and needs versatile young depth.

## Round 7, pick #208 — reserved as the Cousins-trade sweetener first

See `trades/trade_targets.md` for the proposed Kirk Cousins framework. If Washington declines the trade, this pick reverts to best-available at edge, cornerback, safety, or a special-teams contributor — genuinely open, not predetermined.

## Post-draft priority — A.J. Bouye (CB, UDFA)

Call immediately once the draft ends, not after the weekend — the secondary need is real and immediate. Pitch: an open competition for real playing time.

## Rookie-minicamp tryout — Adam Thielen (WR)

Explicitly not treated as a drafted-grade prospect — invited to rookie minicamp on a tryout basis and made to earn a contract through performance there and at OTAs, not signed on projection.
