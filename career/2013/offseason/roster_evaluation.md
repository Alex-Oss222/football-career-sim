# Roster evaluation — Stone's plan

**Status:** Stone's own dated evaluation of the inherited roster and his approach to it across the offseason calendar, written late January-February 2013. This is his recommendation, not a guaranteed outcome. Final roster cuts and released/retained status rest with Caldwell (Authority Map row 7, inferred from his retained general personnel authority) — Stone's evaluation is what he brings to that conversation, not a unilateral decision.

## Initial bucketing

Every inherited player sorted into one of five categories: core, useful, competition, trade candidate, release.

**Offensive core:** Eugene Monroe, Cecil Shorts, Justin Blackmon, Marcedes Lewis, Maurice Jones-Drew (short term), Uche Nwaneri.

**Defensive core/development:** Paul Posluszny, Tyson Alualu, Andre Branch, Mike Harris, Dwight Lowery (if healthy).

**Quarterbacks:** Keep both Blaine Gabbert and Chad Henne temporarily. Neither receives the starting job outright. Stone's stated reasoning: dumping Henne before knowing whether Kirk Cousins is obtainable would be a mistake — see `trades/trade_targets.md`.

## Contract-clearing recommendations (February-March)

**Recommended for release:**
- Guy Whimper — no longer needed once the offensive-line rebuild leans on drafted/free-agent tackle depth.
- Aaron Ross — move on.
- Dawan Landry — shop quietly first; release if no market develops.
- Laurent Robinson — release; his concussion history makes a meaningful trade return unrealistic.

**Not recommended for release yet:**
- Jeremy Mincey — shop quietly, but keep through camp unless a real offer comes in; pass-rush depth is a genuine need.
- Chad Henne — keep until the Cousins question resolves.
- Eugene Monroe — keep. A rookie tackle (if drafted) doesn't make Monroe expendable immediately; two good tackles is a real asset, not a redundancy.

## Evaluation approach across the calendar

Stone's stated intent for what each phase is actually for — an evaluation plan, not a predetermined roster outcome. Actual results (who wins each competition, who makes the 53) are resolved live when each phase is actually simulated.

**Veteran orientation (mid-April):** first real football evaluation. Quarterbacks get reps under observation for footwork, processing speed, accuracy on NFL concepts, pressure response, command, and grasp of Stone's terminology — explicitly not decided after three practices. Offensive line evaluated as a competition, not a fixed depth chart.

**Rookie minicamp (early May, after the draft):** first look at the incoming class and any UDFA signings — technique and assignment processing for linemen, positional versatility reps for defensive backs and receivers, "move him everywhere" usage for pass-catching tight ends.

**OTAs (May-June):** if a quarterback addition occurs, a real competition with an explicit early-rep distribution that shifts based on performance, not a ceremonial exercise. Without an addition, the existing quarterbacks compete directly — Stone's stated view is that signing another mediocre veteran just to say the position changed is not a real solution.

**Training camp (starting late July):** jobs are actually won here. Offensive-line competition is run on performance, not draft status. Quarterback evaluation runs on a stated weighting — decision-making, accuracy, and third-down execution weighted highest; practice statistics weighted lowest without game context — with the job awarded no later than after the third preseason game unless no one has clearly earned it.

**Preseason:** Game 1 evaluates operational competence, not box scores. Game 2 increases the starting offense's workload. Game 3 is the dress rehearsal, with the presumptive starting quarterback getting extended run. Game 4 protects established starters and is used for bottom-of-roster and rookie/UDFA evaluation.

**Week 1 objectives (season opener):** not "make the playoffs or fail." Offensively: install the system, reduce sacks, build a functional third-down offense, and develop the season's rookies and any new quarterback. Defensively: generate real pressure and stop being one of the league's easiest rushing defenses to face, while developing the rookie class. Organizationally, Stone's stated single biggest question for the organization to answer by December: do we have our quarterback.
