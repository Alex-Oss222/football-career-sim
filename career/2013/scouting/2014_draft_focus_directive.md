# 2014 draft: Stone's coaching focus directive (Week 11)

**Status:** Coaching focus, not a final board. Issued by Alex Stone during Week 11 preparation (November 11-17, 2013) to David Caldwell, the scouting department, Mike Tice, Romeo Crennel, the named position coaches and Alan Lowry.
**Authority:** Caldwell retains final authority over the board and every selection (Document 3). This file is a durable plan; it records no evaluation result.

## No-hindsight rule

Each player is evaluated only from information available at the branch date of the evaluation: college tape, measurables and medical or background information as each becomes available on its dated release (`library/2013_draft_information_gates.md` sets the pattern for 2013). No later NFL result, honour or draft position is used. A player's grade and availability are resolved from pre-draft information like every other prospect's; this focus list changes neither. Eligibility is not assumed: an underclassman (for example Trai Turner) is draftable only after a dated declaration, and each player's college status is checked against dated sources before the draft.

## Questions per player (from the directive)

- **Bitonio:** the first NFL job; tackle or guard first; which protection technique translates; speed-to-power; twists and exchanges; whether his movement improves the run game; how much cross-training he can absorb. "Don't write versatile as the grade. Tell me what he can do first."
- **Turner:** anchor, hands, combination blocks, Power and Counter pulls, interior games, pocket depth, playing beside Brewster, and how fast he can learn the protection language; could he compete for a guard job?
- **Telvin Smith:** first defensive job, run-fit responsibility, coverage value, whether speed becomes correct leverage, tackling, package value and a special-teams path to dressing.
- **Leno:** pass-set consistency, recovery, edge speed, the inside counter, balance, run leverage, left or right tackle, and whether he can be a useful reserve before any starting projection.
- **Shelby Harris:** first front position, run fit, rush-lane discipline, reducing inside on passing downs, how many roles a rookie can learn and what gets him active on game day.
- **Paradis:** snap reliability, protection identification, anchor, combination blocks, twist exchange, learning the MIKE/ACE/DEUCE language, and his developmental relationship to Brewster and Meester (succession planning, not a replacement).
- **Butler:** outside or nickel first, press, off-man recognition, recovery, ball tracking, tackling, communication and a special-teams role to dress while developing. Alan Ball's injury may not inflate the grade.

## Report format

For each focus player: (1) strengths supported by tape; (2) weaknesses supported by tape; (3) projected first NFL job; (4) the best objection to drafting him; (5) development cost in practice reps, technique work, cross-training, roster spot and likely game-day role; and a final line: "What would make us change our mind?" Position coaches are asked "Can you teach the job we're projecting?" and "What does that teaching cost?", not "Do you like him?"

## Boundary

Arizona preparation comes first: no coach misses an opponent meeting, install, practice or correction period for prospect work. The scouting staff carries the volume; coaches review assigned cut-ups in set personnel windows.
