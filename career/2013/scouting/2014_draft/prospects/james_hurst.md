### December 1, 2013 evidence cutoff: comparative research report

#### Assessment and source support

Hurst is included to make the tackle discussion broader than Bitonio versus waiting for Leno. North Carolina's 2013 guide supports the senior tackle identity. Keith Beebe's July 31 prospect article offers a favorable view of Hurst's feet, technique and ability to anchor against power. That is an attributed preseason assessment, not a new Jacksonville film finding. [S30–S31](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

The resulting hypothesis is a tackle with a potentially useful protection foundation. The report cannot turn one favorable article into a verified reserve floor or starting grade. A later dated senior-prospect discussion also considers the professional position question, so the left/right projection remains a real evaluation task. [S32](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

**Strengths supported by tape:** The positive protection observations belong to the cited evaluator. No original full-game chart is claimed here. **Weaknesses supported by tape:** None is independently established. Recovery after the first loss and effectiveness outside the evaluator's highlighted situations remain open.

#### Protection evaluation

The most useful comparison with Leno is the quality of a bad snap. Every tackle eventually loses initial leverage. The reserve floor depends on whether he can remain square enough to recover, avoid turning the loss into immediate inside access, and preserve a usable pocket. A prospect's best mirror rep does not resolve that question.

Study speed, power and the counter as connected parts of the same rush. An early set that defeats speed may encourage the defender to cross inside. An anchor that sits too deeply can reduce the quarterback's space even without a clean shed. The protection assessment therefore needs rush context and the eventual pocket shape, rather than a binary sack/no-sack description.

In Stone's HALF and SOLID protections, the tackle also has a relationship with the guard and any attached protector. Evaluate whether he recognizes the exchange without chasing movement that belongs inside. MAX can reduce exposure, but it cannot be the permanent answer for a reserve expected to enter an ordinary passing plan.

#### Run game and first job

For Power and Counter, distinguish securing the initial defender from climbing effectively. For Outside, assess whether Hurst can gain and keep the required position while staying balanced. For the back side of a run, ask whether he closes the pursuit path without depending on a favorable initial alignment.

**Projected first job to investigate:** One-side tackle reserve, with left-side evidence reviewed first because of the college role. Right tackle is a separate technical and communication study. No new player is designated the swing tackle merely because the roster needs one.

**Development cost:** Moderate for one side; substantial for immediate two-sided game-day responsibility. The work would include set landmarks, inside-counter recovery, combination timing and the branch protection language. A sixth-lineman package might create limited early snaps if earned, but it does not itself establish emergency pass protection.

**Coach's answer:** Open. Tice and Yarno need to test whether the favorable preseason technique assessment survives varied opponents and complete games. They also need to compare the resulting role with Bradfield's existing reserve utility.

#### Value, objection and trigger

The best objection is spending above a developmental price on an assumed safe tackle without proving how he handles losing situations. Compare him with Leno for reserve readiness, with Bitonio and Martin for the earlier investment, and with Brandon Thomas only after accounting for Thomas's guard-first study.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. **What would change the view?** Repeatable recovery, competent work on both edges and protection that does not require extensive help would strengthen reserve value. A one-side projection requiring a restricted pass menu would reduce immediate usefulness. No post-cutoff injury, workout or draft outcome is used to influence this report.
