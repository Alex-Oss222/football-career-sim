### December 1, 2013 evidence cutoff: comprehensive research report

**Chronology note:** This retrospective research supplement retains a December 1 evidence cutoff. It does not replace or close the December 13 Phase IV coach-read entry above.

**Report type:** Ex-ante research and conditional teaching analysis, not a completed Tice/Yarno/Bates session. Senior center; no change to Brewster's starting job or Meester's current role. [Source register](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

#### Assessment and first job

The first job to investigate is developmental backup center. The report should be judged on whether Paradis can eventually enter without forcing the offense to abandon its protection system. That is a more useful first standard than predicting that he will succeed Meester or displace Brewster on a timetable.

The attraction is an established college center with a relevant body of work. The central risk is that the mental and physical demands arrive together: identify the front, communicate the point, deliver the snap and immediately block. A player can perform several of those tasks well and still be unusable if the remaining one repeatedly fails.

#### Supporting evidence

Boise State's June 19, 2013 release identifies Paradis as a redshirt senior center. The 2013 preseason preview independently discusses his existing center experience and frames his NFL path as reserve development with physical translation still to establish. Boise State's biography records the earlier move from the defensive line. None of those sources proves competence in Jacksonville's terminology. [S08–S09 and S14](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

**Strengths supported by tape:** No direct charted film is claimed. The supported favorable case is prior center experience and contemporary recognition. Academic achievement, where reported, is not a substitute for protection processing, and team sack totals cannot establish an individual center's reliability.

#### Snap, identify, block

The snap file should separate under-center exchange from shotgun location and timing. Record whether movement of the ball compromises the first blocking step. A clean snap with a lost immediate block and a strong block after an errant snap are different failures. Neither can be hidden inside a general athleticism description.

For MIKE identification, the center should explain the point from the full protection rule, then apply it after a defensive shift. The useful test is not reciting which defender wears the MIKE label. It is determining whom the line accounts for, whom the back scans, and which extra rusher belongs to the quarterback's answer.

ACE and DEUCE are not interchangeable. The center directly owns ACE relationships with a guard; he also needs to understand how his point affects the guard-tackle combination beyond him. In pass protection, a correct verbal declaration must be followed by a workable set and exchange. Knowledge that arrives after the snap is too late.

#### Anchor and combination work

The physical concern to investigate is whether he can preserve pocket depth against a shaded nose or a direct power rush while completing the snap. Evaluate knee bend, base width, hand replacement and the ability to recover after initial displacement. This report does not assert that he fails those tasks.

In the run game, a center may be uncovered on one play and responsible for a difficult reach on the next. Treat those separately. On a combination, he must establish control with the guard and release at the correct time. Leaving early creates penetration; leaving late leaves the linebacker free. A technically useful block need not produce a dramatic knockdown.

**Weaknesses supported by tape:** Insufficient direct evidence to identify a recurring flaw. The known evaluation risk is physical translation, together with the absence of a verified snap-and-protection error record. It would be equally unsupported to label him a weak athlete or a finished technician.

#### Communication burden on Cousins

Bates's question can be answered as a proposed teaching design without inventing a conversation. Cousins should hear one initial point and the necessary change, not competing declarations from every lineman. A rookie center must know which adjustments he owns, which he echoes and when the quarterback's protection change supersedes the original picture.

Use a progression from a static front to a shifted front, then a late pressure presentation. Require the explanation before the snap and review the actual block afterward. If the quarterback must repeatedly repair a correctable identification mistake, the reserve has added a material burden even when he wins his isolated block.

Meester can provide an existing veteran reference if the coaching staff assigns that work; the report does not create a mentorship promise. Brewster remains the starting center. Development repetitions compete with established players' preparation and must be scheduled accordingly.

#### Development cost and game-day pathway

**Practice cost: substantial at first because one position contains multiple simultaneous tasks.** Center-first teaching is preferable to immediate center/guard rotation. Add guard only if the center exchange and point process are stable, or if an explicit roster decision makes the alternative necessary. Historical position conversion does not prove a second NFL job.

The early pathway is reserve center after demonstrated reliability. Carrying a center-only developmental player may require another interior reserve to cover guards. That is a real roster cost. The sixth-round focus is reasonable as a study slot, but it is not permission to assume the player will be there or that the developmental cost is negligible.

#### Best objection and comparison

The strongest objection is selecting a backup whose physical blocking or communication still requires the starter's presence to keep the unit functional. Swanson and Stork provide useful center comparisons: determine whether paying earlier secures a substantially more complete first job, or whether the difference is only public reputation.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. Senior identity and college center experience are supported; learning speed, NFL anchor and game-day reserve readiness remain open.

**What would make us change our mind?** Stable snaps, correct points after movement, sound exchanges and sufficient anchor in the same evaluation sequence would strengthen the acquisition case. A center who can explain the rule but cannot execute it on time is not ready. A narrow center-only role may still be useful, but its roster price must remain visible.
