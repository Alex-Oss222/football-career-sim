### December 1, 2013 evidence cutoff: comprehensive research report

**Chronology note:** This retrospective research supplement retains a December 1 evidence cutoff. It does not replace or close the December 13 Phase IV coach-read entry above.

**Report type:** Ex-ante research and conditional coaching analysis. Oden, Crennel and Lowry remain assigned evaluators; no completed meeting or private workout is invented. [Source register](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

#### Assessment and first job

Investigate outside corner first. There is documented college cornerback production, whereas the packet does not establish a completed nickel apprenticeship. Nickel is a distinct job involving two-way releases, run-force duties and traffic communication; a smaller corner does not automatically belong there.

The useful acquisition case is a developmental outside reserve who can earn an independent special-teams assignment. It is not an immediate solution to Ball's injury. Jacksonville already has Grimes, Harris, Poyer, Bouye and other reserve competition in the current branch; the prospect must be judged against an actual roster function.

#### Evidence and a dated correction

The Gulf South Conference's November 20, 2013 release names Butler a senior defensive back and a repeat first-team selection. That dated release resolves the earlier report's uncertainty about whether his 2013 conference honor was public. His college lists him at 5-foot-11, 180 pounds; those are school-listed dimensions, not combine measurements. [S15–S16](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

West Alabama's biography documents interceptions, pass breakups and return work from 2012. Its later-updated 2013 section is not used to import undated final totals or all-star participation. The prior evidence supports ball involvement and return experience. It does not prove an NFL coverage technique or a coverage-unit job.

**Strengths supported by tape:** No direct full-game film chart is claimed. The supported positive case is sustained recognition at his level, prior pass disruption and documented return work. The report does not convert interceptions into route-recognition certainty or assume every target was defended well.

#### Outside coverage study

In press, determine whether he can disrupt a release while preserving the assigned leverage. A two-hand lunge that wins against one receiver can create an immediate loss against a different release. Evaluate the recovery after a missed strike rather than ending the clip when the first contact is made.

In off coverage, the question is how he manages the receiver's stem, cushion and break point. A clean interception could come from reading the route, reading an obvious quarterback, or taking a risk that was not punished. Those are different projections. Deep-ball work requires locating the ball without losing the receiver or creating contact before the catch point.

For recovery, distinguish straight-line catch-up from the ability to transition without wasting steps. Verified testing is not available at this date, and no speed number is invented. The college competition level is a translation question, not an automatic disqualification.

**Weaknesses supported by tape:** Insufficient direct evidence for a recurring press, transition or recovery defect. The strongest current limitation is how little complete-game evidence the packet has for projecting his coverage against NFL releases and route timing.

#### Tackling, communication and nickel conditions

Outside-corner run support requires crack replacement and a controlled fit relative to the safety and force player. A tackle total cannot show whether a corner protected the boundary or arrived after giving up the required leverage. Oden's eventual file should distinguish willingness to engage from successful technique; this report does not infer either from reputation.

Communication matters against stacks, bunches and motion. The defender must know whether the call requires staying attached, passing a route or resetting leverage. Do not add nickel until those outside rules hold. If nickel later becomes the better projection, establish evidence for defending two-way releases and fitting inside traffic, not merely for being competitive at the catch point.

#### Special-teams pathway

There is a real starting point here: documented college return work. That is stronger than describing an unverified prospect as a natural special-teamer. Nevertheless, a returner must be compared with Jacksonville's existing options for ball security, decisions and utility, and the report does not award him the return job.

Coverage work is separate. Gunner releases, contain responsibilities, avoidance of blocks and open-field tackling need their own evidence. If he is not the primary returner and cannot contribute on coverage, his route to dressing becomes much narrower. That constraint belongs in the roster evaluation even if the developmental corner projection remains attractive.

#### Development cost and objection

**Practice cost: substantial for coverage translation; moderate for a tightly limited initial assignment set.** Start with one outside alignment and the active Cover 1/Cover 3 leverage rules. Teach press and off techniques without confusing them, then add motion and bunch adjustments. Introduce nickel only after the outside baseline is usable. This requires individual technique periods, paired coverage work and team repetitions; a highlight reel cannot replace them.

**Can the job be taught?** The techniques and communication rules can be taught. His speed of learning and capacity to retain corrections are unknown. No invented interview response, personal reference or character score supplies the missing evidence.

The strongest objection is a reserve corner who needs a protected development year while contributing no independent active role. Stone's seventh-round focus limits acquisition expenditure but does not eliminate the roster cost. Cockrell is a useful comparison in the depth of available college-corner evidence; Fuller is a higher-investment comparison for the same broad roster problem. Neither is guaranteed to be available at a particular pick.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. The conference recognition and prior return background are supported. NFL press/off translation, tackling reliability, coverage-team usefulness and market range remain open.

**What would make us change our mind?** Complete games showing consistent leverage, recovery and controlled tackling would strengthen the corner case. Verified return or coverage-team usefulness would establish an active pathway. Repeated dependence on gambling, or inability to stay connected without illegal contact, would weaken it. No later professional outcome is admissible as evidence.
