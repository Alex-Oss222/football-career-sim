#### The center sequence

The meaningful unit of evaluation is the whole play: identify the front, communicate the point and combination, deliver the snap, then execute the block. These actions compete for attention. A correct declaration followed by a poor snap is a failure; a sound individual block after an incorrect point can leave another defender free.

For pass protection, separate a covered center from an uncovered helper. The covered center may have immediate nose contact; the helper must keep eyes and body available for the developing threat. Against a twist, evaluate the handoff and whether the center remains square enough to take the next rusher. Counting clean pockets without identifying those responsibilities would overstate what the tape proves.

For the run game, ACE work is particularly important. The center must establish the first-level block and coordinate the departure with the guard. Inside movement asks for control of the defender's near side; Outside asks for reach and continued positioning. A physical run-blocking reputation cannot answer both questions by itself.

#### First job and teaching burden

**Projected first job to investigate:** Reserve center. Brewster and Meester retain their current roles; this report does not create a starting vacancy. Stork must show enough complete-sequence reliability to enter a game without forcing Cousins to redesign the protection operation.

**Development cost:** Substantial at first because the position combines execution and communication. Existing center experience may reduce unfamiliarity, but the branch vocabulary still needs to be learned. Tice and Yarno would teach the front and combination rules alongside snap mechanics. Bates would test whether the quarterback and center reach the same answer when the defense changes late.

Guard cross-training can improve roster flexibility only after the center job is stable. Assigning every interior position immediately risks producing a reserve who knows several sets of words without controlling any of the associated techniques.

**Coach's answer:** Open. The teachability test is a corrected response that persists across different looks, not a confident verbal explanation. No interview, private meeting or staff opinion is invented.

The strongest objection is assuming an established college center is an easy reserve projection. **Tier:** Not yet graded. **What would change the view?** Consistent snaps, correct late adjustments and pass protection that survives direct contact would strengthen the active-roster case. A recurring tradeoff between communication and execution would make the developmental burden larger than the positional résumé suggests.
