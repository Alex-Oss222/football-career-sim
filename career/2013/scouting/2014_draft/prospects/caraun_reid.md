#### Interior role and run defense

**Projected first job to investigate:** Rotational interior defender, beginning with a three-technique study. This is conditional analysis, not an assigned practice alignment or a claim that Reid already controls that job. A nose projection would require a separate demonstration against direct and combined contact.

For the weak B-gap assignment, evaluate first-step direction, initial hand placement and the ability to retain the called gap when the blocker changes leverage. Quick penetration is useful when it disrupts the intended path. It becomes a liability when it creates space behind the defender or gives the linebacker an unexpected fit.

The decisive run samples include plays directed at him, combinations that delay the second blocker and runs away that require disciplined pursuit. Pursuit effort must preserve the cutback relationship. A defender who abandons his responsibility to chase a visible ball carrier can inflate activity without helping the structure.

#### Pass rush and special teams

Rush evaluation should identify an actual sequence: threat, contact, counter and finish. Separate a blocker beaten cleanly from a pressure created by another defender or an unblocked alignment. On longer plays, assess whether Reid can disengage without losing the quarterback's escape lane.

Documented blocked-kick involvement creates a specific special-teams question. Lowry would need to determine whether the useful element is get-off, leverage, reach, alignment or an assignment advantage that can transfer. It does not establish kickoff coverage, punt coverage or a complete game-day role.

**Development cost:** Moderate to substantial, depending on how much contact and counter technique needs rebuilding. Pleasant would begin with one alignment and the associated run/rush rules. Adding nose, end and multiple movement calls immediately would obscure the first-role evaluation.
