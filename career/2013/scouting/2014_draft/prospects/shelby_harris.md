#### Assessment

Harris merits a substantive historical-football file, but he is not presently a cleared selection candidate. The most useful work is to separate three questions: what his earlier college record supports, what is known about his program status, and what remains necessary before a current physical or role projection can be made.

A late-round label cannot substitute for those answers. Neither a positive projection nor an automatic rejection follows from being on Stone's list. The file must avoid a redemption story, a character verdict or a claim that a missed season has either improved or ruined his football.

#### Evidence and correction to the source gap

Sports Network reported Harris's dismissal on March 4, 2013; the hosted copy carries a later update. [S11](../sources_2013_12_01.md). This improves the source trail available to this report. It does not establish the missing eligibility facts, and no private allegation is inferred.

Illinois State's biography documents defensive-tackle starts and productive 2012 play. A separate school game report from October 27, 2012 records pass-rush contribution against Northern Iowa. These are evidence of past college defensive-line work. They do not verify fall 2013 enrollment, training, current conditioning, medical clearance or draft eligibility. [S12–S13](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

The source record therefore improves the dismissal chronology without resolving every status question. No interview, reference call or personal explanation is invented. In particular, the file does not treat an unsourced account of private training as current football evidence.

#### What the prior football record can support

The earlier interior role is the proper starting point for historical cut-ups. It supports asking whether his disruption came from a repeatable get-off and block defeat, a designed movement, favorable blocking, or pursuit after another defender created the opening. A sack total alone cannot decide among those mechanisms.

**Strengths supported by tape:** There is no independently charted film in this packet. The supported positive is a prior record of interior production and starting participation. It justifies continued research; it does not establish an NFL rush move, consistent anchor or ability to play every front position.

**Weaknesses supported by tape:** Not established. The absence of a current football sample is an evidence problem, not proof of diminished effort or ability. The level-of-competition transition also remains open. An FCS lineman's win must be studied for the transferable technique, rather than either discounted automatically or treated as equivalent to an NFL win.

#### Conditional front-fit analysis

If status is later verified, the alignment study should compare the job he actually played with the responsibilities in Crennel's active front. A weak-side 3-technique must attack the B-gap without opening an uncontrolled escape lane. A closed end must preserve the C-gap and edge relationship against attached surfaces. A nose must handle a different anchor burden. Those are separate evaluations.

This report does **not** assign Harris one of those jobs while the directive's verification gate remains open. It identifies how the eventual decision should be made. If the interior evidence is strongest, begin there; do not make a larger-looking defensive lineman an edge player by default. If an end projection is proposed, require actual edge-control evidence rather than the generic DL/DE designation.

Reducing inside on passing downs adds another assignment set. The staff would need to establish rush-lane integrity, response to draws and screens, and the ability to execute a game without colliding with the other rusher. Penetration that releases the quarterback through an abandoned lane is not a complete rush win.

#### Development cost and activation

**Practice cost: substantial, conditional on eligibility, lawful access and medical clearance.** Current conditioning and readiness need assessment before a contact progression can be specified. Once cleared, begin with one alignment, a limited run-fit assignment and an identifiable rush plan. Add a counter only after the primary move and lane discipline are functional. Cross-training comes after a usable first job.

A defensive-line reserve needs a rotation assignment that survives both run and pass, or a genuinely useful specialist contribution that justifies dressing him. Field-goal block can be investigated, but historical disruption does not automatically establish a special-teams role. If the staff must protect him from too many ordinary situations, the active-roster case is weak.

**Can the job be taught?** That cannot yet be answered for a chosen alignment. The missing current sample and unresolved status prevent a responsible promise about either the training load or the time to usefulness.

#### Best objection and comparison

The strongest objection is the combined uncertainty: selection status, recent football, present physical readiness and an unverified first role. Calling him a sixth-round bargain would hide those costs. Reid is a useful comparison because his current college record offers a different evidence base for a developmental interior player. The comparison should be made at the same football job once Harris is cleared for one.

**Tier:** Not yet graded; not on a cleared acquisition list. Confidence in the prior defensive-tackle record is materially higher than confidence in a December 1 NFL projection.

**What would make us change our mind?** A dated eligibility determination, a current permitted evaluation and independently verified role evidence could move the file from hold to a real acquisition comparison. Unresolved status keeps the hold in place. A disappointing current evaluation would weaken the football case without requiring speculation about private conduct.
