# Kyle Van Noy, OLB

**College and status:** BYU; senior outside linebacker. BYU's January 7, 2013 release records his decision to return for his senior season.
**Focus round:** Class-wide early defensive investment and Day 2 availability comparison, not a promised fourth-round alternative.
**Suggested reviewers:** Crennel and Frank Bush; Pleasant for edge work; Lowry for teams.

## Entries

### December 1, 2013 evidence cutoff: comparative research report

#### Assessment and evidence

Van Noy is useful because he tests a different use of linebacker resources from Telvin Smith. BYU's dated release describes an outside linebacker with 2012 production in pass rushing, coverage takeaways and blocked kicks. That supports investigating a multidimensional college role, while leaving each professional assignment to be proven. [S26](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

A November mock emphasizes coverage and pursuit while questioning whether he should be purchased mainly as a pass rusher. It places him in the second round. The tension between a productive college rush résumé and a more restrained professional rush projection is precisely what the report must resolve. [S17](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

**Strengths supported by tape:** No original tape grade is claimed. The sourced record shows involvement across several defensive jobs. **Weaknesses supported by tape:** The outside-rush limitation is an attributed scouting view, not an established defect from this packet. Production alone cannot identify the quality of the block he defeated.

#### Scheme fit and assignment boundaries

The first Jacksonville study should be a strong-side linebacker role, with selective pressure added only where it fits the current Under structure. A stand-up college rush position cannot simply be relabeled as an NFL defensive end. Taking on an attached tight end, fitting the run and carrying a coverage assignment make different demands from attacking a tackle on a passing down.

For base work, study how he keeps the intended outside relationship, separates from blocks and reacts when the offense changes the strength. For coverage, distinguish a short zone drop from matching a receiver through a route break. Interceptions can show awareness and opportunity without proving man-coverage range.

For rush work, identify whether success comes against tackles, tight ends, backs or protection mistakes. An unblocked pressure is evidence of execution, but not of independent edge-rush ability. The question is whether he can threaten one path, force a response and exploit another while keeping the quarterback contained. Crennel should not need to redesign the front solely to create a statistical role for him.

#### First job and teaching cost

**Projected first job to investigate:** Strong-side linebacker with a limited complementary pressure responsibility. That makes him a role comparison with Christian Jones rather than a direct substitute for Smith's proposed WILL study. Posluszny's current injury does not supply evidence that Van Noy can take over the MIKE's communication duties.

**Development cost:** Moderate for one linebacker job, substantial for a simultaneous linebacker/end conversion. Teach the base fit and adjacent-player relationship first; then add the coverage or rush complement. Loading him with every front and package role could conceal which job he actually performs well.

**Special teams:** Prior blocked-kick involvement is documented. It does not establish coverage-lane discipline, a release against a blocker or finishing in space. Lowry needs those assignments evaluated separately before a dressing pathway is considered secure.

**Coach's answer:** Open. The research supports asking whether a bounded role can preserve his college versatility without creating an excessive teaching burden. It does not report a staff conclusion.

#### Value and strongest objection

The objection is selecting a hybrid without identifying the job that earns his snaps. If he is purchased as an edge rusher but cannot win there independently, the coverage value may be insufficient compensation at an early price. If he can handle the strong-side job and add useful pressure, the roster case is broader.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. **What would change the view?** Independent rush wins and dependable base contact would increase role flexibility. Coverage that is useful only in a narrow zone assignment would reduce it. The contemporary market signal argues for evaluating him before Jacksonville's third-round window, not assuming that window will contain him.
