#### Guard projection and protection

**Projected first job to investigate:** Guard, beginning with the college interior work and the current tackle evidence that bears on that transition. The recommendation is a way to test a roster role, not a conclusion that Thomas cannot play tackle. His value depends on the first useful position, not the total number of positions attached to his name.

The interior study should begin with immediate contact. A tackle has room to shape a set; a guard may have to absorb a defender before gaining the preferred position. Thomas needs to keep a usable base, replace his hands and avoid allowing a quick loss to enter Cousins's throwing space. A movement advantage would help recovery only if the body remains connected through contact.

The exchange language deserves equal attention. In HALF, a guard must know when a rusher is his, when a combination is available and when the center can no longer help. A prospective reserve must handle the team's actual calls after a sudden substitution. Prior college experience shortens the list of unfamiliar situations but does not erase the language change.

#### Run-game fit and versatility

The movement hypothesis makes pulls and second-level work worth investigating. Judge whether he reaches the correct entry point under control, not whether he arrives quickly. For down blocks and combinations, the power question becomes concrete: does his initial contact secure the first level long enough for the adjacent blocker to leave?

Tackle cover remains a separate benefit to establish. If the interior role is sound but the edge protection requires extensive help, he may still be useful as a guard reserve. If he can handle both, the game-day roster value increases. Neither outcome is assumed here.

**Development cost:** Moderate to substantial. The prior interior experience may reduce novelty, but the team would still need guard footwork, shorter pass-protection timing and combination reps. Teaching immediate tackle cover at the same time raises the cost and risks leaving both roles incomplete.

**Coach's answer:** Open. Tice and Yarno should first decide whether the guard projection survives power and compressed space. Only then should they price the additional cross-training. No future practice or medical development is imported.

#### Comparison and objection

Thomas is an alternative to Turner's acquisition uncertainty, not an exact substitute for Turner's right-guard background. Jackson and Richardson offer guard-first comparisons; Bitonio and Martin test how much value the team assigns to tackle projection.

The strongest objection is counting versatility before establishing a functional anchor at one position. **Tier:** Not yet graded. **What would change the view?** Reliable interior contact, correct exchanges and movement that survives congestion would strengthen the guard study. A need for extensive help against power would narrow the role. No verified December 1 range makes Thomas a guaranteed third-round or late-round fallback.
