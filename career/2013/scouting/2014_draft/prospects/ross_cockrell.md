#### Cover 1 and Cover 3 translation

**Projected first job to investigate:** Outside corner, starting with the techniques most clearly represented in the college evidence. Nickel and safety are separate evaluations. A player who understands a college secondary can still require substantial work when the route distribution, field spacing and run responsibilities change.

In Cover 1, evaluate the release, initial leverage and transition through the route break as a connected sequence. A defender may recover against one opponent while losing the same margin against a faster or more precise receiver. The question is whether the original technique remains sound when recovery is no longer enough.

In Cover 3, he must preserve the deep responsibility while processing threats underneath. Pass breakups can result from good recognition, favorable positioning or a late throw. The tape needs to distinguish those causes before a ball-production résumé is translated into professional zone awareness.

At the catch point, separate locating the ball from controlling the receiver's space legally and arriving at the proper time. A successful interception is only one outcome; forcing a difficult catch without compromising leverage may be equally useful evidence. This packet does not invent targets, completion rates or penalties.

#### Run support, teams and development

Run support requires the correct relationship to the inside fit. Oden should evaluate whether Cockrell closes under control, keeps the runner from escaping the intended leverage and finishes rather than simply making contact. Prior tackle totals would not settle those questions.

**Special teams:** Duke's biography records a blocked-field-goal return. Returning a loose ball is not evidence that the player created the block or can cover kicks. Lowry still needs release, lane integrity, block defeat and finishing evidence before assigning a coverage-unit pathway.

**Development cost:** Moderate for a bounded outside role; substantial if immediate nickel or safety cover is added. Begin with one leverage system and its release responses, then add motion and route combinations. The first game-day pathway would be reserve outside corner plus a separately earned teams assignment.

**Coach's answer:** Open. No meeting or learning result is recorded. The available sources support continued evaluation while leaving the speed, contact and communication translation to evidence.

#### Value objection and trigger

The strongest objection is confusing extensive experience with a dependable professional floor. Compare Cockrell with Butler on the actual outside-corner techniques and with Fuller on the larger acquisition decision. Competition level supplies context; it cannot replace the technique comparison.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. **What would change the view?** Repeatable leverage, controlled transitions and reliable tackling would strengthen reserve usefulness. Repeated dependence on recovery or favorable help would narrow the coverage menu. No dated market evidence here proves that Cockrell will reach the seventh round, so he is a comparator rather than a promised replacement pick.
