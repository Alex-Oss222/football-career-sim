### December 1, 2013 evidence cutoff: comprehensive research report

**Chronology note:** This retrospective research supplement retains a December 1 evidence cutoff. It does not replace or close the December 13 Phase IV coach-read entry above.

**Report type:** Ex-ante research and conditional coaching analysis; no additional coaching window is recorded. Tice and Yarno remain the assigned evaluators. [Source register](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

#### Assessment and first job

The first job to investigate is developmental left tackle, followed by a separately earned emergency right-tackle qualification. That is more defensible than immediately calling him a ready swing tackle. Boise State's prior use on both sides gives the second test a basis, but the NFL job still requires evidence at NFL speed and in Jacksonville's protection structure.

The selection question is whether he can become a useful reserve before the roster needs him to start. Bradfield currently provides swing-tackle and sixth-lineman work. Leno must be compared against the function of that roster place, rather than receiving credit simply because he is a younger tackle prospect.

#### Supporting evidence

Boise State's dated June 19 preseason release identifies Leno as a redshirt senior offensive tackle. The August 12 prospect preview describes experience at both tackle spots and a developmental zone-blocking projection, while questioning his bulk. The school biography supports the underlying position history; full 2013 career totals are excluded because Boise State's season is not complete at this cutoff. [S08–S10](../sources_2013_12_01.md).

**Strengths supported by tape:** The report has no independent charted film. The positive evidence is prior tackle experience and an attributed contemporary movement/zone-fit projection. That is enough to justify a reserve-development study, not to certify either side of the line.

The importance of the experience is procedural. A player who has previously operated on each side has encountered different stance and hand relationships. Whether those habits remain useful under a new system must still be tested. Experience is a starting point for instruction, not the completion of it.

#### Pass-set consistency and recovery

The first evaluation should separate getting to the set landmark from remaining functional after contact. Edge speed tests width and timing. The inside counter tests whether he opens his hips too early or loses control of the inside half. Power tests whether he can settle without retreating through the quarterback's launch point.

Recovery is particularly important for a reserve. An emergency player cannot rely on a perfect opening set every repetition. After losing the first contact, can he re-establish useful position without grabbing or redirecting the rusher into Cousins? No answer is recorded yet. The report therefore does not invent a recurring balance defect or a history of holding penalties.

The right-side trial must be real. Flipping the stance changes the post foot, strike timing and relationship to the guard. A left-tackle grade cannot be copied into the right-tackle column. Protecting a heavy formation with extra help also cannot stand in for an isolated dropback assignment.

#### Run-game compatibility

The dated zone-fit opinion makes Outside and backside cutoff work logical places to begin checking the projection. Stone's offense also requires Power, Counter and combinations. The broader run menu matters: an effective reach blocker who cannot secure a down block still requires accommodation.

On DEUCE, require enough movement and control at the first level before climbing. On the backside of a gap run, determine whether penetration arrives before the puller clears. On an attached extra-OL assignment, test the specific leverage without pretending it proves a complete tackle role.

**Weaknesses supported by tape:** Insufficient direct evidence for a diagnosed technique failure. The source's bulk concern is an attributed projection risk. It cannot be converted into a verified weak anchor, nor can later measurements be used to settle it early.

#### Teaching cost and roster economics

**Practice cost: substantial for a dependable two-sided reserve; moderate for a single developmental position.** Establish one stance and protection family first, then introduce the other side with explicit comparison of footwork and adjacent-player communication. He needs paired exchange repetitions with guards as well as isolated edge work. A prospect who looks acceptable in one-on-ones can still fail the reserve job through unhandled games or late pressure.

Game-day usefulness requires a complete emergency package on at least one side and a credible contingency on the other, or a staff-approved alternative arrangement using the existing line. The latter arrangement has a cost: moving multiple starters after one injury disrupts more relationships. That cost belongs in the acquisition comparison.

**Can the job be taught?** The mechanics and language can be taught, and prior college work on both sides makes the attempt reasonable. His correction retention is unknown. No automatic improvement from an NFL strength program is assumed, and adding body mass is not treated as a guaranteed solution to contact problems.

#### Best objection and comparison

The strongest objection is spending a pick and roster spot on a reserve who is not ready to cover an emergency and does not win a separate active role. The fifth-round focus does not remove that cost. Hurst supplies a contemporary tackle benchmark; Thomas supplies a comparison in which guard may be the first NFL job. Neither is presumed available in the fifth round.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. College position history is better established than the reserve floor. No reliable December 1 market range has been confirmed for Leno in this packet.

**What would make us change our mind?** Correct protection on both sides against varied rush types and exchanges would support a genuine swing role. A stable left-side file with persistent right-side failure would keep him as a single-position development player. Repeated losses that require protection help on routine snaps would reduce the reserve value, regardless of attractive movement in the run game.

### Week 15 addendum: evidence through December 13, 2013

**New evidence:** Boise State's December 10 release identifies Leno as a first-team All-Mountain West left tackle. The same dated release reports 38 consecutive starts, including the most recent 25 at left tackle. These are the school's figures as of that release, not later complete-career totals. [W02](../sources_week15_2013_12_13.md).

**Answer to the Phase IV question:** Whether he can dress as a rookie reserve remains OPEN. The new source strengthens the experience record, especially on the left, but does not establish a professional emergency package on either edge.

That distinction is consequential. Prior right-tackle exposure gives a reason to test the other side; the sustained recent left-tackle work gives the first study a clearer starting point. Neither tells the staff that changing stance, post foot and adjacent communication will be immediate. A long starting streak is evidence of college continuity, not proof of independent protection after a sudden NFL substitution.

The next comparison with Hurst should emphasize the response to a lost first exchange. Can the tackle re-establish position without exposing the inside lane or collapsing the pocket? Can he handle a guard-tackle game when the front changes after the original point? The award supplies no answer to those questions, so the teaching proposal should stay narrower than a ready-made swing role.

**Teaching and game-day cost:** One-side development remains the lower-burden plan. A credible swing role requires separately demonstrated right-side protection and communication. If the roster must move a starter to accommodate him after one injury, include that disruption in the reserve cost rather than describing him as uncomplicated depth.

**Best objection after the update:** The fifth-round focus still risks buying a player whose future promise exceeds his immediate emergency utility. The December recognition does not establish that he will be available at that price.

**Tier:** Not yet graded. **Next decision-changing evidence:** Complete protection work on each side and a defined emergency call menu that does not depend on constant additional help. No reserve activation or coach conclusion is recorded.
