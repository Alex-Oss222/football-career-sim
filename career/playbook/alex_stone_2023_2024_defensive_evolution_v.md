# Alex Stone Defensive Playbook

## 2023-2024 Evolution V: Motion and Replacement

> **Team-neutral defensive evolution manual** | Primary play-caller: Alex Stone
> **System sentence:** Change the answer late. Keep the rules fast.

This document evolves the latest prior Stone defense. Silence means the prior rule survives. The purpose is not to replace the system every few seasons, but to record when enough changes affect how the defense is taught, called, matched and simulated.

**Primary evolution:** fast-motion communication and late replacement

**What changes enough to matter:** PUSH/SWITCH/ROCK/STAY language, Amoeba growth, protection-driven replacement pressure, static defensive complements

**Human-system rule:** 2024 cuts disguise that slows the defense more than the offense; late change remains a tool rather than the identity of every snap.

## Evolution Phases

2023 accelerates the exchange. 2024 learns to disguise without dependence.

# 1. EVOLUTION V IDENTITY AND CHANGE RULES

Evolution V is about making the offense solve the defense while the ball is being snapped, without making the defense solve itself.

## 1.1 Evolution Laws

1. **Motion must not dictate one defensive answer.** The call determines whether the defense locks, pushes, switches or stays.
2. **Fast communication beats clever communication.** Late motion receives shorter words and fewer checks.
3. **Replacement pressure attacks protection.** The fourth rusher is chosen to make the offense wrong about count or scan.
4. **Static calls protect disguise.** Under Quarters and normal four-man rush remain answers when motion tries to trigger rotation.
5. **Amoeba is a presentation.** The defense still owns gaps and contain; it is not a separate scheme.
6. **Shell and pressure can change independently.** Two high can pressure; one high can rush four.
7. **Bunch/stack is a distribution problem.** Box, Switch and Push rules are rehearsed rather than improvised.
8. **Zero is selective.** It finishes a protection/matchup problem; it does not define toughness.
9. **Disguise cannot steal get-off.** Pass rushers are allowed to play fast when late movement adds no value.
10. **Heavy personnel still tests the fit.** Three-safety defense stays on the field only when it can tackle and own added gaps.
11. **The weekly menu shrinks after the correction.** A smaller set of well-timed disguises is better than constant movement.

## 1.2 What Does Not Change

- 4-3 Under remains the fit foundation.
- The defense teaches jobs and relationships before pictures.
- A pressure must preserve contain and a named coverage answer.
- Personnel multiplicity is useful only if the current eleven can execute multiple calls.
- The weekly plan is smaller than the master defense.
- Show -> Force -> Counter remains the play-caller's sequencing language.

## 1.3 What Changes Enough to Define Evolution V

| Area         | Evolution IV             | Evolution V                                            |
| ------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Motion       | Fit/shell diagnostic     | Fast motion receives LOCK/PUSH/SWITCH/ROCK/STAY system |
| Pressure     | Two-high sims            | More protection-driven late replacement                |
| Presentation | Mint/Penny plus disguise | Amoeba grows, but remains presentation                 |
| Coverage     | Split-safety rotation    | Bunch/stack BOX and bracket rules mature               |
| Correction   | 2022 fitter precision    | 2024 static complements and communication reduction    |

## 1.4 Historical Boundary

Do not import later 2025-26 fully consolidated mature menu into 2023. 2024 may contain most components but still has its own correction and narrower integration.

# 2. 2023 ACCELERATION AND 2024 CORRECTION

## 2.1 2023: Accelerate the Exchange

Fast motion, condensed releases and sophisticated protection force the defense to communicate later. Stone increases PUSH, SWITCH, ROCK and late replacement pressure so the offense cannot use movement as a clean diagnostic tool.

## 2.2 The 2023 Overcorrection

Some disguise becomes self-inflicted complexity. Safeties rotate too late, pass rushers lose get-off while waiting on checks, and fast motion creates communication errors the offense did not truly earn.

## 2.3 2024: Disguise Without Dependence

The defense keeps the multiplicity but formalizes static answers. A motion-heavy opponent cannot force the defense to spin every snap. Under Quarters, Mint Quarters and base four-man rush become deliberate constraints on the disguise package.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

Three-safety personnel becomes the normal planning center. The defense is still allowed to use a true SAM or third linebacker when the roster or opponent demands it, but the master system no longer requires one body type to own the strong overhang job.

| Role        | Mature function                  | Primary job                                 |
| ----------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Open EDGE   | 5 to wide-9                      | Primary edge rush, contain, occasional drop |
| 3-Technique | Weak 3 / reduced rush alignment  | Interior disruption and games               |
| NOSE        | 0/1/shade                        | Anchor, dent, command doubles               |
| Closed EDGE | 4i to 7                          | Edge set, reduce, power rush                |
| MIKE        | Box communicator                 | Fit, carry, mug, green-dog                  |
| WILL        | Space/fit linebacker             | Pursuit, match, replace                     |
| STAR        | Safety-bodied SAM/slot           | Force, slot match, pressure, rotate         |
| Safety A/B  | Interchangeable by qualification | Post, half, quarter, robber, bracket        |
| Corners     | Outside match players            | Press/off, trap, crack replace              |

## 3.1 Qualification, Not Pretend Interchangeability

Functional interchangeability does not mean every defender is identical. The weekly role map identifies who can play post, quarter, slot man, force, interior mug, edge pressure, tight-end match and robber jobs. The call survives when job ownership changes; the staff does not assign a player work he cannot execute merely to preserve a label.

## 3.2 Defensive-Line Room

The front needs at least one interior player who can anchor 0/1 technique and at least one interior penetrator who can threaten a guard one-on-one. Edge players are graded separately for rush, edge-setting, reduction and drop ability.

## 3.3 Linebacker Room

MIKE and WILL must both understand front declarations, fold fits, back releases and pressure replacement. One may be the better downhill player and the other the better space player; the system uses that difference rather than denying it.

## 3.4 Safety / STAR Room

Safeties are graded by functional jobs: post, half, quarter, slot, bracket, insert, pressure and tight-end match. A defender can be interchangeable in two or three categories without being interchangeable in all of them.

## 3.5 Corner Room

Corners must play man, zone and match leverage, but their run-support education matters too. Crack replacement, cloud force, trap technique and pressure rotation cannot become emergency concepts installed only on Friday.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, MATCHING, AND OPERATING STATES

The defense uses personnel to answer offensive bodies, but it does not want the offense to dictate the call merely by substituting. The staff distinguishes between changing defenders and changing the picture.

## 4.1 MATCH / HOLD / MASK

| State | Meaning | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| MATCH | Substitute into the desired defensive body group after the offense changes personnel. | Heavy groups, specialist rushers, true dime, goal line. |
| HOLD | Keep the current eleven and change front, leverage or assignment. | Prevent personnel from becoming a defensive tell. |
| MASK | Keep the current eleven, preserve a familiar pre-snap picture, and change rush/coverage late. | Protection stress, shell ambiguity, third down. |

## 4.2 Matching Principles

- Match for football reasons: mass, matchup, coverage quality, fatigue or specialist value.
- Do not substitute a pressure package that announces pressure.
- Once favorable personnel is on the field, HOLD is preferred to unnecessary substitution.
- The defense must survive offensive formation transformation without requiring a sideline call for every shift.
- A productive pass rusher or matchup defender is not removed merely to satisfy a rotation target.

## 4.3 Offensive Tempo

If the offense prevents substitution, the defense checks to the most complete call available from the current eleven. Communication gets shorter as tempo increases. Exotic movement is removed before base fit or coverage communication is compromised.

# 5. FRONT ARCHITECTURE

Front is the picture the offensive line must block. Fit is the rule the defense must execute. The system expands the number of pictures only when the new front creates a different blocking problem while preserving understandable fit relationships.

| Front     | Structure                                 | Primary reason                             |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Under     | 4-down home fit                           | Base run geometry and weak 3-tech          |
| Over      | 4-down change-up                          | Move penetration and fit declaration       |
| Mint      | 0/1 plus two 4i techniques                | Close interior space and play split safety |
| Bear      | Interior covered                          | Remove combinations and change pull paths  |
| Penny     | Five LOS threats, one box LB              | Rush ambiguity and edge control            |
| 3-3 / 3-2 | Three true down bodies with movable edges | Passing-down multiplicity                  |
| Amoeba    | Presentation, not base front              | Hide rush source and final gaps            |

## 5.1 Under Foundation

In the normal Under fit, the weak 3-technique owns the weak B-gap, the shade controls the strong A-gap, the closed end controls the strong C-gap, MIKE fits the strong B-gap, WILL fits the weak A-gap, and the overhang owns force/outside fit by call. The open edge owns contain and the weak C-gap unless a pressure or spill tag changes the relationship.

## 5.2 Edge Technique Tags

**BOX:** keep the ball inside and maintain outside leverage.

**SPILL:** attack the kick-out and force the ball to the next support defender.

**SQUEEZE:** close the pull/down-block surface while staying responsible for the runner.

**PEEL:** convert from rush to coverage when the assigned back releases immediately by rule.

## 5.3 Front Movement

Slants, spikes, loops and games are selected to change blocking angles. Movement is not called merely to create activity. Every movement must preserve a named adjacent gap or replacement rule.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION RESPONSE SYSTEM

Offensive movement is treated as a communication problem first and a coverage clue second. The defense does not chase motion simply to appear aggressive. The call determines whether the defense travels, passes, switches or rotates the responsibility.

| Call   | Meaning                                  | Defensive use                             |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| LOCK   | Travel with matchup                      | Man or selected locked match              |
| PUSH   | Pass responsibility across               | Zone/match spacing                        |
| SWITCH | Exchange two assignments                 | Bunch, stack, fast cross                  |
| BUMP   | Reset box/underneath spacing             | Keep fit intact                           |
| ROCK   | Safeties exchange high/low leverage      | Change passing strength without full spin |
| SPIN   | Rotate shell and fitter                  | Late one-high or pressure support         |
| STAY   | Structure remains stable                 | Motion did not earn a change              |
| BOX    | Defend condensed cluster as distribution | Bracket/zone match versus bunch           |

## 6.1 Motion Rules

- Motion cannot make the defense lose the original run gap count.
- A receiver crossing the formation does not automatically prove man coverage.
- The defense must know who becomes force when the overhang travels or pushes.
- Fast motion is handled with fewer words, not more words.
- If communication quality declines, the weekly menu is reduced before base leverage rules are altered.

## 6.2 Static and Repeated-Picture Rule

The offense should not be able to force the same defensive response merely by repeating the same motion. A single presentation can carry LOCK, PUSH, SWITCH or STAY answers depending on the coverage family.

# 7. DEFENSIVE OPERATING SYSTEM

The defense does not ask every player to diagnose the entire offense. It asks each level to identify the information that changes its assignment.

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Seven

1. Offensive personnel and likely eligible distribution.
2. Run surface and formation strength.
3. Backfield set and quarterback run threat.
4. Offensive width: open, normal, condensed, bunch/stack, Empty.
5. Motion/shift reaction required by the call.
6. Protection indicators: back location, tight-end surface, likely slide or Empty count.
7. Situation: down, distance, field zone, clock and likely concept family.

## 7.2 Post-Snap Confirmation

The defense confirms release, run action, pullers and actual rush/coverage relationship. Pre-snap indicators create anticipation, not permission to abandon the call.

## 7.3 Error Hierarchy

The first priority is eliminate explosives created by busted assignments. The second is fit the run with correct leverage. The third is create negative plays. A disguise that creates a free runner for the offense is not a successful disguise.

# 8. RUSH AND PRESSURE SYSTEM

Four-man simulated and replacement pressure is the normal pressure center. Five-man and zero pressures are selected from protection evidence rather than emotional preference.

| Family            | Rush count | Purpose                                            |
| ----------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Base + games      | 4          | Win normal matchups and move protection            |
| Sim / Replace     | 4          | Change rush source while keeping seven in coverage |
| Double Mug        | 3-6        | Make center/back declare protection                |
| Penny/Amoeba      | 3-6        | Hide the fourth rusher and final gaps              |
| Fire Zone         | 5          | Pressure plus three-deep/three-under               |
| Targeted Overload | 5-6        | Attack known slide, scan or weak blocker           |
| Zero              | 6+         | Selective finishing pressure                       |
| Drop 8            | 3          | Counter hot answers and protection release         |

## 8.1 Rush Source Rule

Rush count and rush source are separate. The offense must not be able to infer how many defenders are coming merely from the number aligned near the line.

## 8.2 Protection-Driven Pressure

The weekly pressure plan identifies the center point, half-slide direction, back scan, tight-end protection role, Empty five-man count and weakest individual blocker. Pressure is selected to attack those rules.

## 8.3 Contain and Replacement

Every pressure names the contain player and the interior replacement relationship. A free rusher that opens an unowned quarterback escape lane is graded as a pressure error.

## 8.4 Coverage Must Know the Hot Throw

Before a pressure is activated, the staff identifies the offense's likely immediate answer. The defense may concede one throw by design, but it should know which throw that is and rally with leverage.

# 9. RUN DEFENSE

The run defense is one-gap in teaching even when movement, reduced fronts or spill calls make the picture look more complex. Defenders fit named space and understand who replaces them when pressure changes the first-level gap.

## 9.1 Core Run Problems

| Run family | Defensive priority | Weekly adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Duo | Dent doubles, force the declared linebacker/fitter to stay square | Move 3-tech, fit safety from depth, change surface count |
| Power | Identify kick/log and keep the pull path from becoming clean | Box/spill tag, wrong-arm by plan, backer scrape |
| Counter / GT | Track pullers without over-running false flow | Spill/box mix, reduce edge, scrape exchange |
| Inside Zone | Close vertical seams and control cutback | Slant/lag nose, backside pursuit discipline |
| Outside Zone | Set edge without creating easy cutback | Force/lever rules, reduced front, crack replace |
| Split Zone | Identify slicer and backside edge responsibility | Squeeze/peel/arc alert, safety replace |
| Pin-Pull / Toss | Force runner to declare and defeat perimeter leverage | Crack replace, cloud force, support angle |
| Draw | Maintain rush-lane integrity and second-level patience | Spy/rat only when QB/run threat warrants |

## 9.2 Puller Language

**KICK:** force player expects a kick-out attempt.

**LOG:** if the offense logs the spill player, the next defender must overlap outside.

**WRAP:** second puller becomes a linebacker problem; the fit must pass from line to second level cleanly.

## 9.3 Cutback Rule

The backside edge and pursuit player cannot both chase the front side. Every call defines who owns boot/keep and who closes the cutback lane.

## 9.4 Safety Fit

Split-safety calls still identify a plus-one fitter. A safety can begin deep and enter the run fit after the run declares. Pre-snap depth does not remove run responsibility.

# 10. COVERAGE SYSTEM

The coverage menu is multiple, but the pre-snap shell is intentionally less informative. Two-high is the most common disguise shell, not a coverage declaration.

## 10.1 Coverage Families

| Family | Core calls | Teaching center |
|---|---|---|
| Post | Cover 3, 3 Match, 3 Buzz/Cloud | Middle-third structure, hook/curl leverage, rotation |
| Man | Cover 1, 1 Robber/Rat, selected 0 | Leverage, help location, matchup ownership |
| Split | Quarters, 2/Palms, Tampa, 6, 8 | Vertical distribution, quarter/half leverage, run fit from depth |
| Bracket | Cone, Box, 1 Double, opponent-specific doubles | Remove qualified threat without rebuilding whole call |
| Pressure zone | Fire 3 and related pressure matches | Replace vacated rush area and cap quick throw |

## 10.2 Shell Is Information, Not the Call

The same two-high picture can become Quarters, Cover 6, Tampa, Cover 3 Buzz, Cover 1 or pressure coverage after the snap when the call and personnel permit it. The objective is to delay certainty without delaying defensive execution.

## 10.3 Match Qualification

Match rules identify route distribution, not route names. Defenders react to vertical, under, out, in and release relationships according to their leverage. The defense does not create a separate call for every offensive concept.

## 10.4 Bracket Discipline

A bracket exists because the opponent has a qualified threat or concept worth removing. Brackets are not called merely because a star player is on the field. The staff must identify what the bracket takes away and what space it concedes.

# 11. PLAY-ACTION, MOVEMENT PASS, AND QUARTERBACK CONTROL

The defense respects run action without allowing every linebacker and safety to vacate pass leverage simultaneously.

## 11.1 Run-Key Discipline

Front players defeat blocks first. Second-level defenders read their declared triangle: near guard/tackle action, back track, puller or split-flow player. Safeties trigger from the call, not from a universal "see run, go" rule.

## 11.2 Boot / Slide

The backside edge owns the weekly boot rule: contain, squeeze-and-fall, peel, or pressure replace. The flat defender and crosser player must know who takes the immediate flat and who carries the over.

## 11.3 Fitter-Replacement Rule

If a safety or STAR is the extra run fitter, another defender must understand which route window appears behind that fit. The defense cannot win the run fit by creating an automatic intermediate throw.

## 11.4 Quarterback Movement

Rush lanes are coordinated. Interior pressure should compress the launch point rather than create two open escape alleys. A dedicated spy is a weekly tool, not the default answer to every mobile quarterback.

# 12. SCREEN AND PERIMETER DEFENSE

Screens and perimeter touches are treated as constraint football. The defense does not sell out to stop every bubble at the cost of the core run fit.

## 12.1 Perimeter Rules

- Corner declares crack immediately.
- Next support defender replaces the cracked player.
- Force player keeps outside-in leverage unless the call explicitly spills.
- Pursuit runs through near hip, not behind blockers.
- Defensive linemen retrace when pass set releases suddenly.

## 12.2 Running-Back Screen

Rushers who are invited through must recognize release behavior. The nearest interior defender retraces while second-level defenders overlap inside-out. The goal is to make the screen runner redirect before blockers establish a clean convoy.

## 12.3 Motion Screen

Motion does not override leverage. The defender responsible for the new perimeter space is identified by the motion-response call, not improvised after the ball is thrown.

# 13. ACCESS, READ, KEEP, AND OPTION DEFENSE

The offense can make one defender wrong only if the defense gives that defender both jobs. The weekly plan therefore identifies which calls create true conflict and which calls can exchange the responsibility.

## 13.1 ACCESS Throws

The defense chooses whether to concede immediate perimeter access, tighten corner leverage, or widen an overhang. It does not attempt to erase every hitch/bubble while still fitting the run with the same body.

## 13.2 READ Exchange

Against post-snap run/pass reads, the defense can:

- keep the original conflict player in coverage and replace the run fit;
- trigger the conflict player and cap the throw with safety leverage;
- change the front so the read defender is no longer the expected fitter;
- pressure through the mesh point when weekly evidence supports it.

## 13.3 KEEP

Quarterback keep is handled by call and player threat. The edge defender may box, squeeze, surf or exchange with a scrape player. The defense does not play option football against a quarterback who is not a credible runner merely because the offense has the tag in its book.

# 14. HEAVY PERSONNEL AND EXTRA-OL DEFENSE

Heavy offensive personnel creates a matching decision, not an automatic base-defense call.

## 14.1 Matching Choices

The defense can answer 12/13/21/6OL with:

- normal Big Nickel and fit with safety mass;
- true base with a SAM/third linebacker;
- Bear or reduced front while keeping nickel coverage bodies;
- five-man surface with split safeties;
- specialist goal-line personnel only when field position or body quality demands it.

## 14.2 Heavy-to-Space Rule

If the offense can detach tight ends, F or a sixth lineman eligible surface into spread structure, the defense must know which current defenders own those routes before the shift occurs. Substituting size is useful only if the resulting group can survive the offense's transformation.

## 14.3 Extra Gap Count

The front declaration identifies the added surface and the extra gap it creates. The defense does not treat a sixth lineman as a generic "heavy" alert without naming who owns the new space.

# 15. SITUATIONAL DEFENSE

## 15.1 First and Second Down

Preserve run-fit integrity, make the offense declare its personnel and motion rules, and use four-man rush or replacement pressure to gather protection information.

## 15.2 Third-and-1 to 2

Under/Bear/five-man surfaces rise. Cover 1, Quarters and selective pressure remain available. The defense does not automatically play zero and concede a pick/rub throw.

## 15.3 Third-and-3 to 5

Big Nickel personnel is the normal center. Double Mug, replacement pressure, Cover 1, Quarters, 3 Match and bracket tools all live here.

## 15.4 Third-and-6+

Penny/Amoeba presentations, simulated pressure, drop-eight, brackets and targeted five-man pressures gain value. The defense should make the quarterback confirm both rush and coverage.

## 15.5 Red Zone

Field compression increases the value of bracket, Cover 1, trap/cloud and Bear fronts. Long-developing shell disguise is reduced if it creates alignment stress.

## 15.6 Backed Up

Force the offense to execute. Avoid giving away free explosive access through reckless zero pressure unless the game plan has a clear reason.

## 15.7 Two Minute

Communication speed outranks disguise volume. HOLD current personnel when possible and use the calls players can align to without sideline dependency.

## 15.8 Four Minute

Stop the run first without creating automatic play-action explosives. Make the offense earn first downs and keep the ball inside when clock matters.

# 16. OFFENSIVE RECOGNITION AND DEFENSIVE ANSWERS

The defense studies offensive rules rather than only formation names.

## 16.1 Weekly Recognition Questions

1. Which personnel groups can become Empty without substitution?
2. Which formations carry both downhill run and core pass concepts?
3. Which motion reactions is the offense trying to collect?
4. Which defender is the offense attempting to make the fitter?
5. Which runs create the offense's play-action family?
6. How does the center set protection?
7. What does the back scan?
8. Which edge matchup changes protection?
9. What does the quarterback treat as free ACCESS?
10. Which READ packages are truly active that week?
11. Which formations create condensed crossing or switch releases?
12. Which players can actually execute Choice/Bend/option work?

## 16.2 Answer Categories

The defense answers offensive stress through one of five categories:

1. change the front;
2. change the fitter;
3. change the rush source;
4. change the coverage rotation;
5. hold the picture and execute the base call better.

The fifth answer matters. Not every offensive adjustment deserves another defensive call.

# 17. FRONT, PRESSURE, AND COVERAGE PORTABILITY

The defense's defining multiplicity comes from carrying familiar jobs through different presentations.

| Presentation | Four-man rush | Five-man pressure | Split-safety | Post-safety | Man / bracket |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under | Core | Core | Core | Core | Core |
| Over | Core | Weekly | Core | Core | Core |
| Bear | Core | Core | Weekly | Core | Core |
| Mint / reduced | Core | Weekly | Core | Core | Weekly |
| Penny | Core | Core | Weekly | Core | Core |
| Amoeba | Core | Core | Weekly | Core | Core |

`Core` means the relationship exists in the master system, not that every call is activated from every presentation each week.

## 17.1 Same Picture, Different Category

A Double-Mug picture should be able to produce:

- base four-man rush;
- replacement four-man pressure;
- five-man pressure;
- Fire Zone;
- Quarters;
- Cover 1 Robber;
- drop-eight.

If one picture announces one result, the picture has become a tell.

# 18. WEEKLY GAME-PLANNING PROCESS

The master defense is broader than the Sunday defense.

## 18.1 Offensive Map

The staff charts personnel, formation, motion, run family, protection, quarterback decisions, route families, screen tendencies and situation. The objective is to identify repeated offensive rules that can be stressed without requiring the defense to learn unrelated football.

## 18.2 Weekly Selection Targets

A normal plan activates approximately:

- 3 to 5 front presentations;
- 4 to 6 run-fit change-ups/movements;
- 5 to 8 four-man rush or simulated-pressure calls;
- 3 to 5 five-man pressures;
- 5 to 8 coverage calls/rotations;
- 2 to 4 brackets or opponent-specific tools;
- 1 to 3 zero-pressure calls when matchup warrants;
- dedicated red-zone, third-down, two-minute and four-minute menus.

These are planning bands, not quotas.

## 18.3 Reduction Test

A call is removed from Sunday if the staff cannot answer four questions:

1. Who owns every gap and contain relationship?
2. What offensive problem does the call solve?
3. What quick answer does the offense have against it?
4. What is the next defensive answer if the offense solves the picture?

# 19. INSTALLATION AND PRACTICE SYSTEM

Installation proceeds from rule to presentation.

## 19.1 Install Order

1. Under front, base fit and force language.
2. Cover 1 / Cover 3 leverage and communication.
3. Quarters/split-safety fit.
4. Base four-man rush games.
5. Five-man pressure and Fire Zone.
6. Replacement/simulated pressure.
7. Additional fronts and late rotation.
8. Opponent-specific brackets and situational packages.

## 19.2 Daily Periods

**Run fit:** core runs against changing surfaces.

**Motion communication:** LOCK/PUSH/SWITCH/BUMP/rotation rules at game speed.

**Pressure sort:** defense executes rush while scout offense changes protection.

**Coverage distribution:** bunch, stack, crossers, Empty and heavy-to-space.

**Tackle/finish:** every schematic win still requires leverage and tackling.

## 19.3 Mastery Standard

Players should be able to explain the next job, not merely recite the call. A defender who knows where he aligns but not who replaces him when he pressures has not mastered the system.

# 20. SIMULATION DIRECTIVES FOR 2023-2024

These rules prevent the defense from being flattened into random front/coverage selection.

## 20.1 Call Generation Priority

1. Situation: down, distance, score, field zone and clock.
2. Offensive personnel and current formation family.
3. Weekly run/protection/route map.
4. Defensive personnel currently on field.
5. Front and fit answer.
6. Pressure/rush source only if it solves a documented problem.
7. Coverage and rotation.
8. Sequence relationship to previous calls.
9. Player qualification and fatigue.

Do not randomly choose an exotic presentation and then search for a reason to use it.

## 20.2 Multiplicity Rules

- Same personnel should create multiple presentations.
- Same presentation should create multiple rush/coverage outcomes.
- A new front does not automatically create new fit language.
- If communication errors rise, reduce disguise before changing the core defense.
- A successful pressure can be repeated; artificial variety is not a requirement.

## 20.3 Personnel Rules

- Use the actual roster. Do not invent a third safety capable of slot man, post and box fit if the roster does not contain one.
- A dominant edge can receive more rush opportunities without violating multiplicity.
- A weak coverage linebacker can cause Big Nickel volume to rise.
- A poor tackling safety can reduce split-safety fit volume even if the structure is theoretically attractive.

## 20.4 Pressure Rules

- Simulated pressure requires a defined drop/replacement relationship.
- Five-man pressure requires a protection reason or situational reason.
- Zero pressure is selective unless the weekly opponent specifically justifies more volume.
- Drop-eight exists only if the three-man rush can maintain lanes long enough for the coverage to function.

# APPENDIX D. MATERIAL REDUCED, REJECTED, OR DEFERRED

- multiplicity that creates a second vocabulary for every sub package;
- pressure called without a protection or situational reason;
- every safety being treated as equally interchangeable;
- every two-high call using the same run fitter;
- every motion receiving the same defensive response;
- Amoeba presentation used as an identity rather than a tool;
- zero pressure becoming the default answer to offensive success;
- importing the fully consolidated 2025-26 mature menu as though the 2023-24 correction had already been completed.

# STONE 2023-2024 DEFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** Big Nickel Under/Mint defense with fast-motion communication, late replacement pressure and static complements.
