# Alex Stone Offensive Playbook

## 2018-2020 Evolution III: Constraint and Sequencing

> **Team-neutral evolution manual** | Document revision 3.0 | Primary play-caller: Alex Stone
> **System sentence:** Move the defense before the snap. Attack the rule it uses to move.

This document is the third canonical evolution of Stone's offense. It is an evolution manual layered onto the 2013 Iteration I playbook and the 2016-2017 Evolution II manual. Unless this document explicitly changes a rule, term, concept, protection, personnel relationship, or operating procedure, the earlier rule remains in force.

Evolution III covers three seasons because the system changes in three distinct stages. The 2018 offense expands the marriage between formation, motion, run action, and pass concepts. The 2019 offense is a correction year after defenses prove they can attack a repeated shell. The 2020 offense integrates those lessons into a more balanced, portable system that can operate from 11, 12, 21, 20, and 6OL structures without becoming dependent on any one presentation.

Stone remains the primary game-day play-caller. No coordinator branch is created here. Future staff influences may alter terminology or concept emphasis only when the simulation actually produces them.

# 1. EVOLUTION III IDENTITY AND CHANGE RULES

Evolution II taught the offense to change the picture without changing the people. Evolution III asks a harder question: what does the defense do when the picture changes, and how can the next call attack that reaction?

The motion is no longer valuable merely because a defender follows it. The offense now cares which support player widens, which linebacker bumps, whether the front resets, whether the safety rotates, whether the nickel travels, and whether the defense communicates by pushing the coverage or running with the motion. Those reactions become part of the play-calling sequence.

The offense is still concept based. It does not become a catalog of motion plays. The same Power, Counter, Duo, Drive, Mesh, Y-Cross, Dagger, Post-Cross, and Boot families survive. Motion changes leverage, spacing, force responsibility, and the identity of the conflict player.

## 1.1 The Nine Evolution Laws

1. **Motion needs a job.** Every shift or motion must identify coverage, change leverage, alter the fit, create speed, or protect a later call. Decorative movement is cut.
2. **The first picture is not necessarily the snap picture.** The offense may align in one structure, force the defense to declare, then shift or motion into another legal structure.
3. **The same action must carry multiple outcomes.** Jet action cannot mean Jet Sweep. Outside action cannot mean Boot. Counter pull cannot mean Counter. Each major action needs a run, pass, and constraint answer.
4. **Do not let a successful shell become a prison.** If the defense builds a front or coverage specifically for one repeated personnel/formation family, change the body structure or the point of attack before adding more window dressing.
5. **Physical answers remain first-class answers.** When a defense removes space with width, a six-man surface, or aggressive edge play, Stone is willing to run Duo, Power, Counter, Insert, Trap, or Wham directly at the structure.
6. **READ is a family, not a suffix.** Post-snap run/pass decisions are installed as complete concepts with one conflict defender and a practiced answer. The offense still refuses to attach a throw to every run.
7. **Quarterback running is conditional.** The offense learns option math without requiring a running quarterback. If the player can punish an unblocked defender, KEEP can activate. If he cannot, that menu stays closed.
8. **Personnel diversity must create uncertainty, not bureaucracy.** More packages are not automatically better. The best groupings are the ones that can credibly run, throw, protect, and change formation.
9. **A mature weekly plan gets narrower, not larger.** The master system expands. The Sunday call sheet becomes more connected and more selective.

## 1.2 What Does Not Change

The following remain canonical:

- concept before formation;
- personnel changes the matchup, formation changes the picture;
- the offense can win under center or in shotgun;
- Power and Counter remain identity calls;
- zone concepts complement rather than replace the gap game;
- Stone prefers a quarterback who can operate from the pocket and extend structure when necessary;
- two or three backs may play meaningful snaps without an equal-carry rule;
- HOLD and PRESS are weapons only when the current eleven can execute the next call correctly;
- the extra offensive lineman is not a short-yardage declaration;
- shots need a reason;
- the offense teaches jobs and concepts rather than memorized formation-specific route strings;
- Show -> Stress -> Punish remains the play-caller's sequencing language.

## 1.4 Human-System Rule

Evolution III contains corrections, not uninterrupted progress. Some 2018 ideas are deliberately cut in 2019 because the defense can predict the shell. A few motion packages produce no advantage and disappear. Some READ calls work against one defensive structure and are poor against another. Stone is allowed to be wrong, adapt, and keep old football that still works.

The simulation must not treat a concept as permanently promoted merely because it appears once in Evolution III.

# 2. 2018 INSTALL, 2019 COUNTER, 2020 INTEGRATION

## 2.1 2018: Marry the Action

The 2018 offense is the aggressive extension of Evolution II. Stone sees how much stress can be created when motion, condensed formations, run action, and crossing routes all begin from nearly identical pictures.

The five 2018 priorities are:

1. increase condensed and bunch structures in 11 and 12 personnel;
2. use Jet and Zip closer to the snap when the motion affects the force player or coverage leverage;
3. expand the READ master menu from four calls to six without making all six weekly;
4. increase play-action crossers, Post-Cross, Flood, and Leak off real run families;
5. build full three-call series in which the same formation and motion produce a run, a movement pass, and a constraint.

2018 is the highest-tempo offensive expansion of this iteration. Stone is willing to lean on one shell for a stretch if the defense cannot solve it.

## 2.2 2019: Answer the Answer

The 2019 correction is one of the most important developmental years in Stone's offense.

The lesson is simple: if the offense repeatedly gives the defense the same personnel width, backfield picture, and motion timing, a good defense can build an answer specifically for that shell. More decoration is not always the answer.

The 2019 priorities are:

- reduce any tendency toward 11-personnel dependency;
- increase 12 and 21 usage when the opponent builds nickel or wide-front answers to 11;
- use Duo, Power, Counter, Insert, and direct downhill runs against widened or six-man surfaces;
- vary the point at which motion occurs instead of using one predictable jet timing;
- promote RETURN because it can force communication without always changing who receives the ball;
- use shifts and TRADE to alter the surface before the snap;
- carry more same-action play-action from heavier bodies;
- create a small optional KEEP module for quarterbacks whose athletic profile makes the edge defender accountable;
- increase protection answers against mugged linebackers and simulated pressure looks.

2019 is not Stone abandoning the modern ideas he installed in 2018. It is Stone refusing to become dependent on one successful presentation.

## 2.3 2020: Integrate the Branches

By 2020, the system has three complete ways to create conflict:

- **body conflict:** the defense must decide how to match 12, 21, 20, 13, and 6OL personnel that can still spread and throw;
- **picture conflict:** the same personnel can align condensed, open, empty, wing, bunch, stack, or under center;
- **action conflict:** motion and run action can lead to run, READ, play-action, movement pass, screen, or shot.

The 2020 priorities are:

1. make the offense equally comfortable attacking from 11, 12, or 21 when the weekly matchup calls for it;
2. use 12 and 21 as balanced groupings instead of run declarations;
3. increase under-center play-action and designed rollouts where the protection and quarterback benefit;
4. make RETURN, JET, ZIP, TRADE, and condensed releases part of the weekly sequencing language;
5. formalize the five-man/empty protection communication system;
6. add a dedicated fourth-down call-sheet menu;
7. reduce redundant concepts that do not create a different defensive problem.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

Personnel remains a method of changing matchups, not a statement of offensive philosophy. Stone does not chase a league-average personnel percentage. The weekly plan begins with the opponent's defensive matching rules.

## 3.1 Planning Centers, Not Quotas

The following ranges are planning centers for a normal game. They are not mandatory snap allocations.

| **Group** | **2018 Planning Center** | **2019-20 Planning Center** | **Primary Reason**                                                  |
|-----------|--------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 11        | 48-60%                   | 42-57%                      | Broadest dropback/tempo shell and easiest formation transformation. |
| 12        | 18-26%                   | 20-30%                      | Run/pass balance, play action, empty conversion, surface control.   |
| 21        | 8-14%                    | 10-18%                      | Under-center force, movable F, split-flow action, space conversion. |
| 20        | 4-9%                     | 4-10%                       | Two-runner space package and back matchup creation.                 |
| 13        | 2-6%                     | 2-7%                        | Heavy-to-pass, red zone, backed-up, shot and surface packages.      |
| 10        | 2-7%                     | 2-8%                        | Two-minute, spread pressure, selected matchups.                     |
| True 00   | Rare                     | Rare                        | Specialist empty only when roster quality justifies it.             |
| 6OL       | Weekly package           | Weekly package              | Surface, puller, protection, and presentation tool.                 |

The simulation may move outside these bands when score, injuries, opponent personnel, or a specific matchup demands it.

## 3.2 11 Personnel

11 remains Stone's largest normal grouping, but 2019 eliminates the assumption that 11 must be the offense's center every week.

11 must carry:

- under-center and gun runs;
- condensed and wide spacing;
- Jet/Zip/Return motion;
- Empty without substitution;
- Power, Counter, Duo, Inside, Outside, Split, Pin-Pull, and Jet;
- core quick game and dropback concepts;
- boot and movement pass;
- READ packages;
- screen answers;
- PRESS tempo.

If the defense builds a specialized 11-personnel answer, Stone is willing to leave 11 rather than continuously adding tags to beat that answer.

## 3.3 12 Personnel

The HEAVY/FLEX distinction from Evolution II remains, but the labels describe the body profile available that week rather than separate offenses.

**12 HEAVY** favors attached Y bodies, gap surfaces, 7-man protection, and under-center action.

**12 FLEX** favors one attached Y and one movable Y/F who can become slot, wing, stack, or detached receiver.

By 2020, both variants must support:

- Ace and Wing runs;
- Trey and Bunch passing;
- Empty conversion;
- Jet/Return motion by a TE or receiver when legal and useful;
- Duo/Power/Outside/Split;
- Boot/Flood/Post-Cross;
- Mesh/Drive/Y-Cross;
- READ Pop/Glance answers;
- six- and seven-man protection.

The defense should not be able to declare base defense and assume the ball will stay between the tackles.

## 3.4 21 Personnel

21 is re-promoted in 2019 because a movable F can create a different kind of multiplicity than a third receiver.

The F may align as:

- offset fullback;
- wing;
- sniffer;
- slot;
- split receiver in limited concepts;
- motion player;
- insert/lead blocker;
- split-flow blocker;
- protection piece;
- flat, wheel, angle, or leak receiver.

The personnel grouping must be able to reach I, Offset I, Strong/Weak, Wing, Twins, Slot, Trey-like, and selected Empty pictures without substituting.

The F is not required to be a traditional fullback. The role may be filled by a fullback, H-back, second tight end, large running back, or hybrid player who can execute the weekly job list.

## 3.5 20 Personnel

20 remains a two-runner package, not a two-runner quota.

The core rule remains: both backs must have distinct reasons to be on the field. The second runner cannot merely stand beside the primary runner and chase the same interior carry.

Normal 20 jobs include:

- one runner in the backfield and one in the slot;
- split backs with opposite release threats;
- orbit/jet decoy from a back with real touch ability;
- one runner as Insert/Arc player;
- Texas/Angle and Wheel combinations;
- screen misdirection;
- motion to Empty;
- dual protection release rules.

If neither runner creates a matchup or assignment conflict, Stone uses another grouping.

## 3.6 13 Personnel

13 becomes slightly more dangerous as a pass shell without becoming a normal-down base grouping.

The 13 PASS body variant remains. Its weekly calls may include:

- Power/Counter/Duo;
- Toss/Crack;
- Boot/Flood;
- Post-Cross;
- TE Delay Screen;
- Leak;
- max-protection Post or Corner;
- Empty-like spread only when the third TE can credibly detach.

13 is especially useful when the defense answers it with large base personnel and Stone has at least one TE who can win in space.

## 3.7 Running Back Room

The rotation principle is unchanged but more clearly separated from touches.

Two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time in the same game. That means real snaps in normal football, not ceremonial packages. It does **not** mean the carries must be divided evenly.

A normal Stone backfield can produce any of the following:

- one back with 18 carries and another with 8 carries plus routes/protection;
- two backs each with 10-14 carries because both are producing;
- one runner functioning mainly as a receiving/space player;
- a third runner with a smaller but real series or situational package;
- a hot runner staying in beyond the planned rotation;
- a back receiving many snaps and few touches because his protection, route, motion, or decoy role matters.

The simulator must never force a distribution merely to satisfy the existence of a rotation plan.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, ROTATION, AND TEMPO

## 4.1 Operating States Remain

The three commands remain:

- **ROTATE:** use normal planned substitutions and fresh bodies;
- **HOLD:** keep the current eleven, change the picture;
- **PRESS:** keep the current eleven and increase operational speed.

Evolution III changes how Stone sequences them.

## 4.2 Rotation Is Now Series-Aware

A player may rotate by series, by role block, or by situation.

Example running-back pattern:

1. RB1 opens the drive in normal-down football.
2. RB2 enters for a planned 20/11 space sequence.
3. If RB2 gains rhythm, Stone may keep him for the next normal-down call.
4. RB1 may return for protection or short-yardage even if RB2 had the previous carries.
5. RB3 enters only if his weekly role creates a real advantage.

This is not a scripted hockey line change. It is a freshness and matchup system.

## 4.3 HOLD Becomes a Counter to Defensive Matching

Stone increasingly uses HOLD after he learns how the defense matched the current personnel.

Examples:

- defense matches 12 with base -> HOLD and detach Y2 into Empty or Bunch;
- defense matches 12 with nickel -> HOLD and condense into Duo/Power surface;
- defense matches 21 with base -> HOLD and move F to slot/wing for Drive or Flood;
- defense matches 20 with dime/nickel -> HOLD and return both backs to the box for Duo/Inside/Counter.

The offense is exploiting the defensive personnel already on the field, not merely playing fast for its own sake.

## 4.4 PRESS Blocks

By 2020 Stone commonly builds two- to four-play PRESS blocks on the call sheet.

A PRESS block must use:

- one personnel group;
- no substitution;
- two or three formations the current eleven already owns;
- calls from the normal concept language;
- simple protection rules.

Example:

12 FLEX: Trey Drive -> Ace Duo -> Empty Stick -> Trey Jet Boot

The block ends if the offense loses communication, the defense creates an unpracticed pressure picture, or game state demands a substitution.

## 4.5 Defensive Substitution Rule

Stone does not try to illegally deny the defense a matching opportunity after the offense substitutes. If Stone substitutes, the defense is permitted its normal response.

The advantage comes from deciding **when not to substitute** once the desired matchup is on the field.

# 5. FORMATION ARCHITECTURE

## 5.1 Formation Width Becomes a Game-Plan Variable

Evolution II promoted compressed and stack releases. Evolution III formalizes formation width as part of the weekly plan.

Stone uses three width families:

**OPEN:** normal or wide receiver splits. Best for isolating leverage, identifying shell structure, and creating clear run-box counts.

**NORMAL:** standard system spacing. Carries the broadest menu.

**NASTY:** reduced splits, bunch, stack, or condensed structures. Best for crossing releases, crack angles, motion access, play-action overs, and creating difficult run-support fits.

NASTY is a modifier, not a new formation family.

Examples:

Doubles Nasty

Trey Nasty

Bunch Nasty

Ace Nasty

## 5.2 Condensed Formation Rules

Condensed structure is used to:

- create two-way release space;
- force corners to play in traffic;
- improve crack and insert angles;
- make motion cross the formation faster;
- make deep over routes originate from less predictable landmarks;
- disguise whether a receiver is blocking, crossing, or releasing vertically;
- create room outside for the perimeter run game.

It is not used merely because condensed offense is fashionable.

If the defense compresses with the formation and creates a crowded box, Stone can widen back out or use Empty instead of endlessly adding more condensed tags.

## 5.3 Surface Language

The offense now identifies the run surface independently from the receiver picture.

Common surfaces:

- **OPEN:** no attached TE to the called edge;
- **Y:** one attached TE;
- **WING:** attached Y plus wing/sniffer;
- **DOUBLE:** two attached/near-attached eligibles creating an extra gap;
- **SIX:** extra offensive lineman creates the surface;
- **OVER:** tackle-over or unbalanced surface when legally declared and set.

The quarterback and line communicate the surface before discussing the decorative receiver picture.

## 5.4 Formation Reset Rule

A shift is useful only if the offense can become legally set and preserve the concept's landmarks.

Evolution III expands:

- TRADE: Y/F changes side or surface;
- SHIFT: multiple eligibles reset together;
- RETURN: a motion player crosses and comes back before the snap or resets into a new alignment;
- EMPTY: back or F leaves the backfield to create five-out spacing;
- BACK: an empty-aligned player returns to the backfield before the snap.

The defense should sometimes have to make two strength calls before the ball is snapped.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION SYSTEM

## 6.1 Motion Jobs

Every motion is classified by job:

| **Job** | **Purpose**                                                       |
|---------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ID      | Identify man/zone, travel, push, or rotation.                     |
| FIT     | Move the force/support player or change the box fit.              |
| SPEED   | Give the motion player running speed at or near the snap.         |
| STACK   | Create a free release or traffic.                                 |
| SURFACE | Change the run strength or eligible surface.                      |
| SPACE   | Widen or compress a coverage player.                              |
| KEY     | Create a false run/pass key for second-level defenders.           |
| SETUP   | Establish an action that a later complementary call will exploit. |

A motion may perform more than one job. It must perform at least one.

## 6.2 JET

JET becomes a true family rather than merely a sweep action.

The same JET presentation may lead to:

- Jet Sweep;
- Power away;
- Counter away;
- Duo toward or away depending on surface;
- Outside Zone with jet action;
- Jet Boot;
- Jet Flood;
- Jet Screen;
- Jet Read Glance;
- play-action Post-Cross;
- throwback screen.

The motion player's touch rate must remain low enough that the defense cannot key the exchange but high enough that defenders must honor the possibility.

## 6.3 ZIP

ZIP remains short, fast motion by a receiver, Y, F, or back to change leverage or surface.

2018 use is primarily alignment and leverage.

By 2020 ZIP can also:

- create a stack release;
- reduce a split immediately before the snap;
- insert a blocker into the core;
- change the identity of the flat player in a pass concept;
- create a screen blocker;
- reset the run strength.

ZIP is not later-era cheat motion. The player must still execute the timing and alignment rules of this era's system.

## 6.4 RETURN Promotion

RETURN moves from secondary tool to normal weekly motion by 2019.

Stone uses RETURN when he wants the defense to communicate twice without necessarily changing the final formation.

Examples:

- Jet across, return to original side, snap Power;
- slot across, defense pushes coverage, return and snap Mesh;
- F across to wing, return to backfield, snap Counter;
- Y trade across, return to original wing, snap Boot.

The defense's first adjustment is part of the information Stone wants.

## 6.5 ORBIT Remains Controlled

Orbit motion receives more weekly usage than in Evolution II but does not become a foundation.

Approved jobs:

- screen misdirection;
- option eye control;
- perimeter touch;
- return-to-backfield presentation;
- split-flow deception.

If Orbit does not affect a defender, it is removed from that week's plan.

## 6.6 Motion Timing Menu

Stone now deliberately varies motion timing:

- **EARLY:** motion begins soon after the huddle break to create a new formation and let the QB diagnose the settled defense;
- **NORMAL:** conventional motion with time for the defense to communicate;
- **LATE:** motion begins late enough that the defense must adjust quickly, but the offense still preserves legal timing and assignment clarity;
- **SNAP:** selected motion continues through the snap when the concept is designed for it.

The same motion family should not use the same timing on every call.

# 7. QUARTERBACK OPERATING SYSTEM

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Six

The Iteration II quarterback process expands from the original Pre-Snap Five into six questions:

1. What is the personnel matchup?
2. What is the front and run surface?
3. Where is the conflict defender?
4. What coverage indicator changes with motion?
5. What is the pressure declaration and protection answer?
6. Is there a free access throw or shot alert that overrides the base progression?

The quarterback does not diagnose every defensive nuance. He identifies the information that changes the call.

## 7.2 ACCESS, READ, KEEP Separation

The quarterback must know which type of decision exists before the snap.

**ACCESS:** pre-snap leverage decision. Throw or run is selected before the ball is snapped.

**READ:** post-snap run/pass decision on one defined conflict defender.

**KEEP:** post-snap run/keep decision in which the quarterback may become the runner. KEEP is a separate module and is never implied by the word READ.

This separation prevents the offense from becoming mentally muddy as option mechanics expand.

## 7.3 Movement Rules

The quarterback's mobility remains functional:

- execute boot/keeper footwork;
- change launch point;
- climb or escape the pocket when structure breaks;
- punish an uncovered edge when KEEP is installed;
- preserve passing posture as long as the concept remains alive.

Scrambling remains an extension of the pass. Designed QB running remains roster dependent.

## 7.4 Alert Hierarchy

The quarterback uses this order:

1. free access;
2. called shot alert;
3. READ/KEEP defender if the call contains one;
4. base concept progression;
5. checkdown;
6. controlled scramble or throwaway.

A quarterback may not invent an alert merely because he prefers a matchup.

## 7.5 Coverage Movement with Motion

The quarterback is taught four simple responses to motion:

- **TRAVEL:** defender runs with the motion player. Man probability rises.
- **PUSH:** coverage bumps responsibilities across the formation. Zone/match probability rises.
- **ROTATE:** safety shell changes. Shot and seam rules update.
- **HOLD:** defense does not materially change. Motion may have changed run leverage or created speed even if coverage stayed static.

The quarterback reports what happened, not why the defense did it.

# 8. PROTECTION SYSTEM

Protection evolves because the offense is using more Empty, more condensed releases, more movement passes, and more defenses are presenting pressure from second-level locations.

## 8.1 Core Protection Families

The existing families remain:

- Half-Slide;
- Full Slide;
- BOB/Man;
- six-man protection;
- seven-man protection;
- Max;
- movement protection;
- screen protection.

Evolution III adds clearer adjustment language rather than a completely new protection system.

## 8.2 SCAN

SCAN tells the back to check the declared interior/second-level threat before releasing.

SCAN is useful when:

- linebackers mug both A-gaps;
- the defense presents one more possible rusher than the line can sort pre-snap;
- the called route can survive a slightly delayed back release.

The back scans inside-out according to the weekly declaration.

Expected result: protection stability at the cost of immediate route availability.

## 8.3 FAN

FAN widens the protection or run-pass edge communication toward an unusually wide edge alignment.

FAN may apply to:

- pass protection;
- outside movement protection;
- selected run schemes whose edge surface changes because of width.

The call prevents the tackle and adjacent blocker from using normal spacing rules against an abnormal edge.

## 8.4 Empty Five-Man Rules

By 2020 Stone formalizes Empty communication.

The line identifies:

1. the five most dangerous immediate rush threats;
2. the point linebacker;
3. which potential sixth rusher becomes the quarterback's hot responsibility;
4. whether the protection is sliding or locked to a side.

The quarterback must know the hot throw before the cadence reaches the final phase.

Empty is not called if the weekly receiver group cannot execute the hot answers.

## 8.5 Protection by Concept

| **Concept Type**   | **Normal Protection** | **Adjustment**                                     |
|--------------------|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Quick game         | Five- or six-man      | Get the ball out; do not overprotect.              |
| Mesh/Drive/Y-Cross | Six-man / Half-Slide  | SCAN vs mug pressure if back route is not primary. |
| Dagger/Post-Cross  | Six- or seven-man     | MAX when shot reason outweighs route count.        |
| Boot/Flood         | Movement protection   | FAN edge if width threatens launch point.          |
| Empty              | Five-man              | Hot rule mandatory.                                |
| Deep PA            | Seven-man / Max       | Route count reduced to preserve the shot.          |

## 8.6 Pressure Control Through Call Type

Stone does not answer every pressure by changing protection.

The four answers are:

- protect it;
- throw behind it;
- move the launch point;
- run at the space the pressure vacated.

The weekly plan must contain examples of all four.

# 9. RUN GAME EVOLUTION

The run game remains Stone's clearest point of continuity. Evolution III does not replace his physical identity with outside-zone imitation. It expands how the same downhill and zone families are dressed and sequenced.

## 9.1 2020 Core Run Hierarchy

**CORE:** Power, Counter/GT, Duo, Outside Zone, Split Zone.

**CORE/WEEKLY:** Inside Zone, Pin-Pull, Jet Sweep.

**WEEKLY:** DART, Toss/Crack, Draw, Insert variations.

**CHANGE-UP:** Trap, Wham, Reverse, End Around, specialty QB KEEP runs, specialty unbalanced runs.

The weekly center of gravity may move. Against a light box, Duo may become the most-called run. Against fast-flow linebackers, Counter and split-flow may lead. Against heavy interior structure, Outside/Pin-Pull may carry the perimeter plan.

## 9.2 POWER

Power remains identity football.

New Evolution III pairings:

- Power + Jet action;
- Power + Return;
- Power + quick ACCESS throw;
- Power + READ Glance against a defined second-level conflict;
- Power + Boot/Flood complement;
- Power from 12 FLEX after the defense matches nickel;
- Power from 6OL spread presentation.

The front-side double and backside pull rules remain familiar. The presentation changes more than the blocking concept.

**Expected Yardage: 3-6.**

## 9.3 COUNTER / GT

Counter becomes the most important pull-action family in the system.

By 2020 the master family includes:

- GT Counter;
- Guard-H Counter where personnel permits;
- Counter from gun and under center;
- Counter with Jet action opposite;
- Counter Read Glance;
- Counter Boot;
- Counter screen complement;
- 6OL Counter with the extra OL changing the surface.

The offense should be able to show the same pull action and produce a handoff, pass, boot, or constraint.

**Expected Yardage: 4-8.**

## 9.4 DUO

Duo is now a permanent core call.

Stone values Duo because it allows a downhill mentality without relying on a puller and gives the back clear leverage decisions against lighter boxes.

Duo is especially useful from:

- 11 Nasty;
- 12 Ace/Trey;
- 21 Offset;
- 20 with the second back displaced;
- 6OL balanced surface.

Primary complements:

- Duo Read Pop;
- Duo Play-Action Dagger;
- Duo Leak;
- Duo Now access;
- Duo from Empty-return shift.

**Expected Yardage: 4-7.**

## 9.5 OUTSIDE ZONE

Outside Zone remains a major family but no longer carries the burden of being the universal answer.

The 2018 phase uses extensive Jet/Zip action.

The 2019 correction adds:

- more formation-width variation;
- more 12/21 surfaces;
- less predictable motion timing;
- direct downhill complements when the defense widens to stop the edge.

The back presses the landmark and makes the same disciplined read. Stone will not turn every outside-zone call into a bounce race.

**Expected Yardage: 4-8.**

## 9.6 SPLIT ZONE

Split Zone becomes a foundational action because its slicer can perform multiple jobs.

New tags:

- SLICE: normal split-flow block;
- BLUFF: slicer shows split flow and releases without blocking the edge;
- LEAK: slicer delays and becomes a receiver;
- ARC: slicer bypasses the edge and works second level/perimeter;
- RETURN: slicer motions before returning to split-flow responsibility.

The line still blocks the core zone concept. The tag changes the movable player's job.

**Expected Yardage: 4-7.**

## 9.7 INSIDE ZONE

Inside Zone remains simple by design.

It is most valuable when:

- the defense empties the box to handle motion;
- the offense wants a stable READ platform;
- the quarterback needs a fast run check;
- Stone wants a low-install complement to a more complex weekly plan.

Inside Zone should not acquire enough tags to become harder to teach than Power or Counter.

**Expected Yardage: 4-6.**

## 9.8 DART

DART remains player dependent. It is promoted only in weeks where the tackle can pull in space and the front gives usable angles.

Evolution III adds:

- DART from 11 Nasty;
- DART from 12 with Y protecting the vacated edge;
- DART Jet action;
- DART Boot change-up.

DART is not a required core call merely because Stone likes tackle movement.

**Expected Yardage: 4-7.**

## 9.9 PIN-PULL

Pin-Pull becomes Stone's primary perimeter answer when the defense creates favorable down blocks and reach angles.

The motion relationship expands:

- Jet can become the runner;
- Jet can hold force while the back receives the ball;
- Return can create a crack angle;
- a tight split receiver can pin support;
- F/Y can lead outside.

**Expected Yardage: 4-8.**

## 9.10 TOSS / CRACK Promotion

Toss/Crack moves from lower weekly usage to a genuine perimeter family in selected game plans.

Reasons:

- condensed receiver splits improve crack angles;
- heavier personnel can create extra edges;
- motion can remove or widen support;
- it gives the defense a different perimeter track than Outside Zone or Jet Sweep.

Stone prefers Toss/Crack when the roster has receivers willing to block and a back who can press the edge without dancing.

**Expected Yardage: 4-9.**

## 9.11 JET SWEEP

Jet Sweep remains a real carry, not fake motion theater.

The runner can be a receiver, back, or selected TE/F athlete. The concept is called enough to force edge respect but not enough that the defense can trigger on the motion alone.

**Expected Yardage: 4-9.**

## 9.12 INSERT Package

INSERT is a role tag, not a brand-new blocking family.

A movable F/Y/R enters the box and inserts on a second-level defender while the line executes Duo, Inside Zone, or selected gap rules.

Uses:

- create an extra gap without changing the core line call;
- punish defenses that empty the box for space personnel;
- let 20 or 21 personnel create downhill runs from spread pictures.

The tag is cut if the insert player cannot consistently identify the correct second-level target.

# 10. PASS GAME EVOLUTION

The pass game gets more connected, not much larger.

## 10.1 2020 Core Pass Hierarchy

**CORE:** Stick, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Post-Cross.

**CORE/WEEKLY:** Four Verticals, Levels, Choice, Sail/Flood, Mills/Post-Dig.

**WEEKLY:** Snag, Smash, Curl-Flat, Texas/Angle, Shallow, Whip/Pivot.

**CHANGE-UP:** Scissors, double moves, specialty isolation routes, opponent-specific shot concepts.

The important Evolution III change is not that Stone discovers new route combinations. It is that the concepts are being released from more condensed, moving, and heavier pictures.

## 10.2 MESH

Mesh becomes even more useful because bunch and Nasty alignments create release problems for man coverage.

New normal variants:

- Mesh Rail;
- Mesh Return;
- Mesh from 12 FLEX;
- Mesh with back Wheel alert;
- Mesh Sit versus zone;
- Mesh from Empty conversion without substitution.

The mesh depth and spacing rules do not change.

**Expected Yardage: 6-11.**

## 10.3 DRIVE

Drive remains one of Stone's cleanest portable concepts.

Evolution III additions:

- Drive from condensed twins;
- Drive with Jet clearing action;
- Drive with F/Y as shallow player in 21/12;
- Drive Choice, allowing the intermediate route to settle or continue according to leverage;
- Drive from PRESS Empty.

**Expected Yardage: 8-15.**

## 10.4 Y-CROSS

Y-Cross is promoted into the center of the intermediate explosive plan.

The crosser may be Y, F, slot, or selected receiver depending on personnel. The role is more important than the position label.

New tags:

- OVER: deeper cross landmark;
- RAIL: back releases vertically outside;
- LEAK: opposite TE/F delays before releasing;
- ALERT POST: outside shot if safety structure permits.

**Expected Yardage: 10-17.**

## 10.5 DAGGER

Dagger remains core and is increasingly used behind run-action presentations.

Normal forms:

- Gun Dagger;
- Play-Action Dagger;
- Duo Dagger;
- 12 FLEX Dagger;
- Empty Dagger;
- Dagger with Jet clear.

The clear route must actually remove the deep defender. Stone does not call Dagger if the weekly receiver cannot threaten the required vertical space.

**Expected Yardage: 10-20.**

## 10.6 POST-CROSS

Post-Cross becomes the primary deep play-action family.

The offense can build it off:

- Outside action;
- Counter action;
- Split action;
- Jet action;
- 12/21 under-center structures;
- selected gun play-action.

The quarterback reads safety structure and crosser leverage. Protection may be six, seven, or max depending on the weekly rush problem.

**Expected Yardage: 15-30+.**

## 10.7 MILLS / POST-DIG

Mills remains the clean vertical answer to a middle-field safety who can be manipulated by the dig.

Evolution III uses it more often as an ALERT or play-action complement rather than a high-volume pure dropback call.

**Expected Yardage: 12-24.**

## 10.8 CHOICE

Choice expands because Stone's roster philosophy values interchangeable backs and tight ends.

Approved Choice players:

- slot receiver;
- receiving TE;
- F/H hybrid;
- runner with demonstrated route skill.

The route is not automatically available to every player who happens to align in the slot.

Normal choice rules remain leverage based: sit, break out, break in, or continue only within the concept's defined menu.

**Expected Yardage: 5-12.**

## 10.9 FOUR VERTICALS

Four Verticals survives but becomes more formation-sensitive.

Stone prefers:

- seams from 12 FLEX;
- switch releases from stack/bunch;
- Bender rules for the inside receiver when coverage structure creates the window;
- outside isolation only when the matchup warrants it.

It is not called simply because the defense has two safeties.

**Expected Yardage: 15-30+.**

## 10.10 Route Tags Added or Promoted

| **Tag** | **Job**                                                                              |
|---------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| OVER    | Deepen a cross into the intermediate/deep over window.                               |
| RAIL    | Release the back or displaced player vertically outside.                             |
| LEAK    | Delay a Y/F before releasing behind flow.                                            |
| BENDER  | Inside vertical bends according to safety leverage.                                  |
| LOCK    | Preserve backside isolation route instead of converting into the front-side concept. |
| SIT     | Crosser or option route settles in zone when the defined window is reached.          |
| SLIDE   | Y/F crosses behind the line into the flat on movement action.                        |

These tags are used only where the concept rules remain clear.

## 11.1 The Run Action Must Be Real

Stone does not call play-action from a run family the offense does not actually execute.

The weekly play-action menu is built only after the run menu is selected.

Each major run family should have at least one high-frequency pass complement and one shot/change-up complement.

## 11.2 Core Action Families

### OUTSIDE ACTION

Primary passes:

- Boot Flood;
- Slide;
- Post-Cross;
- Leak;
- deep comeback/post change-up.

### COUNTER ACTION

Primary passes:

- Counter Boot;
- Counter Post-Cross;
- Counter Leak;
- throwback screen.

### DUO / INSIDE ACTION

Primary passes:

- Duo Dagger;
- Pop;
- Y-Cross;
- Rail/Angle complement.

### SPLIT ACTION

Primary passes:

- Split Leak;
- Split Flood;
- Post-Over;
- Bluff flat.

### JET ACTION

Primary passes:

- Jet Boot;
- Jet Flood;
- Jet Post-Cross;
- Jet Screen/Throwback.

## 11.3 SLIDE Promotion

SLIDE becomes a major movement-pass tag by 2020.

The Y/F initially presents run-action responsibility, crosses behind the line, and appears late in the flat while the quarterback changes the launch point.

The concept is valuable because it:

- creates an easy completion;
- punishes aggressive edge pursuit;
- keeps the quarterback on schedule;
- looks like real run action;
- allows the offense to move the pocket without demanding a deep developing route.

**Expected Yardage: 5-11.**

## 11.4 Play-Action Volume Rule

Stone is willing to use play-action on first down, second-and-medium, after unsuccessful runs, and while trailing.

A previous run does not need to gain five yards for its action to remain useful. The key question is whether the defensive structure still respects the run responsibility.

## 11.5 Designed Rollout Rule

Designed rollout is used to:

- simplify the launch point;
- punish edge crash;
- change protection geometry;
- pair with under-center action;
- give a semi-mobile quarterback controlled movement.

The quarterback remains a passer first. Rollout does not imply sprint-to-run.

# 12. ACCESS, READ, AND KEEP PACKAGES

## 12.1 ACCESS Remains Pre-Snap

ACCESS remains the simplest conflict tool.

Normal ACCESS throws:

- Now;
- Smoke;
- Hitch;
- Bubble;
- quick Slant;
- fast Out when leverage allows.

The quarterback decides before the snap whether the access throw is free enough to replace the run.

## 12.2 READ

READ remains post-snap and identifies one defender who cannot correctly defend both components of the play.

The master menu grows, but the weekly menu stays small.

### 2018 MASTER READ MENU

1. Inside Read Glance.
2. Split Read Slant.
3. Duo Read Pop.
4. Outside Read Bubble.
5. Counter Read Glance.
6. Power Read Flat.

Normally three are active in a game plan.

### 2019-2020 MASTER ADDITIONS

7. Pin-Pull Read Now.
8. Split Read Pop/Leak, only when the route timing and blocker assignment are compatible.

Normally three to five total READ calls are active in a week.

## 12.3 COUNTER READ GLANCE

**Run:** Counter/GT.

**Pass:** backside Glance.

**Conflict:** second-level defender fitting the pull action while occupying the glance window.

**QB rule:** if the defender triggers into the fit and the window remains clean, throw. If he stays in the window, hand the ball.

**Expected Yardage:** 4-8 run / 7-14 pass.

## 12.4 POWER READ FLAT

**Run:** Power.

**Pass:** fast flat/arrow by Y/F/R depending on formation.

**Conflict:** force/overhang player.

**QB rule:** if the overhang inserts hard and the flat is uncovered, throw. Otherwise run the called Power.

**Expected Yardage:** 3-6 run / 4-9 pass.

This is not the college QB Power Read concept. Stone's terminology describes the run/pass conflict, not a quarterback sweep read.

## 12.5 PIN-PULL READ NOW

**Run:** Pin-Pull.

**Pass:** immediate Now/Smoke to isolated receiver.

**Conflict:** perimeter support count.

**QB rule:** if support is overcommitted to the run surface and the receiver has free leverage, throw Now. Otherwise execute Pin-Pull.

**Expected Yardage:** 4-8 run / 4-8 pass.

## 12.6 KEEP Module

KEEP is installed separately from READ.

Baseline semi-mobile quarterbacks may carry zero to two KEEP calls in a week.

Approved master calls by 2020:

- Zone Keep: zone action with edge read and QB keep only if the end crashes beyond the defined threshold;
- Counter Keep: counter action with a controlled quarterback keep complement;
- Boot Keep: movement pass where the quarterback may run after the pass progression is exhausted, not a designed first read.

If the roster contains a true running quarterback, the simulation may expand the module. That expansion is roster-driven and does not rewrite Stone's entire offense.

## 12.7 READ Guardrails

- one defined conflict defender;
- no full-field post-snap RPO progression;
- offensive line must know whether downfield-release restrictions matter;
- ball must be delivered on schedule;
- if the picture is muddy, hand the called run unless the package's rule says otherwise;
- READ does not replace normal dropback passing;
- a successful READ call does not automatically increase next week's READ volume.

# 13. SCREEN AND PERIMETER GAME EVOLUTION

## 13.1 Screen Families

**CORE/WEEKLY:** RB Slow, Now/Smoke, Bubble.

**WEEKLY:** Tunnel/Jail, TE Delay, Slip, Middle, Throwback.

**CHANGE-UP:** Double screen, fake screen shot, reverse screen, specialty OL screen.

## 13.2 Motion-Screen Marriage

Jet, Zip, Return, and Orbit can change who blocks and where the screen appears.

Examples:

- Jet action -> RB Slow opposite;
- Return -> Tunnel to original side;
- Orbit -> Throwback;
- Zip -> Now screen with immediate stack blocker;
- 12 FLEX -> TE Delay after receiver motion clears the flat.

The screen must exploit a defensive reaction created by the presentation. Stone avoids screens that require five unrelated moving parts solely to look clever.

## 13.3 Perimeter Touch Rule

A perimeter touch may go to:

- WR on Jet;
- RB on Toss/Pin-Pull;
- F/Y on flat or leak;
- WR/RB on screen;
- selected athlete on End Around or Orbit.

The offense does not need a designated gadget player. Weekly skill determines who owns the role.

# 14. 6OL AND OFFENSIVE-LINE PACKAGE EVOLUTION

Stone's OL package is one of the clearest ways his offense becomes personally distinctive.

He did not invent the extra offensive lineman. His innovation is treating the extra lineman as a **formation and concept tool**, not merely a declaration of short-yardage run intent.

## 14.1 6OL Structures

By 2020, 6OL can appear as:

- **SIX HEAVY:** extra OL attached to create a large surface;
- **SIX BALANCED:** extra OL helps create symmetric edges;
- **SIX WING:** declared eligible OL aligns in a wing/near-TE location when legal;
- **SIX OVER:** tackle-over or unbalanced surface with eligibility communication handled correctly;
- **SIX SPREAD:** heavy bodies remain on the field while receivers/TEs create a spread picture;
- **SIX SHIFT:** offense begins heavy and legally shifts to another surface before the snap.

## 14.2 Eligibility Rule

Any offensive lineman used as an eligible receiver or aligned in a role that requires reporting must satisfy league reporting, numbering, formation, and set requirements.

The deception is schematic, not procedural trickery.

## 14.3 6OL Run Menu

Primary calls:

- Power;
- Counter/GT;
- Duo;
- Outside Zone;
- Pin-Pull;
- DART when the extra tackle creates the correct pull surface;
- Toss/Crack;
- Sneak/Follow in short yardage.

## 14.4 6OL Pass Menu

Primary calls:

- Boot Flood;
- Post-Cross with seven-man or max protection;
- Leak by a legal Y/F while 6OL sells run surface;
- TE Delay Screen;
- quick Stick/Flat from SIX SPREAD;
- throwback screen;
- max-protection shot.

The rule is explicit: **if SIX only produces a run call in the weekly plan, Stone has made the package a tell and must either add a pass or reduce the package.**

## 14.5 Puller Package

The offensive line maintains a weekly puller map.

Each guard and tackle is graded for:

- short pull;
- long pull;
- kick-out;
- wrap;
- perimeter lead;
- space tracking;
- recovery when the defender wrong-arms the pull.

Stone may build a weekly run menu around the best puller rather than assuming both sides are equally capable.

**Punish:** Outside Post-Cross.

## 16.5 COUNTER SERIES

**Punish:** Counter Post-Cross.

## 16.6 12 FLEX MATCHUP SEQUENCE

1. Trey Nasty, Drive.
2. Ace, Duo.
3. Same personnel, Empty Stick.
4. Return to Ace, Duo Dagger play action.
5. If nickel remains light, Power or Counter.
6. If base remains, detach Y2 and return to space.

The sequence is driven by how the defense matches 12, not by a pre-scripted desire to call every play.

## 16.7 21 MOVE SEQUENCE

1. Pro, Power.
2. F Zip to Wing, Outside Zone.
3. F to slot, Drive.
4. F Return to backfield, Counter.
5. Boot Flood with F in flat/slide role.
6. HOLD and repeat the personnel until the defense changes the answer.

## 16.8 Two-Runner Sequence

20 personnel demonstrates the difference between playing time and shared backfield usage.

1. RB1 backfield, RB2 slot, Inside Zone.
2. same people, RB2 motion, Mesh Rail.
3. split backs, Texas/Angle.
4. RB2 backfield, RB1 slot, Counter.
5. one runner leaves on next ROTATE call if the matchup is no longer useful.

Neither runner is guaranteed a carry. Both receive legitimate playing time and functional assignments.

# 17. WEEKLY GAME-PLANNING AND CALL-SHEET CHANGES

## 17.1 Defensive Map Questions

The staff now answers these questions before selecting the weekly menu:

1. How does the defense match 11, 12, 21, and 20 personnel?
2. Does motion create TRAVEL, PUSH, ROTATE, or HOLD?
3. Who becomes the force player after Jet/Zip?
4. Does the front reset when the surface changes?
5. How does the defense fit condensed formations?
6. What is its answer to split-flow action?
7. What front appears against under-center 12/21?
8. What front appears against condensed 11?
9. Does the defense use mugged linebackers, simulated pressure, or late safety rotation?
10. What does the defense do after the same formation repeats twice?
11. How does it defend 6OL, and does it substitute?
12. Which defender can be placed in a READ conflict without muddying the quarterback's rule?

# 18. INSTALLATION AND PRACTICE CHANGES

Evolution III cannot be installed by adding more meeting-room slides. The practice structure changes so players rehearse defensive reactions, not merely assignments.

## 18.1 Six-Install Evolution Plan

### INSTALL 1: Width and Surface

- OPEN / NORMAL / NASTY;
- surface calls;
- formation reset rules;
- Duo/Power/Outside from multiple widths.

### INSTALL 2: Motion Jobs

- Jet/Zip/Return;
- ID/FIT/SPEED/KEY jobs;
- timing variations;
- motion communication versus man/zone indicators.

### INSTALL 3: Action Families

- Outside + Boot/Slide;
- Counter + Post-Cross/Throwback;
- Duo + Dagger/Pop;
- Split + Leak.

### INSTALL 4: READ and KEEP

- active weekly READ calls;
- one-defender rules;
- optional KEEP module;
- exchange mechanics and ball security.

### INSTALL 5: Pressure and Empty

- SCAN;
- FAN;
- five-man Empty declaration;
- hot answers;
- movement protection.

### INSTALL 6: Situational and Series

- red zone;
- third down;
- four minute;
- two minute;
- GO menu;
- Show -> Stress -> Punish sequences.

## 18.2 Reaction Period

A new practice period is added.

The defense is instructed to vary how it responds to motion and personnel:

- travel;
- push;
- rotate;
- hold;
- reset front;
- refuse to reset front;
- mug pressure;
- drop out.

The offense must execute the same call correctly regardless of which legal defensive response appears.

## 18.3 Same-Action Period

The offense runs three calls from the same action:

1. base run;
2. movement/READ complement;
3. shot or screen punishment.

The goal is identical initial presentation, not identical final result.

## 18.4 Puller Period

Guards and tackles rehearse:

- wrong-arm response;
- spill response;
- kick vs log decision;
- wrap tracking;
- long-pull path;
- perimeter leverage;
- pull-action play protection.

## 18.5 Skill Rotation Period

Backs, TEs, F players, and receivers rotate through role stations rather than touch stations.

Stations include:

- protection;
- motion landmarks;
- insert/arc rules;
- screen timing;
- route conversion;
- ball-carry track;
- condensed release.

A player earns game snaps by owning his jobs, not by being promised a touch count.

## 18.6 Mastery Tests

### Quarterback

- identify ACCESS vs READ vs KEEP before the snap;
- declare pressure and hot answer in Empty;
- describe motion response as TRAVEL/PUSH/ROTATE/HOLD;
- execute play-action footwork from under center and gun;
- protect the ball when the READ picture is muddy.

### Running Backs

- execute Power/Counter/Duo/Zone tracks;
- SCAN protection;
- motion to slot/empty;
- screen timing;
- understand when rotation changes role but not carry priority.

### Tight Ends / F

- attached, wing, sniffer, slot, and backfield alignments;
- SLICE/BLUFF/LEAK/ARC;
- condensed releases;
- protection and insert rules;
- legal motion/set requirements.

### Receivers

- NASTY split landmarks;
- crack responsibilities;
- Jet/Zip timing;
- crossing-route collision avoidance;
- screen blocking;
- route tags without formation-specific memorization.

### Offensive Line

- surface declaration;
- puller map;
- Duo/Power/Counter/Zone technique;
- SCAN/FAN communication;
- Empty five-man identification;
- 6OL eligibility and formation checks.

# 19. SIMULATION DIRECTIVES FOR 2018-2020

## 19.1 Evolution Stage Selection

**2018:** use Evolution III-A. Emphasize condensed formations, Jet/Zip action, expanded READ, and aggressive same-shell sequencing.

**2019:** use Evolution III-B. Preserve 2018 tools but reduce shell predictability. Increase 12/21 answers, downhill counters, Return motion, and protection adjustment. Optional KEEP module becomes available.

**2020:** use Evolution III-C. Integrate the three-year system. 11 remains common but no grouping is mandatory. 12/21 are balanced, movement passes are mature, Empty rules are formalized, and a dedicated GO menu exists.

## 19.2 Call Generation Priority

The simulator should generate calls in this order:

1. situation and game state;
2. weekly concept tier;
3. personnel matchup;
4. formation width/surface;
5. motion or shift only if it serves a job;
6. base concept;
7. tag/READ/ACCESS/protection adjustment if justified;
8. expected-yardage profile.

Do not randomly choose a formation and then search for a concept that fits it.

## 19.3 Personnel Rules

- 11 is normally the single largest grouping, not a required majority.
- 12 or 21 may become the weekly center of gravity if defensive matching creates an advantage.
- 20 requires two legitimate runner roles.
- 13 requires either surface value or a credible pass reason.
- 6OL must carry at least one pass or movement complement when used meaningfully.
- true 00 remains rare.
- player availability overrides theoretical package diversity.

## 19.4 Rotation Rules

- two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time;
- meaningful playing time can include carries, routes, protection, motion, screens, and decoy work;
- touch share is not preset;
- a hot player may remain in the game;
- a tired or compromised player may rotate out even if he is nominally RB1;
- do not substitute solely to satisfy a rotation counter;
- do not use two backs together unless their assignments create distinct stress.

## 19.5 Motion Rules

- motion must have ID, FIT, SPEED, STACK, SURFACE, SPACE, KEY, or SETUP value;
- 2018 may lean heavily on Jet/Zip but should not reach later-era universal motion rates;
- 2019 varies timing and uses Return more often;
- 2020 integrates motion broadly but still allows static snaps;
- Orbit stays secondary;
- no cheat motion or later-era speed-start shortcuts;
- do not make motion mandatory on every play from a motion-friendly formation.

## 19.6 READ Rules

- 2018 master menu: six families, usually three active;
- 2019: six to seven master calls, normally three or four active;
- 2020: up to eight master calls, normally three to five active;
- one conflict defender per call;
- READ is not attached universally;
- if offensive-line legality or timing makes the pass unsafe, the call is removed from that week's menu.

## 19.7 KEEP Rules

- KEEP is optional and quarterback dependent;
- default semi-mobile QB usage is low;
- a true running quarterback may increase usage substantially, but the offense still retains Stone's pass and run concepts;
- do not transform every READ into quarterback run football merely because KEEP exists;
- quarterback health/game state may reduce KEEP immediately.

## 19.8 Run Distribution Rules

Stone does not chase a predetermined carry total for one back or one run concept.

The simulator should favor:

- Power/Counter/Duo when the defense is light, widened, or structurally soft inside;
- Outside/Pin-Pull/Toss when support angles favor the perimeter;
- Split/Inside when simplicity or edge control is valuable;
- Jet when the defense fails to honor motion or the perimeter count is favorable;
- change-up runs only when their specific defensive reason exists.

## 19.9 Play-Action Rules

Play-action is not conditioned on the previous run being successful.

Use is driven by:

- whether the run action is credible in the formation/personnel;
- linebacker/safety fit behavior;
- protection viability;
- game-plan sequencing.

Under-center play-action remains a major Stone tool.

## 19.10 Expected Yardage Rules

Expected Yardage remains a planning band, not an outcome generator by itself.

Actual simulation outcome must also account for:

- player ability;
- blocking matchup;
- defensive call;
- leverage;
- field position;
- down/distance;
- pressure;
- execution error;
- broken tackle or yards-after-catch ability;
- game state.

## 19.11 2020 Boundaries

Do not import the following into Evolution III:

- the later league-wide two-high/light-box response as Stone's fully defined system problem;
- 2023-24 cheat motion;
- universal motion-at-snap;
- motion rates that make static formations exceptional;
- later condensed-formation extremes;
- universal pistol/gun option offense;
- a large designed quarterback-run package unless the simulated roster actually provides that player;
- later coordinator branches that have not occurred;
- 2025-26 mature Stone answers.

# APPENDIX C. HISTORICAL GROUNDING NOTES

These notes define the historical rails used to keep Evolution III plausible. They do not mean Stone copies any one real coach or scheme.

1. **11 personnel remained the league's dominant grouping, but its dominance created its own defensive answers.** PFF reported 11 personnel at 58 percent league-wide in 2017, after 59 percent in 2016. NFL Next Gen Stats later noted that the 2018 Rams used 11 personnel on 91.2 percent of regular-season snaps, an extreme example of one-shell football. The Patriots' Super Bowl LIII plan demonstrated the danger of allowing the shell itself to become predictable. Evolution III therefore lets Stone use repeated pictures aggressively in 2018 but explicitly corrects shell dependency in 2019.
2. **Condensed formations and motion were becoming defining tools.** Later NFL Next Gen Stats review of the Shanahan coaching influence identified the Rams as the league leader in condensed-formation rate in 2017, 2018, 2019, and 2020. NFL analysis of the 2019 49ers reported shift or motion on roughly 78 percent of offensive snaps. Stone's 2018-20 offense therefore raises condensed structure and motion, while preserving a lower-volume boundary so the later motion era still has somewhere to evolve.
3. **Motion was being used to change defensive eye discipline and run fits, not only to identify man coverage.** NFL coverage of the Rams and 49ers described fly-sweep action, shifts, and motion as central to creating lanes, changing strength calls, and making similar plays look alike. Evolution III formalizes motion jobs and timing rather than making every play a motion call.
4. **The 2019 Ravens demonstrated how dramatically quarterback-run math could change the run game.** NFL.com reported Baltimore using option concepts on 45.8 percent of its rushes through Week 12 of 2019. Stone's quarterback baseline remains pocket-oriented, so Evolution III does not copy Baltimore. Instead it creates a separate optional KEEP module that can expand only if the simulated quarterback warrants it.
5. **Heavy and two-TE structures remained useful as balanced offensive groupings.** PFF's study of 2018-20 personnel found that 12 and 21 personnel were close to run/pass balanced and used play action on a large share of dropbacks. Evolution III therefore re-promotes 12 and 21 as complete offensive groupings rather than treating them as short-yardage bodies.
6. **League offense still lived primarily in 11 personnel in 2019.** PFF's 2019 concept review put league-wide 11-personnel usage at 59.8 percent, with 12 personnel next at 20.5 percent. Stone's wider personnel distribution is therefore a character/system trait, not a claim that the league had returned to two-back base offense.
7. **Under-center play action and designed rollouts remained valuable tools.** NFL Next Gen Stats analysis of 2020 later found strong results for designed rollouts and noted that most designed rollouts included play action. NFL analysis also found quarterbacks had produced better first-down/touchdown rates and yards per attempt on under-center play action than shotgun play action since the start of 2018. Evolution III preserves Stone's under-center physicality rather than following a gun-only trend.
8. **Fourth-down decision-making became more aggressive by 2020.** NFL Next Gen Stats later reported that when its model recommended going for fourth down by at least two percentage points, teams followed the recommendation 31 percent of the time in 2017 and 53 percent in 2020. Stone's 2020 evolution therefore adds a dedicated GO menu without asserting that the playbook itself makes the decision.

# APPENDIX D. MATERIAL REDUCED, REJECTED, OR DEFERRED

A mature system should contain ideas that were tried and not promoted.

## D.1 2018 Ideas Corrected in 2019

**One-shell dependency:** Stone can use one personnel/formation shell heavily when the defense has no answer, but he rejects building the entire offense around a single repeated shell after 2018. The system returns to broader personnel stress.

**Automatic Jet timing:** Jet remains important, but the snap point is varied. Defenders cannot be allowed to trigger solely from the motion timing.

**More READ equals more offense:** rejected. The master menu grows, but the weekly menu remains limited.

## D.2 Ideas That Remain Secondary

**Orbit:** useful, but too easy to become decoration. Weekly only when it changes an actual rule.

**DART everywhere:** still dependent on tackle movement skill and defensive angles.

**True 00:** Stone prefers creating Empty from 11/12/20 because those bodies preserve run and return-to-backfield threats.

**Large QB run menu:** roster dependent. The Lamar-era option explosion teaches Stone math, not that every quarterback should be used the same way.

## D.3 Deferred to Evolution IV or Later

- a fully defined answer to league-wide split-safety/light-box defense;
- more explicit attacks on two-high shells through the run game and intermediate middle;
- later motion-at-snap acceleration;
- cheat motion and speed-start concepts;
- 2023-24 condensed-formation extremes;
- later perimeter motion packages designed to manufacture free releases almost every snap;
- mature 2025-26 protection and adjustment structure;
- coordinator-tree branches that have not happened in the simulation.

## D.4 Canonical Rule Going Forward

Evolution IV evolves the 2020 offense, not the 2013 book directly. It may simplify, remove, or reshape any Evolution III tool that no longer solves the defensive problem.

# STONE 2018-2020 OFFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** physical downhill multiple offense + concept passing + personnel portability + purposeful motion + connected same-action series.

**System sentence:** Move the defense before the snap. Attack the rule it uses to move.

**Core runs:** Power / Counter-GT / Duo / Outside / Split. Inside remains stable. Pin-Pull and Jet are near-core weekly tools. Toss-Crack rises when perimeter angles favor it.

**Core passes:** Stick / Mesh / Drive / Y-Cross / Dagger / Post-Cross. Four Verticals, Choice, Levels, Flood, and Mills remain major secondary calls.

**Play-action:** Outside Boot-Slide / Counter Post-Cross / Duo Dagger / Split Leak / Jet Flood.

**Personnel:** 11 is usually largest, but 12 and 21 can become the weekly center when the defense overfits 11. 20 is a true two-runner matchup package. 13 and 6OL must carry credible pass answers.

**Back principle:** two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time. Playing time does not imply equal carries or simultaneous backfield usage.

**Motion principle:** motion must identify, move, widen, compress, key, or set up a defender. If it changes nothing, cut it.

**READ:** up to eight master families by 2020, normally three to five active. One conflict defender per call.

**KEEP:** optional quarterback-dependent module. The baseline offense does not require a running quarterback.

**OL principle:** guards and tackles are movement tools. 6OL is a formation/protection/run-pass package, not a short-yardage tell.

**Tempo:** ROTATE to manage bodies, HOLD to keep a matchup, PRESS to attack it before the defense can change personnel.

**Play-caller:** Show -> Stress -> Punish, while tracking the defense's reaction and preferred next answer.
