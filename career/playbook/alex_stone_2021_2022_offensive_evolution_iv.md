# Alex Stone Offensive Playbook

## 2021-2022 Evolution IV: Box and Shell

> **Team-neutral evolution manual** | Document revision 4.0 | Primary play-caller: Alex Stone
> **System sentence:** Count the box. Stress the fit. Make the rotation wrong.

This document is the fourth canonical evolution of Stone's offense. It is layered onto the 2013 Iteration I playbook, the 2016-2017 Evolution II manual, and the 2018-2020 Evolution III manual. Unless this document explicitly changes a rule, concept, protection, personnel relationship, motion rule, or operating procedure, the latest prior rule remains in force.

Evolution IV begins from the 2020 offense, not from the original 2013 baseline. The offense already owns personnel portability, connected motion, same-action series, mature play action, ACCESS, READ, optional KEEP, flexible 12 and 21 personnel, and a real six-offensive-lineman package. The 2021-2022 problem is different. Defenses increasingly present two-deep safety shells, lighter boxes, quarters and match structures, and late post-snap rotation designed to reduce explosives without surrendering the run. Stone's answer is not one play family. It is a new way to identify where the run fitter comes from and then make that defender wrong.

Stone remains the primary game-day play-caller. No coordinator branch is created in this document. Staff influence belongs in the simulation only when an actual coach enters Stone's tree.

# 1. EVOLUTION IV IDENTITY AND CHANGE RULES

Evolution III taught Stone to move the defense and sequence the reaction. Evolution IV changes the question again. The offense now asks: **where does the defense intend to find the extra run fitter after the snap?**

A two-high shell is not automatically soft. A six-man box is not automatically a free run. Modern split-safety defenses can play with both safeties deep before the snap and still fit the run by triggering a safety, nickel, corner, or linebacker after the ball moves. They can spill pulling schemes toward fast support. They can rotate from a two-high picture into one-high coverage late. They can switch crossing routes rather than chase them. They can keep seven defenders available for the pass and still close the run with speed and leverage.

Stone's response is therefore not "run whenever two safeties are deep." The response is to identify the likely fit source, force that player to declare, and connect the run, play action, intermediate pass, screen, and shot to the same decision.

## 1.1 The Ten Evolution Laws

1. **The shell is information, not the answer.** Two-high before the snap does not guarantee two-high after the snap. One-high does not guarantee man or Cover 3. The quarterback and play-caller must expect rotation.
2. **Count the box, then identify the fitter.** A light box can still be sound if a safety or nickel is assigned to trigger quickly. The offense attacks the actual fit rule, not a raw number alone.
3. **Make the safety participate.** If the defense keeps both safeties deep, run efficiently and force one of them closer. If a safety inserts, attack the space he vacates or the route leverage his movement creates.
4. **Do not turn patience into surrender.** Checkdowns and efficient runs are useful, but the offense still needs explosives. Shots are now created by rotation, fit, leverage, and sequencing rather than by repeatedly throwing into capped coverage.
5. **Intermediate space is premium space.** Digs, crossers, choice routes, benders, sail routes, and play-action windows between linebackers and safeties become more important when defenses protect the deep field.
6. **Heavy personnel must still threaten space.** 12, 21, 13, and 6OL are used to test the defensive shell. If the defense stays light and two-high, run. If it inserts or substitutes heavy, spread, shift, and throw.
7. **READ stays disciplined.** The offense may expand post-snap conflict tools, but the quarterback still reads one defined defender. A safety cap can veto a package before the snap; the quarterback does not improvise a second post-snap read tree.
8. **Pressure disguise is part of the shell problem.** The same defenses that hide safety rotation can present mugged linebackers, simulated pressure, and four-man rushes from unusual locations. Protection rules must be simple enough to survive disguise.
9. **Running back rotation remains a personnel tool, not a statistical target.** Fresh runners, route ability, protection ability, and different run styles matter. Carries are earned by the game, not preassigned to create a committee box score.
10. **The weekly plan gets more connected as the master system grows.** Stone adds answers to defensive trends, but Sunday carries only the answers tied to the opponent's actual shell, fit, pressure, and personnel rules.

## 1.2 What Does Not Change

The following remain canonical:

- concept before formation;
- personnel changes the matchup, formation changes the picture;
- Stone can win under center or in shotgun;
- Power and Counter remain identity runs;
- Duo is now a permanent co-headliner rather than a secondary complement;
- zone concepts remain necessary complements to the gap game;
- the quarterback baseline remains pocket-oriented and semi-mobile;
- two or three backs may play meaningful snaps without an equal-carry rule;
- HOLD and PRESS remain matchup weapons, not default tempo states;
- 6OL is not a short-yardage declaration;
- motion must have a job;
- READ and KEEP remain separate decisions;
- shots need a reason;
- Show -> Stress -> Punish remains Stone's play-calling language.

## 1.4 Human-System Rule

Evolution IV includes a correction inside the two-year window.

In 2021, Stone sees more light boxes and split safeties and initially becomes more aggressive about running directly at them. That is often correct, but not automatically. Good defenses can fit the run from depth, wrong-arm pullers, spill the ball toward fast support, and trigger defensive backs without abandoning the deep shell.

The 2022 offense corrects the simplification. Stone still runs at light boxes, but he stops treating box count as a complete diagnosis. The play-caller now tracks **who is making the tackle**, where that player aligned before the snap, and what space appears when that player triggers. The offense then attacks the fit source with play action, intermediate routes, perimeter runs, or formation changes.

The simulator must preserve this correction. 2021 can be more box-count aggressive. 2022 must be more fit-source precise.

# 2. 2021 INSTALL AND 2022 CORRECTION

## 2.1 2021: Force the Fit

The 2021 offense begins with a simple pressure point: defenses are increasingly willing to keep both safeties deep, live in light boxes, and make offenses drive the field without explosives.

Stone's 2021 priorities are:

1. promote Duo into the first line of the weekly run plan alongside Power and Counter;
2. increase direct runs from 12, 21, 13, and 6OL when the defense refuses to add a box player;
3. formalize OPEN and CLOSED shell identification without treating either as a coverage call;
4. promote Levels and Choice as intermediate answers;
5. add the BEND route tag to selected seam and vertical jobs;
6. carry more play action designed to replace an inserting safety rather than only to hunt a 20-yard shot;
7. increase practice against quarters, match quarters, Cover 6, and late safety rotation;
8. make SCAN a normal weekly protection adjustment rather than a special pressure answer;
9. continue using motion to change the force and fit picture, but do not add motion for volume alone.

The 2021 call sheet is more willing to take repeated 4- to 8-yard gains if the defense insists on protecting explosives. Stone does not enjoy passive football. He accepts it when it forces the defense to change.

## 2.2 The 2021 Overcorrection

The offense can become too literal if it treats every two-high shell as a green light for Duo or Power.

The defensive response appears in several forms:

- a safety inserts late and closes the extra gap;
- the nickel becomes the force player while the safety replaces him;
- an edge wrong-arms the pull and spills the ball to pursuit;
- a linebacker folds behind the front while both safeties initially remain deep;
- a corner cracks the run fit when the receiver blocks inside;
- a light box slants or moves the front to destroy the expected double team.

Stone's lesson is that **six in the box can still be seven in the fit**.

That lesson creates the 2022 phase.

## 2.3 2022: Punish the Trigger

The 2022 offense does not retreat from the run. It becomes more exact about why the run is called and what comes next.

The 2022 priorities are:

- identify the primary secondary fitter in each formation family;
- pair every major downhill run with at least one pass or perimeter answer that attacks the fitter's trigger;
- increase BEND, Choice, Levels, Drive, Sail, and Dagger against defenses protecting the deep field;
- use play action to create intermediate gains even when the defense never fully abandons two-high structure;
- increase formation shifts from heavy bodies into spread pictures after the defense declares its matching plan;
- use 12 FLEX and 21 MOVE to force linebackers and safeties into coverage jobs without surrendering the run threat;
- formalize selected LOCK protection rules against simulated pressure and elite edge matchups;
- reduce pure Four Verticals calls that have no shell or leverage reason;
- keep READ active but prevent glance/pop packages from throwing blindly into capped safeties;
- continue rotating multiple runners while allowing the best current matchup to dominate touches.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

Personnel remains Stone's first method of asking the defense a question. Evolution IV uses that question more deliberately: **Will the defense keep its split-safety structure when the offense adds gaps and blockers?**

## 3.1 Planning Centers, Not Quotas

| **Group** | **2021 Planning Center** | **2022 Planning Center** | **Primary Reason**                                                                                     |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 11        | 40-54%                   | 38-52%                   | Broadest dropback and tempo shell; easiest route to Empty and wide spacing.                            |
| 12        | 22-31%                   | 23-33%                   | Force a box decision while preserving full pass-game and Empty conversion.                             |
| 21        | 10-18%                   | 10-19%                   | Add a movable fitter problem through F, lead/insert action, split flow, and spread conversion.         |
| 20        | 4-9%                     | 4-10%                    | Two-runner matchup package, Choice/Angle/Wheel space, and Empty conversion.                            |
| 13        | 3-7%                     | 3-8%                     | Add gaps without surrendering pass threat; useful against defenses determined to stay nickel/two-high. |
| 10        | 2-7%                     | 2-8%                     | Two-minute, pressure spacing, selected Empty and matchup plans.                                        |
| True 00   | Rare                     | Rare                     | Specialist Empty only. Stone prefers reaching Empty without declaring it by personnel.                 |
| 6OL       | Weekly                   | Weekly                   | Direct shell test, surface manipulation, tackle-pull, protection, and heavy-to-spread presentation.    |

These are planning centers. A roster with poor tight-end depth may live more heavily in 11. A roster with two complete tight ends may use 12 as the weekly center. The system does not force a historical percentage onto players who cannot execute it.

## 3.2 11 Personnel

11 remains the easiest package for PRESS and the widest dropback menu, but it loses any residual privilege as the automatic starting point.

11 must carry:

- Duo, Power, Counter, Inside, Outside, Split, Pin-Pull, and selected Toss/Jet;
- OPEN and CLOSED shell answers without changing personnel;
- condensed, normal, wide, stack, bunch, and Empty structures;
- BEND-tagged seam concepts;
- Levels, Choice, Drive, Dagger, Y-Cross, Mesh, Sail, and Vert;
- READ and ACCESS;
- full screen menu;
- protection answers against mug and simulated pressure;
- PRESS tempo.

If the defense can remain in nickel, two-high, and light box while still controlling the run, Stone may change personnel instead of endlessly tagging 11.

## 3.3 12 Personnel

12 becomes one of Stone's strongest shell-testing groups.

The existing HEAVY/FLEX distinction remains.

**12 HEAVY** means both tight ends can function on the line or edge and the offense can create a legitimate seven- or eight-man surface.

**12 FLEX** means at least one tight end can detach, motion, or own a receiver-level route job.

Both must carry:

- Duo, Power, Counter, Outside, Split, Inside, and selected Toss/Pin-Pull;
- under-center and gun structures;
- Trey, Ace, Wing, Bunch, reduced splits, and Empty conversion;
- Y-Cross, Drive, Dagger, Levels, Choice, Sail, Post-Cross, and selected Four Verticals;
- BEND on qualified Y/H players;
- six- and seven-man protection;
- READ Pop/Glance/Stick families;
- Boot/Slide/Leak action.

The 2021 shell test is direct: if the defense leaves six in the box and both safeties deep versus a real 12 HEAVY surface, Stone is willing to run repeatedly.

The 2022 correction is equally direct: if the defense fits those runs efficiently from depth, Stone tracks the inserting player and builds the next call around him.

## 3.4 21 Personnel

21 MOVE becomes more valuable against split-safety defenses because F can create a gap without declaring where the gap will exist at the snap.

F can:

- align offset and lead;
- insert through either interior gap;
- become wing or sniffer;
- arc to an overhang;
- motion to the flat;
- flex into the slot;
- become the second back in split flow;
- scan in protection;
- run Choice, Angle, Wheel, Flat, or Leak;
- trade the surface before the snap.

The grouping must be able to shift from I or Offset I into Twins, Trey-like, Wing, Slot, or Empty structures without substitution.

21 is not used to recreate old I-formation football for nostalgia. It is used because the F body can make a light-box defense declare how it intends to fit an additional gap.

## 3.5 20 Personnel

20 TWIN remains a two-runner package, not a carry-sharing command.

The package earns weekly space when the second runner can perform at least two of the following:

- legitimate carry threat;
- Choice/Angle route;
- slot alignment;
- motion job;
- protection scan;
- screen threat;
- Insert/Arc assignment;
- Wheel or rail route.

A common Evolution IV use is to align both runners, force the defense to declare whether a linebacker or safety owns the detached back, then shift one runner into the backfield and run Duo, Inside, or Counter against the resulting box.

## 3.6 13 Personnel

13 is promoted slightly because it is a clean test of a defense committed to light boxes and split safeties.

The package must still carry pass answers. Core 13 answers include:

- Power;
- Counter;
- Duo;
- Split Zone;
- Toss/Crack when surface allows;
- Boot Flood;
- Y-Cross;
- TE Choice;
- Leak;
- Post-Cross;
- max-protect shot;
- shift to a spread 3x1 or 2x2 presentation when the bodies allow it.

If the defense substitutes goal-line personnel, Stone can spread it. If the defense stays nickel with both safeties deep, Stone can run at the reduced mass. The package fails if the pass threat is fake.

## 3.7 Running Back Room

The running back rule does not change: two or three runners may receive legitimate playing time without predetermined carry shares.

Evolution IV adds a **drive-function chart** rather than a touch quota.

Each active runner is graded weekly in:

- downhill core runs;
- perimeter runs;
- protection and SCAN;
- Choice/Angle routes;
- screens;
- motion jobs;
- ball security;
- four-minute reliability;
- two-minute route/protection value.

A back can play 20 meaningful snaps and receive five carries if his other jobs matter. Another back can receive 18 carries because the front and game state favor his style. The simulator must not force statistical balance merely because multiple backs were activated.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, ROTATION, AND TEMPO

ROTATE, HOLD, and PRESS remain the operating states.

## 4.1 ROTATE: Test the Match

Evolution IV uses ROTATE early in games to answer two questions:

1. How does the defense match 11, 12, 21, 13, and 6OL?
2. Does the safety shell change with the offensive body group, or does the defense try to hold the same split-safety structure?

Stone may deliberately use three different personnel groups in the opening 10 to 15 plays to learn the matching rule. This is not random substitution. It is reconnaissance.

## 4.2 HOLD: Keep the Shell You Want

If a defense remains in a favorable light package against 12, 21, 13, or 6OL, HOLD becomes more valuable.

Stone can:

- stay in the same personnel;
- move from under center to gun;
- widen or compress the formation;
- shift a Y or F;
- create Empty;
- run the ball;
- throw quick game;
- call play action;
- PRESS if the communication advantage is clear.

The objective is to make the defense solve multiple structures with the same bodies it chose for the first call.

## 4.3 PRESS

PRESS is not used merely because the defense is two-high. It is used when the current eleven can attack the shell without substituting.

The 2021-22 PRESS menu commonly includes:

- Inside;
- Duo;
- Split;
- Draw;
- Stick;
- Spacing;
- Slant-Flat;
- Levels;
- Drive;
- Mesh;
- Choice;
- Dagger;
- Smoke/Bubble/Now;
- one or two active READ calls;
- one shell-triggered shot.

Power and Counter remain available if the line can sort the front without sacrificing tempo discipline.

## 4.4 Rotation and Long Drives

Split-safety defenses often prefer to force the offense through long drives. Stone's substitution philosophy becomes useful here.

A 12-play drive does not require the same tailback, receiver, or tight end to play every snap. Stone can rotate fresh skill players while keeping the concept menu stable. The defense cannot assume that a second back entering means Draw or that a second tight end means Power.

The offense may temporarily abandon normal rotation if one runner, receiver, or tight end owns a matchup. Rotation serves winning, not the other way around.

# 5. FORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Evolution IV does not add a large new formation dictionary. It changes which existing pictures are emphasized against split-safety and match structures.

## 5.1 Formation Objectives

Every weekly formation must answer at least one of these questions:

- Can it change the box count?
- Can it change the identity of the secondary fitter?
- Can it isolate a safety or overhang in run/pass conflict?
- Can it force the defense to declare match rules?
- Can it create a pass matchup without surrendering the run threat?
- Can it reach another picture without substitution?

If a formation does none of those things, it is decoration.

## 5.2 NUB and Closed-Surface Promotion

NUB structures receive more weekly use. A closed or reduced side with a tight end surface and one isolated receiver can stress split-safety rules because the defense must decide whether the safety fits the extra gap, helps on the isolated receiver, or stays deep for play action.

Normal NUB calls include:

- Duo to the surface;
- Power away or toward the surface;
- Counter back to the NUB;
- Split Zone away;
- Boot away;
- Y-Cross;
- Dagger;
- isolated X Choice/Go/Glance alert;
- BEND by the Y or H when the middle remains open.

## 5.3 Trey and Wing Remain Central

Trey and Wing remain among Stone's best structures because they can create a true run surface while keeping three receiving threats to one side after a shift or release.

Evolution IV emphasizes:

- Duo from Trey;
- Counter away from Trey;
- Split Zone to/from Wing;
- Jet/Return motion through Trey;
- Dagger and Levels from the same picture;
- Y-Cross with BEND or RAIL tags;
- Empty conversion from 12 FLEX and 21 MOVE.

## 5.4 Width as a Fit Tool

The formation-width rules from Evolution III remain. Evolution IV changes why the staff uses them.

**Wide splits** can pull corners and overhangs away from the core and make the defense reveal whether a safety must become the fitter.

**Reduced splits** can crack or stalk support players, create space for crossers, and force match defenders to communicate through traffic.

**Tight/bunch/stack** can reduce clean press access and create uncertain run support angles.

The offense does not automatically condense against two-high. Width is selected according to the fitter and match rules.

## 5.5 Empty as a Shell Declaration Tool

Stone still prefers reaching Empty through 11, 12 FLEX, 21 MOVE, or 20 TWIN rather than declaring true 00.

Empty serves three Evolution IV jobs:

1. force the defense to reveal how linebackers and safeties distribute eligible receivers;
2. identify whether a safety will spin down once the box is empty;
3. create a clear protection and hot picture for the quarterback.

The offense may shift back into a run-capable backfield after the defense declares its Empty rules.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION SYSTEM

Motion remains purposeful. Evolution IV does not yet enter the later high-volume fast-motion era.

## 6.1 Motion Jobs

A motion or shift must do at least one job:

- identify TRAVEL/PUSH/ROTATE/HOLD;
- change the box count;
- change the likely secondary fitter;
- move an overhang away from the run;
- create a crack or insert angle;
- create speed to the perimeter;
- alter the passing strength;
- create a later constraint from the same action.

## 6.2 RETURN as a Shell Test

RETURN becomes especially useful because it can force the defense to communicate twice without necessarily changing the final formation.

Stone uses RETURN to see:

- whether the nickel travels or pushes;
- whether the safety rotates with the first motion and resets with the return;
- whether the box count changes;
- whether the front adjusts strength;
- whether the secondary fitter becomes obvious.

If the defense communicates cleanly and nothing changes, RETURN loses value for that game.

## 6.3 JET and ZIP

JET and ZIP remain active, but Stone is less interested in motion for its own sake than in what the defense does with it.

JET series answers can include:

- Jet Sweep;
- Duo away;
- Counter back;
- Split Zone away;
- Jet Flood;
- Jet Screen;
- Jet Play-Action Cross;
- READ if the active package is built for it.

ZIP can be used to change leverage and produce a late stack/bunch release without requiring a sweep threat.

## 6.4 ORBIT Remains a Change-Up

ORBIT remains lower volume. It is useful when it creates a real wheel, pitch, screen, or false-flow answer. It is not called merely because movement looks modern.

## 6.5 Historical Motion Boundary

Evolution IV may use motion at the snap and may time selected JET/ZIP calls tightly. It does not yet install a broad high-speed short-motion package designed primarily to manufacture free releases and horizontal acceleration. That later development belongs to Evolution V.

# 7. QUARTERBACK OPERATING SYSTEM

The quarterback's job becomes harder because the defense is trying to make the pre-snap picture unreliable. Stone responds by simplifying the information that actually changes the call.

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Seven

The quarterback now answers seven questions:

1. What offensive personnel matchup do we have?
2. What is the front and immediate box count?
3. Is the pre-snap shell OPEN or CLOSED?
4. Who is the likely conflict or secondary fit player?
5. What changed with motion or shift?
6. What is the pressure declaration and protection answer?
7. Is there free ACCESS or a called shot alert that overrides the base concept?

The quarterback is not asked to name every coverage. He is asked to identify the information that changes his decision.

## 7.2 OPEN and CLOSED

**OPEN** means no single deep-middle safety is occupying the middle of the field before the snap. The defense may be in Cover 2, quarters, Cover 6, disguise, or something else.

**CLOSED** means a single deep-middle player is present before the snap. The defense may be in Cover 1, Cover 3, robber, disguise, or something else.

These are diagnostic words, not huddle calls. They do not guarantee the post-snap coverage.

## 7.3 Post-Snap Rotation Vocabulary

Stone keeps post-snap communication compact.

- **STAY:** shell retains its basic depth and structure.
- **SPIN:** a safety rotates toward the box or middle and the shell closes.
- **POACH:** backside safety crosses into a route window or robs an inside route.
- **DRIVE:** a safety/DB triggers downhill aggressively on run or underneath route action.

The quarterback does not shout these terms during the play. They are film-room language used to connect defensive reactions to later calls.

## 7.4 Shell Does Not Change the Progression by Itself

The quarterback still runs the called concept progression. OPEN/CLOSED information changes:

- route conversions that are already built into the concept;
- BEND rules;
- shot alerts;
- READ safety-cap checks;
- the play-caller's next call.

The offense avoids a system in which every route becomes an option route based on safety movement.

## 7.5 BEND Rule

**BEND** is a route tag, not a new concept family.

A qualified seam/vertical receiver may bend into the open middle between safeties if the post-snap shell stays open and the leverage allows it. If the shell closes or a poach player occupies the bend window, the route stays vertical or follows the base concept rule.

BEND is installed only for players who can read the shell consistently. It may belong to Y, H, or a slot receiver depending on personnel.

Normal BEND homes:

- Four Verticals;
- Y-Cross backside seam;
- Dagger clear route;
- 12 FLEX seam concepts;
- selected play-action seams.

## 7.6 Alert Hierarchy

1. free ACCESS;
2. called shot alert with a valid leverage trigger;
3. safety-cap check if READ contains a glance/pop/seam answer;
4. READ or KEEP defender if the call contains one;
5. base concept progression;
6. checkdown;
7. controlled scramble or throwaway.

The safety-cap check is a veto, not a second post-snap read. It tells the quarterback when a packaged throw is structurally capped before he begins the actual READ.

# 8. PROTECTION SYSTEM

Evolution IV protection is built for defenses that can show pressure without sending all of the displayed rushers.

## 8.1 Core Families Remain

- Half-Slide;
- Full Slide;
- BOB/Man;
- six-man;
- seven-man;
- Max;
- movement protection;
- screen protection;
- Empty five-man rules.

SCAN and FAN remain active adjustments.

## 8.2 SCAN Promotion

SCAN moves from a frequent adjustment to a normal weekly tool.

The back checks the declared interior/second-level threat inside-out before releasing. The weekly protection meeting defines the most dangerous add-on players.

SCAN is particularly useful against:

- mugged linebackers;
- double-A-gap presentation;
- creeper/simulated pressure looks;
- safeties threatening late insertion;
- fronts that create uncertainty about the fourth rusher.

The offense accepts a slower checkdown release when protection clarity matters more.

## 8.3 LOCK Tag

**LOCK** is added as a short protection tag in 2022.

LOCK tells the designated tackle to stay man-to-man on the declared edge threat rather than joining the normal slide relationship. The remaining blockers can slide or sort inside according to the base protection.

Use LOCK when:

- an elite edge rusher should not be passed through traffic;
- simulated pressure repeatedly changes the identity of the inside rushers;
- the backside edge is the one defender Stone does not want involved in the protection exchange;
- the quarterback's launch point benefits from a firmer edge.

LOCK is not a universal answer. It can create a difficult interior count if the offense uses it against the wrong pressure structure.

## 8.4 Simulated-Pressure Rule

The line protects threats, not bluffing bodies.

The weekly plan identifies:

- likely four-man simulated pressures;
- common drop-out defenders;
- most dangerous replacement rushers;
- hot responsibility if the defense creates a true sixth threat;
- which concepts can throw into the vacated space without changing protection.

Stone's four pressure answers remain:

1. protect it;
2. throw behind it;
3. move the launch point;
4. run at the space it vacates.

## 8.5 Protection by Concept

| **Concept Type**        | **Normal Protection**           | **Evolution IV Adjustment**                                                                 |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Quick / ACCESS          | Five- or six-man                | Ball out. Do not waste routes overprotecting a short throw.                                 |
| Levels / Drive / Choice | Six-man / Half-Slide            | SCAN if the back is not the primary matchup.                                                |
| Mesh / Y-Cross          | Six-man / Half-Slide            | Use bunch/stack spacing versus match; SCAN vs interior disguise.                            |
| Dagger / Mills          | Six- or seven-man               | LOCK or Max when the shot reason and edge matchup justify it.                               |
| Under-center PA         | Six/seven-man action protection | Protect the run picture first; do not ruin the fake with exotic pass rules.                 |
| Boot / Slide            | Movement protection             | FAN edge when width threatens launch point.                                                 |
| Empty                   | Five-man                        | Point and hot rule mandatory.                                                               |
| 6OL shot                | Seven-man / Max                 | Reduce route count only when the defensive shell has been manipulated enough to justify it. |

# 9. RUN GAME EVOLUTION

Evolution IV is not a philosophical return to old-school football. The run game becomes more important because defenses are choosing to protect the deep field with fewer immediate bodies in the box. Stone's answer is still modern: run with physicality, then punish the fit rule the run creates.

## 9.1 2022 Core Run Hierarchy

**Identity core:** Duo, Power, Counter/GT. **Stable core:** Inside Zone, Outside Zone, Split Zone. **Near-core weekly tools:** Pin-Pull, Toss/Crack, Jet Sweep. **Opponent tools:** Trap, Wham, DART, Insert, Lead, Draw. **QB-dependent:** KEEP and QB-involved option variants.

## 9.2 DUO

Duo becomes the clearest Evolution IV promotion.

Why it fits Stone:

- it is downhill;
- it creates double teams;
- it can be run from 11, 12, 21, 13, and 6OL;
- it does not require the offense to declare a puller;
- the back can read linebacker fit and safety trigger;
- it pairs naturally with play action and BEND routes.

Normal structures:

- Ace;
- Trey;
- Wing;
- NUB;
- Gun Doubles;
- Gun Trey;
- 21 Offset;
- 6OL Heavy.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards**.

2022 coaching point: the back is not told "two-high means downhill no matter what." The back presses the double team, reads the linebacker, and anticipates the late fitter. The play-caller tracks which defender is consistently finishing the play.

## 9.3 POWER

Power remains an identity call.

Evolution IV emphasis:

- run Power at light boxes when the pull path is clean;
- use Power from heavier personnel to force the safety fit;
- run Power away from declared formation strength often enough to preserve the formation rule;
- pair Power with flat, leak, cross, and post answers when the force/safety overcommits;
- use 6OL and tackle-over surfaces to change the kick-out picture.

Expected Yardage: **3-6 yards**.

## 9.4 COUNTER / GT

Counter remains one of Stone's most valuable answers because split-safety defenses often rely on fast flow and secondary trigger rules.

Evolution IV Counter families:

- Guard-H Counter;
- GT Counter;
- Counter away from Jet;
- Counter from Trey/NUB;
- Counter from 12 and 21;
- Counter with READ only when the conflict structure is clean;
- 6OL Counter with tackle or sixth lineman as movement piece.

Expected Yardage: **4-8 yards**.

2022 correction: if the defense consistently wrong-arms the pull and spills the ball, Stone does not simply call more Counter. He can answer with Pin-Pull, Toss/Crack, Boot/Slide, or play action attacking the replacing support player.

## 9.5 INSIDE ZONE

Inside Zone becomes a cleaner immediate answer to light boxes because it requires less backfield decoration and can be called quickly from PRESS.

Normal uses:

- 11 Doubles/Trips;
- 12 Flex;
- Empty return-to-backfield shift;
- 20 TWIN;
- Draw-like tempo variation.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards** when the weekly box picture favors it, **4-6** as the baseline planning band.

## 9.6 OUTSIDE ZONE

Outside Zone remains core but is not treated as the universal answer to split-safety defense.

It is strongest when:

- the front is reduced enough to create a real stretch angle;
- the edge cannot easily spill the ball into free support;
- Jet/Return motion changes force responsibility;
- Boot/Slide action has already stressed the backside.

Expected Yardage: **4-8 yards**.

## 9.7 SPLIT ZONE

SLICE, BLUFF, ARC, and LEAK remain the main split-action family.

Evolution IV uses Split Zone to manipulate the exact defender the defense expects to become the extra fitter.

- SLICE blocks him.
- BLUFF bypasses him and changes his eyes.
- ARC releases to the next-level support player.
- LEAK turns the split-flow player into a pass threat.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards**.

## 9.8 PIN-PULL AND TOSS/CRACK

These perimeter families rise when the defense is fitting downhill runs from depth.

The objective is not to avoid physical football. It is to make the secondary fit in space and to change the angle of the support player.

Stone can pair:

- Pin-Pull with Return;
- Toss with Crack;
- Toss away from heavy surface;
- Pin-Pull from 12 FLEX;
- perimeter action with Boot/Leak back inside.

Expected Yardage: **4-9 yards**.

## 9.9 TRAP AND WHAM

Trap and Wham become more useful weekly change-ups against light fronts that are penetrating, slanting, or squeezing aggressively to compensate for fewer box bodies.

They remain opponent tools, not core calls.

Expected Yardage: **3-6 yards**.

# 10. PASS GAME EVOLUTION

Evolution IV passing is built around one reality: if the defense is determined to keep the ball in front of it, the offense must be efficient underneath and intermediate without losing the ability to create explosives when the shell finally changes.

## 10.1 2022 Core Pass Hierarchy

**Core:** Stick, Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Mesh. **Promoted to near-core:** Levels, Choice, Sail/Flood. **Shot core:** Post-Cross, Mills/Post-Dig, tagged Vert/BEND. **Weekly:** Snag, Spacing, Slant-Flat, Texas/Angle, Scissors, Pivot/Whip, selected double moves.

## 10.2 LEVELS Promotion

Levels becomes one of the most useful split-safety answers because it attacks the horizontal space between underneath defenders and deep safeties without requiring a deep throw.

Normal jobs:

- shallow control;
- intermediate in-breaker;
- deeper dig/in route;
- backside access or isolation;
- back outlet.

Expected Yardage: **7-13 yards**.

Levels is especially useful when linebackers are expanding under Dagger or Y-Cross and safeties refuse to drive downhill.

## 10.3 CHOICE

Choice is promoted when the roster has a qualified route runner at H, Y, F, or R.

The qualified receiver can attack leverage with a limited option menu. Stone does not allow every player to freelance.

Normal Choice homes:

- 11 slot;
- 12 FLEX Y/H;
- 21 MOVE F;
- 20 TWIN detached back;
- Empty conversion.

Expected Yardage: **5-12 yards**.

Choice is valuable against match structures because it makes one defender play leverage without allowing the entire defense to pass routes off cleanly.

## 10.4 DRIVE

Drive remains a core portable call.

Evolution IV adds more use from heavy personnel and play-action-like presentation. The dig and shallow can be owned by different bodies without changing the quarterback's picture.

Expected Yardage: **8-15 yards**.

## 10.5 Y-CROSS

Y-Cross remains a central Stone concept because it attacks multiple levels and travels well across personnel.

Evolution IV emphasis:

- RAIL to punish linebackers who carry the crosser;
- BEND on backside seam when the middle stays open;
- LEAK from 12/21 action;
- reduced split to create crosser runway;
- play-action versions when the safety fitter is aggressive.

Expected Yardage: **10-17 yards**.

## 10.6 DAGGER

Dagger remains one of Stone's best intermediate/deep split-safety calls.

The clear route stresses the deep shell. The dig attacks behind linebackers. BEND may replace or modify the clear job when the weekly coverage structure warrants it.

Expected Yardage: **10-20 yards**.

Stone does not call Dagger merely because he sees two safeties. He calls it when the hook players, match rules, or safety spacing create the dig window.

## 10.7 MESH

Mesh remains core, but match coverage changes how it is presented.

Evolution IV uses:

- Bunch Mesh;
- Stack Mesh;
- Mesh Rail;
- Mesh with a sit/spot answer;
- Mesh from 12 FLEX or 21 MOVE when the matchup favors it.

The offense does not assume crossers automatically beat modern match coverage. Release structure and the complementary route matter.

Expected Yardage: **6-11 yards**.

## 10.8 SAIL / FLOOD Promotion

Sail and Flood rise against quarters and split-safety structures because they can high-low the outside coverage player without requiring the quarterback to attack the middle of the shell.

Expected Yardage: **9-16 yards**.

The outside vertical or clear must actually threaten the deep defender. A lazy clear converts the concept into three receivers occupying the same space.

## 10.9 FOUR VERTICALS

Four Verticals is not removed. Its usage changes.

Pure all-go versions decline. More calls contain:

- BEND;
- seam read by rule;
- isolated outside alert;
- back control route;
- 12 personnel seam matchup.

Expected Yardage: **15-30+ yards**.

Stone's rule: if both safeties are comfortably capping the vertical routes and no matchup is won, do not force the ball deep just because the play name says Vert.

## 10.10 MILLS / POST-DIG

Mills remains a valuable rotation answer. If the safety spins down or jumps the dig, the post can replace him. If the safety stays high, the dig can become the efficient completion.

Expected Yardage: **12-24 yards**.

## 10.11 New/Promoted Route Tags

| **Tag** | **Job**                                                                                  |
|---------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| BEND    | Seam/vertical bends into open middle by rule when qualified and uncapped.                |
| RAIL    | Back/F releases vertically up the sideline after initial flat or backfield presentation. |
| SIT     | Crosser/inside receiver settles in the open underneath zone by concept rule.             |
| RETURN  | Existing motion tag increasingly paired with Choice, Levels, and run-fit tests.          |
| LOCK    | Protection tag, not route tag. Listed on call sheet when edge ownership matters.         |

# 11. PLAY-ACTION AND MOVEMENT PASS EVOLUTION

The biggest Evolution IV play-action change is philosophical: **play action no longer needs to produce a deep shot to be successful.**

If the run action makes the safety fitter step downhill and creates a 12-yard dig, over, bend, or choice window, the concept has done its job.

## 11.1 Core Action Families

### DUO ACTION

Primary answers:

- Duo Dagger;
- Duo Levels;
- Duo Bend;
- Duo Choice;
- Duo Post-Cross;
- Duo Leak.

Duo action is especially useful because the blocking surface can look physically identical to the run without requiring a puller to leave the picture.

### POWER ACTION

Primary answers:

- Power Flood;
- Power Cross;
- Power Flat/Leak;
- Power Post;
- Power Choice from F/Y.

The puller or downhill backfield action is used to trigger force and fit defenders.

### COUNTER ACTION

Primary answers:

- Counter Post-Cross;
- Counter Y-Cross;
- Counter Leak;
- Counter Sail;
- Counter Screen change-up.

### OUTSIDE ACTION

Primary answers:

- Boot Flood;
- Slide Cross;
- Sail;
- Leak;
- comeback/over combinations according to weekly coverage.

### SPLIT ACTION

Primary answers:

- Split Leak;
- Split Cross;
- Split Flat-Wheel;
- Split Dagger change-up.

## 11.2 Safety-Fit Pairing Rule

For every top-three weekly run, the call sheet identifies:

1. the likely secondary fitter;
2. the normal run that forces him to trigger;
3. the pass concept that replaces his movement;
4. the perimeter answer if he overplays inside;
5. the shot if the shell finally rotates or compresses.

Example:

**Duo Series**

- Run: Trey Right, Duo.
- Efficient pass: Trey Right, Duo Levels.
- Fitter replacement: Trey Right, Duo Bend.
- Perimeter answer: Trey Right, Return Toss.
- Shot: Trey Right, Duo Post-Cross, Alert X.

The exact calls can change weekly. The relationship is the system.

## 11.3 Boot and Slide

Boot/Slide remain useful because they:

- move the launch point away from simulated pressure;
- attack the flat and over behind a triggered support player;
- reduce the quarterback's need to hold the ball against disguised coverage;
- preserve under-center credibility.

Stone reduces naked movement calls when the opponent's edge players ignore run action and hunt the quarterback.

## 11.4 Shot Rule

A deep shot needs one of these reasons:

- safety rotation created single-high or isolated leverage;
- the defense inserted the fitter early in previous calls;
- a corner is playing an aggressive run-support technique;
- a match rule leaves a qualified receiver isolated;
- the offense has earned a max-protection two-man route with a favorable matchup;
- field position and game state justify the risk.

The offense does not force explosives against a shell built specifically to prevent them.

# 12. ACCESS, READ, AND KEEP PACKAGES

## 12.1 ACCESS Remains Pre-Snap

Normal ACCESS throws remain:

- Now;
- Smoke;
- Hitch;
- Bubble;
- quick Slant;
- fast Out when leverage is clean.

ACCESS is still selected before the snap. The quarterback does not hold the ball waiting to see whether a corner changes his mind.

## 12.2 READ

READ remains a one-defender post-snap decision.

The master menu may expand, but the weekly menu remains small.

### 2021 MASTER READ MENU

1. Inside Read Glance.
2. Split Read Slant.
3. Duo Read Pop.
4. Outside Read Bubble.
5. Counter Read Glance.
6. Power Read Flat.
7. Pin-Pull Read Now.
8. Duo Read Stick.
9. Split Read Pop.

Normally four to five are active in a week.

### 2022 MASTER ADDITION

10. Counter Read Stick/Spot, installed only when the conflict defender and route spacing are clean.

Normally four to six are active. Stone does not carry all ten merely because they exist in the master book.

## 12.3 Safety-Cap Rule

Glance, Pop, and seam-related READ throws require a pre-snap cap check.

If the safety alignment or weekly rotation tendency makes the throw structurally dangerous, the quarterback removes the throw from the decision before the snap and treats the call as the run component plus any remaining legal answer.

The quarterback is not asked to read the linebacker and then locate a rotating safety late. That would violate the one-conflict-defender rule.

## 12.4 DUO READ STICK

Concept: Duo run paired with a Stick/flat conflict on the second-level defender responsible for fitting the run or expanding to the route.

Use when:

- the overhang is the declared fitter;
- the safety stays capped enough to discourage Pop/Glance;
- the offense wants a shorter answer than a glance.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 run / 4-8 pass**.

## 12.5 KEEP

KEEP remains quarterback dependent.

Baseline Stone offense does not require designed quarterback rushing.

If the quarterback can punish an unblocked edge, the weekly KEEP menu may include:

- Split Keep;
- Counter Keep;
- Zone Keep;
- short-yardage read/keep;
- boot keeper.

Evolution IV does not become the 2022 Philadelphia offense or a Baltimore-style quarterback-run system merely because those structures exist elsewhere. The module expands only if Stone actually has that quarterback.

## 12.6 READ Guardrails

- one conflict defender per call;
- safety-cap check can veto the throw before the snap;
- no universal RPO attachment to every run;
- no late throw after the concept's legal timing is gone;
- offensive line blocking must match the package rules;
- weekly menu normally four to six calls;
- if the defense consistently clouds the read, call a normal run or pass instead of adding a third decision.

# 13. SCREEN AND PERIMETER GAME EVOLUTION

Screens remain a constraint family, not a substitute for solving the defense normally.

## 13.1 Core Screen Families

- RB Slow;
- RB Slip;
- Middle Screen;
- TE Screen;
- WR Now/Smoke;
- Bubble;
- Jail/Tunnel when the matchup supports it;
- selected motion screen.

## 13.2 Split-Safety Screen Rule

Deep safeties can rally to perimeter screens. Stone therefore does not assume a two-high shell automatically creates free yards outside.

The screen is favored when:

- the overhang is tucked into the run fit;
- the defense is presenting pressure;
- corners are playing with enough cushion to concede the throw;
- motion creates a numbers advantage;
- the offense has established an interior run that makes support step inward.

## 13.3 Perimeter Run/Screen Pairing

A common Evolution IV pairing:

- Duo inside;
- Now screen outside;
- Return Toss as perimeter run;
- play-action Bend behind the fitting safety.

The defense is forced to choose which space receives the extra body.

# 14. SIX-OL AND OFFENSIVE-LINE PACKAGE EVOLUTION

The 6OL system becomes even more valuable in a split-safety era because it adds a gap without forcing Stone to add a traditional skill-position blocker.

## 14.1 6OL Objectives

6OL can:

- create seven- or eight-man surfaces;
- force the defense to decide whether both safeties can remain deep;
- add a tackle as puller;
- protect a deep or intermediate play-action concept;
- shift from heavy to unbalanced spread;
- trade the eligible surface;
- declare a heavy picture and throw from it;
- keep the same skill players on the field while changing the mass distribution.

## 14.2 Core 6OL Run Menu

- Power;
- Counter/GT;
- Duo;
- Outside Zone;
- Split Zone;
- Pin-Pull;
- Toss/Crack;
- selected Wham/Trap.

## 14.3 Core 6OL Pass Menu

- Boot Flood;
- Post-Cross;
- Dagger;
- Y-Cross;
- Leak;
- Levels;
- max-protect shot;
- TE/tackle-eligible release only when eligibility and player skill make it credible.

## 14.4 Pulling Tackle and Sixth-Lineman Rule

Stone continues to treat guards and tackles as movement players. The tackle or sixth lineman may become:

- kick-out blocker;
- wrap puller;
- pin-pull lead;

The staff does not design a pull merely to be novel. The movement must create a leverage advantage or change the defensive fit.

The extra lineman's reporting/eligibility status must be a practiced operational detail. The offense cannot gain schematic value by creating administrative confusion for itself.

# 15. SITUATIONAL OFFENSE

## 15.1 First and Second Down

Evolution IV adds a specific call-sheet box for **Second-and-Medium** because split-safety defenses often create long drives in which staying ahead of the sticks matters.

Preferred second-and-4 to 6 calls include:

- Duo;
- Counter;
- Inside;
- Split;
- play-action Levels;
- Drive;
- Choice;
- Y-Cross;
- ACCESS when free;
- one shell-triggered shot.

Stone is willing to call a pass on second-and-5 and a run on second-and-10. The shell, fit, and game state matter more than a rigid tendency chart.

## 15.2 Third-and-1 to 2

Primary menu:

- Power;
- Duo;
- Counter;
- Insert;
- QB sneak if available;
- 6OL downhill;
- Boot/Leak constraint;
- quick Choice/Pick answer when the box is overloaded.

## 15.3 Third-and-3 to 5

Primary menu:

- Choice;
- Drive;
- Mesh;
- Stick;
- Levels;
- Duo/Inside against a pass-light box;
- movement pass;
- one READ call if the weekly structure is clean.

## 15.4 Third-and-6+

Primary menu:

- Dagger;
- Drive;
- Y-Cross;
- Mesh Rail;
- Levels;
- Mills;
- screen against pressure tendency;
- Empty with defined hot rules.

The quarterback is discouraged from forcing the ball beyond the sticks when the coverage concedes a catch-and-run chance underneath.

## 15.5 Red Zone

Split-safety spacing changes in the red zone because safeties no longer have unlimited depth. Stone shifts emphasis toward:

- Power/Duo/Counter;
- condensed Choice;
- Snag;
- Mesh with tight release control;
- Sail/Corner-Flat;
- TE option;
- play-action Leak;
- bunch/stack rubs within legal spacing;
- 6OL run/pass pairings.

BEND loses value as field depth compresses unless the route is specifically redesigned for the red zone.

## 15.6 Two Minute

Two minute remains mostly 11/10 or Empty-capable 12 FLEX.

Priorities:

- clear protection;
- sideline and middle-field clock rules;
- Drive/Levels/Choice/Stick;
- Draw/Inside against abandoned boxes;
- screen against pressure;
- selective shot only when shell or clock situation warrants it.

## 15.7 Four Minute

Four-minute offense uses Stone's rotation philosophy deliberately.

Two or three runners may cycle through meaningful snaps to preserve ball security and physical freshness. The offense can stay in 12, 21, or 6OL and still throw Boot, Cross, or Choice if the defense overcommits to the run.

The objective is first downs, not merely consuming individual snaps.

## 15.8 Fourth Down

The 2020 GO menu survives. Evolution IV adds shell/fit notes to the call sheet.

**GO 1:** Power, Duo, Counter, sneak, 6OL, Boot Leak. **GO 2-3:** Duo/Inside, Choice, Drive, Mesh, movement pass, selected READ. **GO 4-6:** Dagger, Drive, Choice, Levels, Y-Cross, Empty hot/choice concepts.

The playbook supplies the menu. Game-management logic determines whether Stone goes for it.

## 16.4 COUNTER FIT SERIES

2. Wing Left, Counter again if the spill/fit remains favorable.
3. Wing Left, Counter Leak if the support player attacks the pull path.
4. Wing Left, Return Pin-Pull if the defense is wrong-arming and spilling inside schemes.
5. Wing Left, Counter Post-Cross when the safety trigger creates the route window.

## 16.5 12 FLEX SHELL TEST

The same 12 FLEX group can:

- align Ace and run Duo;
- shift to Trey and run Counter;
- detach H and call Choice;
- shift to Empty and call Levels;
- return H to the core and run Split;
- call Dagger/BEND if the defense keeps both safeties high;
- HOLD/PRESS without changing personnel.

## 16.6 21 MOVE FIT TEST

The same 21 MOVE group can:

- show I and run Power;
- shift F to wing and run Split;
- motion F to slot and call Choice;
- empty the backfield and call Drive;
- return F and run Duo;
- Boot/Leak from the same action.

The defense must solve the body without knowing where the gap or route strength will finish.

## 18.2 Shell-to-Fit Period

The defense aligns in OPEN or CLOSED shell, then inserts different fit players after the snap.

The offense must execute the same run correctly against:

- safety fit;
- nickel fit;
- linebacker fold;
- corner replace;
- front movement.

The period prevents players from assuming the pre-snap shell tells the entire story.

## 18.3 Rotation Reaction Period

Quarterbacks and receivers practice three post-snap outcomes from the same pre-snap picture:

- STAY;
- SPIN;
- POACH.

The goal is not to create a new progression for every rotation. It is to execute the existing concept rule correctly and report what the defense did for the next call.

## 18.4 BEND Period

Only qualified BEND players participate.

The drill requires synchronized recognition between quarterback and receiver. If either player is uncertain, the route follows the base vertical rule. Stone prefers a missed bend opportunity to a turnover caused by two different pictures.

## 18.5 Simulated-Pressure Period

The defense presents six or seven possible rushers but sends four or five from changing locations.

The offense practices:

- point declaration;
- SCAN;
- LOCK;
- hot responsibility;
- throw-behind-pressure concept;
- run into vacated space;
- movement pass.

## 18.6 Puller and Secondary-Fit Period

Power and Counter are practiced against:

- spill;
- box;
- wrong-arm;
- force replacement;
- safety trigger.

The period ends with a paired perimeter or play-action answer so the puller scheme is taught as part of a series rather than in isolation.

# 19. SIMULATION DIRECTIVES FOR 2021-2022

## 19.1 Evolution Stage Selection

**2021: use Evolution IV-A.** Emphasize box count, Duo promotion, heavier shell tests, Levels/Choice promotion, BEND introduction, and more direct running against light boxes. Stone can occasionally overvalue the box count before fully solving the secondary-fit problem.

**2022: use Evolution IV-B.** Preserve the physical answer but make the fit source central. Increase fitter-replacement play action, 12/21/13/6OL shifts, intermediate pass answers, safety-cap discipline, SCAN/LOCK protection, and rotation-triggered shots.

## 19.2 Call Generation Priority

Generate calls in this order:

1. situation and game state;
2. weekly concept tier;
3. offensive personnel matchup;
4. defensive front and box count;
5. shell and expected fit source;
6. formation width/surface;
7. shift or motion only if it serves a job;
8. base concept;
9. ACCESS/READ/KEEP/protection tag only if justified;
10. Expected Yardage profile.

Do not generate "two-high -> run" as a deterministic rule.

## 19.3 Personnel Rules

- 11 is normally common, not mandatory majority.
- 12 or 21 may become the weekly center if the defense refuses to add mass or changes coverage rules poorly.
- 13 and 6OL are real run/pass packages.
- 20 requires two runners with distinct jobs.
- true 00 remains rare because Stone prefers reaching Empty from portable personnel.
- personnel usage follows roster ability and opponent response, not fixed historical percentages.

## 19.4 Rotation Rules

- two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time;
- no forced equal carries;
- no automatic two-back formation merely because multiple backs are active;
- a hot runner can stay in the game;
- protection, route, screen, and motion ability count as meaningful snaps;
- ROTATE/HOLD/PRESS should respond to the defensive matchup.

## 19.5 Shell Rules

- OPEN/CLOSED is pre-snap diagnostic information only;
- post-snap rotation can change the actual coverage;
- the simulator should model STAY, SPIN, POACH, and DRIVE reactions according to defensive scheme/tendency;
- Stone should learn from repeated fit/rotation behavior during the game;
- a safety deep at the snap can still become the run fitter.

## 19.6 Run Distribution Rules

- Duo, Power, and Counter/GT are the physical center;
- Inside is a frequent fast answer to light boxes;
- Outside and Split remain core complements;
- Pin-Pull and Toss/Crack rise when secondary fit and spill behavior favor the perimeter;
- Trap/Wham/DART are opponent tools;
- the simulator should not force one runner to receive feature-back volume;
- the simulator should also not suppress a runner who is dominating the matchup.

## 19.7 Pass Rules

- Levels and Choice receive more weekly weight than in Evolution III;
- Dagger, Y-Cross, Drive, Mesh, Sail/Flood remain major calls;
- BEND is a tag, not a new universal route system;
- Four Verticals remains available but pure all-go usage declines without a shell reason;
- checkdowns and intermediate completions are acceptable when the defense protects explosives;
- Stone still hunts explosives after rotation or leverage creates a reason.

## 19.8 READ Rules

- master menu may contain up to ten families by 2022;
- weekly active menu normally four to six;
- one conflict defender per call;
- safety-cap check can remove a glance/pop/seam answer before the snap;
- no universal RPO attachment;
- if the defense clouds the read, call a normal run/pass rather than adding complexity.

## 19.9 KEEP Rules

- KEEP remains optional and quarterback dependent;
- no designed quarterback-run identity unless the actual simulated quarterback warrants it;
- Stone can use a mobile quarterback more aggressively than the baseline without changing the master identity;
- if the quarterback is a pocket-only player, KEEP can disappear entirely.

## 19.10 Protection Rules

- SCAN becomes common versus mug/simulated pressure;
- LOCK becomes available in 2022 but must be matchup justified;
- Empty requires point and hot identification;
- movement pass and run checks can be protection answers;
- the simulator should not solve every pressure by adding blockers.

## 19.11 Play-Action Rules

- play action is tied to real weekly runs;
- intermediate play action is as legitimate as a deep shot;
- a safety-fitter trigger is a major call-generation reason;
- under-center and gun action both remain valid;
- Boot/Slide volume depends on edge discipline.

## 19.12 Expected Yardage Rules

Expected Yardage remains a planning band.

It should be modified by:

- down and distance;
- field zone;
- defensive shell and fit;
- pressure;
- personnel matchup;
- weather only when materially relevant to football execution;
- game state;
- player ability.

Do not treat a concept's band as a deterministic outcome.

## 19.13 Evolution IV Boundaries

The 2021-22 simulator must not import:

- later high-volume cheat motion;
- 2023-24 league-wide motion-at-snap acceleration as if it already exists here;
- a full modern Dolphins-style speed-motion structure;
- coordinator branches that have not occurred;
- 2025-26 mature Stone answers;
- a running-quarterback offense unless the roster actually provides that quarterback.

# APPENDIX C. HISTORICAL GROUNDING NOTES

These notes define the historical rails used to keep Evolution IV plausible. They do not mean Stone copies any one real coach or scheme.

1. **Two-high shells and light boxes became a major league-wide defensive response in 2021.** The Ringer, citing TruMedia, reported that defenses used six or fewer defenders in the box on 29 percent of run plays in 2021, up from 22.3 percent in 2020, while quarters usage also increased. Evolution IV therefore makes shell and box recognition a central planning problem.
2. **The Fangio/Staley structure was designed to stop explosives without simply conceding the run.** NFL.com described the system as using two-high pre-snap shells, post-snap rotation, matchup coverage, and lighter boxes while still fitting the run with secondary players from depth. Evolution IV therefore rejects the simplistic rule that a light box is automatically a weak box.
3. **Defenses increasingly forced elite passing offenses to accept underneath gains and efficient rushing opportunities.** NFL.com analysis in 2022 described the growing use of two-deep safety structures intended to eliminate big plays and force offenses to earn yards underneath. Evolution IV promotes Levels, Choice, intermediate play action, and patient drives without abandoning explosive intent.
4. **The 2022 offensive landscape showed that there was no single correct counter.** Philadelphia led the NFL with 32 rushing touchdowns, while Kansas City led the league in passing yards and passing touchdowns. Stone's answer is therefore system-based: physical run answers, intermediate passing, play action, and shell-triggered shots coexist rather than the offense becoming run-first by doctrine.
5. **Quarterback mobility remained a major offensive advantage, but it was not universal.** By 2022 several top offenses used quarterbacks as real run-game numbers, while other elite offenses remained primarily pass-driven. Stone's KEEP module therefore remains available but quarterback dependent instead of becoming the master identity.
6. **Post-snap disguise and match principles remained essential parts of the defensive answer.** NFL.com analysis of Fangio structures emphasized post-snap safety movement and matchup concepts that can switch crossing routes. Evolution IV therefore changes how Mesh and crossers are presented and adds more Choice/Levels/BEND answers rather than assuming motion or crossing routes automatically win.

# STONE 2021-2022 OFFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** physical downhill multiple offense + concept passing + personnel portability + shell/fit identification + disciplined option tools.

**System sentence:** Count the box. Stress the fit. Make the rotation wrong.

**Core runs:** Duo / Power / Counter-GT. Inside, Outside, and Split remain stable core. Pin-Pull and Toss/Crack rise when the secondary is fitting downhill. Trap/Wham remain opponent tools.

**Core passes:** Stick / Drive / Y-Cross / Dagger / Mesh. Levels and Choice are promoted. Sail/Flood is a major outside answer. Post-Cross and Mills remain shot families.

**BEND:** route tag for qualified seam/vertical players against uncapped open-middle structure. It is not a universal option route.

**Personnel:** 11 remains common. 12, 21, 13, and 6OL deliberately test whether the defense can keep both safeties deep while fitting additional gaps. 20 remains a true two-runner matchup package.

**Back principle:** two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time. Playing time does not imply equal carries or simultaneous backfield usage.

**Shell principle:** OPEN/CLOSED is pre-snap information, not the coverage answer. Track the actual fitter and post-snap rotation.

**READ:** up to ten master families by 2022, normally four to six active. One conflict defender per call. Safety cap can veto selected throws before the snap.

**KEEP:** optional quarterback-dependent module. The baseline system still does not require a running quarterback.

**Protection:** SCAN is weekly. LOCK is available in 2022. Empty requires point/hot rules. Protect, throw behind pressure, move the launch point, or run into the vacated space.

**OL principle:** guards, tackles, and 6OL remain movement tools. 6OL is a shell test, run/pass package, and protection structure, not a short-yardage tell.

**Tempo:** ROTATE to test matching, HOLD to keep a favorable shell, PRESS when the current eleven can attack before the defense substitutes.

**Play-caller:** Show -> Stress -> Punish. In Evolution IV, the "Stress" target is often the player who must leave the shell and fit the run.
