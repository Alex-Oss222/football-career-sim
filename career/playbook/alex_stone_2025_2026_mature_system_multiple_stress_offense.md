# Alex Stone Offensive Playbook

## 2025-2026 Mature System: Multiple Stress Offense

> **Team-neutral standalone master book** | Document revision 6.0 | Primary play-caller: Alex Stone
> **System sentence:** Change the problem. Keep the answer familiar.

This is the first fully consolidated mature system for Stone's offense. It stands on its own. A coach, player, or simulator should not need the 2013 book or Evolutions II through V to operate this system. The earlier books explain how Stone arrived here; this book defines what survives by 2025-26.

The mature offense is still recognizably the same system that began in 2013: concept-based passing, physical downhill runs, extensive personnel portability, purposeful substitution, under-center and shotgun competence, pulling guards and tackles, controlled quarterback movement, and a willingness to use tight ends, backs, fullback/H roles, and extra offensive linemen as real offensive pieces. What changes is how completely the pieces now fit together.

The 2025 season is treated as a complete historical season. The 2026 portion of this book is the current operating state, not a forecast of an unfinished season. Stone does not know future 2026 outcomes. Unless the simulation creates a new staff branch, firing, roster constraint, or later evolution, this is the mature master system in force.

# 1. SYSTEM IDENTITY AND MATURE COACHING LAWS

Stone's mature offense is multiple in bodies, pictures, width, motion, and tempo, but compact in the rules the offense must actually remember. The defense is asked to solve a new problem every few snaps. The offense is asked to solve familiar concepts from new locations.

The system is neither a spread offense that happens to carry heavy packages nor a heavy offense that occasionally spreads out. It can play from 11 with five eligible threats, from 13 with three attached surfaces, from 21 with a movable F, from 20 with two true runners, or from 6OL with an eligible tackle. The point is not to collect formations. The point is to make the defense reveal how it intends to match the people and fit the gaps, then use the same small collection of offensive rules against that answer.

The mature Stone offense also treats protection as an offensive skill rather than an emergency. Motion, chips, extra surfaces, quick access, movement passes, and route spacing all have protection consequences. A call that creates a beautiful route picture but leaves the quarterback exposed to the opponent's best pressure rule is not a complete call.

## 1.1 The Twelve Mature Laws

1. **Concept survives presentation.** The concept is the offensive answer. Personnel, formation, motion, and protection change the defensive problem without forcing the quarterback to learn a new progression every time.

2. **Personnel asks a question.** Heavy personnel asks whether the defense will stay small or substitute size. Spread personnel asks whether it will widen the box or stay condensed. Stone does not assume either answer.

3. **Heavy does not mean slow; light does not mean finesse.** 13 personnel can empty the backfield. 11 personnel can run Duo or Power. 20 can be a protection package. 6OL can throw.

4. **Run downhill into displaced fits.** Motion and formation are useful partly because they move support. When a defender leaves the core, the offense is willing to attack the space with Duo, Power, Counter, Insert, or Split.

5. **Motion must earn practice time.** Every motion must change leverage, fit, spacing, release, pressure declaration, or a later constraint. Decorative movement is removed.

6. **Stillness belongs to the motion system.** Every major fast-motion series has a static companion so the defense cannot use the motion as the play call.

7. **Protection is part of the concept.** The call includes an answer to the opponent's rush structure. SCAN, LOCK, SORT, CHIP, movement, and quick access are integrated into the weekly plan.

8. **Help without surrender.** Tight ends, backs, and F players may chip an elite edge and still release. Extra bodies exist to improve both protection and route structure, not merely to stay in and block.

9. **READ one defender, not eleven.** Post-snap options identify one conflict player with a rehearsed fallback. Stone does not attach a read to every run or ask the quarterback to improvise the structure.

10. **Fresh players, real roles, no touch quotas.** Multiple backs and skill players can play meaningful snaps. A hot player can remain on the field. Rotation is a resource, not an accounting system.

11. **Offensive linemen are geometry tools.** Guards and tackles can pull, wrap, lead, report eligible, change surfaces, and alter protection pictures. 6OL is not a short-yardage declaration.

12. **The weekly menu gets smaller as the master system grows.** The mature book is broad. Sunday is selective. Stone wants connected answers, not maximum volume.

## 1.2 What the Mature Offense Is Not

- not a copy of any one coaching tree, even though its roots are visible;

- not a universal RPO offense;

- not a motion-at-snap offense that becomes ordinary when the motion is removed;

- not a feature-back offense that requires one runner to absorb every useful snap;

- not a heavy-personnel offense that automatically runs when a third tight end or sixth lineman enters;

- not a quarterback-run system by default;

- not a 700-play catalog in which every formation variation is treated as a separate play;

- not a system that changes terminology every time a new idea is imported.

| **Tier**    | **Meaning**                                                                    | **Typical Families**                                                                            |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY    | Installed first, carried every week, and called from several personnel groups. | Duo, Power, Counter/GT, Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Mesh, Spacing, core play-action, base screens   |

## 1.4 Play-Caller Operating Rules

- **Show -> Stress -> Punish:** remains the sequencing language.

- **Ask, do not guess.** Early calls are used to learn how the defense matches 12, 21, 13, motion, Empty, and 6OL.

- **Return to the picture.** When the defense shows its adjustment, come back to the same formation or action with the answer.

- **Do not chase numerical balance.** Run/pass balance means the defense cannot erase one category without exposing another.

- **Take free yards.** Access throws and easy completions are allowed to be boring if they force the defense to change.

- **Shots need evidence.** A deep call is attached to leverage, rotation, field zone, a protection advantage, or a previous setup.

- **Ride a real advantage.** Stone is not required to rotate away from a back, tight end, receiver, or formation that the defense cannot solve.

- **Cut dead calls.** A concept can remain in the master book and disappear from the weekly sheet for months.

# 2. CALL LANGUAGE AND TEACHING STRUCTURE

The mature language remains short because the complexity lives in shared definitions. Stone does not respond to a larger system by turning the huddle call into a paragraph. Personnel is normally signaled before the huddle. Default protection, formation strength, and concept rules are omitted unless they need to change.

## 2.1 Base Call Order

`FORMATION -> SHIFT/MOTION -> CONCEPT/RUN -> DIRECTION OR TAG -> PROTECTION MODIFIER`

- Trey Right, Drive

- Ace Left, Duo R

- Bunch Right, Flash, Mesh

- Wing Left, Return, Counter R

- Gun Trips, Inside, Access Bubble

- Heavy Right, Post-Cross, Chip X

- Trey Left, Flash, Duo, Read Stick

- Empty Right, Choice, Sort

## 2.4 Receiver Split Standards

| **Split Family** | **Landmark / Purpose**                                                                                       |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Normal           | Outside receiver near top of numbers; slot owns two-way room.                                                |
| Reduced          | Outside receiver inside numbers or closer to core for crossers, crack blocks, and outside-release space.     |
| Tight            | Compressed roughly 4-6 yards from core for bunch/stack releases, crack support, chips, and switch games.     |
| Wide             | Forces corner/safety width and expands the box.                                                              |
| Stack            | One receiver directly behind/slightly inside another; release responsibility is taught by concept.           |
| Nub              | One eligible to a side or closed surface with weak-side isolated eligible. Used to declare coverage and fit. |

Splits are adjusted by opponent technique. The offense protects concept spacing, not painted yard marks. A receiver can reduce to gain an in-breaking runway, widen to remove an overhang, or compress to create a chip/release angle as long as the rest of the concept preserves its relationship.

## 2.5 Mature Route Depth Standards

| **Route**     | **Normal Landmark**                                                                    |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hitch / Spot  | 5-6 yards, work back downhill and present numbers.                                     |
| Stick         | 5-6 yards, leverage stem then out/sit by rule.                                         |
| Shallow       | 3-5 yards across; never drift uphill.                                                  |
| Dig           | 12-15 normal; 15-18 in Dagger or shot family.                                          |
| Curl          | 10-12 yards, work back downhill.                                                       |
| Corner / Sail | 10-14 stem before break unless red-zone spacing compresses it.                         |
| Post          | 10-12 stem minimum on normal shot; maintain opposite-safety spacing.                   |
| Cross / Over  | 12-18 depending concept and formation; stay behind underneath defenders and below cap. |
| Seam          | Vertical through landmark; Bend only by rule or tag.                                   |
| Choice        | Landmark depends leverage; receiver and QB must share the same leverage rule.          |
| Go / Fade     | Win stack or preserve sideline room; QB throws by leverage rather than fixed landmark. |

## 2.6 Mature Tag Discipline

Tags are allowed to make the defense wrong, not to rescue a bad base concept. A weekly call should normally carry zero or one route tag and zero or one protection modifier. Calls with multiple independent exceptions are cut unless they are a rehearsed situational play.

- **Alert:** outside progression unless leverage is clean enough to take immediately.

- **Bend:** vertical player bends away from cap or into open middle by rule.

- **Switch:** two eligible players exchange release lanes while preserving concept spacing.

- **Rail/Wheel:** back/F converts to vertical outside track.

- **Leak:** eligible delays through protection/run-action picture before releasing.

- **Chip:** eligible strikes designated edge before releasing to assigned route.

- **Stay:** eligible remains in protection and concept converts to reduced route count.

- **Read:** post-snap run/pass conflict with one declared key.

- **Access:** pre-snap free-throw replacement of the called run.

- **Keep:** optional quarterback run responsibility when player skill and weekly plan permit it.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

Personnel is Stone's longest-running source of innovation. By 2025 the league is again using more multi-tight-end, fullback, and extra-lineman structures, but Stone's mature answer is not simply to get bigger. The system uses bodies to ask the defense whether it wants size or speed on the field, then changes the picture after the answer.

If the defense substitutes base against 12/13/21, Stone can detach bodies, motion to Empty, and attack linebackers in space. If the defense stays nickel or big nickel, he is willing to run directly at the lighter box or use extra surfaces to create additional gaps. The grouping itself never determines the call.

## 3.1 Planning Centers, Not Quotas

| **Personnel** | **Normal 2025-26 Planning Center** | **Primary Reason**                                                                     | **Required Credibility**                                                 |
|---------------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 11            | 34-48%                             | Broadest tempo/dropback shell and easiest fast-motion distribution.                    | Duo/Power/Counter, core dropback, motion, Empty, screens, PA.            |
| 12            | 24-34%                             | Two-surface football, chips, heavy-to-spread conversion, seam/Choice matchups.         | Full run core, core passes, Empty/3x1, chip/release, READ.               |
| 21            | 9-17%                              | Movable F, under-center force, split-flow, two-back structure, protection flexibility. | Power/Duo/Counter, Outside/Split, play action, spread conversion.        |
| 20            | 4-9%                               | Two true runners, matchup creation, screen/protection variety, motion to Empty.        | Run threat from either back, routes, screens, pass protection.           |
| 13            | 6-14%                              | Force personnel mismatch; create extra gaps without surrendering pass game.            | Power/Duo/Counter, PA, Cross/Drive/Levels, Flex/Empty conversion.        |
| 10            | 2-7%                               | Two-minute, pressure identification, pure spacing.                                     | Quick game, Mesh/Drive/Vert, Draw, screens, five-man protection rules.   |
| True 00       | Rare                               | Specialist Empty package only when roster quality warrants it.                         | Five eligible receivers who can identify pressure and execute hot rules. |

**6OL is an overlay, not a separate percentage bucket.** It can appear from nominal 11, 12, 13, or 21 structures depending on which eligible is removed or reclassified.

## 3.2 11 Personnel: The Tempo and Distribution Shell

- carries every major run family except the most specialized heavy insert calls;

- owns the widest PRESS menu;

- supports FLASH, ZIP, JET, RETURN, DRIFT, and EXIT;

- can reach Empty without substitution;

- must be able to play condensed or wide so 11 itself is not a formation tell;

- is allowed to leave the field when the defense has built a specific 11-personnel answer.

## 3.3 12 Personnel: HEAVY and FLEX

The HEAVY/FLEX labels describe available body skills, not separate offenses.

| **Variant** | **Body Requirement**                                      | **Offensive Stress**                                                        |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 12 HEAVY    | Both Y bodies can function attached and block real edges. | Creates extra gaps, chip surfaces, 7-man protection and under-center force. |
| 12 FLEX     | At least one Y/H is a credible detached receiver.         | Can become Trey, Trips, Bunch, Stack or Empty while retaining run threat.   |

- Both HEAVY and FLEX variants must carry Duo, Power, Counter, Outside, Split, Drive, Y-Cross, Levels, Choice, Flood and play-action shots.

- One Y/H should be able to chip and release against an elite edge without the route becoming decorative.

- When the defense answers with base personnel, 12 FLEX should be able to create four immediate receiving threats without substituting.

- When the defense stays nickel, 12 HEAVY should have enough attached blocking to force the fifth defensive back to fit the run.

## 3.4 21 Personnel: MOVE F

F survives because Stone never defined the position as one body type. The mature F can be a traditional fullback, H-back, second tight end, large tailback, or hybrid receiver/blocker. The player must execute more than one category of work or the substitution becomes a tell.

- offset, I, wing, sniffer, slot, and split alignment;

- Insert / lead / arc / split-flow blocks;

- CHIP / protection adjustment;

- flat, wheel, angle, choice, leak, or shallow routes;

- JET/RETURN/DRIFT motion when the player has enough movement skill;

- conversion to 3x1 or Empty without personnel change.

## 3.5 20 Personnel: TWIN Runners

20 is the place where Stone can use two tailbacks together, but the package exists only when the two backs create different problems on the same snap. It does not exist to let two runners compete for the same A-gap carry.

- either back can be primary runner;

- one back can flex, EXIT, or motion while the other remains in the backfield;

- one can protect while the other releases;

- wheel/angle and screen combinations are major reasons to activate the package;

- a back who cannot protect or align outside narrows the package and may reduce weekly usage.

## 3.6 13 Personnel: Mature Heavy-to-Space Package

The mature system finally allows 13 to become a real weekly center of gravity when the roster has three playable tight ends. It is not automatically a goal-line package.

| **13 Picture** | **Primary Stress**                                                                             |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 13 HEAVY       | Three attached/wing bodies create extra gaps and force base or big nickel.                     |
| 13 FLEX        | One Y detaches or motions; defense must declare whether a linebacker/safety follows.           |
| 13 EMPTY       | Back or Y exits, creating Empty spacing against personnel chosen to fit heavy run.             |
| 13 NUB/OVER    | Creates closed surface plus isolated receiver and forces strength checks.                      |
| 13 MAX/SHOT    | Protection looks like heavy run; one or two TEs release late into vertical/crossing structure. |

The package is cut back immediately if the third tight end cannot block enough to create a real run threat or cannot execute enough route/protection work to avoid a one-call tell.

## 3.7 Running Back Room

Stone wants at least two backs trained on the normal identity run menu whenever the roster permits. A third back can own a narrower role, but that role must contain more than one category of threat.

- Playing time is planned; touches are earned and situation-dependent.

- RB1 can stay on the field for an entire drive if he is producing, protecting, and not fatigued.

- RB2 is not restricted to relief carries; he must be able to execute real pass, screen, and protection work.

- RB3 may own a speed, screen, two-minute, return, short-yardage, or special-motion package, but the staff avoids one-call substitution tells.

- Two backs on the field together are a package choice, not the default expression of the rotation philosophy.

## 3.8 Tight End / H Room

- The room is graded by roles: attached blocker, move route player, chip/release player, protection piece, motion player, and special teams availability.

- The offense does not pretend every TE can execute every role. The concept remains stable while job ownership changes.

- At least one Y must be trusted in pass protection communication if 12/13 is going to be a major weekly package.

- A third TE earns 13-personnel volume only by creating either a new gap, a new matchup, or a new protection solution.

## 3.9 Wide Receiver Room

- X must win enough isolated routes to prevent the defense from overloading the core.

- Z/H roles carry more motion and condensed-release burden.

- Receivers must block legally enough for crack, toss, pin-pull, screen, and perimeter series to remain credible.

- Fast motion is not restricted to the fastest receiver; timing, ball security, route discipline, and blocking responsibility matter.

- A receiver can be rotated for freshness without the offense promising a target count.

## 3.10 6OL Overlay

T6 is a real offensive role. The sixth lineman may align attached, wing-like, tackle-over, unbalanced, or eligible. He can down block, pull, wrap, lead, chip a surface, pass protect, or release on a narrowly practiced eligible route. The defense should not be able to hear the report and know that Power is coming.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, ROTATION, AND TEMPO

Stone substitutes more than average over the course of a game, but not on every snap. The mature substitution system deliberately alternates between changing bodies and trapping the defense with the bodies already on the field.

## 4.1 ROTATE / HOLD / PRESS

| **State** | **Meaning**                                                           | **Best Use**                                                                  |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ROTATE    | Change one or more offensive bodies before the snap sequence.         | Freshness, matchup testing, introducing 12/13/21/20/6OL, injury management.   |
| HOLD      | Keep current eleven and change formation, width, backfield or motion. | Preserve favorable defensive personnel while changing the picture.            |
| PRESS     | Same eleven plus tempo.                                               | Attack communication or personnel before the defense can substitute or reset. |

## 4.2 Rotation Principles

- Rotate for football reasons: fatigue, role quality, matchup, protection, series design, or special package.

- Do not rotate a productive player merely to satisfy a pregame snap target.

- Never let the second back, third tight end, or sixth lineman announce one call family.

- Long drives can use planned two- or three-play sub-waves, but Stone can abort the rotation if the defense is trapped in a favorable package.

- Receivers and tight ends may rotate as aggressively as backs when their jobs include repeated motion, crack blocking, or chip work.

- When the offense substitutes, the defense receives its legal opportunity to match. Stone does not pretend substitution itself creates tempo advantage.

## 4.3 PRESS Menu

The mature PRESS menu is concept-based and normally 30-40 calls for a veteran quarterback, but only a subset is active each week.

- Duo / Inside / Split / selected Power or Counter when the front rule is clean;

- Stick / Spacing / Slant-Flat;

- Mesh / Drive / Shallow / Choice;

- Y-Cross / Dagger when protection is declared;

- Smoke / Now / Bubble;

- RB Slip / Slow;

- one or two READ calls;

- one Alert shot;

- selected FLASH/EXIT only if motion timing does not damage protection communication.

## 4.4 Tempo Commands

| **Command** | **Meaning**                                                                        |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| NORMAL      | Standard huddle or normal no-huddle cadence.                                       |
| PRESS       | Operate quickly with current personnel and a limited call language.                |
| FREEZE      | Get aligned, use cadence or look to expose pressure/rotation, then reset or check. |
| MILK        | Drain clock while maintaining enough cadence variation to prevent jump timing.     |

## 4.5 Playing-Time Planning

The weekly staff may set snap bands by player to protect conditioning and role balance, but the simulator must not turn those bands into equal carries or targets. Playing-time plans are soft constraints. Production, injury, pass protection, game state, and opponent matchup can override them.

# 5. FORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Formation is the picture, not the play. The mature offense keeps a finite set of formation families and uses width, surface, backfield, and motion modifiers to create the illusion of a much larger inventory.

## 5.1 Core Formation Families

| **Family**                | **Normal Structure**                           | **Primary Stress**                                                         |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Doubles                   | 2x2 open or condensed                          | Balanced quick game, box count, two-way motion.                            |
| Trey                      | 3x1 with Y/H creating strong surface           | Best all-purpose shell for run, Cross, Dagger, motion and READ.            |
| Trips                     | 3x1 detached or compressed                     | Coverage declaration, flood/spacing, fast motion.                          |
| Bunch                     | Three-man cluster                              | Man/switch stress, release control, screen and rub spacing.                |
| Stack                     | Two or more receivers vertically layered       | Press avoidance, switch releases, condensed passing.                       |
| Ace                       | Under-center/pistol balanced TE surface        | Duo, Outside, Power, play action.                                          |
| Wing                      | Y plus wing/H surface                          | Counter, Split, Pin-Pull, motion, boot.                                    |
| Pro / I                   | Traditional two-back structures                | Power, Lead/Insert, Counter, Outside, shot play-action.                    |
| Nub                       | Closed surface with isolated eligible opposite | Shell/fit declaration, weak-side isolation, Counter/Duo.                   |
| Empty                     | Five eligible distributed                      | Pressure declaration, quick game, Choice, spacing, QB draw/keep if active. |
| Heavy / Over / Unbalanced | Extra surface or shifted line                  | Gap creation, 13/6OL deception, play-action.                               |

## 5.2 Formation Modifiers

- **Tight / Nasty:** compress eligible splits to create releases, crack angles, chips, and run support.

- **Wide:** expand the shell and box.

- **Over:** move an eligible or tackle to overload one surface.

- **Nub:** remove a receiver from one side and force the defense to define strength.

- **Stack / Bunch:** control press and switch rules.

- **Closed:** attached Y closes one side; does not guarantee run.

- **Open:** no attached surface to the declared side; does not guarantee pass.

- **Pistol:** back directly behind quarterback; keeps downhill and perimeter options alive.

- **Gun:** normal shotgun spacing.

- **Under:** under-center action and run track.

## 5.3 Width Is a Game-Plan Variable

Stone does not carry one universal split width. The same Trey personnel can be Wide to remove the apex, Normal for balanced spacing, or Tight to create switch releases, crack support, and chip angles. The weekly call sheet notes the preferred width against each coverage/fit family.

## 5.4 Formation Transformation

The mature system expects its best personnel groups to reach at least three structurally different pictures without substitution. Examples:

| **Personnel** | **Picture 1** | **Picture 2**         | **Picture 3**    | **Picture 4**             |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------------|------------------|---------------------------|
| 11            | Doubles       | Trey/Trips            | Bunch/Stack      | Empty                     |
| 12            | Ace/Wing      | Trey                  | Bunch/Stack      | Empty/Flex                |
| 21            | I/Pro         | Wing/Offset           | Slot/Trey-like   | Empty/20-look             |
| 20            | Split backs   | Doubles with F flexed | Trips via motion | Empty                     |
| 13            | Heavy/Wing    | Nub/Over              | Trey/Bunch       | selected Empty            |
| 6OL overlay   | Heavy surface | Tackle-over           | Unbalanced       | eligible spread/condensed |

## 5.5 Ball Location

- Boundary formations need enough room for the concept; do not call a wide-field Sail into no grass.

- Condensed formations can create useful field-side release space near the hash.

- Empty and bunch structures change when the ball is on a tight hash; landmark rules preserve spacing.

- Red-zone width is compressed by field geometry, so route depth and motion timing shorten rather than forcing normal-field landmarks.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION SYSTEM

The mature motion system is broad but not chaotic. The words describe distinct paths and timing. The quarterback and skill players also share a reaction vocabulary so motion reveals a defensive rule instead of producing a vague impression.

## 6.1 Motion Families

| **Motion** | **Primary Path**                                       | **Primary Jobs**                                                  |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| JET        | Fast across formation near snap                        | Perimeter threat, force movement, run fake, receiver speed.       |
| ZIP        | Fast short motion across/into formation                | Leverage, bunch/stack creation, crack/flat control.               |
| RETURN     | Cross then return toward original side                 | Communication stress, false strength, counter constraint.         |
| ORBIT      | Deep arc behind backfield                              | Change-up eye control, screen/reverse constraint.                 |
| TRADE      | Y/WR changes side or surface and becomes set           | Surface/strength change, front declaration.                       |
| SHIFT      | Multiple players reset and become set                  | Formation transformation, personnel disguise.                     |
| FLASH      | Late short fast motion, often entering snap with speed | Running release, support displacement, quick touch or decoy.      |
| DRIFT      | Controlled lateral movement at lower speed             | Zone spacing, switch rule, fit influence without screaming speed. |
| EXIT       | Back/F/slot leaves core toward perimeter or Empty      | Widen box, reveal pressure, create immediate receiving threat.    |

## 6.2 Motion Jobs

1. **Identify:** reveal TRAVEL, PUSH, SWITCH, SPIN, STAY, or BOX reaction.

2. **Leverage:** change the defender's angle at the snap.

3. **Fit:** move a support player or force a fit exchange.

4. **Release:** give a receiver or back running speed into his route.

5. **Surface:** change which side has the extra gap or attached body.

6. **Pressure:** force communication, reveal a blitzer, or change protection count.

7. **Constraint:** create an expected movement so the static or opposite-side answer becomes better.

## 6.3 Defensive Motion-Reaction Language

| **Call** | **Observed Rule**                                                       | **Offensive Meaning**                                                |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| TRAVEL   | Defender follows motion with same assignment.                           | Likely man or locked matchup; confirm post-snap.                     |
| PUSH     | Defense passes responsibility to next defender while maintaining shell. | Attack communication seams or opposite leverage.                     |
| SWITCH   | Two defenders exchange assignments around bunch/stack/motion.           | Use vertical/under exchanges, run away, or static complement.        |
| SPIN     | Safety/second-level player rotates with or after motion.                | Track new fitter and cap; shot/read may change.                      |
| STAY     | Structure barely moves.                                                 | Motion did not create leverage; use static concept or run the space. |
| BOX      | Defense brackets/boxes condensed release area.                          | Use run, outside isolation, delayed route, or opposite-side concept. |

## 6.4 Motion Direction Rule

Motion direction cannot reliably predict point of attack. A rightward FLASH must be able to lead to a run right, run left, pass right, pass left, screen, or shot. If self-scout shows that one motion direction predicts one family, the package is reduced until the constraint is installed.

## 6.5 Static Complement Rule

For every major fast-motion series, Stone carries at least one high-confidence call from the same starting formation with no motion. The static call can be the base run, quick game, play action, or screen. The defense must respect the possibility that nothing will move.

## 6.6 Motion Timing and Cadence

- Motion cannot begin until protection communication is ready unless the call explicitly uses the motion to set protection.

- The snap landmark is practiced by motion family, not guessed by the quarterback.

- PRESS removes late motion when the timing burden causes protection or alignment errors.

- Freeze cadence can be paired with set shifts, but illegal simulated-snap movement is never tolerated.

- Motion players are rotated for workload when the package requires repeated full-speed movement.

# 7. QUARTERBACK OPERATING SYSTEM

The quarterback's mature job is to make the offense simpler after the snap. He does not need to solve the entire defensive call. He must identify enough structure to place the protection, locate the likely conflict, know where the free throw lives, and then execute the concept's normal progression.

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Nine

1. **Personnel / down / field:** know the situation and the offense's purpose for the call.

2. **Front:** even, odd, bear/tite, five-man, wide, or reduced surface.

3. **Point / Mike:** set the protection/run declaration.

4. **Shell:** OPEN or CLOSED before motion.

5. **Fitter:** identify the player most likely to enter the run fit if the shell stays light.

6. **Pressure surface:** mugged backers, overloaded edge, slot pressure, or simulated-pressure structure.

7. **Leverage:** free access throw, isolated matchup, or route alert.

8. **Motion rule:** TRAVEL / PUSH / SWITCH / SPIN / STAY / BOX if movement is called.

9. **Clock / cadence:** confirm timing, substitution status, and snap command.

## 7.2 OPEN and CLOSED

OPEN means two safeties remain visibly high enough to present a split structure before the snap. CLOSED means one safety is clearly in the post or the shell has otherwise rotated to a single-high picture. These are starting descriptions, not coverage guarantees.

## 7.3 Fit-Source Rule

A light box is useful only if the quarterback knows who is expected to repair it. The fitter may be a safety, nickel, linebacker, or edge replacement. Stone's 2022 correction remains permanent: do not call a run merely because six defenders are in the box; call it because the fit geometry favors the run or because the called action can make the fitter wrong.

## 7.4 Alert Hierarchy

1. Free ACCESS throw with clean leverage when game situation permits it.

2. Protection emergency / immediate hot answer.

3. Called concept primary progression.

4. Alert shot only when the leverage and protection criteria are satisfied.

5. Checkdown / outlet.

6. Extension: reset, escape, or throw away before turning structure into chaos.

## 7.5 BEND Rule

BEND applies to designated vertical routes against split-safety or rotating caps. The receiver stays vertical until the cap declares. He bends into open grass only by installed landmark rule. The quarterback never invents a bend that the receiver has not been taught.

## 7.6 Extension and Scramble

- Step up before leaving clean structure.

- When breaking contain, keep eyes downfield and follow route-conversion rules.

- Receivers nearest sideline work deep or back; interior receivers find open windows by weekly scramble rule.

- Quarterback running after progression is different from KEEP. Scramble yards are available without making the offense a designed-QB-run system.

- Protect the football. A four-yard completion or throwaway is preferred to an unnecessary late cross-field throw.

## 7.7 Cadence

- Normal mixed cadence prevents defenders from keying motion timing.

- FREEZE can expose pressure or movement, but the offense must be able to snap the ball if the defense does not reveal.

- Hard count is situational, not a personality trait.

- Motion and cadence are married in practice so the quarterback does not rush the snap merely to "hit" a moving player.

# 8. PROTECTION SYSTEM

Protection in the mature Stone offense combines classic five-, six-, and seven-man structures with route-integrated help. The system wants the quarterback to know where the unblocked or late rusher can come from without keeping every eligible receiver in protection.

## 8.1 Core Protection Families

| **Protection** | **Normal Structure**                                                   | **Primary Use**                                                      |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| SCAT           | Five-man / quick six-man by release rule                               | Quick game and defined rhythm throws. Ball must solve pressure.      |
| HALF           | Half-slide / half-man                                                  | Base dropback protection; portable across formations.                |
| SOLID          | Six-man firm protection with back or Y included                        | Intermediate concepts and pressure games.                            |
| MAX            | Seven or eight protectors                                              | Shot plays, heavy play-action, dangerous rush matchups.              |
| TURNBACK       | Movement / boot protection back across action                          | Boot, slide, waggle families.                                        |
| SCAN           | Back scans inside-out or across for most dangerous second-level threat | Mugged backers, late pressure, uncertain second-level insert.        |
| LOCK           | Declare one defender or side that will not be passed off               | Protect weak link, avoid confusion on overload/stunt.                |
| SORT           | Line/back/TE sort simulated-pressure picture by count and release rule | Creepers, replacement pressure, drop-out edges, late insert rushers. |

## 8.2 CHIP Modifier

CHIP is now a mature part of Stone's passing game, especially from 12, 13, condensed 11, and 21. It buys time against a premium edge without automatically removing the chipper from the route.

- CHIP names the edge or side, not a generic "help somewhere" instruction.

- Y/F/R strikes enough to alter the rusher's track, then releases on the concept landmark.

- If the rusher spikes inside or drops, the chipper follows the release rule rather than chasing him across the formation.

- CHIP routes are designed with later timing. The quarterback cannot expect an immediate flat from a player who must first contact the edge.

- Multiple chips are allowed in weekly shot plans when the defense has two elite edge threats, but the route concept must still have enough eligible receivers to function.

## 8.3 SCAN / LOCK / SORT Rules

| **Tag** | **Quarterback / Center Job**                                        | **Back / Y Job**                                                                 |
|---------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| SCAN    | Set point and identify most dangerous inside threat.                | Scan inside-out or declared cross-face path; release if clean by rule.           |
| LOCK    | Declare man/side that cannot be passed off.                         | Help opposite or release according to call.                                      |
| SORT    | Count likely rushers, identify drop candidate and replacement lane. | Fit into declared sort; do not chase a dropping edge and leave replacement free. |

## 8.4 Simulated-Pressure Rule

OPEN shell is not low-pressure football. Defenses can show five or six, rush four, drop an edge, and insert a linebacker or safety. The offensive rule is to protect space and count, not jerseys. A rusher who drops is replaced by the next threat entering that gap; the line does not follow him into coverage.

## 8.5 Empty Protection

- Identify the five most immediate rushers and the quarterback's hot side.

- If the defense can bring six, the ball must leave to a built-in answer unless the call uses a motion/shift to bring a protector back.

- Empty can be reached from 11, 12, 20, 21, and selected 13 so the defense cannot assume personnel tells protection.

- EXIT motion that empties the backfield is cancelled or altered if the protection call requires that back in the core.

## 8.6 Protection by Concept

| **Concept Family**           | **Default Mature Protection** | **Common Adjustment**                  |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Stick / Spacing / quick game | SCAT / five-man               | Access, hot, or quick release.         |
| Mesh / Drive / Shallow       | HALF                          | SCAN vs mugged interior.               |
| Y-Cross / Levels / Choice    | HALF or SOLID                 | CHIP edge, LOCK weak link.             |
| Dagger / Mills / Post-Cross  | SOLID                         | CHIP, MAX, or SORT by rush plan.       |
| Four Verticals               | HALF / SOLID                  | CHIP or MAX when edge rush dictates.   |
| Boot / Slide                 | TURNBACK                      | MAX edge when launch point needs time. |
| Screen                       | Show protection then release  | Weekly screen blocking rules.          |

## 8.7 Protection Self-Scout

- Do not let MAX protection always equal shot.

- Do not let CHIP side always predict throw side.

- Do not let 12/13 personnel automatically mean six- or seven-man protection.

- Do not let Empty automatically mean quick game.

- Track sacks/pressures by call family, not just by lineman, so the staff can identify scheme-caused problems.

# 9. MATURE RUN GAME

The mature run game is physical first and varied second. Stone still wants defenders to defeat blocks, fit the correct gap, and tackle fresh runners for four quarters. The difference from 2013 is that motion, personnel, and shell identification now change where the defense has to make those fits.

## 9.1 Mature Run Hierarchy

| **Tier**    | **Run Families**                                                                |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY    | Duo / Power / Counter-GT                                                        |
| CORE        | Outside Zone / Split Zone / Inside Zone                                         |
| WEEKLY      | Pin-Pull / Toss-Crack / DART-Tackle Wrap / Lead-Insert / Jet-Flash Sweep / Draw |
| CHANGE-UP   | Trap / Wham / Reverse / Orbit handoff                                           |
| SITUATIONAL | Sneak / Follow / Goal-line wedge or specialty heavy run                         |

## 9.2 DUO

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                    | **Base Formations**              |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 4-7                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 20 selected, 6OL | Ace, Trey, Wing, Pro, Heavy, Nub |

**Purpose:** Vertical double-team run that makes the second-level fitter wrong. It is Stone's cleanest mature answer to light or displaced boxes.

**Core rules:** Create vertical doubles whenever the front allows. The back presses the interior track, keys the declared linebacker/fitter, and stays patient enough to force that defender to commit. The attached surface must secure the edge without turning the play into outside zone.

**Mature complements:** FLASH/EXIT can widen the fitter; Insert can add an F to the second level; Read Stick or Access Smoke can punish overhang leverage; Duo action builds Dagger and leak passes.

**Sample calls:** Ace Right Duo R; Trey Left Flash Duo R; 13 Nub Duo L

## 9.3 POWER

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**       | **Base Formations**                        |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 3-7                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 6OL | Ace, Pro, I, Wing, Trey, Heavy, Unbalanced |

**Purpose:** Downhill gap run with a puller creating a defined point of attack.

**Core rules:** Frontside down blocks protect interior gaps. Backside guard is primary puller unless weekly variant changes it. Kick or log the force defender by rule; lead through first inside color. The back reads the puller and the created gap, not open grass before the block declares.

**Mature complements:** FLASH or Jet can move the force player; Counter and Power Pass punish flow; 6OL can create a second surface; CHIP/PA off Power can produce vertical shots.

**Sample calls:** Ace Right Power R; 12 Wing Zip Power L; 6OL Heavy Power R

## 9.4 COUNTER / GT

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                    | **Base Formations**                     |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 4-8                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 20 selected, 6OL | Wing, Trey, Pro, Heavy, Gun Strong, Nub |

**Purpose:** Misdirection gap family that punishes fast flow and overplayed Duo/Power/motion.

**Core rules:** Back takes controlled counter step. Guard kicks/logs; tackle or H wraps according to variant. Frontside protects inside gaps before climbing. GT remains the normal two-puller expression when tackle movement is available.

**Mature complements:** RETURN and FLASH away from the run are major mature complements. Counter Boot, Counter Post-Cross, Counter Screen, and Counter Read Glance all live off the same flow.

**Sample calls:** Wing Left Return Counter R; Trey Right GT L; 13 Over Counter R

## 9.5 OUTSIDE ZONE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**           | **Base Formations**             |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------------|
| CORE     | 4-9                  | 11, 12, 13 selected, 21 | Ace, Pro, Wing, Trey, I, Pistol |

**Purpose:** Stretch the front, make the defense run, then cut decisively.

**Core rules:** Reach/covered-uncovered rules by surface. Back presses outside leg of TE/tackle and earns the cutback by pursuit. The offense does not bounce simply because the defense is difficult inside.

**Mature complements:** JET/FLASH/RETURN can widen force. Boot/Flood and split action punish pursuit. Pin-Pull is the alternative when reach leverage is poor.

**Sample calls:** Ace Right Outside R; Trey Left Flash Outside L; Pro Right Outside L

## 9.6 SPLIT ZONE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**                 |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| CORE     | 4-7                  | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Wing, Trey, Ace, Gun Strong, Pistol |

**Purpose:** Inside-zone track with a split-flow Y/H/F controlling the backside edge.

**Core rules:** OL blocks Inside Zone. Split player crosses behind the line and executes SLICE, SIFT, BLUFF, ARC, LEAK, or CHIP-release according to the tag. Back maintains normal inside-zone track.

**Mature complements:** The mature family is valuable because the split player can block, bluff, arc for KEEP, leak, or chip/release from the same action.

**Sample calls:** Wing Right Split L; Trey Left Split Bluff R; 12 Ace Split Leak

## 9.7 INSIDE ZONE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**      | **Base Formations**                                 |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| CORE     | 4-6                  | 10, 11, 12, 20, 21 | Doubles, Trips, Trey, Gun Strong, 20 Spread, Pistol |

**Purpose:** Stable zone answer that supports ACCESS, READ, PRESS, and spread spacing.

**Core rules:** Covered/uncovered zone rules. Back presses A/B landmark and makes one decisive cut. Do not abandon the interior before making the second level fit it.

**Mature complements:** Access Bubble/Now, Read Glance, Split tag, and Draw/PA complements make Inside useful even when it is not the weekly identity run.

**Sample calls:** Gun Trey Inside R; Doubles Inside L; 20 Exit Inside R

## 9.8 PIN-PULL

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**       | **Base Formations**                  |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-9                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 6OL | Trey, Wing, Heavy, Unbalanced, Bunch |

**Purpose:** Perimeter run that replaces difficult reach blocks with down blocks and pullers.

**Core rules:** Covered linemen pin inside; uncovered linemen pull. First puller handles force, second turns up on support. Back stays behind pullers long enough for leverage, then gets vertical.

**Mature complements:** Condensed receivers create legal crack angles; motion can move force; Toss/Crack and screen complements keep the perimeter family from becoming one look.

**Sample calls:** Trey Right Pin-Pull R; 13 Wing Pin-Pull L; 6OL Over Pin-Pull R

## 9.9 TOSS / CRACK

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**                 |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-9                  | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Pro, I, Wing, Trey, Nub, Unbalanced |

**Purpose:** Get the ball to the edge with angles and make support defeat blocks in space.

**Core rules:** Back gains width without drifting too deep. Receivers stalk or crack legally by alignment and angle. Guard/T6 may lead by weekly plan. Force must be identified before the snap.

**Mature complements:** Motion away can hold support; toss fake can feed Counter or play action; crack surface can be shown without actually tossing.

**Sample calls:** Pro Right Toss R; Trey Left Toss Crack L; 13 Nub Flash Toss R

## 9.10 DART / TACKLE WRAP

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                | **Base Formations**        |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-7                  | 11, 12, 13, 20 selected, 6OL | Doubles, Trey, Wing, Heavy |

**Purpose:** Use tackle movement to create a gap/pull answer without relying on the guard every time.

**Core rules:** Backside tackle pulls and wraps to declared second-level target while interior linemen down/block back. The package is active only when tackle movement skill and front structure justify the practice cost.

**Mature complements:** DART can pair with FLASH away, Access, or play action. It is never installed simply because it is fashionable.

**Sample calls:** Gun Trey Dart R; 12 Wing Flash Dart L

## 9.11 LEAD / INSERT

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                      | **Base Formations**      |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 3-7                  | 21, 20, 12/13 with H, 6OL selected | I, Pro, Wing, Ace, Heavy |

**Purpose:** Create a second-level collision with F/H/extra body and force the defense to fit downhill.

**Core rules:** OL bases/combos down linemen; lead player inserts on declared linebacker or fit player. Back follows the created path with one decisive cut.

**Mature complements:** Insert can be paired with Duo/Zone; F can bluff insert and release; 21 can shift to spread so the defense cannot treat the package as old I-formation football.

**Sample calls:** I Right Lead R; Ace Insert Duo L; 21 Wing Insert Zone R

## 9.12 TRAP / WHAM

| **Tier**  | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**               |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| CHANGE-UP | 3-6                  | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Ace, Pro, Wing, Heavy, Gun Strong |

**Purpose:** Use penetration against the defense and change the blocking angle on an interior problem player.

**Core rules:** Trap leaves target initially unblocked for a puller. Wham releases the target for Y/H/F to block from an unexpected angle. Back path is tight and immediate.

**Mature complements:** Best as a targeted weekly answer, not a volume identity. PA/Leak can punish defenders who begin squeezing the action.

**Sample calls:** Pro Right Trap L; 13 Wing Wham R

## 9.13 JET / FLASH SWEEP

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**               | **Base Formations**               |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-10                 | 10, 11, 12, 20, 21 selected | Trips, Trey, Wing, Doubles, Bunch |

**Purpose:** Give real perimeter touches to fast motion so the defense must honor the movement.

**Core rules:** Motion player reaches practiced exchange landmark at speed. Interior action holds linebackers. Perimeter blocking is taught like a real run, not a gadget. The same motion must also produce non-touch calls.

**Mature complements:** Flash Duo, Jet Power, counter away, screen away, and PA all prevent the motion from becoming a handoff tell.

**Sample calls:** Trey Right Jet L; Doubles Flash Sweep R

## 9.14 DRAW

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                        | **Base Formations**               |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-8                  | 10, 11, 20, selected Empty-motion-in | Doubles, Trips, Spread, 20 Spread |

**Purpose:** Use pass posture and rush lanes against pressure-oriented or light boxes.

**Core rules:** OL shows pass set before engaging lanes. Back delays enough to let second level expand, then gets vertical. Quarterback sells drop without making the exchange late.

**Mature complements:** Lead Draw and motion-to-draw can keep pressure packages honest. Draw is not forced against muddy interior fronts.

**Sample calls:** Gun Doubles Draw; 20 Spread Lead Draw

## 9.15 QB SNEAK / FOLLOW

| **Tier**    | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**       | **Base Formations**             |
|-------------|----------------------|---------------------|---------------------------------|
| SITUATIONAL | 1-3+                 | 11, 12, 13, 21, 6OL | Under-center compressed / heavy |

**Purpose:** Win inches with minimum exchange risk.

**Core rules:** Center/guards create immediate vertical displacement. Quarterback follows declared crease. Follow adds legal back/TE support according to current rules and weekly coaching.

**Mature complements:** Freeze cadence and formation variation prevent the sneak from becoming automatic unless the defense concedes it.

**Sample calls:** Heavy Sneak; 13 Follow

| **Structure**           | **Preferred Answers**                                   | **Cautions**                                                                       |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| Big nickel vs heavy     | Duo/Power/Counter at the DB fit; play action behind him | Do not assume "nickel" is small if safeties are built to fit.                      |

## 9.18 Back Tracks

- **Duo:** press interior doubles and make the declared fitter choose the wrong gap.

- **Power:** downhill to inside hip of puller, press until kick/log declares.

- **Counter:** controlled counter step, then get downhill behind pullers.

- **Inside Zone:** press frontside A/B landmark, one decisive cut based on down-lineman/linebacker flow.

- **Outside Zone:** press outside leg of TE/tackle, stretch pursuit, plant when leverage is won.

- **Split Zone:** same as Inside Zone; do not chase split-flow player with your eyes.

- **Pin-Pull/Toss:** stay behind lead leverage, then get north before sideline becomes extra defender.

- **DART:** follow wrap path but cut inside/out based on linebacker reaction, not predetermined bounce.

# 10. MATURE PASS GAME

The pass game remains concept-based. Each concept is a set of jobs and spacing relationships that can be performed by different bodies. The mature system prefers concepts that survive personnel changes, motion, condensed releases, and protection adjustments without changing the quarterback's core picture.

## 10.1 Mature Pass Hierarchy

| **Tier**    | **Families**                                                                 |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY    | Drive / Y-Cross / Dagger / Mesh / Spacing / Choice                           |
| CORE        | Stick / Levels / Flood-Sail / Four Verticals-Switch / Post-Cross             |
| WEEKLY      | Shallow / Texas-Angle / Mills-Post Dig / Snag-Smash / Slant-Flat / Curl-Flat |
| CHANGE-UP   | Whip/Pivot / Scissors / Out-and-Up / special bunch rubs                      |
| SITUATIONAL | Goal-line pick/return, Hail Mary, two-point specials                         |

## 10.2 STICK

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**      | **Base Formations**         |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| CORE     | 4-7                  | 11, 12, 20, 21, 10 | Doubles, Trips, Trey, Empty |

**Purpose:** Quick zone/man leverage concept built around a stick/sit player, flat control, and outside spacing.

**Core rules:** Quarterback works leverage on the stick/flat defender. Receiver sits versus space and breaks away from leverage by installed rule. Outside receiver preserves vertical or hitch control.

**Mature usage:** Access-friendly, PRESS-friendly, and useful from 12/21 after spread conversion. Duo Read Stick uses the same leverage language.

## 10.3 SPACING

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                   | **Base Formations**                |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 4-8                  | 10, 11, 12, 20, 21, selected 13 | Doubles, Trips, Empty, Bunch, Trey |

**Purpose:** Efficient zone-spacing concept that creates multiple short windows without forcing the ball underneath one defender.

**Core rules:** Eligible players occupy separate underneath windows with clear leverage rules. QB does not stare at one stationary receiver; he distributes by expansion and collision.

**Mature usage:** FLASH/EXIT can change apex leverage. Static companion is mandatory. Excellent against split-safety zone and PRESS.

## 10.4 SLANT-FLAT

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**  |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|----------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 5-9                  | 10, 11, 12, 20 | Doubles, Trips, Trey |

**Purpose:** Fast horizontal/inside-out stress on corner, apex, or linebacker.

**Core rules:** Slant wins inside leverage; flat controls outside. QB throws off immediate leverage and pressure. Backside route is alert or hold concept.

**Mature usage:** Pairs naturally with Access, Inside Zone, and condensed motion. Reduced when defenses squat or rob inside lanes.

## 10.5 SNAG / SMASH

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**                     |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|-----------------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 6-12                 | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Bunch, Trey, Trips, Red-zone compressed |

**Purpose:** Triangle/high-low family that controls flat/corner defenders.

**Core rules:** Snag uses sit/corner/flat relationship. Smash uses hitch/corner high-low. QB reads leverage and cap, not route names in isolation.

**Mature usage:** Strong red-zone and boundary answer; motion/stack can help releases but static versions remain.

## 10.6 MESH

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                   | **Base Formations**                |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 6-12                 | 10, 11, 12, 20, 21, selected 13 | Doubles, Bunch, Trips, Empty, Trey |

**Purpose:** Man/zone crossover concept built around two shallow crossers and a high/flat answer.

**Core rules:** Crossers maintain 3-5 yard depth and rub legally through spacing rather than intentional picks. QB identifies man/zone reaction post-snap and works matchup/high-low accordingly.

**Mature usage:** Bunch/stack/FLASH releases create clean starts. SWITCH/BOX reactions are tracked. Mesh remains one of the best portable concepts in the book.

## 10.7 DRIVE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**               | **Base Formations**                |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------------------|------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 8-15                 | 11, 12, 20, 21, 13 selected | Doubles, Trey, Trips, Bunch, Empty |

**Purpose:** Dig + shallow family that creates a vertical stretch on underneath defenders.

**Core rules:** One player clears, one owns the dig, one the shallow, one flat/control, one outlet/protection. QB reads underneath displacement into the dig/shallow window.

**Mature usage:** Stone's best concept-portability example. FLASH/RETURN can change which body owns shallow/flat without changing the progression.

## 10.8 SHALLOW

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**      | **Base Formations**          |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 6-12                 | 10, 11, 12, 20, 21 | Doubles, Trips, Bunch, Empty |

**Purpose:** Low cross with intermediate in-breaker and vertical clear.

**Core rules:** Shallow stays on its rail. Intermediate route works behind underneath flow. QB takes shallow versus pressure/man or works behind it versus zone.

**Mature usage:** Useful as PRESS and pressure answer; reduced when spacing overlaps with Drive/Mesh in the weekly plan.

## 10.9 Y-CROSS

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**           |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 10-18                | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Trey, Wing, Ace, Trips, Heavy |

**Purpose:** Vertical clear plus deep crossing route with backside/flat control.

**Core rules:** Cross stays behind linebackers and under cap. QB reads safety/leverage and progression to cross, intermediate control, then outlet.

**Mature usage:** One of Stone's most durable Coryell-rooted concepts. Motion widens collision players; CHIP can protect the edge without removing Y from the concept when another player owns the cross.

## 10.10 LEVELS

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**               | **Base Formations**         |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| CORE     | 7-14                 | 11, 12, 20, 21, 13 selected | Trips, Trey, Doubles, Empty |

**Purpose:** Layered in-breakers attack zone spacing at multiple depths.

**Core rules:** Routes maintain vertical separation. QB identifies which underneath defender is forced to choose between levels. Do not let both in-breakers collapse into one window.

**Mature usage:** Promoted during the split-safety era and remains a high-confidence 2025-26 answer.

## 10.11 DAGGER

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**            |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 10-20                | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Trips, Trey, Bunch, Ace action |

**Purpose:** Vertical clear/seam with deep dig behind it.

**Core rules:** Clear route removes or pins cap defender. Dig enters behind underneath level at 15-18. QB confirms protection and throws dig into created window rather than chasing seam unless tagged/alerted.

**Mature usage:** Duo Action Dagger is a mature centerpiece. CHIP/SORT variants protect the development time.

## 10.12 SAIL / FLOOD

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**            |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| CORE     | 9-17                 | 11, 12, 13, 21 | Trips, Trey, Wing, Boot, Bunch |

**Purpose:** Three-level stretch to one side: vertical/corner, intermediate out, flat.

**Core rules:** QB reads deep-to-short or leverage-defined progression based on movement. Flat player must not climb into intermediate window.

**Mature usage:** DRIFT/FLASH can alter flat defender. Boot Flood remains a core movement concept.

## 10.13 TEXAS / ANGLE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                     | **Base Formations**                      |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 6-12                 | 11, 20, 21, 12 with qualified Y/F | Doubles, Trey, 20 Spread, Pro motion-out |

**Purpose:** Back/F angle route attacks linebacker leverage inside.

**Core rules:** Clear space with outside/inside route relationship. Runner stems out then breaks inside underneath. QB must confirm traffic and protection.

**Mature usage:** Excellent when Stone rotates multiple backs because the defense cannot assume the entering back is only a runner.

## 10.14 MILLS / POST-DIG

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**       | **Base Formations**            |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 12-25+               | 11, 12, 13 selected | Doubles, Trips, Trey, heavy PA |

**Purpose:** Post over dig stresses middle safety and robber rules.

**Core rules:** Post threatens cap; dig attacks vacated intermediate window. QB throws post only when leverage and protection justify it.

**Mature usage:** Pairs with SPIN/rotation tendencies and heavy play-action. Still a reasoned shot, not a random vertical call.

## 10.15 FOUR VERTICALS / SWITCH VERT

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**           | **Base Formations**                |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------|------------------------------------|
| CORE     | 15-30+               | 10, 11, 12, 13 FLEX, 20 | Doubles, Trips, Trey, Empty, Bunch |

**Purpose:** Stretch deep coverage with seams and outside verticals.

**Core rules:** Receivers preserve lane spacing. Seam can BEND by installed cap rule. SWITCH release changes who owns outside/inside lane without changing the quarterback's high-level read.

**Mature usage:** Fast motion can provide running start, but static verticals must remain credible. Protection and cap structure determine whether this is a true shot or seam-access concept.

## 10.16 POST-CROSS

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                | **Base Formations**         |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| CORE     | 15-30+               | 11, 12, 13, 21, 6OL selected | Ace, Trey, Wing, Heavy, Pro |

**Purpose:** Play-action/shot family combining post and deep cross behind run reaction.

**Core rules:** Post controls cap, cross works behind second-level fit, outlet replaces vacated flat/underneath space. Protection is part of the call.

**Mature usage:** Counter and heavy personnel are primary mature launch families.

## 10.17 CHOICE

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**                              | **Base Formations**                 |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| IDENTITY | 6-13                 | 11, 12 FLEX, 20, 21 MOVE, selected 13 FLEX | Doubles, Trips, Empty, Stack, Bunch |

**Purpose:** Qualified receiver chooses break by leverage using shared QB/receiver rules.

**Core rules:** Only players who have mastered leverage rules own Choice. The route is not "get open however you want." QB and receiver must see the same defender and same leverage.

**Mature usage:** A mature answer to two-high zone and matchup coverage; motion can create cleaner leverage but cannot replace route discipline.

## 10.18 CURL-FLAT

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel** | **Base Formations**  |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|----------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 7-12                 | 11, 12, 21    | Doubles, Trey, Trips |

**Purpose:** High-low the flat defender with curl settling behind underneath width.

**Core rules:** Curl works to 10-12 and returns downhill; flat controls width. QB throws by defender expansion.

**Mature usage:** Useful when defenses widen aggressively to motion or quick game, but often reduced if Spacing/Stick already solve the week.

## 10.19 WHIP / PIVOT

| **Tier**  | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**   | **Base Formations**          |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| CHANGE-UP | 5-10                 | 10, 11, 12 FLEX | Bunch, Stack, Doubles, Empty |

**Purpose:** Man-leverage breaker that sells inside before returning out or vice versa.

**Core rules:** Receiver wins with body control and exact landmark; QB throws on break leverage. Do not call it into zone simply because the route looks clever.

**Mature usage:** Low-volume man answer, especially in red zone or third-and-medium.

## 10.20 Route Tags

| **Tag**      | **Meaning**                                                                |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ALERT POST   | Designated eligible takes shot post when leverage/protection satisfy rule. |
| BEND         | Vertical bends away from cap or into open middle by installed landmark.    |
| WHEEL / RAIL | Back/F turns vertical outside.                                             |
| SWITCH       | Two eligible players exchange release lanes.                               |
| LEAK         | Delayed eligible releases from block/action picture.                       |
| CHOICE       | Qualified receiver runs leverage-option family.                            |
| CORNER       | Designated route converts to corner while rest of concept remains.         |
| GO           | Designated player clears vertically.                                       |

## 10.21 Receiver Release Rules

- Against press, win the concept lane, not an arbitrary outside release.

- Condensed stacks and bunches use release spacing to avoid illegal picks and collisions.

- FLASH/SWITCH timing is rehearsed so receivers do not occupy the same lane at the snap.

- If the defense top-locks an inside break, receiver uses installed leverage conversion rather than freelance improvisation.

- Chip/release players accept later timing and must not drift into another receiver's landmark.

## 10.22 Catch and Run Rules

- Quick-game receiver gets vertical when leverage permits; do not turn five free yards into a sideways race.

- Crossers secure the catch before outrunning pursuit.

- Backs on screens/angles use blockers and landmarks rather than immediately bouncing to sideline.

- Boundary awareness is part of two-minute and four-minute coaching, not an afterthought.

# 11. PLAY-ACTION AND MOVEMENT PASSES

Play action must resemble a real run in the mature system. The line, back track, motion, and surface should all belong to a run the defense has had to respect. Stone does not call theatrical fakes that the offense never actually runs.

## 11.1 Core Action Families

| **Run Action**   | **Primary Pass Families**                   | **Defensive Rule Attacked**                                        |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Duo              | Dagger, Leak, Choice, Post-Dig              | Linebacker/safety fit into interior doubles.                       |
| Power            | Post-Cross, Flood, TE leak, shot            | Puller/force reaction and downhill fit.                            |
| Counter / GT     | Post-Cross, Counter Boot, deep Over, screen | Fast flow to pullers and backside support.                         |
| Outside Zone     | Boot Flood, Slide, Cross, throwback         | Pursuit and flat/edge chase.                                       |
| Split Zone       | Leak, Boot, Cross, Read constraint          | Backside edge and split-flow reaction.                             |
| Jet / Flash      | Flood, Choice, vertical, screen away        | Perimeter support and motion communication.                        |
| Heavy / 13 / 6OL | Max shot, Dagger, Cross, Leak               | Base personnel, run-fit posture, and reduced coverage flexibility. |

## 11.2 Shot Discipline

- Shot must have a protection answer.

- Shot must have a coverage/fit reason or a prior setup.

- If the defense refuses the run fake and stays deep, quarterback takes intermediate or outlet answer.

- Heavy personnel does not automatically mean max protection; some of the best heavy shots release three or four big bodies into routes.

- CHIP can preserve five-man route structure better than keeping the TE in all play.

## 11.3 Boot / Slide

Boot and slide remain movement tools for a semi-mobile pocket quarterback. The goal is not to turn him into a runner. Change the launch point, simplify the half-field picture, slow backside pursuit, and create flat/cross/corner spacing.

- Outside Zone remains the cleanest normal boot action.

- Split Zone can create leak/slide variants.

- Counter Boot is a weekly misdirection answer.

- Quarterback has run/throw boundary only when the edge is clean and game situation permits it.

- TURNBACK protection and edge-control rules are practiced with the exact launch point.

## 11.4 Fitter-Replacement Shots

A major mature rule from the split-safety era survives: when a safety/nickel must enter the run fit, the offense builds routes into the space he leaves. Stone does not simply "throw deep versus two-high." He identifies the player who has to repair the box and attacks his movement with Dagger, Cross, Levels, Choice, or Mills.

# 12. SCREEN AND PERIMETER GAME

Screens are constraint calls, pressure answers, and ways to give different players touches without forcing the base run game through one back. The mature screen menu is smaller than the old catalog but each family has clear blocking and setup rules.

## 12.1 Core Screen Families

| **Family**              | **Expected Yardage** | **Primary Use**                                                      |
|-------------------------|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| NOW / SMOKE             | 4-8                  | Take free cushion, punish soft corners, Access companion.            |
| BUBBLE / FLASH BUBBLE   | 5-10                 | Numbers/leverage outside; motion speed can help but is not required. |
| WR TUNNEL / JAIL        | 6-12                 | Invite rush/coverage depth and bring blockers inside-out.            |
| RB SLOW / SLIP          | 7-13                 | Pressure answer; strongest when rushers widen or chase action.       |
| RB MIDDLE               | 7-14                 | Punish aggressive interior rush and mugged backers.                  |
| TE DELAY                | 6-12                 | Use heavy/chip picture to release behind rush.                       |
| SCREEN AWAY FROM MOTION | 6-13                 | Punish defenses that over-shift support toward FLASH/JET/RETURN.     |

## 12.2 Screen Blocking Rules

- Every screen has a legal release count for linemen. Do not improvise downfield timing.

- Perimeter receivers understand stalk/crack responsibility before the ball is snapped.

- Quarterback sells normal drop/action long enough to influence rush but not long enough to invite contact.

- Back or receiver catches behind leverage and follows the designed wall before cutting.

- Screen is checked or killed when the defense has the exact numbers/technique designed to destroy it.

## 12.3 Screen Sequencing

| **Show**                    | **Screen / Constraint**                                |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| FLASH / Jet toward strength | RB Slip or WR screen away.                             |
| Heavy 13 / 6OL protection   | TE Delay or RB Middle.                                 |
| Repeated dropback from 11   | RB Slow / Tunnel.                                      |
| Access throws outside       | Fake Now / inside run or double-move weekly change-up. |
| Counter pull action         | Counter screen / throwback.                            |

## 12.4 Gadgets

Flea flicker, reverse, throwback, receiver pass, and double-screen ideas live in the change-up folder. The weekly staff carries very few. They are selected because the opponent has created a specific tendency, not because Stone needs a gadget quota.

# 13. ACCESS, READ, AND KEEP PACKAGES

The mature system keeps three decision families separate so the quarterback's mental burden stays clean.

| **Family** | **Decision Timing**  | **Normal Question**                                                                      |
|------------|----------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ACCESS     | Pre-snap             | Is the perimeter throw free enough to replace the called run?                            |
| READ       | Post-snap            | Can one declared defender correctly defend both run and pass component?                  |
| KEEP       | Post-snap / run mesh | Does the optional quarterback run create a real numbers advantage with this quarterback? |

## 13.1 ACCESS

- Now / Smoke / Hitch / Bubble / quick Slant / fast Out by leverage.

- Quarterback decides before the snap unless the call specifically uses a post-snap READ.

- Access does not change OL run rules.

- If the defense crowds the throw, hand the called run unless the offense has checked to another call.

- Access volume is situational; Stone is willing to take repeated free throws until the defense spends a defender to remove them.

## 13.2 Mature READ Master Menu

| **READ Family**     | **Run**             | **Pass Conflict**         | **Typical Key**           |
|---------------------|---------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Inside Read Glance  | Inside Zone         | Glance / slant behind fit | Apex / backside LB        |
| Split Read Slant    | Split Zone          | Slant / stick             | Backside overhang         |
| Duo Read Stick      | Duo                 | Stick / sit               | Second-level fitter       |
| Outside Read Bubble | Outside Zone        | Bubble                    | Force / apex              |
| Counter Read Glance | Counter             | Glance                    | Backside fitter           |
| Power Read Flat     | Power               | Flat / pop                | Force / second-level edge |
| Duo Read Pop        | Duo                 | TE pop/seam               | Safety or fit replacement |
| Flash Read Choice   | Duo/Inside selected | Choice / quick in-out     | Motion-adjusted apex      |

The master menu is larger than the weekly menu. A normal week activates roughly three to five READs. One call has one primary conflict defender and one practiced fallback if motion or rotation changes the picture.

## 13.3 Safety-Cap Rule

If the pass component runs directly into an intact safety cap, the READ is not "throw because the linebacker stepped up." The quarterback confirms that the safety cannot erase the route. When cap leverage closes the throw, the ball stays with the run or progresses to the installed fallback.

## 13.4 Motion-Key Transfer Rule

Fast motion can move the intended key. The quarterback is not allowed to invent a new defender after the snap. Each motion-key READ has one declared transfer rule, such as "if nickel travels, key the replacing safety" or "if defense pushes and maintains shell, hand the run." If the defense presents an unpracticed exchange, default to the base run or kill the call before the snap.

## 13.5 KEEP

KEEP remains optional. The baseline quarterback does not need to be a designed runner. When the roster has a quarterback whose legs materially change the math, Stone can activate a small menu: Split Keep, Zone Keep, Counter Keep, and selected short-yardage follow. The menu expands only if the player is durable, decisive, and worth the exposure.

## 13.6 Guardrails

- No universal RPO attachment.

- No post-snap "read the whole defense" instruction.

- No fast motion added to a READ if timing prevents the quarterback from seeing the key.

- No KEEP call simply because the quarterback can scramble.

- No throwing into a capped window just because the run fitter moved.

# 14. 6OL AND HEAVY-PERSONNEL SYSTEM

The mature heavy system is one of Stone's clearest fingerprints. By 2025 the league has moved toward more multiple-TE, fullback, and extra-lineman offense, but Stone's heavy-personnel approach has been developing for years: get bigger without giving the defense a clean run/pass answer.

## 14.1 6OL Roles

| **T6 Role** | **Description**                                                                     |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| SURFACE     | Attached extra gap, normal heavy run/pass surface.                                  |
| OVER        | Tackle-over / unbalanced alignment changes strength and count.                      |
| PULL        | T6 or normal tackle becomes puller/wrapper in selected Power/Counter/Pin-Pull/DART. |
| PROTECT     | Extends edge in SOLID/MAX or creates chip wall.                                     |
| ELIGIBLE    | Reports eligible and can run narrowly practiced leak/flat/seam route.               |
| DECOY       | Creates heavy declaration, then offense shifts or motions to different picture.     |

## 14.2 Core 6OL Run Menu

- Duo

- Power

- Counter / GT

- Outside Zone

- Pin-Pull

- Toss-Crack

- Insert / Lead

- DART / tackle wrap

- Sneak / Follow

## 14.3 Core 6OL Pass Menu

- Heavy play-action Post-Cross;

- Duo Action Dagger;

- Boot/Flood from tackle-over or unbalanced surface;

- TE/Y/F leak behind max-look protection;

- quick Access throw when defense overpacks the box;

- selected eligible T6 flat/leak when declared and practiced;

- screen away from the heavy surface.

## 14.4 13 + 6OL Boundary

13 personnel and 6OL can both create heavy surfaces, but they solve different problems. 13 preserves three eligible tight ends and better spread conversion. 6OL gives more true blocking mass and protection. The weekly plan chooses the body that creates the better mismatch; Stone does not carry both at maximum volume merely to look multiple.

## 14.5 14 / Seven-Lineman Specialty

A four-tight-end or seven-lineman structure can exist as a short-yardage, goal-line, or specific matchup package if the active roster supports it. It is not a mature base package. The system refuses to install a body grouping that cannot carry at least one credible pass or misdirection answer.

## 14.6 Heavy Personnel Decision Tree

1. If defense substitutes base: detach/flex, use Choice/Cross/PA, and attack linebackers in space.

2. If defense stays nickel/big nickel: run at the fit and force the extra DB to tackle blocks.

3. If defense uses big safeties and still fits the run well: create more width, Empty conversion, and chip-release passing.

4. If defense over-shifts to the heavy surface: run/throw away from strength or use counter/misdirection.

5. If defense treats 6OL as automatic run: take Access, leak, boot, or max-protect shot when protection and leverage agree.

# 15. SITUATIONAL OFFENSE

## 15.1 First and Second Down

- Use personnel early to learn matching rules.

- Keep identity runs available from both heavy and light groups.

- Use Access and quick game to prevent free extra fitters.

- Call play action from runs the defense has actually had to fit.

- Avoid creating third-and-long merely to preserve a run/pass ratio.

## 15.2 Third-and-1 to 2

- Sneak/Follow when interior leverage is favorable.

- Duo / Power / Counter / Insert from multiple personnel pictures.

- Quick Choice, Stick, flat, or leak when defense overpacks the core.

- Motion is reduced if it delays the snap or creates illegal alignment risk.

- 4th-down thinking affects 3rd-down call aggressiveness when field position permits.

## 15.3 Third-and-3 to 5

- Spacing, Stick, Mesh, Drive, Choice, Snag, Texas/Angle.

- PRESS from favorable personnel when defense cannot substitute.

- RB/TE chip-release can protect enough time without shrinking route count.

- QB scramble conversion rules matter because man coverage can vacate the middle.

## 15.4 Third-and-6+

- Drive, Dagger, Y-Cross, Levels, Mesh, Choice, selected screen.

- SORT/SCAN/CHIP plan must be explicit against simulated pressure.

- Empty is used only with a clear hot structure.

- Draw remains available when rush lanes and field position justify it.

- Four Verticals is a matchup/coverage call, not a desperation default.

## 15.5 Red Zone

| **Zone**         | **Primary Stress**                                                               |
|------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| High red (20-13) | Condensed crossers, Duo/Power PA, Choice, Flood, heavy-to-spread.                |
| Low red (12-6)   | Bunch/stack releases, Power/Duo, Split leak, TE/Y matchup, QB movement.          |
| Goal line (5-in) | Sneak/Follow, Power/Counter, 13/6OL surfaces, play-action leak, quick flat/pick. |

## 15.6 Backed-Up

- Secure operation first: clean cadence, protection, and ball security.

- Use Duo/Power/Inside/Split and defined quick game.

- Avoid slow-developing lateral calls that risk negative plays near the goal line.

- Play action is available when protection is trustworthy and field position can flip.

## 15.7 Two Minute

- 11, 10, 12 FLEX, and 20 can all function without huddle depending personnel.

- Prioritize Spacing, Stick, Drive, Mesh, Choice, sideline outs, and screens with clock awareness.

- PRESS language stays concept-based so the offense does not need a second two-minute offense.

- Substitution is minimized unless injury, matchup, or clock stoppage permits it.

- Boundary decisions are coached before the snap: first down, out of bounds, spike, or timeout logic.

## 15.8 Four Minute

- Use personnel variation to force the defense to keep multiple run fits alive.

- Duo/Power/Counter/Outside remain core; play-action keeps safeties honest.

- MILK cadence changes enough to prevent defensive jump timing.

- Rotation keeps backs, Y/F, and receivers fresh for blocking without forcing equal carries.

- Ball security and in-bounds finish outrank explosive hunting.

## 15.9 Fourth Down

The call sheet has a dedicated GO menu by distance and field zone. Stone does not simply reuse third-down calls.

| **Distance** | **Normal Menu**                                                                                   |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1            | Sneak/Follow, Duo, Power, Counter, quick flat/Choice, heavy leak.                                 |
| 2-3          | Duo/Power with Access, Mesh/Drive/Choice, boot flat/cross, selected READ.                         |
| 4-5          | Spacing/Drive/Mesh/Choice, bunch switch, Texas/Angle, screen if pressure tendency warrants.       |
| 6+           | Best protection + best concept, not automatic empty. Dagger/Y-Cross/Drive/Mesh/Levels by matchup. |

## 15.10 Two-Point Menu

- Bunch/stack Choice or whip;

- Heavy run with leak complement;

- Boot flat/cross;

- Duo Read Stick or selected READ;

- 20-personnel angle/wheel;

- one weekly special only if practiced at full speed.

## 15.11 Sudden Change and End-of-Half

- After turnover: one Alert shot is available if field position/protection support it, otherwise run the normal offense.

- End-of-half with few seconds: sideline, end-zone, or Hail Mary menu is explicit before the drive.

- No gadget is called merely because the moment feels dramatic.

# 16. DEFENSIVE RECOGNITION AND OFFENSIVE ANSWERS

Stone's defensive background still matters here. The offense does not need a defensive coordinator's entire playbook, but it must share language for fronts, shells, fit sources, match rules, pressure, and motion reactions.

## 16.1 Front Families

| **Front**                    | **Recognition**                                | **Primary Offensive Questions**                                                  |
|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Even / Over / Under          | Four down with normal 3-tech/1-tech spacing.   | Where is 3-tech? Which edge is force? Can Duo/Power create doubles?              |
| Odd                          | Nose + two ends / stand-up edges.              | How is center secured? Which backer is inserted?                                 |
| Mint / Tite                  | Interior techniques close B gaps, edges wider. | Can Outside/Pin-Pull create width? Is Duo clean? Who fits C/D gap?               |
| Bear                         | Interior OL covered.                           | Avoid forcing muddy interior; use perimeter, Counter away, Trap/Wham target, PA. |
| Five-man surface             | Five immediate LOS threats.                    | Count gaps, identify drop/insert possibility, use Access/PA.                     |

## 16.4 Pressure Families

- **MUG:** linebackers walked into interior gaps.

- **SIM / CREEPER:** defense shows more rushers than it sends; a traditional rusher may drop while a second-level defender replaces him.

- **OVERLOAD:** more threats than blockers to one side.

- **SLOT / SAFETY:** pressure from perimeter/secondary.

- **PEEL:** rusher converts to coverage on releasing back while another defender rushes.

- **GREEN DOG:** man defender rushes when his coverage responsibility stays in protection.

The protection call must identify the likely space of pressure. The quarterback does not receive credit for correctly naming a blitz if the offensive rule still leaves the replacement rusher free.

## 16.5 Big Nickel and Heavy-Personnel Response

Modern defenses can stay in nickel against 12/13/21 by using large safeties and hybrid defenders. Stone therefore never assumes a defensive back is a weak run player. The question is whether that defender can fit the extra gap while also handling the pass responsibility created by the heavy body.

## 17.4 Self-Scout Constraints

## 19.3 Position Mastery Tests

- Can set front/point/protection and identify shell/fitter without delaying cadence.

- Can describe every active READ key and fallback.

- Can operate PRESS without losing protection communication.

- Can identify when Access is free and when safety cap removes the throw.

- Can explain the next call Stone is likely to use after a defensive adjustment.

### Running Backs

- Master identity run tracks and at least one zone/perimeter family.

- Execute SCAN/SORT/CHIP-related protection responsibilities.

- Run screen and route package without creating substitution tells.

- Understand EXIT/FLASH timing when assigned.

### Tight Ends / F

# 20. SIMULATION DIRECTIVES FOR 2025-2026

This section is written for the simulation engine. It exists to prevent the mature book from being flattened into random play selection, rigid percentages, or artificial statistical balance.

## 20.1 Operating Season Selection

- **2025:** Use the full 2025-2026 Mature System. Heavy personnel, chip/release, 13/6OL, motion, and protection sorting are fully available.

- **2026:** Use the same mature master system unless the simulation creates a new coordinator influence, major roster-driven branch, firing, or later evolution. Do not invent future 2026 league outcomes.

- If Stone is fired before either season, stop applying future Stone evolution to the team that fired him unless his next job carries the system forward.

## 20.2 Call Generation Priority

1. Situation: down, distance, score, field zone, clock, possession strategy.

2. Weekly defensive map: personnel match, front, shell, fitter, pressure, edge problem.

3. Active personnel and player fatigue/availability.

4. Weekly concept tier and current game efficiency.

5. Formation width / surface.

6. Motion or static choice only if it serves a job.

7. Base concept.

8. Protection / Access / READ / tag adjustment if justified.

9. Expected Yardage profile and risk level.

10. Sequence relationship to previous and future calls.

Do not randomly choose a formation and then search for a concept that fits it.

## 20.3 Personnel Rules

- 11 is common but is not required to be a majority.

- 12, 21, or 13 may become the weekly center when defensive matching creates an advantage.

- 6OL is an overlay and can appear on normal downs.

- Heavy personnel must carry credible pass and spread answers.

- If the roster lacks three playable TEs, reduce 13 rather than inventing competent players.

- If a hybrid F is unavailable, 21 may become more traditional or shrink.

## 20.4 Rotation Rules

- Two or three backs may receive meaningful playing time.

- Playing time does not imply equal carries, equal targets, or alternating series.

- A productive/hot back can exceed his normal snap band.

- A second back can play many snaps through protection, motion, routes, screens, or decoy work with few carries.

- Receivers/TEs may also rotate aggressively when motion/blocking workload is high.

- Do not remove a player from a favorable matchup merely because a rotation was scheduled.

## 20.5 Run Distribution Rules

- Duo / Power / Counter-GT should account for a large share of identity run calls but not a fixed percentage.

- Zone family usage follows front and roster fit.

- Perimeter runs rise when support and edge leverage permit them.

- DART is active only if tackle movement is a real strength.

- Jet/Flash touches are real but not mandatory.

- When one runner is clearly producing better decisions or breaking tackles, Stone can feed him more without abandoning the rotation philosophy.

## 20.6 Pass Rules

- Core concept selection depends on coverage/match rules and protection, not only down-and-distance.

- Spacing/Stick can be repeated if the defense concedes efficient yards.

- Shots require trigger/protection/setup.

- Heavy personnel passing increases when defense answers with base.

- Choice is restricted to qualified players.

- No route family gets universal personnel portability if the roster cannot execute the jobs.

## 20.7 Motion Rules

- Motion is not mandatory on a target percentage.

- Fast motion must have a job and a static companion.

- Motion direction cannot reliably predict play direction.

- If timing errors rise, reduce motion before changing the core concept.

- Track defensive reaction language and use it to choose later calls.

- Do not treat TRAVEL as automatic man coverage.

## 20.8 Protection Rules

- Edge-rush quality can materially change personnel, CHIP, and protection selection.

- Use SORT/SCAN against simulated/mug structures rather than simply keeping more blockers in.

- CHIP can be frequent while preserving route release.

- Do not call slow-developing concepts repeatedly behind a protection matchup that is losing.

- If one lineman is struggling, LOCK/help can be used, but the offense should also change launch point, timing, or personnel rather than asking the same player to lose alone.

## 20.9 READ Rules

- Activate roughly three to five READ families in a normal week.

- One declared conflict defender per READ.

- Use safety-cap rule before throwing.

- Motion-key READs have a practiced transfer/fallback.

- If the defense presents an unpracticed exchange, default to base run/kill, not improvisation.

- No universal RPO attachment.

## 20.10 KEEP Rules

- KEEP is inactive or very small for a normal pocket-oriented quarterback.

- Expand only if the quarterback's athletic value is worth the hit exposure and practice time.

- Scrambles do not automatically justify designed KEEP volume.

- Late-game/short-yardage quarterback runs may exist even when the normal KEEP menu is small.

## 20.11 Human-System Rules

- Stone can make a bad call. The simulation does not need to portray perfect counter-selection.

- A concept can be cut after repeated failure even if it is theoretically sound.

- A coach can return to a call that previously failed if the defensive reaction changed.

- Weekly plans should contain asymmetry: some concepts have many presentations, some only one or two.

- Not every personnel group gets every concept.

- Player skill can override nominal system preference.

- Injuries can simplify the system rather than generating replacement competence automatically.

- Unexpected success can create a temporary tendency that later opponents attack.

- The offense should evolve through evidence, not because the simulator knows future NFL trends.

## 20.12 Expected Yardage Rules

- Expected Yardage is a planning band, not deterministic output.

- Down/distance conversion can make a play successful below its normal band.

- Explosives can exceed the band naturally.

- Negative plays remain possible based on execution, defense, and game context.

- Use the band to classify Short / Medium / Chunk / Shot, not to force statistical averages.

# APPENDIX C. HISTORICAL GROUNDING NOTES

These notes define the historical rails used to keep the mature system plausible. They do not mean Stone copies any one real coach or offense.

1. **Heavy personnel accelerated sharply in 2025.** NFL Next Gen Stats reported that offenses used personnel packages with fewer than three wide receivers on 41.7 percent of plays, the highest rate of the tracking era, and the league reached 44.5 percent heavy sub-package usage from Week 10 onward. This supports Stone allowing 12, 13, 21, fullback, and extra-lineman structures to carry major weekly volume without making 11 disappear.

2. **Heavy personnel worked because it created a personnel dilemma, not because it guaranteed rushing success.** Defenses played base more often in 2025, but the best offenses also threw efficiently when they forced those heavier defenders onto the field. Stone therefore treats heavy personnel as a question: if the defense gets big, spread or pass; if it stays small, run the fit.

3. **Multiple-tight-end passing reached a new high.** Next Gen Stats reported multiple TEs on 25.5 percent of pass plays in 2025, the highest rate it had tracked since 2016. This supports the mature 12/13 package and the requirement that big bodies can route, chip, and detach.

4. **Chip blocks became a major protection trend.** Next Gen Stats reported league-wide chip usage on 26.8 percent of pass plays in 2025, up from 23.9 percent in 2024 and 21.3 percent in 2023. It also found that chips delayed pressure arrival substantially. Stone therefore promotes CHIP from a useful tag into a mature weekly protection-route tool.

5. **Motion remained a league-wide language rather than one gimmick.** Next Gen Stats expanded motion classification for 2025 after league motion use had reached a new apex the prior season. Stone keeps his own Jet/Zip/Return/Flash/Drift/Exit terminology but treats timing and purpose as distinct variables.

# STONE 2025-2026 OFFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** physical downhill multiple offense + concept passing + personnel portability + late leverage + protection-aware sequencing.

**System sentence:** Change the problem. Keep the answer familiar.

**Identity runs:** Duo / Power / Counter-GT. Core complements: Outside / Split / Inside. Weekly perimeter/movement: Pin-Pull / Toss-Crack / Jet-Flash / DART / Insert.

**Identity passes:** Drive / Y-Cross / Dagger / Mesh / Spacing / Choice. Core complements: Stick / Levels / Flood / Four Verticals / Post-Cross.

**Personnel:** 11 is common, not sacred. 12/21/13 can be the weekly center. 20 is a true two-runner matchup package. 6OL is a complete run/pass overlay.

**Back principle:** two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time. Playing time does not imply equal carries, equal targets, or simultaneous backfield use.

**Motion:** Jet / Zip / Return / Orbit / Trade / Shift / Flash / Drift / Exit. Motion must change leverage, fit, spacing, release, pressure, or a later constraint. Major fast-motion series carry static companions.

**Motion reaction:** TRAVEL / PUSH / SWITCH / SPIN / STAY / BOX.

**Protection:** Scat / Half / Solid / Max / Turnback + Scan / Lock / Sort. CHIP is a mature weekly route-protection modifier.

**Decision football:** Access is pre-snap. Read is post-snap with one key. Keep is quarterback dependent.

**OL principle:** guards, tackles, and T6 are movement and surface tools. Heavy personnel cannot become a run tell.

**Tempo:** ROTATE to change bodies, HOLD to preserve the defensive matchup, PRESS to exploit it.

**Play-caller:** Ask early. Show -> Stress -> Punish. Return to the picture after the defense reveals its answer. Reduce the Sunday menu until execution is faster than the defense's communication.
