# Alex Stone Offensive Playbook

## 2023-2024 Evolution V: Motion and Leverage

> **Team-neutral evolution manual** | Document revision 5.0 | Primary play-caller: Alex Stone  
> **System sentence:** Change the leverage at the snap. Keep the concept recognizable.

This document is the fifth canonical evolution of Stone's offense. It is an evolution manual layered onto the 2013 Iteration I playbook and Evolutions II through IV. Unless this manual explicitly changes a rule, term, concept, protection, personnel relationship, or operating procedure, the latest prior rule remains in force.

Evolution V covers two seasons because the offense changes in two distinct steps. In 2023, Stone accelerates the pre-snap game and makes late motion a true leverage tool. In 2024, he corrects the predictable parts of that acceleration. Motion remains important, but the offense learns to punish switching, top-locking, pressure disguise, and defenses that use the motion itself as an offensive tell.

Stone remains the primary game-day play-caller. No coordinator branch is created here. Any future historical or fictional coordinator influence must occur in the simulation before it enters the canonical system.

# 1. EVOLUTION V IDENTITY AND CHANGE RULES

Evolution IV taught the offense to identify the shell, find the fitter, and punish the rotation. Evolution V asks a different question: can the offense change the location and timing of the conflict so late that the defense must solve it while the play is beginning?

That does not mean motion becomes the offense. The same Stone concepts survive. Duo is still Duo. Power is still Power. Drive is still Drive. Y-Cross is still Y-Cross. The motion can change the receiver's release, the support player's leverage, the width of the box, the point of the fit, or the defense's communication rule without changing the underlying concept.

The offense therefore becomes faster before the snap while trying to become simpler after it. A receiver may enter the snap with speed, but the quarterback should not have to memorize a new progression because of it. A back may exit the core late, but the line should still block the same run rule. A defender may travel, push, switch, or spin with motion, but the offense is trying to make one existing rule wrong rather than inventing a new play for every response.

## 1.1 The Eleven Evolution Laws

1. **Motion needs a football job.** It must change leverage, create speed, move a fit, force communication, declare pressure, or protect another call. If it does none of those things, remove it.
2. **Motion-at-snap is a timing tool, not a religion.** The offense may snap with a player moving, but it also must be able to win from the same formation while completely still.
3. **The same motion must produce different outcomes.** A fast receiver cannot always receive the ball, clear the edge, or run the same route. Motion to the right must be able to produce a run right, run left, pass right, pass left, screen, or shot.
4. **Do not confuse travel with man coverage.** Modern defenses can pass motion through zones, switch assignments, top-lock routes, or rotate after the snap. Motion tells the quarterback what rule the defense used, not automatically what coverage was called.
5. **Speed is useful only when the offense can control the clock.** Late motion that creates delay-of-game risk, illegal motion, or rushed protection communication is a bad trade.
6. **Static football is the constraint on motion football.** If the defense begins cheating toward the expected movement, line up in the same shell, do not move, and attack the new leverage.
7. **Physical runs remain first-class answers.** The motion can widen a nickel or safety, but Duo, Power, Counter, and Insert still punish the space that defender abandoned.
8. **Two-high does not guarantee low pressure.** A defense can preserve split-safety structure and still create simulated pressure or late blitzes. Protection rules must identify rush responsibility without assuming the shell predicts the rush.
9. **Personnel portability outranks personnel percentage.** Stone does not chase another coach's 11, 12, or 21 usage. He wants his best groupings to create multiple credible pictures.
10. **Playing time is not a touch quota.** Multiple backs and skill players can play meaningful snaps without forced carry equality, alternation, or simultaneous backfield use.
11. **The Sunday menu must stay smaller than the master system.** Evolution V increases the available presentation library, but the weekly call sheet should become more connected, not more crowded.

## 1.2 What Does Not Change

The following remain canonical:

- concept before formation;
- personnel changes the matchup, formation changes the picture;
- Stone can win under center, in pistol, or in shotgun;
- the quarterback is pocket-oriented and semi-mobile by default;
- Power, Counter/GT, and Duo are identity-level physical runs;
- Inside, Outside, and Split Zone remain stable complements;
- guards and tackles are movement tools;
- 6OL is not a short-yardage declaration;
- 12 and 21 personnel are real passing groups;
- two or three backs may receive meaningful playing time without an equal-carry rule;
- ACCESS is pre-snap, READ is post-snap, KEEP is quarterback dependent;
- expected yardage is a planning range rather than a promise;
- Show -> Stress -> Punish remains the play-caller's sequencing language;
- shots need a reason;
- motion may identify a rule but does not replace the quarterback's post-snap confirmation.

## 1.4 Human-System Rule

Evolution V contains a real coaching correction. The 2023 offense has weeks where Stone moves too much. Timing gets crowded. Receivers arrive late. The quarterback waits on motion that did not change anything. A defense begins passing the motion cleanly and stealing the expected opposite-side answer. A protection check becomes late because a receiver is still crossing the formation when the defense changes the front.

Stone does not answer those problems by abandoning motion. In 2024 he categorizes it better, cuts movements that do not change leverage, builds a no-motion answer beside every major fast-motion series, and makes the protection call more important than the aesthetic value of the movement.

The simulation must allow a 2023 package to be reduced or removed in 2024 even if it produced some explosives. Evolution is not a straight line.

# 2. 2023 ACCELERATION AND 2024 CORRECTION

## 2.1 2023: Accelerate the Leverage

The 2023 offense pushes a trend Stone has been building toward since Evolution II. Pre-snap movement becomes less about showing one formation and reaching another, and more about arriving at the snap with a defender still solving leverage.

The six 2023 priorities are:

1. install a late short-motion family that can create a running release without requiring a new pass concept;
2. pair that motion with Duo, Power, Counter, Outside, Split, and perimeter runs;
3. use motion to create cleaner releases for Choice, Drive, Mesh, Y-Cross, Flood, and vertical concepts;
4. add pressure answers that do not require the quarterback to identify every possible rusher before the snap;
5. increase the ability of 12, 21, and 20 personnel to reach spread pictures through motion rather than substitution;
6. make the same moving player useful as ball carrier, route runner, decoy, blocker, and coverage-control piece.

Stone does not try to be the league's motion leader by percentage. The objective is to force the defense to make a decision late while the offense continues to execute familiar concepts.

## 2.2 The 2023 Overcorrection

The aggressive 2023 form of the package creates four problems.

First, some motion becomes self-scoutable. When FLASH always precedes an outside release or a perimeter touch, the defense begins cheating the answer.

Second, defenders become better at passing motion. Corners and safeties can SWITCH or PUSH assignments instead of chasing across the formation. A motion designed to prove man coverage therefore becomes misleading if the quarterback treats travel as the only meaningful reaction.

Third, motion can damage offensive operation. Late movement compresses the communication window for the center, quarterback, and protection unit. It can also create illegal motion or delay problems when the player does not reach the correct landmark on time.

Fourth, a defense can use the offense's own movement as the trigger for pressure or rotation. Stone realizes that the motion itself may be telling the defense when to spin a safety, replace a nickel, or bluff one rusher while inserting another.

The correction is not less creativity. It is more discipline.

## 2.3 2024: Motion Without Dependence

The 2024 offense keeps the speed but changes the rules around it.

The seven 2024 priorities are:

- pair every major fast-motion series with a credible no-motion complement;
- make motion capable of producing plays both toward and away from the movement;
- distinguish TRAVEL, PUSH, SWITCH, and SPIN reactions instead of treating motion as a binary man/zone indicator;
- add SORT protection against simulated pressures and late second-level replacement rushers;
- use CHIP deliberately from condensed, 12, 21, and 13 personnel when one edge rusher is changing the protection plan;
- promote selected 13-personnel and 6OL spread conversions without making heavy personnel the league-wide center of the offense yet;
- narrow the weekly motion menu so the offense executes a few timing families at full speed rather than many at partial mastery.

By the end of 2024, Stone's offense can be motion-heavy without being motion-dependent.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

Personnel remains a method of changing the defensive problem. The 2023 league itself demonstrates that successful offenses can live at opposite personnel extremes. Stone therefore refuses to treat one grouping as modern and another as outdated.

## 3.1 Planning Centers, Not Quotas

| **Group** | **2023 Planning Center** | **2024 Planning Center** | **Primary Reason**                                                               |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 11        | 40-55%                   | 38-54%                   | Broadest pass menu, PRESS tempo, fast receiver motion, Empty conversion.         |
| 12        | 20-30%                   | 21-31%                   | Run/pass balance, chip help, condensed motion, Empty conversion, vertical seams. |
| 21        | 10-18%                   | 10-20%                   | Movable F, under-center force, split-flow, motion and spread conversion.         |
| 20        | 4-10%                    | 4-11%                    | Two-runner space package, EXIT/FLASH by a back, screen and matchup game.         |
| 13        | 2-7%                     | 3-10%                    | Heavy-to-pass, closed-surface stress, selected FLEX/EMPTY transformation.        |
| 10        | 2-8%                     | 2-8%                     | Two-minute, pure spread, pressure identification, selected matchups.             |
| True 00   | Rare                     | Rare                     | Specialist empty only when the active roster justifies it.                       |
| 6OL       | Weekly package           | Weekly package           | Surface, protection, tackle movement, motion presentation, run/pass deception.   |

These are planning centers. Score, injury, opponent matching rules, and roster quality can move the offense outside the bands.

## 3.2 11 Personnel

11 remains the largest normal grouping because it carries the broadest communication and tempo menu. It is not automatically the best grouping against every opponent.

11 must carry:

- Duo, Power, Counter/GT, Inside, Outside, Split, Pin-Pull, Toss/Crack, Jet;
- FLASH, RETURN, ZIP, JET, DRIFT, and EXIT motion where the eligible player can execute it;
- condensed, stack, bunch, wide, NUB, Trey, Trips, Doubles, and Empty pictures;
- Stick, Spacing, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Levels, Choice, Flood, Four Verticals, Post-Cross;
- ACCESS and selected READ;
- movement pass and boot;
- screen answers both toward and away from motion;
- PRESS tempo.

A 2024 defense that builds a specific switch package for 11-motion football can be answered by removing the motion, changing width, or leaving 11 entirely.

## 3.3 12 Personnel

12 becomes one of Stone's best answers to the modern defensive dilemma because it can create an extra gap without surrendering four-vertical or Empty potential.

The HEAVY/FLEX distinction remains a body-profile description, not a separate offense.

**12 HEAVY** favors two credible attached blockers, Power/Duo/Counter surfaces, chip help, seven-man protection, and under-center action.

**12 FLEX** favors at least one Y/H who can align detached, motion, run Choice/Cross/Seam, and convert the grouping to Empty or 3x1 without substitution.

By 2024 both versions must support:

- FLASH or DRIFT by the movable Y/H when the player can execute the path;
- EXIT to Empty or wide slot;
- Duo/Power/Outside/Split;
- Drive/Y-Cross/Levels/Choice;
- Flood/Post-Cross/Vert;
- CHIP or release-by-rule;
- READ Pop/Stick/Glance packages;
- screen or leak answer.

The offense does not pretend a blocking Y and a flex Y are the same athlete. The concept remains stable while job ownership changes.

## 3.4 21 Personnel

21 remains a Stone grouping because F is a role, not a historical fullback stereotype.

F may be:

- traditional fullback;
- H-back;
- second tight end;
- large tailback;
- second true runner;
- hybrid receiver/blocker.

The F must be able to perform more than one category of work. A one-job fullback substitution is a defensive tell.

In Evolution V, 21 must be capable of reaching:

- I and Offset I;
- Wing and Sniffer;
- Twins and Slot;
- Trey-like 3x1;
- condensed bunch;
- selected Empty;
- fast motion by F or Z;
- EXIT by a back;
- split-flow and lead action;
- Drive, Cross, Choice, Flood, play-action, and screen concepts.

If the defense responds to 21 with base personnel, Stone can spread it. If it stays nickel, Stone can create gap and surface stress. The grouping is valuable because it can do both.

## 3.5 20 Personnel

20 remains a true two-runner package, not a demand that both runners stand in the backfield.

Normal structures include:

- one back in the backfield, one at slot;
- split backs;
- one runner at F/wing;
- one runner in FLASH/EXIT motion;
- one back as protection/chip player while the other releases;
- Empty conversion with both runners detached.

The offense can call the same run family with R or F as the runner depending on alignment. This is not a carry alternation rule.

Two-runner playing time remains driven by player quality and matchup. One back can receive most carries while another plays substantial snaps as route, protection, screen, motion, or perimeter player.

## 3.6 13 Personnel

13 stays a change-up in 2023 and becomes a more serious weekly tool in selected 2024 plans.

The three-TE group must be able to create at least three of the following:

- heavy closed surface;
- balanced double-tight surface;
- TE wing/sniffer;
- one detached Y/H;
- two detached eligible players;
- Empty or near-Empty presentation;
- CHIP help with four eligible release threats;
- boot/leak;
- max-protect shot;
- Power/Counter/Duo/Outside from the same initial picture.

Stone does not carry 13 merely because the roster lists three tight ends. At least one must create a receiving or movement problem for the defense.

## 3.7 Running Back Room

The core rule is unchanged: two or three runners can receive meaningful playing time without a promised touch split.

Evolution V adds three operational requirements:

- at least two backs should be able to execute the base protection language whenever roster quality permits;
- any back used in fast motion must also carry a credible non-touch job from the same path;
- a back cannot be inserted only for screens, only for pass protection, or only for one outside run unless injury forces the issue.

A normal Stone game can produce one back with 18 carries, another with 7 carries and 5 targets, and a third with 12 snaps and 2 touches. It can also produce two backs near the same carry count. The playbook does not force either box score.

## 3.8 Skill-Position Rotation

Wide receivers and tight ends are cycled more aggressively than a static depth chart suggests when role overlap allows it. The reason is operational, not ceremonial freshness.

A substitute must be able to preserve the next call family. If Z2 enters, the defense should not know that Jet or a screen is coming. If Y2 enters, the offense should not lose the pass menu. If a speed receiver enters only for FLASH, the package has failed.

The game plan therefore identifies:

- which X/Z/H players can own FLASH;
- which can crack or insert on perimeter runs;
- which Y/H players can CHIP and release;
- which backs can EXIT or align at H;
- which players can preserve PRESS tempo after rotation.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, ROTATION, AND TEMPO

ROTATE, HOLD, and PRESS remain the three operational states. Evolution V changes how motion interacts with them.

## 4.1 ROTATE: Change Bodies, Then Test the Rule

Stone continues to rotate more than average when role overlap permits. ROTATE is most useful when the staff wants to learn how the defense matches 12, 21, 20, 13, or 6OL.

After the defensive personnel appears, the offense can use a shift or motion to determine how those defenders handle space. The substitution gets the bodies Stone wants; the motion tests the rule those bodies use.

## 4.2 HOLD: Preserve the Matchup, Change the Leverage

HOLD becomes even more valuable in Evolution V because the current eleven can create more snap pictures.

A held 12-personnel group may move from Ace to Trey to Empty. A held 21 group may move from I to Wing to Slot. A held 20 group may become 3x1 or Empty. FLASH/EXIT/RETURN allows Stone to stress the same defensive personnel without giving it a substitution window.

## 4.3 PRESS

PRESS does not automatically mean motion.

The 2023 offense experiments with more fast-motion PRESS calls. The 2024 rule is stricter: motion remains in PRESS only if the unit can align, identify protection, start the motion, and snap without speeding up the quarterback's communication.

The veteran PRESS menu may include:

- Duo / Inside / Split;
- Counter when the front communication is clean;
- Stick / Spacing / Slant-Flat;
- Mesh / Drive / Y-Cross;
- Choice;
- Dagger;
- Four Verticals;
- Smoke / Bubble / Now;
- RB Slip / Slow;
- one or two READ calls;
- FLASH Choice or FLASH Drive;
- one EXIT-to-Empty call;
- one alert shot.

If a motion call creates a late protection declaration, it leaves PRESS for that week.

## 4.4 Motion Burden Rule

Every fast-motion call creates an operational cost. The staff tracks:

- alignment error;
- motion-timing error;
- false start/illegal motion risk;
- play-clock stress;
- protection communication delay;
- receiver route-depth distortion;
- backfield mesh interference.

A motion package that creates explosive plays but also creates repeated operational errors can be reduced. Stone values repeatable offense more than novelty.

## 4.5 Long Drives and Rotation

The offense still uses rotation to keep multiple skill players involved over long drives. The presence of fast motion does not change the touch rule.

A back may leave after six snaps because protection and motion workload are high. Another may enter and immediately run the same Duo/Drive/Screen families. The substitution is not a preplanned carry transfer.

# 5. FORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Evolution V does not add dozens of formation names. It adds more ways for the existing formations to change leverage near the snap.

## 5.1 Formation Objectives

Every weekly formation must answer at least one of these questions:

- Can it create a new surface?
- Can it force a corner or nickel to change leverage?
- Can it hide which eligible owns the motion?
- Can it reach another picture without substitution?
- Can it create a clean fast-motion runway?
- Can it preserve a physical run from the same picture?
- Can it support the protection required by the pass family?

A formation that exists only to look different is removed.

## 5.2 Condensed Structure Remains Major

Condensed formations remain useful because they:

- create free outside grass;
- force corners to declare leverage;
- shorten crack/insert paths;
- help create crossing traffic;
- allow CHIP help without obvious alignment change;
- make FLASH and RETURN motion harder to pass cleanly;
- create both inside and outside release threats.

But 2024 adds a rule: condensed cannot become a universal starting picture. The defense must still see wide splits, open Trips, and true Empty so it cannot set every coverage and pressure rule to tight formations.

## 5.3 Formation Width + Motion Pairing

Width is now paired with motion timing.

| **Starting Width** | **Motion Objective**                                                             | **Common Answers**         |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Tight/Condensed    | Create running release, widen support, force switch communication.               | FLASH, RETURN, JET, DRIFT. |
| Normal             | Identify travel/push and change slot leverage.                                   | ZIP, RETURN, EXIT.         |
| Wide               | Compress late, change crack angle, create box stress.                            | RETURN, JET inward, TRADE. |
| Bunch/Stack        | Force switch/banjo rules and release a player cleanly.                           | FLASH out, RETURN, EXIT.   |
| NUB/Closed         | Move the support defender away from the isolated surface or bring him back late. | JET, RETURN, FLASH.        |

## 5.4 Bunch and Stack

Bunch and Stack become more than man-beaters. They are communication structures against match coverage.

The offense tracks whether the defense:

- BOXES the bunch;
- top-locks the point or fast-motion receiver;
- SWITCHES in/out routes;
- pushes the nickel/safety with motion;
- locks one matchup while zoning the others.

The response may be a run, not another route combination.

## 5.5 Empty and EXIT

EXIT gives Stone a faster way to create Empty or near-Empty without a full shift.

Normal EXIT jobs:

- move R or F from the core to the slot;
- force a linebacker to widen;
- identify whether a safety or nickel replaces him;
- create immediate Access numbers;
- create a lighter interior box for Duo/Draw from a return alignment;
- create Choice or screen leverage.

The quarterback must complete the protection declaration before relying on the EXIT picture. Empty spacing does not excuse an unblocked rusher.

## 5.6 Static Complement

Every major motion shell receives at least one no-motion companion call from the same formation.

Examples:

- Condensed Trey FLASH Drive / Condensed Trey Drive;
- Bunch FLASH Choice / Bunch Choice;
- Wing RETURN Counter / Wing Counter;
- Doubles EXIT Inside / Doubles Inside;
- 12 FLEX FLASH Flood / 12 FLEX Flood.

The defense must not be allowed to trigger its adjustment merely because a player starts moving.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION SYSTEM

Evolution V is the largest motion expansion in Stone's career to this point, but the system remains organized by job and timing.

## 6.1 Retained Motion Families

The following remain in force:

- JET: full-speed cross-formation motion, often at or near snap;
- ZIP: fast cross or short path used to change strength/leverage;
- RETURN: motion out and back, or across then reset/return by installed path;
- ORBIT: deep backfield path, still a change-up rather than a base identity;
- TRADE: TE/wing/surface relocation with set requirement when applicable;
- SHIFT: legal multi-player formation change with required set;
- BUNCH/STACK adjustment: alignment change used to create release or communication stress.

## 6.2 New Motion: FLASH

**FLASH** is Stone's short, fast motion family.

The player begins off the line, uses a short burst immediately before the snap, and remains moving laterally or away from the line as required by NFL motion rules. The purpose is to create a running release and force the defender to solve leverage while the ball is being snapped.

FLASH is not defined by who receives the ball.

Legal football jobs include:

- free release for an outside or crossing route;
- widen the apex/overhang;
- force a corner to travel or pass the assignment;
- create a crack/insert angle;
- clear the edge for Duo/Power away;
- threaten quick screen while the offense runs inside;
- create a wheel/rail path;
- arrive as blocker on perimeter run;
- create a decoy for a static backside shot.

If FLASH becomes a target tell, its usage is reduced.

## 6.3 New Motion: DRIFT

**DRIFT** is controlled motion-at-snap at less than full Jet/Flash speed.

Its purpose is not to win a footrace. It is used to:

- widen a zone defender one step;
- test switch communication;
- create a new route stem;
- move a back or slot into checkdown space;
- change the flat defender on Flood/Sail;
- create a better insert or arc angle.

DRIFT is especially useful when a defense has begun overreacting to FLASH speed.

## 6.4 New Motion: EXIT

**EXIT** moves an eligible player away from the core toward a detached or Empty alignment.

Common EXIT players are R, F, H, or Y/H in FLEX personnel.

EXIT can produce:

- Empty;
- 4x1-like spacing;
- 3x1 with a back detached;
- quick perimeter Access;
- a lighter box;
- an easier pressure declaration;
- a matchup for Choice/Angle/Wheel.

The same personnel must still be able to return to a physical run picture without substituting.

## 6.5 Motion Timing Categories

The call language remains short. Timing is installed by family rather than recited in long calls.

**EARLY:** player moves, reaches landmark, and becomes set when required. Primary job is formation change or coverage declaration.

**LATE:** player begins close enough to the snap that the defense must adjust while communication is live. FLASH and many JET/ZIP calls live here.

**AT-SNAP:** player is legally moving when the ball is snapped. The path and cadence are practiced to a consistent landmark.

The quarterback can abort the late-motion timing and reset if the offense is not legally organized. A five-yard delay is better than a turnover or illegal-motion sequence only when game situation allows; otherwise the call may be killed before that point.

## 6.6 Defensive Motion Reactions

Evolution V expands the reaction vocabulary.

| **Reaction** | **Meaning**                                                                                   | **Offensive Use**                                                                                   |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| TRAVEL       | One defender follows the motion player through or across the formation.                       | Potential man leverage, but confirm post-snap; attack vacated space or matchup.                     |
| PUSH         | Defense passes the motion through zones and pushes another defender onto the new area.        | Attack the communication window, the displaced underneath defender, or run into the original space. |
| SWITCH       | Two defenders exchange assignments rather than follow routes across.                          | Use switch-beaters, run at the exchange point, or hold static and attack their new leverage.        |
| SPIN         | Safety/secondary rotates after motion or snap.                                                | Apply Evolution IV rotation rules; identify fitter/replacement and shot trigger.                    |
| STAY         | Front and second level remain structurally stable despite motion.                             | Do not force the motion answer. Run the base concept or take leverage if truly free.                |
| BOX          | Multiple defenders bracket/zone a bunch or compressed group instead of matching individually. | Run at the numbers, attack the outside edge of the box, or use spacing/sit routes between zones.    |

Motion is therefore information, not proof.

## 6.7 Motion Direction Rule

A core Evolution V rule:

**The defense must not be able to infer the point of attack from the direction of motion.**

For each major late-motion family, the weekly plan should include at least three of the following:

- play toward motion;
- play away from motion;
- throw to motion;
- throw away from motion;
- fake motion and screen elsewhere;
- motion as blocker/insert;
- motion as pure clear/decoy.

## 6.8 Static Constraint Rule

If the defense begins triggering a pressure, rotation, or run fit off FLASH/JET/RETURN, Stone calls the same formation without movement and attacks the space created by the anticipated adjustment.

Stillness is part of the motion system.

# 7. QUARTERBACK OPERATING SYSTEM

The quarterback remains a pocket operator first. Evolution V asks him to process more pre-snap movement without increasing the number of post-snap progressions.

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Eight

Before the snap, the quarterback identifies:

1. personnel and down/distance;
2. box count and front;
3. OPEN/CLOSED shell;
4. pressure surface and likely free threat;
5. fit source from the latest film rule;
6. motion reaction if motion occurs;
7. cap/leverage on Access, Bend, or shot routes;
8. clock, cadence, and whether the offense can legally complete the motion.

He does not need to know the final coverage before the snap.

## 7.2 Motion Is a Rule Detector

The quarterback tracks what the defense does with motion, not just who moves.

If a corner travels, that is useful. If a safety pushes a nickel and the corner stays, that is also useful. If no one moves but the front bumps, that is useful. The quarterback should be able to say what rule changed.

A motion that produces no useful information may still be valuable for leverage or speed, but the quarterback must not invent meaning that is not there.

## 7.3 FLASH Window Rule

FLASH can create an early throwing window because the receiver begins the route with speed.

The quarterback follows this hierarchy:

1. if FLASH creates the installed immediate leverage win and the protection allows it, take it;
2. if the defense caps or switches the motion, continue the normal concept progression;
3. do not stare at the motion player simply because he moved;
4. if the motion changes the pressure picture, protection responsibility outranks the fast-window temptation.

## 7.4 TRAVEL, PUSH, SWITCH, SPIN

The quarterback uses these words in film study and sideline communication.

**TRAVEL:** defender follows the motion player.

**PUSH:** coverage responsibility is passed to a new defender in the motion direction.

**SWITCH:** defenders exchange route ownership after or near the snap.

**SPIN:** secondary rotates the shell or fit structure.

The same defense can use more than one reaction in a game.

## 7.5 BEND

BEND remains a qualified route tag, not a free-form option route.

Evolution V adds motion presentation:

- FLASH can remove or widen the underneath collision player before a BEND;
- EXIT can create a clearer inside seam count;
- condensed alignment can force outside leverage before the bend window opens.

The cap rule remains. If the safety structure keeps the bend capped, the receiver stays on the installed vertical/seam answer or converts only by existing rule.

## 7.6 Pocket and Extension Rule

Stone still prefers the quarterback to solve the play from structure.

When the pocket breaks:

- climb before drifting backwards;
- escape through a declared lane when possible;
- keep route-side eyes alive on boot/slide concepts;
- scramble for available yards when coverage turns its back;
- do not turn fast motion into an excuse to hold the ball waiting for an off-script explosive.

The system is designed to create cleaner defined windows so the quarterback does not have to play hero ball.

## 7.7 Cadence and Motion Control

The quarterback owns the final legality of the operation.

He may:

- hold the cadence to let motion reach the landmark;
- speed the cadence when the defense is late to communicate;
- abort a motion tag and reset to the static companion if the unit is disorganized and time permits;
- use the motion player's landmark rather than a memorized stopwatch.

The offense practices cadence with the actual motion speed of the player running it. A fast Z and a heavy F cannot be timed by the same count.

# 8. PROTECTION SYSTEM

The defensive problem changes again. Split-safety structure no longer implies passive rush, and simulated pressure can create free threats without sending a traditional all-out blitz.

## 8.1 Core Protection Families Remain

The existing protection language survives:

- SCAT / five-man;
- HALF / half-slide;
- SOLID / six-man;
- MAX;
- TURNBACK for selected movement concepts;
- SCAN by the back;
- LOCK when one matchup or edge must remain fixed.

Evolution V adds better sorting and chip rules rather than replacing the base system.

## 8.2 SORT Tag

**SORT** is a 2024 protection tag for pressure structures in which the defense presents more potential rushers than it intends to send and repeatedly exchanges who drops and who replaces.

SORT tells the protection unit:

- honor the declared point and down-lineman threats first;
- pass games and replacement rushers by zone relationship rather than chasing a mugged linebacker who bails;
- back scans the installed second-level side/inside-out rule;
- quarterback owns the true free defender if the defense still creates one;
- route structure must contain a fast answer behind the vacated pressure area.

SORT is not universal. If a simple HALF or SOLID call handles the opponent cleanly, use the simpler protection.

## 8.3 CHIP Tag

**CHIP** tells an eligible to make controlled contact with a designated edge before entering the route.

The chip player must understand:

- who owns the rusher if he spikes inside;
- route timing after contact;
- when contact would destroy the route and therefore should be removed;
- whether the defense is using the chip as a trigger to green-dog or replace another rusher.

Normal CHIP candidates are Y, H, F, or R. The tag is especially useful from condensed formations where the eligible can affect the rusher without advertising max protection.

## 8.4 Motion Protection Rule

Motion cannot erase protection responsibility.

Before a back or tight end leaves the core on EXIT/FLASH, the offense must know:

- whether he was part of the protection count;
- whether his departure changes the point;
- whether the defense can insert a linebacker through the vacated area;
- who becomes the hot receiver if the defense pressures that space.

The staff does not install a pretty motion that leaves the quarterback unprotected against the opponent's best pressure.

## 8.5 Split-Safety Pressure Rule

Evolution IV taught OPEN/CLOSED as coverage and fit information. Evolution V adds a protection warning:

**OPEN does not mean low pressure.**

A defense can align two high, mug second-level defenders, send pressure from one side, and still rotate into a sound deep structure.

The quarterback and center therefore separate two questions:

1. What is the shell?
2. What is the protection threat?

The answers may be different.

## 8.6 Pressure Answers by Category

| **Defensive Problem**               | **Primary Stone Answer**                                                                                      |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Mugged linebackers who drop/replace | SORT or HALF + fast answer behind replacement.                                                                |
| Dominant edge                       | CHIP, LOCK, movement launch point, or run directly at/inside him.                                             |
| Slot pressure                       | motion/formation can widen or identify it; quick flat/Choice/spacing answer; protection adjustment if needed. |
| Drop-eight / low rush               | do not keep unnecessary protectors in. Release and use spacing/Choice/Drive.                                  |
| Zero pressure                       | condensed rubs, Mesh, Choice, fast motion release, max-protect shot only when matchup warrants.               |
| Two-high pressure                   | separate shell from rush; use SORT/SCAN, attack vacated underneath zone, or run at replacement structure.     |

## 8.7 Empty Protection

Empty remains a declaration tool but becomes more dangerous against disguise.

The offense must have:

- point rule;
- five-man assignment;
- hot receiver by pressure side;
- one built-in fast answer;
- one movement or check answer if the weekly plan expects overload;
- kill back to a non-Empty picture when the defense's structure makes the call unnecessarily fragile.

# 9. RUN GAME EVOLUTION

Motion changes leverage, but the run game remains Stone football. The offense does not become a collection of perimeter gimmicks.

## 9.1 2024 Core Run Hierarchy

1. Duo
2. Power
3. Counter / GT Counter
4. Outside Zone
5. Split Zone
6. Inside Zone
7. Pin-Pull
8. Toss/Crack
9. Jet / motion sweep
10. DART / tackle-wrap package when personnel and front justify it

The top five are identity families. The exact weekly order can change by opponent.

## 9.2 DUO

Duo remains one of the best answers to light boxes and fast second-level defenders because it allows the offense to create vertical movement and force the linebacker/safety fit to declare.

Evolution V additions:

- FLASH can widen the apex before Duo;
- EXIT can remove a linebacker or safety from the core;
- RETURN can bring a blocker back toward the fit after the defense widens;
- Duo may run toward or away from the motion;
- FLASH Choice/Play-Action can use the same shell.

The back still presses the installed track and reads the linebacker/fit relationship. Motion does not change the back's core footwork unless the play is specifically tagged.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards**.

## 9.3 POWER

Power remains a Stone identity call.

Evolution V uses motion to:

- widen the force defender;
- create a better insert/kick angle;
- remove a nickel from the box;
- set up Power away from obvious motion;
- build Power Play-Action/Leak from the same fast picture.

Pulling guard technique remains physical and direct. The offense does not slow the puller to wait for motion unless the play is explicitly designed that way.

Expected Yardage: **3-7 yards**.

## 9.4 COUNTER / GT

Counter becomes one of Stone's best anti-motion-overreaction calls.

A common series is:

- FLASH toward field;
- defense widens support;
- Counter back into boundary or opposite B/C gap;
- later FLASH Flood or Post-Cross from same initial picture.

Tackle and guard pulls remain central. The motion player may become crack, arc, flat, rail, or pure decoy depending on the concept.

Expected Yardage: **4-8 yards**.

## 9.5 OUTSIDE ZONE

Outside Zone gains more speed and perimeter conflict without becoming the entire run identity.

Evolution V variants include:

- FLASH outside to widen force;
- JET across to create cutback hesitation;
- RETURN to change the edge after the defense widens;
- EXIT to remove a second-level player;
- Boot/Slide/Flood off identical tracks.

Stone still expects the back to press the track and make one decisive cut. The motion should create space, not turn the run into dancing.

Expected Yardage: **4-9 yards**.

## 9.6 SPLIT ZONE

Split Zone keeps the SLICE/BLUFF/LEAK/ARC family from Evolution III and IV.

Evolution V adds late motion so the defense must distinguish the fast-motion player from the split-flow blocker.

Potential pairings:

- FLASH + Split;
- FLASH + Split Leak;
- RETURN + Split Arc;
- EXIT + Split Read Slant;
- static Split after repeated motion.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards**.

## 9.7 INSIDE ZONE

Inside remains the simplest zone complement and a useful PRESS call.

Its value increases when the defense widens or bumps with motion. Stone does not add unnecessary tags merely because the front moved one player.

Expected Yardage: **4-6 yards**.

## 9.8 PIN-PULL AND TOSS/CRACK

Perimeter football becomes more dangerous because motion can change who has the force responsibility just before the snap.

Pin-Pull and Toss/Crack may use:

- FLASH as crack/lead player;
- RETURN to create a late numbers change;
- JET fake opposite the run;
- receiver insert from condensed split;
- tackle/guard pull combinations where the weekly blocking plan supports them.

Stone likes these concepts against lighter edges and defenses that fit aggressively from safeties.

Expected Yardage: **4-9 yards**.

## 9.9 JET AND FLASH RUNS

Jet Sweep remains a real carry, not a fake-only action.

FLASH itself is not automatically a sweep. Selected players can receive a touch on:

- quick handoff/flip when legal and practiced;
- orbit/toss relation;
- touch pass by installed rule;
- return counteraction.

The staff does not force touches to justify the motion. The threat exists because the player can receive the ball, not because he must.

Expected Yardage: **4-10 yards** on true perimeter touches.

## 9.10 DART / TACKLE-WRAP PACKAGE

Stone's interest in moving offensive linemen produces a more formal tackle-wrap package in selected 2024 plans.

DART is used when:

- the tackle is athletic enough to pull cleanly;
- the defensive front gives a favorable down-block angle;
- motion can widen the force player;
- the backside edge can be controlled by block, read, or formation.

The package is weekly, not universal. A tackle who cannot execute the pull removes the concept regardless of schematic appeal.

Expected Yardage: **4-7 yards**.

# 10. PASS GAME EVOLUTION

The 2023-24 pass game responds to two realities: defenses are increasingly comfortable living in zone and split-safety structures, and motion is increasingly common enough that defenses have developed communication answers to it.

Stone therefore wants motion to improve the release or spacing of concepts he already trusts.

## 10.1 2024 Core Pass Hierarchy

**Primary core:** Drive, Y-Cross, Dagger, Mesh, Levels, Choice.

**Quick core:** Stick, Spacing, Slant-Flat, Access throws.

**Major secondary:** Sail/Flood, Four Verticals, Post-Cross, Mills/Post-Dig, Snag, Texas/Angle, Shallow.

The weekly plan can promote a secondary concept when the opponent gives the right rule.

## 10.2 SPACING Promotion

Spacing is promoted because modern zone structures frequently concede underneath completion windows while protecting explosives.

Stone's Spacing concept is not a passive five-yard surrender call. It is used to:

- make underneath defenders expand horizontally;
- create a sit window for the H/Y/R;
- set up a later seam or BEND;
- pair with FLASH or EXIT so a defender must adjust leverage before settling into zone;
- create a reliable PRESS answer.

Expected Yardage: **4-8 yards**.

## 10.3 DRIVE

Drive remains one of the most portable concepts in the system.

Evolution V variants:

- FLASH shallow;
- FLASH clear with static shallow from opposite side;
- RETURN shallow;
- EXIT to create empty-side dig leverage;
- condensed dig/drive;
- static Drive from the same shell after repeated motion.

The quarterback still reads the same installed high-low/inside-out relationship. Motion changes the release, not the concept identity.

Expected Yardage: **8-15 yards**.

## 10.4 Y-CROSS

Y-Cross continues to attack behind linebackers and in front of safeties.

Evolution V emphasizes:

- fast motion to widen the underneath collision player;
- DRIFT to change the flat defender;
- RAIL/WHEEL by the motion player;
- BEND backside when the safety cap permits;
- static Cross when the defense begins overpassing motion.

Expected Yardage: **10-18 yards**.

## 10.5 CHOICE

Choice becomes one of the best uses of fast motion because a qualified receiver can enter the snap with leverage already changing.

FLASH Choice rules:

- receiver still runs the installed leverage tree, not an improvised route;
- the defense may top-lock or switch, so the quarterback confirms the actual matchup;
- if motion creates zone push rather than true man leverage, receiver uses the zone sit/settle rule;
- the concept can be called from WR, Y/H, F, or R roles when that player has earned the route responsibility.

Expected Yardage: **6-13 yards**.

## 10.6 MESH

Mesh remains a man/pressure answer but is modified against switch and box techniques.

The offense can use:

- condensed releases;
- one FLASH crosser and one static crosser;
- sit route behind the mesh against zone;
- wheel/rail off fast motion;
- motion into bunch then Mesh;
- static Mesh after the defense begins overcommunicating the motion.

Receivers do not chase rubs that destroy legal spacing or timing.

Expected Yardage: **6-12 yards**.

## 10.7 DAGGER

Dagger remains a major intermediate/deep concept against middle-field structure.

Evolution V adds:

- FLASH clear to widen/occupy underneath coverage;
- play-action Duo/Power variants;
- BEND-compatible inside clear by rule;
- CHIP protection when the dig needs development time;
- static companion from the same condensed shell.

Expected Yardage: **10-20 yards**.

## 10.8 LEVELS

Levels remains promoted from Evolution IV.

Motion can change which defender owns the shallow/intermediate window, but the quarterback should still work the installed high-low progression.

The concept is especially useful when a defense protects the roof and rallies underneath.

Expected Yardage: **7-14 yards**.

## 10.9 SAIL / FLOOD

Sail/Flood gains more motion variety because DRIFT and FLASH can change the flat defender just before the snap.

The three-level stretch remains:

- deep corner/sail;
- intermediate out/over;
- flat/control.

The movement player does not have to own the flat. Sometimes he clears the flat defender so another eligible owns the low route.

Expected Yardage: **9-17 yards**.

## 10.10 FOUR VERTICALS / SWITCH VERT

Four Verticals survives but the presentation changes.

Evolution V adds selected **SWITCH** release tags from condensed, stack, and bunch structures. SWITCH changes release ownership, not the entire vertical progression.

FLASH may create a running start for one vertical, but the route must stay legal and the quarterback must respect the cap. The offense does not throw a vertical merely because the receiver entered the snap with speed.

Expected Yardage: **15-30+ yards**.

## 10.11 MILLS / POST-DIG and POST-CROSS

These remain shot families, not random calls.

Evolution V triggers include:

- safety driving motion too aggressively;
- repeated PUSH/SWITCH communication that opens the opposite post;
- linebackers widening with fast motion and vacating dig windows;
- defense overplaying perimeter screens or quick Access;
- max/CHIP protection advantage against a specific rusher.

Expected Yardage: **15-30+ yards**.

## 10.12 New/Promoted Route Tags

| **Tag**    | **Meaning**                                                                                  |
|------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| BEND       | Existing seam/vertical conversion by cap rule.                                               |
| SWITCH     | Exchange release/stem responsibility between two eligible players by installed concept rule. |
| SIT        | Qualified route settles in zone window rather than running through occupied space.           |
| RAIL       | Fast outside/upfield release by back/F/H when concept calls for it.                          |
| ALERT POST | Existing shot alert when safety leverage permits.                                            |
| CHIP       | Protection-route tag; eligible chips designated edge then enters installed route.            |

Play action remains valuable because Stone's run game is real. Motion expands the number of ways the offense can make the defense react to the same initial picture.

## 11.1 Core Action Families

Primary pass answers:

- Duo Dagger;
- Duo BEND;
- Duo Cross;
- Duo Choice;
- FLASH Duo -> Flood;
- static Duo shell -> Post-Cross.

### POWER ACTION

Primary pass answers:

- Power Post-Cross;
- Power Over;
- Power Leak;
- FLASH Power -> Flat/Wheel;
- max-protect post.

### COUNTER ACTION

Primary pass answers:

- Counter Post-Cross;
- Counter Boot opposite pull;
- Counter Flood;
- FLASH one way -> Counter action -> throw back across.

### OUTSIDE ACTION

Primary pass answers:

- Outside Boot;
- Outside Slide;

### SPLIT ACTION

Primary pass answers:

- Split Leak;

## 11.2 Motion-Action Pairing Rule

A major motion path should connect to at least:

- one real run;
- one play-action pass;
- one quick/perimeter answer;
- one static complement.

This prevents the defense from treating motion as an isolated gadget family.

## 11.3 Boot and Slide

Boot/Slide remain useful launch-point changes against fast edges and simulated pressure.

Evolution V adds:

- FLASH away from boot to widen support;
- FLASH toward boot as clear/flat player;
- DRIFT by Y/H into delayed flat;
- static boot from a shell previously used with motion.

The quarterback still must get depth and width efficiently. Motion is not permission to drift into the edge rusher.

## 11.4 Fitter-Replacement Shot

Evolution IV's fitter-replacement principle remains.

Evolution V adds a motion component: when motion causes the safety/overhang to become the replacement fitter, the next call can attack the space behind that replacement with Cross, Dagger, Post-Cross, Flood, or BEND.

The shot is earned by the defensive reaction, not by a predetermined script number.

## 11.5 Play-Action Protection Rule

Because defenses can pressure from split-safety structure, the offense cannot assume play-action itself will hold every rusher.

The weekly plan chooses among:

- full run-action protection;
- CHIP + release;
- seven-man protection;
- half-roll/boot;
- quick play-action with defined first window.

# 12. ACCESS, READ, AND KEEP PACKAGES

## 12.1 ACCESS Remains Pre-Snap

ACCESS remains the simplest way to take free leverage.

Normal ACCESS throws:

- Now;
- Smoke;
- Hitch;
- Bubble;
- quick Slant;
- fast Out;
- motion-created quick flat when installed.

The quarterback decides before the snap whether the throw is free enough to replace the run.

## 12.2 READ

READ remains post-snap with one declared conflict defender.

Evolution V does not respond to modern football by attaching a read to every run. The master menu grows only when a new read creates a different defensive problem.

Normal 2024 master families:

1. Inside Read Glance.
2. Split Read Slant.
3. Duo Read Pop.
4. Outside Read Bubble.
5. Counter Read Glance.
6. Power Read Flat.
7. Duo Read Stick.
8. Split Read BEND when cap rules permit.
9. Outside Read Now.
10. Counter Read Stick/Spot.
11. selected motion-read package where motion relocates the key.

Normally four to six are active in a game plan.

## 12.3 Motion-Key Rule

Motion can change which defender is in conflict.

The offense must define before game day:

- original key;
- whether the key travels, pushes, or is replaced;
- whether the quarterback transfers the read to a new defender;
- or whether the call automatically becomes the base handoff/throw if the original key leaves.

The quarterback does not invent a new read on the field.

## 12.4 Safety-Cap Rule

The Evolution IV safety-cap rule remains.

Fast motion does not override it. A Glance/BEND/Pop can still be capped by a safety who maintains leverage despite motion.

The quarterback should not confuse speed with openness.

## 12.5 KEEP

KEEP remains optional and quarterback dependent.

A semi-mobile baseline quarterback may carry:

- naked keep;
- zone keep versus completely wrong edge;
- short-yardage follow/keep if physically appropriate;
- boot scramble rule;
- one or two opponent-specific read keeps.

A more athletic quarterback can expand the package. The offense still does not require it.

## 12.6 READ + Fast Motion Guardrails

- one conflict defender per call;
- motion timing cannot hide the mesh from the quarterback;
- do not transfer the key without a practiced rule;
- do not pair a deep read throw with protection that cannot survive the conflict time;
- motion player must know whether he is route, blocker, decoy, or ball carrier before the call;
- static companion must exist for heavily used packages.

# 13. SCREEN AND PERIMETER GAME EVOLUTION

The screen/perimeter game becomes one of the main ways Stone punishes defenders who overreact to motion and pressure.

## 13.1 Core Screen Families

- Smoke/Now;
- Bubble;
- Tunnel/Jail;
- RB Slow;
- RB Slip;
- TE delay/screen;
- middle screen;
- motion screen;
- screen away from motion.

## 13.2 FLASH NOW / FLASH BUBBLE

FLASH can create immediate width and a running start for a quick throw, but the offense must not make it automatic.

The ball is thrown only when:

- numbers are favorable;
- blocker leverage is sound;
- corner/overhang alignment gives useful space;
- the defense has not triggered an obvious trap/replace answer.

Expected Yardage: **5-10 yards**.

## 13.3 Screen Away from Motion

One of the most important 2024 complements is to move the defense one way and screen the other.

Examples:

- FLASH Z right, RB Slip left;
- RETURN H to field, Tunnel boundary;
- JET fake, TE delay opposite;
- EXIT back to slot, middle screen to another back/receiver by weekly design.

The offense wants pursuit and communication to become liabilities.

## 13.4 Perimeter Run/Screen Pairing

Stone pairs:

- Jet Sweep / Jet Bubble;
- Pin-Pull / Now;
- Toss-Crack / Flash screen;
- Outside Zone / Boot flat;
- Counter away / motion screen toward;
- Duo inside / Flash Bubble outside.

The defense should have to choose which space it wants to overload.

## 13.5 ORBIT and Reverse Remain Change-Ups

Orbit, reverse, and double-action perimeter plays remain opponent-specific.

They are not promoted merely because motion is popular. Stone wants the defense to practice them without the offense spending too many weekly reps on them.

# 14. 6OL AND OFFENSIVE-LINE PACKAGE EVOLUTION

Stone's OL package system continues to develop. The sixth lineman is still an offensive role, not a declaration of intent.

## 14.1 6OL Objectives

The extra lineman can:

- create a true extra gap;
- align as eligible Y by declaration and formation rule;
- form tackle-over or unbalanced surface;
- trade across the formation before set;
- pull or wrap on selected runs;
- chip/protect before release when eligible;
- create max-protect shot structure;
- force the defense into heavier personnel and then be spread by the remaining eligibles.

## 14.2 Motion from 6OL

The motion player is usually not 6OL. The offense uses skill motion to make the defense solve two problems at once:

- extra surface/gap from 6OL;
- late leverage change from FLASH/JET/RETURN/EXIT.

This creates runs both toward and away from the heavy surface.

## 14.3 Core 6OL Run Menu

- Power;
- Counter/GT;
- Duo;
- Outside Zone;
- Split Zone;
- DART/tackle wrap;
- Pin-Pull/Toss in selected weekly plans;
- short-yardage Insert.

## 14.4 Core 6OL Pass Menu

- Boot Flood;
- Post-Cross;
- Y-Cross;
- TE/eligible leak;
- Dagger with max or chip protection;
- screen away from heavy surface;
- FLASH/RETURN play-action constraint.

## 14.5 Pulling Tackle and Sixth-Lineman Rule

Stone continues to experiment with offensive linemen as movement pieces, but the rule remains player dependent.

A tackle or 6OL can become:

- wrap puller;
- perimeter escort;
- insert blocker;
- kick player;
- backside cutoff/seal player;
- pass-protection helper.

The staff does not force the role onto a player who lacks movement ability.

## 14.6 13 + 6OL Boundary

Late 2024 allows more heavy multiplicity, but Evolution V stops short of making heavy personnel the universal offensive answer. That larger structural shift, if it occurs, belongs in the 2025-26 mature system.

# 15. SITUATIONAL OFFENSE

## 15.1 First and Second Down

Normal priority:

- force the defense to defend a real run;
- use motion to change leverage rather than manufacture a call;
- take free Access when available;
- call play action after the fit reaction is established;
- use Spacing/Drive/Choice to accept efficient underneath windows;
- preserve shot intent when the safety or corner overreacts.

## 15.2 Third-and-1 to 2

Preferred families:

- Duo;
- Power;
- Counter;
- Insert;
- QB sneak/follow by personnel ability;
- quick Choice/flat;
- play-action leak after established heavy tendency.

Late motion is used only if it improves the fit and the operation is clean. Stone will not trade a short-yardage execution advantage for unnecessary movement.

## 15.3 Third-and-3 to 5

Preferred families:

- Drive;
- Choice;
- Mesh;
- Spacing;
- Stick;
- Duo/Draw by box;
- FLASH Choice;
- RETURN Drive;
- quick motion screen when leverage is obvious.

## 15.4 Third-and-6+

Preferred families:

- Dagger;
- Levels;
- Y-Cross;
- Drive;
- Mesh;
- selected Four Verticals;
- screen versus pressure tendency;
- SORT/SCAN protection;
- CHIP when one edge dictates the plan.

Motion is used to create release or pressure information, not merely to prove man coverage.

## 15.5 Red Zone

Field compression changes the value of motion.

Red-zone priorities:

- bunch/stack release;
- FLASH to create leverage quickly;
- Duo/Power/Counter against light boxes;
- Sprint/Boot/Flood;
- Choice by best matchup;
- TE/F leak;
- quick flat/whip/pivot;
- 13/6OL heavy-to-pass;
- static complement after repeated motion.

The offense reduces long-developing crossers when the field no longer provides depth.

## 15.6 Backed-Up Offense

Inside the offense's own 10:

- motion is reduced if it creates operational risk;
- Duo/Power/Inside/Split become preferred run calls;
- quick game and Drive remain available;
- max-risk lateral screens and elaborate reverse action are reduced;
- protection clarity outranks matchup hunting.

## 15.7 Two Minute

Two-minute remains concept-driven and communication-first.

Motion rules:

- EXIT and one or two FLASH calls may remain if the unit can align quickly;
- long shifts and decorative motion are cut;
- receiver substitutions are limited unless clock stops;
- PRESS language remains the primary operating system;
- boundary, timeout, and spike rules outrank motion sequencing.

## 15.8 Four Minute

Stone does not simply become heavy and predictable.

Four-minute priorities:

- Duo/Power/Counter;
- 12/21/6OL when matchup favors it;
- boot/flat and safe play action;
- motion used to improve run fit, not to extend the play clock unnecessarily;
- preserve enough pass threat to prevent all-out downhill defense.

## 15.9 Fourth Down

The GO menu remains dedicated.

Motion is used on fourth down only when:

- the timing has been rehearsed;
- it creates a known leverage advantage;
- it does not compress the protection call;
- the static companion has also been practiced.

Stone does not introduce a new motion trick on fourth down because it looked good on the whiteboard that week.

- Half/SORT Drive;

- align identical shell;
- no movement;
- snap on earlier cadence;
- attack the defender who anticipated motion;
- return to motion later only after the defense stops cheating.

The no-motion call is intentionally part of the series architecture.

# 17. WEEKLY GAME-PLANNING AND CALL-SHEET CHANGES

## 17.1 Defensive Map Questions

The staff must answer:

1. How does the defense respond to late fast motion: TRAVEL, PUSH, SWITCH, SPIN, STAY, BOX?
2. Does the response change by personnel grouping?
3. Who becomes the new force player after motion?
4. Who fits Duo/Power when the apex widens?
5. Does the safety drive motion or stay capped?
6. Does the defense top-lock the fast-motion receiver?
7. Does it trigger pressure off motion or Empty?
8. Can it pressure from two-high without declaring the blitzer?
9. Which rusher requires CHIP or LOCK?
10. Which fronts make DART/tackle wrap viable?
11. How does it fit 13/6OL without giving up vertical or leak threats?
12. What is the defense's static answer after it has prepared for motion?
13. What coverage windows are intentionally conceded underneath?
14. Which defender tackles poorly in space after motion creates a catch?
15. What is the best no-motion call against the defense's expected motion check?

## 17.2 Weekly Selection Targets

A normal game plan should identify:

- 5-7 core runs;
- 2-4 perimeter/change-up runs;
- 5-7 core pass concepts;
- 3-5 quick/access concepts;
- 3-5 play-action concepts;
- 3-5 screens;
- 4-6 READ packages at most;
- 0-4 KEEP calls depending on quarterback;
- 3-5 primary motion families;
- at least one static complement for each major late-motion series;
- 2-4 pressure-specific protections/answers;
- 3-6 6OL/13 calls when active;
- dedicated red-zone, backed-up, two-minute, four-minute, and fourth-down menus.

The master system is much larger. The weekly menu should not be.

- static companion;
- pressure answer;

- first movement cue;
- snap landmark;
- quarterback cadence;
- route depth after moving start;
- legal lateral/backward path;

The scout defense rotates through:

- TRAVEL;
- PUSH;
- SWITCH;
- SPIN;
- STAY;
- BOX.

The quarterback must identify the reaction without changing the installed progression unless the concept specifically requires it.

## 18.4 Static Complement Period

The offense runs the same formations without motion after several fast-motion reps.

The goal is to teach players that stillness is not a different offense and to make the defense's anticipation visible.

## 18.5 Pressure SORT Period

- double mug;
- one mug plus edge replace;
- slot pressure;
- simulated pressure with dropping end;
- split-safety blitz;
- zero bluff that becomes four-man rush.

The line and back must sort the protection without chasing defenders who drop.

# 19. SIMULATION DIRECTIVES FOR 2023-2024

## 19.1 Evolution Stage Selection

**2023: use Evolution V-A.** Emphasize aggressive adoption of late fast motion, FLASH, expanded motion-at-snap, condensed releases, motion-to-run pairing, and experimentation with pressure answers. Allow some overuse and operational mistakes as a believable part of the evolution.

**2024: use Evolution V-B.** Preserve fast motion but require static complements, cleaner motion categories, SORT protection, more CHIP usage, broader 13/6OL spread conversion, and better answers to SWITCH/PUSH/top-lock rules.

## 19.2 Call Generation Priority

The simulator should generate calls in this order:

1. situation and game state;
2. weekly concept tier;
3. personnel matchup;
4. defensive shell/fit/pressure map;
5. formation and width;
6. motion only if it serves a job;
7. base concept;
8. tag/protection/READ adjustment if justified;
9. static or return-call relationship;
10. expected-yardage profile.

Do not choose a flashy motion first and search for a play to attach to it.

## 19.3 Motion Planning Rules

For a normal healthy roster:

- 2023 total shift/motion may center roughly in the 58-70% range, with motion-at-snap on a smaller but significant subset;
- 2024 total shift/motion may center roughly in the 60-72% range, but raw volume can decrease if execution or opponent rules make motion unhelpful;
- these are planning centers, not quotas;
- Stone can exceed them for a matchup or operate below them when the roster lacks suitable movers;
- no player must receive a touch merely because he goes in motion;
- fast motion cannot be used on every snap or every pass concept.

## 19.4 Personnel Rules

- 11 is usually the largest single grouping, not a required majority.
- 12 or 21 can become the weekly center if the defense matches poorly.
- 20 remains a real two-runner package, not a carry-sharing mandate.
- 13 can rise in 2024 if the third TE is a credible route/motion/blocking piece.
- 6OL remains weekly when roster health permits.
- personnel does not dictate motion owner; WR, Y/H, F, or back may move if qualified.

## 19.5 Rotation Rules

- multiple backs and skill players may receive real playing time;
- no equal-touch algorithm;
- a productive player may stay on the field;
- substitution should not reveal run/pass or motion family;
- HOLD/PRESS can override planned rotation when the defense is trapped in a favorable package;
- fatigue, protection skill, and motion workload can influence playing time.

## 19.6 Run Distribution Rules

The simulator should preserve a physical center:

- Duo/Power/Counter remain the primary downhill identity;
- Outside/Split/Inside remain stable complements;
- Pin-Pull/Toss/Jet rise when motion creates edge leverage;
- run direction must not automatically follow motion direction;
- do not manufacture 1,900-yard feature-back seasons merely because one back is RB1;
- do not artificially cap a back who is dominating the matchup.

## 19.7 Pass Rules

- prioritize Drive/Y-Cross/Dagger/Mesh/Levels/Choice as connected concepts;
- Spacing becomes a stronger efficient-zone answer;
- motion may create a first window but does not replace the concept progression;
- intermediate completions are acceptable against deep-shell defenses;
- shots require a trigger;
- motion-created vertical releases still obey cap/safety rules;
- static versions must remain viable.

## 19.8 Motion Reaction Rules

The simulation can assign defensive responses such as:

- TRAVEL;
- PUSH;
- SWITCH;
- SPIN;
- STAY;
- BOX.

Stone should adapt within the game when one response repeats. A repeated defensive SWITCH rule may lead to more runs, spacing, static routes, or opposite-side shots rather than simply more motion.

## 19.9 READ Rules

- one conflict defender per call;
- four to six weekly packages normally active;
- motion-key transfer must be defined before the game;
- safety cap remains in force;
- do not attach READ to every run;
- if motion removes the original key and no transfer rule exists, use the installed fallback.

## 19.10 KEEP Rules

KEEP remains optional.

- baseline quarterback: small menu;
- athletic quarterback: menu may expand;
- no forced designed-run identity;
- scramble remains separate from called KEEP.

## 19.11 Protection Rules

- SCAN remains weekly;
- LOCK remains matchup specific;
- SORT activates against disguise/simulated-pressure opponents;
- CHIP can be used from Y/H/F/R roles;
- OPEN shell does not guarantee low pressure;
- motion cannot remove a protector without a replacement rule;
- movement passes remain a protection answer.

## 19.12 Play-Action Rules

- pair every major run action with real complementary pass structure;
- motion should not create a different fake that the run never uses;
- fitter-replacement shots remain trigger based;
- heavy personnel must carry real pass answers;
- boot/slide remain available from under center, pistol, and selected gun looks.

## 19.13 Expected Yardage Rules

Expected Yardage is attached to concept family and relevant tagged variation.

The simulator may adjust the band for:

- field zone;
- down/distance;
- defensive structure;
- player mismatch;
- protection burden;
- whether the play is a shot, chain call, or constraint.

It should not assign separate pseudo-scientific yardage to every formation permutation.

## 19.14 Evolution V Boundaries

Do not import:

- completed 2025-26 mature Stone system;
- future coordinator branches;
- a universal heavy-personnel revolution that has not yet become Stone's baseline;
- a requirement that motion-at-snap appear on every series;
- a quarterback-run offense unless the simulated quarterback justifies it;
- later solutions that have not been earned by 2024 film and roster experience.

# APPENDIX C. HISTORICAL GROUNDING NOTES

These notes define the historical rails used to keep Evolution V plausible. They do not mean Stone copies any one real coach or system.

1. **Shift and motion usage had become a league-wide offensive movement by 2023.** Next Gen Stats reported that league-wide shift/motion usage rose to 54 percent of plays in 2023, while Miami used it on 80 percent and San Francisco remained near the league lead. Evolution V therefore allows Stone to move well beyond the selective motion-at-snap usage of Evolution IV without making motion itself the system identity.
2. **Condensed formations also became substantially more common.** Next Gen Stats reported a 30 percent league-wide condensed-formation rate in 2023, up from 11 percent in 2016, while average formation width shrank. Evolution V retains Stone's existing condensed structure but pairs it with fast motion and required wide/static complements so it does not become one repeated shell.
3. **There was no single modern personnel answer.** In 2023 the Rams used 11 personnel at an extreme rate, while Miami and San Francisco used far less 11 and led the league in 21 personnel usage. Evolution V therefore keeps Stone's personnel-flexible identity rather than copying one tree's preferred grouping.
4. **Miami's 2023 short fast motion showed how a receiver could enter the route with a running start while remaining within NFL motion rules.** NFL.com described the tactic as creating a running start for vertical and crossing routes. Stone's FLASH family is a project-specific translation of the underlying leverage principle, not a copy of Miami's terminology or full offense.
5. **Split-safety and zone usage continued to rise.** Next Gen Stats reported zone coverage on 71.6 percent of plays in 2023, the highest rate since 2018, with league-wide split-safety usage reaching 42.1 percent. Evolution V therefore promotes Spacing, Choice, intermediate windows, and patient progression while preserving shots when the defense overreacts.
6. **Schemed pressure and simulated-pressure structures were a defining defensive problem.** Next Gen Stats highlighted Kansas City's league-leading unblocked pressure production in 2023, while Baltimore used blitzes and simulated pressures from varied personnel and alignments. Evolution V therefore adds SORT, clarifies CHIP, and explicitly separates shell identification from protection identification.
7. **By 2024, motion-at-snap had become important enough for Next Gen Stats to create formal shift/motion classifications, including motion-at-snap and categories such as fly, jet, glide, orbit, and exit.** Stone keeps his own terminology, but the historical environment supports treating timing and motion type as distinct operational variables.
8. **The 2024 season also showed defenses could pressure aggressively from split-safety looks.** Minnesota under Brian Flores paired a high blitz rate with frequent split-safety structure, reinforcing Evolution V's rule that OPEN shell cannot be treated as low-pressure football.
9. **Multiple-tight-end offense was already rising before the later heavy-personnel surge.** NFL.com noted increased 12-personnel use and, in later review of 2024, identified Pittsburgh and Arizona as early high-volume 13-personnel examples. Evolution V therefore allows a selected 13 FLEX package in 2024 but defers a broader heavy-personnel shift to the next era.

# STONE 2023-2024 OFFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** physical downhill multiple offense + concept passing + personnel portability + split-safety/fit identification + high-purpose motion.

**System sentence:** Change the leverage at the snap. Keep the concept recognizable.

**Core runs:** Duo / Power / Counter-GT / Outside / Split. Inside remains stable. Pin-Pull, Toss/Crack, Jet, and selected DART are weekly perimeter or movement tools.

**Core passes:** Drive / Y-Cross / Dagger / Mesh / Levels / Choice. Spacing is promoted. Stick remains quick core. Flood, Four Verticals, Mills/Post-Dig, and Post-Cross remain major secondary or shot families.

**Motion:** JET / ZIP / RETURN / ORBIT / TRADE / SHIFT survive. FLASH, DRIFT, and EXIT are added. Motion direction cannot dictate point of attack. Static companions are required for major fast-motion series.

**Motion reaction:** TRAVEL / PUSH / SWITCH / SPIN / STAY / BOX. Motion identifies the defense's rule, not automatically its coverage.

**Personnel:** 11 is usually largest. 12 and 21 can become the weekly center. 20 is a true two-runner matchup package. 13 grows selectively in 2024. 6OL remains a complete run/pass package.

**Back principle:** two or three runners may receive meaningful playing time. Playing time does not imply equal carries or simultaneous backfield use.

**Protection:** SCAN weekly; LOCK matchup specific; SORT for simulated-pressure structures; CHIP for designated edge help. OPEN shell does not guarantee low pressure.

**READ:** normally four to six active. Motion can relocate the conflict player only through a practiced transfer/fallback rule. Safety cap remains.

**KEEP:** optional quarterback-dependent module. Baseline offense remains pocket-oriented and semi-mobile.

**OL principle:** guards, tackles, and 6OL remain movement tools. 6OL can run, protect, shift surface, and support motion without becoming a short-yardage tell.

**Tempo:** ROTATE to test the match, HOLD to preserve it, PRESS only with motion the unit can execute without compromising communication.

**Play-caller:** Show -> Stress -> Punish. In 2024, add a fourth question: what happens if we show the motion and do not move?
