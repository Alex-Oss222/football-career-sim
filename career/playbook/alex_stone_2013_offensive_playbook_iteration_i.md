# 1. SYSTEM IDENTITY AND COACHING LAWS

The offense is multiple in presentation and deliberately compact in its actual teaching. It is not a library of hundreds of unrelated plays. The same concepts reappear from different personnel, formations, shifts, motions, backfield alignments, and protection structures. The defense is forced to identify people and pictures; the offense is allowed to keep speaking the same language.

Stone's passing structure begins with an Erhardt-Perkins idea: teach complete route concepts rather than making formation the identity of the play. His most recent offensive influence adds the other half of the system: quick throws, pre-snap movement, matchup creation, and vertical shots built from the same presentation. The run game is more physical than the title "spread" would imply. Under center matters. Pullers matter. Fullbacks, H-backs, tight ends, and extra offensive linemen have real jobs. Shotgun exists to create space and simplify access, not to erase downhill football.

## 1.1 The Seven Laws

1. **Concept before formation.** A player learns the job inside a concept, then learns where that job lives from each formation.
2. **Personnel changes the matchup; formation changes the picture.** Do not change the core concept merely because a different athlete performs a job.
3. **Run downhill before running sideways.** The core run menu must move defenders with angles, double teams, pulls, and insert blocks. Perimeter runs punish overcommitment; they are not the entire identity.
4. **Make the easy throw expensive to take away.** Quick game and access throws force corners, overhangs, and linebackers to declare leverage. When they cheat, take the shot that the cheat created.
5. **The same look must carry more than one answer.** Run, play-action, screen, and dropback concepts should share enough pre-snap structure that the defense cannot identify the play from alignment alone.
6. **Fresh players are useful only if the defense cannot identify the call from the substitution.** Rotation is aggressive, but roles overlap. A new back does not mean a new run family. A second tight end does not automatically mean run.
7. **Calls are sequenced.** A call can be correct because of what it sets up three snaps later. Stone's call sheet tracks setup, answer, and shot relationships.

## 1.2 What the Offense Is Not

The offense is not a pure spread system, not a pure Coryell offense, not a West Coast progression menu, not a read-option offense, and not a feature-back system. It can look like each of those things for a series without becoming any of them.

It also does not require every concept to exist from every personnel group. Portability is a weapon, not a quota. Some plays are intentionally narrow because their value comes from a specific alignment, blocker, or matchup.

## 1.3 2013 Core / Weekly / Change-Up Structure

| **Tier**  | **Meaning**                                                                                             | **Examples**                                                                                                       |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| CORE      | Installed from the first week of camp and carried every game unless personnel makes it impossible.      | Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Outside Zone, Stick, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Four Verticals, Boot Flood, RB Slow Screen |
| WEEKLY    | Fully taught but emphasized according to opponent structure.                                            | Duo, Split Zone, Pin-Pull, Dagger, Sail, Snag, Texas, Mills, TE screen, 20-personnel packages                      |
| CHANGE-UP | Small-volume answer. May disappear for several weeks and return when a defense gives the right trigger. | Wham, Trap, Jet, End Around, Scissors, Out-and-Up, flea flicker, selected 6OL leaks                                |

## 1.4 Play-Caller Operating Rules

Stone calls the offense. The weekly call sheet therefore includes not only plays, but the reason each play exists in sequence.

**Show -> Stress -> Punish.** Show a picture. Stress the defender responsible for the conflict. Punish his adjustment.

**Return calls.** If the defense solves a play by overplaying one rule, come back to the same formation and make that rule wrong.

**Do not chase numerical balance.** A 50/50 run-pass split has no value by itself. Balance means the defense cannot remove one category without exposing another.

**Take the free five.** If leverage gives an uncontested hitch, smoke, bubble, or flat and the game situation allows it, take it until the defense closes the space.

**Shots need a reason.** Deep calls are attached to a coverage trigger, field position, tendency, or a previous setup. Four Verticals is not a random lottery ticket.

# 2. CALL LANGUAGE AND TEACHING STRUCTURE

Stone keeps calls modular and short. The complexity lives in shared definitions, not in a sentence-length play name. Personnel is usually signaled from the sideline before the huddle and is not repeated in the spoken call unless confusion is possible.

## 2.1 Base Call Order

**FORMATION -> SHIFT/MOTION -> CONCEPT/RUN -> DIRECTION OR TAG -> PROTECTION (only when non-default)**

Examples:

- Trey Right, Drive
- Ace Left, Power R
- Bunch Right, Zip, Mesh
- Pro Left, Counter R
- Gun Trips, Inside, Access Bubble
- Heavy Right, Post-Cross, Max

If the concept is symmetrical, no direction is spoken. If the formation establishes strength but the run goes away from it, run direction is explicit.

## 2.2 Vocabulary Rules

| **Word Type** | **Rule**                                                  | **Examples**                                                 |
|---------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Formation     | One or two words. Personnel is separate.                  | Doubles, Trey, Trips, Bunch, Ace, Wing, Pro, I, Empty, Heavy |
| Motion        | One short word describes path/timing.                     | Zip, Jet, Return, Orbit, Trade, Shift                        |
| Pass concept  | One concept name recalls all route jobs.                  | Stick, Mesh, Drive, Cross, Dagger, Sail, Vert                |
| Run           | Concept plus direction when required.                     | Power R, Counter L, Inside R, Outside L                      |
| Route tag     | Changes one eligible without renaming the entire concept. | Alert Post, Wheel, Choice, Corner, Go                        |
| Protection    | Omitted when the concept has its default.                 | Scat, Half, Solid, Max, Turnback                             |
| Tempo         | Separate sideline command.                                | Normal, Press, Freeze, Milk                                  |

## 2.3 Teaching Jobs Instead of Memorizing Routes

A pass concept is taught as a set of jobs. Example: **Drive** contains a clear player, a dig player, a shallow player, a flat/control player, and a checkdown/protection player. Those jobs can belong to different positions in different personnel groups. The quarterback's picture stays stable even when the names on the jerseys change.

This is the center of Stone's personnel innovation. A tight end, fullback, halfback, slot receiver, or second tight end may own the same job from different presentations. The position coach teaches how his player executes the job; the quarterback room learns the concept as one thing.

## 2.4 Run Direction and Strength

Formation strength is called independently from run direction. Trey Right, Power L is legal and intentional. The offense must be able to run toward or away from the declared strength so the defense cannot treat the formation call as the run key.

## 2.5 Position Labels and Eligibility Language

The system uses role labels so calls remain stable when personnel changes.

| **Label** | **Normal Player**                          | **Teaching Meaning**                                                                                                  |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| X         | Boundary/outside receiver                  | Usually on line. Primary isolated vertical/in-breaker candidate.                                                      |
| Z         | Movable outside receiver                   | Off line more often than X. Can motion, reduce split, or become point man in bunch.                                   |
| H         | Slot / move player                         | Slot receiver, second TE, second back, or H-back depending personnel. The label describes the job, not the body type. |
| Y         | Tight end                                  | Attached, wing, flex, or detached. Often owns seam/cross/flat jobs.                                                   |
| R         | Primary tailback                           | Run path, protection, screen/checkdown, or flex job.                                                                  |
| F         | Fullback / second back / second movable TE | Insert, lead, arc, motion, flat, protection, or receiver job.                                                         |

A 12-personnel call may put the second tight end at H. A 20-personnel call may put the second back at H or F. A 21-personnel shift may move F from the backfield to a wing. The call does not change merely because the athlete filling H changed.

## 2.6 Receiver Split Standards

The playbook uses default split families rather than memorizing a yard mark for every formation.

- **Normal:** outside receiver near top of numbers; slot with enough room for two-way release.
- **Reduced:** outside receiver inside numbers or closer to core. Used for crossers, crack blocks, and vertical stems that need space outside.
- **Tight:** compressed within roughly 4-6 yards of the core. Used for bunch/stack releases and run support.
- **Wide:** widen outside receiver to force corner/safety width and expand the box.
- **Stack:** one receiver directly behind or slightly inside another. Front receiver owns release responsibility; back receiver delays enough to avoid collision.

Splits are adjusted by opponent technique. If a corner is hard outside leverage, a receiver may reduce to create more room for the in-breaker. If an overhang is crowding the core, widen the slot to force a declaration.

## 2.7 Route Depth Standards

| **Route**     | **2013 Landmark**                                                                       |
|---------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hitch / Spot  | 5-6 yards, present numbers to QB                                                        |
| Stick         | 5-6 yards, leverage stem then out/sit by rule                                           |
| Shallow       | 3-5 yards across, never drift uphill                                                    |
| Dig           | 12-15 yards for normal Drive/Levels; 15-18 for Dagger                                   |
| Curl          | 10-12 yards, work back downhill                                                         |
| Corner / Sail | 10-14 stem before break unless red-zone spacing compresses it                           |
| Post          | 10-12 stem minimum on normal shot, maintain room from opposite safety                   |
| Cross / Over  | 12-18 depending concept and formation; stay behind linebackers and below deep safety    |
| Seam          | Vertical through landmark, bend only by tag/rule                                        |
| Go / Fade     | Win stack or maintain sideline space; QB throws by leverage, not predetermined landmark |

Route depth can move a yard or two for field position, hash, and defensive leverage. The concept's spacing relationship matters more than a painted number on the grass.

# 3. PERSONNEL ARCHITECTURE

The 2013 offense is deliberately more personnel-diverse than a typical one-grouping offense, but it does not substitute merely to look clever. Each grouping must carry enough of the core menu that the defense cannot identify the call from the package.

The percentages below are planning centers, not quotas. A weekly plan may move substantially when the opponent, injuries, or available skill sets demand it.

| **Personnel**         | **Typical Planning Center** | **Primary Function**                                                            | **Must Be Able To**                                                              |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 11 (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR) | 40%                         | Base multiplicity. Best balance of space, run numbers, and concept portability. | Run Power/Inside/Outside; execute full quick and dropback menu; motion to empty. |
| 12 (1 RB, 2 TE, 2 WR) | 22%                         | Force base personnel or create TE mismatches.                                   | Run downhill; spread into 2x2/3x1; carry Cross, Sail, Mesh, shots, screens.      |
| 21 (2 RB, 1 TE, 2 WR) | 13%                         | Under-center physical game, split-flow, lead and play-action.                   | Run Power/Counter/Lead/Outside; motion FB/H; throw from Pro and empty-out looks. |
| 20 (2 RB, 0 TE, 3 WR) | 7%                          | Two-runner space package.                                                       | Run Inside/Power/Draw; flex one back; screen and angle game; motion to empty.    |
| 10 (1 RB, 0 TE, 4 WR) | 8%                          | Space, two-minute, pressure identification.                                     | Quick game, Mesh/Drive/Vert, Draw, limited Inside, screens.                      |
| 13 (1 RB, 3 TE, 1 WR) | 6%                          | Heavy without surrendering pass threat.                                         | Power/Counter/Duo, boot, Cross, Sail, TE leak, max-protect shot.                 |
| 22 (2 RB, 2 TE, 1 WR) | 4%                          | Short yardage, goal line, four-minute; occasional tendency breaker.             | Lead/Power/Counter, play-action flat/cross, boot, selected shot.                 |

## 3.1 Running Back Room Rule

The offense does not require a default feature back. Two or three runners should receive legitimate playing time if they can execute the core menu. That does **not** mean equal touches, and it does not mean two backs must share the field.

All backs are expected to learn the base run families. Specialized skill can change where a player is emphasized, but the defense should not be able to say, "Back B entered, therefore screen or draw." Hot hand, ball security, pass protection, game state, and matchup can override the planned rotation at any time.

## 3.2 Tight End / H-Back Rule

The Y is not restricted to an attached position. In 11 and 12 personnel, a tight end may be attached, off the ball, winged, flexed, or motioned. The second tight end in 12 is often the formation-changing piece because he can move the offense from heavy to spread without substituting.

## 3.3 Fullback Rule

In 21 and 22 personnel, the fullback is a football player rather than a permanent I-back. He may lead, insert, arc, sift, protect, release to the flat, align as an H, or shift to a wing. When the roster has two halfbacks better suited to 20 personnel, the offense uses them instead of forcing a traditional fullback onto the field.

## 3.4 Wide Receiver Rule

Receivers are taught outside, slot, reduced, and motion alignments. The offense wants enough cross-training that a substitute can enter without requiring a new play family. The best receiver may move to the slot or reduced split to avoid a matchup or create one; the formation is not a depth chart.

# 4. SUBSTITUTION, ROTATION, AND TEMPO

Stone substitutes more aggressively than average, especially at running back, tight end, and the third/fourth receiver spots. The purpose is fresh legs, matchup control, and uncertainty, not theatrical complexity. Because an offensive substitution gives the defense an opportunity to match, the system deliberately alternates substitution-heavy stretches with no-substitution stretches.

## 4.1 Three Operating States

| **State** | **Meaning**                                                                            | **Use**                                                                                                                           |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ROTATE    | Normal operation. Change one or more skill players between snaps or series.            | Keep backs/receivers fresh; test defensive substitution rules; present different body types without changing concepts.            |
| HOLD      | Keep the current eleven, huddle or operate at normal pace, and change formation/roles. | Prevent the defense from using the offensive package as a play key. Move from heavy to spread or vice versa with the same people. |
| PRESS     | Keep the current eleven and go no-huddle/fast.                                         | Trap a favorable defensive package, simplify communication, force vanilla checks, and attack fatigue.                             |

## 4.2 Rotation Principles

- Running backs rotate by series, workload, and role, not by a rigid carry count.
- A back who is producing can stay in. The system does not remove a hot player to satisfy a rotation chart.
- Tight ends and receivers may rotate inside a drive if the next concept benefits from a different blocker or route profile.
- After a substitution, expect the defense to match. Use the information gained from the match.
- After the defense reveals the desired package, HOLD or PRESS can keep it on the field.
- The same core run and pass calls remain available after most substitutions.

## 4.3 Tempo Commands

| **Tempo** | **Definition**                                                                                                                               |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| NORMAL    | Huddle or controlled no-huddle. Full call and normal cadence.                                                                                |
| PRESS     | Same personnel, rapid operation. Formation can change; call menu narrows to mastered families.                                               |
| FREEZE    | Line up early, use cadence and dummy communication to force the defense to declare or jump. Snap remains available if the look is favorable. |
| MILK      | Four-minute pace. Break huddle late enough to reduce remaining clock while preserving time to identify pressure and motion.                  |

## 4.4 Sideline Personnel Signaling

Personnel is sent before the spoken play call whenever possible. The huddle therefore hears Trey Right, Drive, not a long sentence beginning with "twelve personnel." The sideline uses a small set of package signals and the quarterback confirms the count before entering the huddle.

If a substitution arrives late enough that the defense is still matching, Stone slows the operation rather than trying to steal a substitution foul. Once the defense has matched, the next decision is whether to remain in ROTATE or shift to HOLD/PRESS.

## 4.5 No-Huddle Communication

PRESS does not require a second playbook language. The quarterback uses one of three mechanisms:

1. **Full verbal call at line:** same short call structure, communicated loudly when stadium conditions allow.
2. **Wristband family number:** number identifies a complete call already installed that week. The wristband contains only the weekly menu, not the whole playbook.
3. **Signal + word:** sideline signals formation; quarterback gives concept word and cadence.

The offense should be able to PRESS with roughly 20-25 calls, not all calls. If a play requires a paragraph to communicate, it does not belong in PRESS tempo.

## 5.2 Formation Selection Rule

A weekly plan should normally major in six to eight formation families, not the entire book. The remaining families stay available as change-ups. Formation volume is allowed to shrink if the roster is young or the opponent's rules are simple enough that extra pictures add no value.

## 5.3 Formation Modifiers

Modifiers change geometry without creating a new family.

| **Modifier** | **Meaning**                                               |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| TIGHT        | Reduce receiver splits toward core.                       |
| WIDE         | Expand receiver splits and widen apex defenders.          |
| OPEN         | Detach Y or remove attached surface from called strength. |
| CLOSED       | Attach Y to complete a surface.                           |
| Y-OFF        | Y aligns off line as wing/H.                              |
| NUB          | Single eligible or TE-only surface opposite strength.     |
| OVER         | Unbalanced/tackle-over presentation by weekly plan.       |
| STACK        | Vertical receiver stack.                                  |
| BUNCH        | Three-man compressed cluster.                             |
| PLUS / MINUS | Back offset toward / away from declared strength in gun.  |

A modifier is used only if it changes a coaching rule. There is no value in naming five formations that are functionally the same picture.

## 5.4 Ball Location and Formation

From the hash, the formation call considers actual available grass. Trips into the boundary can be useful for compressed spacing and backside isolation; it is not automatically wrong. Wide-field bunch and stack packages are preferred when the concept needs room for switch releases. The weekly plan marks formations as **Field**, **Boundary**, or **Either** only when the geometry materially changes the play.

# 6. SHIFT AND MOTION SYSTEM

Motion has a job. It is used to identify coverage, change leverage, create a blocker, alter the strength call, or disguise the final formation. The 2013 offense uses movement aggressively but does not yet build every snap around at-snap motion.

| **Call** | **Path / Timing**                                                                                      | **Primary Purpose**                                                                 |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ZIP      | Fast motion across the formation, usually by slot/WR; snap can occur after the player clears the core. | Leverage, man-zone indicator, quick edge access.                                    |
| JET      | Full-speed motion designed to meet the snap near the QB.                                               | Sweep threat, widen edge, hold second-level flow. Low-volume in 2013.               |
| RETURN   | Cross formation, then reverse and reset before snap.                                                   | Expose man coverage and force communication without changing final formation.       |
| ORBIT    | Deep motion behind QB/back.                                                                            | Misdirection, screen/option leverage, occasional perimeter handoff. Change-up only. |
| TRADE    | TE/H changes sides and resets.                                                                         | Change strength, create unbalanced surface, force front reset.                      |
| SHIFT    | Two or more eligibles reset together and become stationary before the snap.                            | Transform personnel picture, especially 12/21 to spread or back.                    |

## 6.1 Motion Rules

- Never motion merely to make the call look sophisticated.
- If the motion creates a favorable leverage throw, the QB may take the tagged access throw.
- If the defense bumps with motion, record who moved and who did not. That information belongs on the sideline tendency board.
- Heavy personnel shifts are especially valuable because they can force a base-defense declaration before the offense spreads the same people.

## 6.2 Shift Rules

All shifting players must become set before the snap as required. The quarterback owns the final set confirmation. If one player misses the shift landmark, RESET the formation rather than allowing a procedural penalty for the sake of preserving the original picture.

For 12 and 21 personnel, the highest-value shift is often **heavy-to-space**: show attached or backfield bodies, allow the defense to match and declare its rules, then detach one or two eligibles without substituting. The reverse **space-to-heavy** shift can create a late surface for Power/Counter after the defense widened.

## 6.3 Motion Identification Notes

The sideline charts motion response with simple marks:

- RUN: defender travels with motion player, strong man indicator.
- BUMP: secondary/linebackers exchange responsibilities.
- ROLL: safety structure rotates with motion.
- HOLD: defense barely adjusts, often zone or locked call.

The offense is not trying to prove the coverage with certainty. It is collecting useful evidence for the next call.

# 7. QUARTERBACK OPERATING SYSTEM

The quarterback is a distributor and problem-solver first. Mobility extends the structure; it does not replace it.

## 7.1 Pre-Snap Five

Before every snap, the quarterback confirms five things:

1. **Mike / point**: who sets protection and combination rules.
2. **Box**: number and location of immediate run defenders.
3. **Shell**: middle open, middle closed, rotation indicator.
4. **Pressure**: width, mugged linebackers, overload, zero-pressure threat.
5. **Leverage**: access throw, press matchup, isolated safety/linebacker.

## 7.2 Freedom Package

| **Command** | **QB Authority**                                                                                              |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ALERT       | Take the tagged shot or matchup only if the pre-snap trigger is clean. If not, run the called play.           |
| KILL        | Move from Play A to the paired Play B based on the defined box/front rule. No improvising a third play.       |
| ACCESS      | Throw the attached quick route instead of handing off when the leverage/count rule is won.                    |
| RESET       | Cancel motion or return to base alignment when timing or defensive disguise makes the called movement unsafe. |
| HOT         | Replace the unblocked/extra rusher with the designated immediate route.                                       |

## 7.3 Movement and Scramble Rules

Boots, nakeds, sprint-outs, and half-rolls are called movement passes. Uncalled movement begins only after the pocket has declared. Step up before escaping sideways when the interior is clean. On scramble, receivers follow the scramble rules taught by side: deep works deep/vertical, intermediate works sideline or open window, short works with QB, backside crosses late only when space is clear.

Designed QB keeps exist on selected zone-read or short-yardage calls, but the 2013 offense does not build weekly volume around quarterback rushing.

## 7.4 Cadence System

Stone uses cadence as an offensive tool without turning every snap into a gimmick.

- **ON ONE:** normal operation. Best for rhythm and movement timing.
- **ON TWO:** variation used enough that the defense cannot jump the first sound.
- **QUICK:** snap on the first ready sound. Used after formation is established and protection is clean.
- **FREEZE:** hard count/dummy communication. QB retains permission to snap if the defense gives the desired look.

Motion calls have a defined snap window. The QB does not hold a Jet motion player at full speed waiting for a dramatic cadence change.

## 7.5 Ball Security and Decision Hierarchy

The quarterback's first obligation is possession. A concept does not justify a contested throw simply because it is called. On early downs, a checkdown that preserves 2nd-and-manageable is often correct. On 3rd-and-8, the same checkdown may be wrong unless it has a realistic catch-and-run path to the sticks.

On deep shots, overthrow is preferable to a late underthrow into a capped receiver. On access throws, hesitation is a signal to hand the ball off or execute the original call rather than throwing late behind an advancing line.

# 8. PROTECTION SYSTEM

Protections are taught as families and married to route timing. Stone prefers the quarterback to know where the weak point is rather than pretending every pressure can be blocked.

| **Protection** | **Structure**                                                                 | **Primary Use**                                       | **QB Responsibility**                                       |
|----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| SCAT           | Five-man protection. Line accounts for five; back releases immediately.       | Quick game, spacing, empty-like distribution.         | Know the immediate hot answer. Ball out.                    |
| HALF           | Half-slide: one side zone/slides, backside man rules with RB scan/check.      | Base dropback. Mesh, Drive, Cross, Dagger.            | Set point; identify backside pressure threat.               |
| SOLID          | Six-man protection with RB committed unless late release is built in.         | Intermediate concepts and manageable pressure.        | Work full concept; checkdown may be delayed.                |
| BOB            | Big-on-big/man declaration with back responsible for designated LB/DB threat. | Clear pressure looks, selected empty-to-back motions. | Confirm matchups; throw hot versus one-more-than-can-block. |
| MAX            | Seven- or eight-man protection. Two or three-man route.                       | Shot plays, deep play-action.                         | Hold safety with eyes and deliver to vertical matchup.      |
| TURNBACK       | Play-action protection selling downhill run with line/backfield action.       | Power/Counter/Lead shots.                             | Carry fake, reset depth, expect edge leakage late.          |
| NAKED          | Run-action sell with unblocked edge by design.                                | Boot/naked.                                           | Gain depth away from edge; flat-crosser-deep progression.   |

## 8.1 Protection Defaults

Quick concepts default to SCAT or built-in immediate answers. Core five-man dropback concepts default to HALF. Intermediate calls from heavy personnel generally default to SOLID. Shot calls default to MAX or TURNBACK unless the weekly plan says otherwise. The call only names protection when changing that default.

## 8.2 Pressure Answers

- **Zero pressure:** immediate matchup/rub, max protect shot if alerted, or kill to quick concept.
- **Double A-gap mug:** identify which linebacker is real; back scan works inside-out; center can reset the point.
- **Boundary overload:** slide/half-slide toward overload or replace pressure with quick access.
- **Wide-9 edge:** chip, reduced split, 6OL help, quick game, or empty chip-release from a back/TE. Do not repeatedly ask the tackle to lose the same matchup in space.

## 8.3 Offensive Line Communication

The center sets the initial point in normal protections. Guards and tackles echo only the information that changes their rule. Stone does not want five linemen making five independent declarations.

Common line words:

- **MIKE:** protection/run identification point.
- **ACE:** center-guard combination.
- **DEUCE:** guard-tackle combination.
- **TREY:** tackle-tight end combination.
- **FAN:** widen responsibilities to account for edge/overload.
- **PINCH:** compress toward interior pressure.
- **LOCK:** man responsibility, no normal zone handoff.
- **ME / YOU:** two-man clarification for a twist or combination.

The exact word is less important than universal meaning. A weekly opponent-specific term may be added, but base language does not change with opponents.

## 8.4 Back Protection Rules

The back's protection technique is part of his playing-time qualification. He must know where the protection is short, identify his scan, and understand when he is free to release.

- **Check-release:** confirm assigned threat does not rush, then release on designated route.
- **Scan:** work inside-out or outside-in according to protection, with eyes through the linebacker level.
- **Insert block:** step into interior gap to meet add-on pressure before it reaches QB depth.
- **Chip-release:** strike edge with useful force, then enter route without drifting into another receiver's spacing.

A runner who cannot execute protection may still have a package, but his entry cannot advertise "run only." The weekly plan must give him at least one pass or screen from the same personnel/formation picture.

## 8.5 Twist and Game Rules

Half-slide protections pass normal interior games on the slide side. Man side uses set relationships and communication. Against exotic fronts, the offense may choose to **zone the problem** rather than identify every possible rusher individually. The quarterback is responsible for the extra defender the protection cannot account for.

# 9. RUN GAME

The 2013 run game is physical, downhill, and multiple. Gap schemes carry the personality; zone schemes keep fronts honest and support play-action. Perimeter runs make force defenders tackle in space. Under-center and shotgun versions share blocking language where possible.

Expected Yardage is the normal planning band on a clean execution against the intended look. Explosives can exceed it; short-yardage calls can be successful below it if they convert the situation.

## 9.1 POWER

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**           | **Base Formations**            |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| CORE     | 3-6                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 22, 6OL | Ace, Pro, I, Wing, Trey, Heavy |

**Purpose:** Downhill gap run with a double-team surface and a puller leading through the point of attack.

**Core rules:** Down blocks secure interior gaps; backside guard is primary puller. Kick or log the force defender by rule; lead through the first color inside. Back presses downhill and reads the puller, not open grass at the snap.

**Useful tags/complements:** Kick, Log, Read (rare), Toss fake, Alert pass

**Sample calls:** Ace Right Power R; Pro Left Power R; Heavy Right Power L

## 9.2 COUNTER / TREY

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**           | **Base Formations**                   |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| CORE     | 4-7                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 22, 6OL | Wing, Trey, Pro, I, Heavy, Gun Strong |

**Purpose:** Misdirection gap run that punishes fast flow and overplayed Power.

**Core rules:** Back takes controlled counter step. Guard pulls to kick/log; tackle or H wraps according to weekly rule. Frontside blockers protect inside gaps before climbing.

**Useful tags/complements:** GT, H-wrap, Counter Boot, Counter Screen

**Sample calls:** Wing Left Counter R; Trey Right GT L; Pro Right Counter L

## 9.3 DUO

| **Tier** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**       | **Base Formations**         |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| WEEKLY   | 4-6                  | 11, 12, 13, 21, 6OL | Ace, Trey, Wing, Heavy, Pro |

**Purpose:** Vertical double-team run that looks like inside zone but lets the back read the linebacker rather than the first down lineman.

**Core rules:** Create as many vertical doubles as the front permits. Back presses frontside A/B track and makes the linebacker wrong. Tight end must hold the edge without turning the play into wide zone.

**Useful tags/complements:** Insert, Alert Smoke

**Sample calls:** Ace Right Duo R; Heavy Left Duo L

## 9.4 LEAD / ISO

**Core rules:** Base/combo the down linemen; lead player inserts on the declared linebacker. Tailback follows the lead path with one decisive cut.

**Sample calls:** I Right Lead R; Pro Left Iso L

## 9.15 Run Blocking Technique Vocabulary

Stone's run game uses a small technique dictionary across multiple schemes.

| **Technique** | **Coaching Point**                                                                                |
|---------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| DRIVE         | Vertical displacement with square hips; do not turn the defender before securing movement.        |
| REACH         | Gain outside half and keep shoulders from opening too early.                                      |
| SCOOP         | Backside combination to cut off pursuit and climb when secure.                                    |
| DOWN          | Block inside gap defender with leverage; protect puller's path.                                   |
| KICK          | Puller attacks inside half of force defender and creates inside lane.                             |
| LOG           | If force spills aggressively inside, puller pins him inward and ball wraps outside.               |
| WRAP          | Second puller inserts inside kick/log and climbs to linebacker.                                   |
| INSERT        | Back/H enters created interior gap for second-level defender.                                     |
| SIFT          | H/Y crosses formation, bypassing wrong-color threat to reach designated edge/second-level player. |
| ARC           | H/Y bypasses edge to block support, leaving edge for QB/read or boot influence.                   |
| CRACK         | Receiver blocks inward support defender legally from proper alignment and angle.                  |

| **Run**      | **Even / Over**                                          | **Odd**                                                 | **Bear / Covered Interior**                                       | **Wide Edge**                                                                       |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Power        | Base core call; double at point and pull backside guard. | Good if surface can create down blocks and puller path. | Check interior gaps carefully; may run away or use Counter.       | Excellent downhill answer if edge vacates box.                                      |
| Counter      | Strong versus fast-flow even fronts.                     | Good with clear kick/wrap IDs.                          | Can be muddy if both guards/tackles covered; weekly rule decides. | Use if wide edge chases initial flow.                                               |
| Inside Zone  | Core answer.                                             | Center/nose combination must be defined.                | Often checked away unless access/perimeter numbers are favorable. | Strong interior answer.                                                             |
| Outside Zone | Reach leverage determines side.                          | Useful if edges are controllable.                       | Preferred over forcing inside if perimeter is soft.               | Must avoid running directly into a widened elite edge unless surface can reach him. |

## 9.17 Back Tracks

The back learns tracks, not improvised dancing.

- **Power:** downhill track to inside hip of puller, press until kick/log declares.
- **Counter:** counter step just large enough to influence flow, then get downhill behind pullers.
- **Inside Zone:** press frontside A/B landmark, one decisive cut based on down lineman/linebacker flow.
- **Outside Zone:** press outside leg of TE/tackle, force defense to run, then plant once leverage is won.
- **Duo:** press interior double teams and make the linebacker choose the wrong gap.
- **Toss/Pin-Pull:** stay behind pullers long enough for leverage; turn north before sideline becomes an extra defender.

A back is allowed to create after the designed read. He is not allowed to abandon the structure before forcing defenders to honor it.

## 9.18 Run Family Pairings

| **Show**     | **Come Back With**           | **Why**                                                          |
|--------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Power        | Counter / Power Pass         | Punish fast flow and force/fit aggression.                       |
| Inside Zone  | Split Zone / Access Bubble   | Change backside edge responsibility without changing OL picture. |
| Outside Zone | Boot Flood / Cutback Counter | Punish pursuit and edge chase.                                   |
| Toss         | Counter / Toss play-action   | Make support widen, then hit inside or behind it.                |
| Heavy Duo    | Max-protect Post-Cross       | Force safeties/linebackers into run posture before shot.         |
| Jet Sweep    | Power away / Jet fake screen | Hold overhang and second-level pursuit.                          |

# 10. CORE PASS GAME

Pass concepts are defined by jobs, not by a fixed formation. The assignment table below gives the normal job structure. Exact player ownership changes with personnel and formation.

## 10.1 STICK

| **Tier** | **Depth Class** | **Expected Yardage** | **Personnel**  | **Base Formations**         | **Default Protection** |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|----------------|-----------------------------|------------------------|
| CORE     | Short           | 4-7                  | 10, 11, 12, 20 | Doubles, Trips, Trey, Empty | SCAT                   |

**Purpose:** Quick leverage concept to attack apex/flat defenders and keep the offense on schedule.

**Job structure:** Outside clear/hitch; inside stick at 5-6; flat control; backside access; check/release.

**QB read:** Leverage pre-snap, then flat defender: stick -> flat. Backside access is live if isolated.

**Tags:** Choice, Go, Slant, Access

**Best use:** Zone cushion, pressure, manageable down-and-distance

**Sample calls:** Gun Trey Stick; Empty Trips Stick Choice

## 10.2 SPACING

**Job structure:** Three sit/hook windows with flat control and backside isolation.

**QB read:** Work leverage side; inside-out through first two windows. Do not throw late into a closing hook defender.

**Tags:** Corner, Pivot, Go backside

**Sample calls:** Doubles Spacing; Empty Bunch Spacing Corner

## 10.21 Core Concept Variants

The offense does not create a separate play name for every presentation. These are examples of how the same concept changes ownership while preserving the quarterback's picture.

### Stick Variants

**11 Trey Stick:** Y runs stick; slot/No. 3 controls flat; outside receiver clears or hitches by weekly tag. Best base version.

**12 Ace-to-Empty Stick:** shift second TE or back out, keep Y as stick player, isolate X backside. The defense has already matched 12 personnel before the offense creates five-out spacing.

**20 Trips Stick:** one back flexes to No. 3 and can own flat/access job; other back remains protection/check. Useful against linebackers matched to a back.

### Mesh Variants

**11 Bunch Mesh:** two crossers release from bunch/stack with legal spacing; sit route settles over ball against zone.

**12 Trey Mesh:** Y and slot/second TE can become crossers. The defense may have base linebackers on the field, creating the matchup Stone wants.

**21 Pro Mesh:** Y crosses with WR/FB variation; F can release to flat after showing protection. This is a tendency breaker from a run-looking group.

### Drive Variants

**11 Trey Drive:** slot shallow, Z/X dig depending formation, Y flat/control. Core version.

**20 Doubles Drive:** second back flexes as shallow or control player; linebackers must decide whether to remain matched to backs in space.

**21 Pro Drive:** Y can shallow, Z digs, F controls flat, R checks protection. Run posture remains credible.

### Y-Cross Variants

**11 Trey Cross:** Y is primary cross; outside post/vertical clears safety.

**12 Wing Cross:** move TE/H can cross while attached Y holds or runs vertical. This lets Stone choose the better route runner without changing the concept word.

**13 Heavy Cross:** one TE crosses, one protects/release-controls, one holds edge/seam. Low frequency but valuable against base defense.

### Dagger Variants

**11 Trips Dagger:** inside seam clears; No. 2/3 runs dig based on call.

**10 Doubles Dagger:** two vertical threats widen safeties, dig attacks middle. Best when pressure is manageable.

**12 Trey Dagger:** TE seam can create conflict for safety while WR works dig. The run threat keeps linebackers from gaining excessive depth.

### Four Verticals Variants

**11 Doubles Vert:** two seams plus outside verticals; back checks/releases.

**12 Trey Vert:** TE seam is a real primary possibility against linebacker/safety; backside receiver is isolated.

**20 Empty Vert:** one back flexes and can become seam/wheel. This is a weekly call only when backs can credibly run the route.

### Post-Cross Variants

**12 Ace Post-Cross:** hard run presentation, outside post plus TE/WR deep over, max protection available.

**21 Pro Post-Cross:** lead-action presentation, post occupies middle safety, cross emerges behind linebackers.

**6OL Heavy Post-Cross:** extra protection and heavy personnel force run posture. Route count is small; shot must be triggered by coverage, not hope.

## 10.22 Receiver Release Rules

- Versus press, win leverage without destroying timing. A receiver on a quick concept cannot take three seconds to make a beautiful release.
- Stack/bunch players avoid initiating illegal picks. Create traffic by alignment and route spacing, not by running through defenders.
- Vertical clear routes must actually threaten vertically. Jogging a clear allows the safety or corner to poach the primary route.
- Crossers maintain depth. Two receivers at the same depth turn concept spacing into a collision.
- Against zone, settle only when the concept gives permission. Freelance sitting destroys the quarterback's progression.

## 10.23 Catch and Run Rules

Short-game receivers catch with the expectation of getting vertical unless leverage clearly dictates otherwise. The sideline is not the goal. On third down, the sticks change the decision: a receiver may need to attack the marker rather than maximize theoretical yards after catch.

## 10.24 Route Tags

| **Tag**    | **Effect**                                                                                        |
|------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ALERT POST | Designated outside or slot receiver converts to post only when the pre-snap matchup is favorable. |
| GO         | Convert designated route to vertical clear/shot.                                                  |
| WHEEL      | Flat/back route becomes wheel up sideline.                                                        |
| CHOICE     | Eligible runs the defined leverage option for that concept.                                       |
| BENDER     | Inside vertical may bend away from leverage into open middle.                                     |
| SIT        | Crosser/whip settles in zone instead of running through traffic.                                  |
| RETURN     | Motion plus route return used to expose man or create stack leverage.                             |

## 10.25 Special Pass Calls

A few routes are carried as tags rather than entire families:

- **Out-and-Up:** tagged to an outside receiver after Curl-Flat/Smoke tendencies are established. Expected yardage 15-28 on completion.
- **Sluggo:** slant-and-go tag against corners jumping quick slants. Expected yardage 15-30+.
- **Wheel:** back/slot converts flat path up sideline. Expected yardage 10-25 depending coverage.
- **Double Post:** shot tag from condensed 12/13 personnel when safeties overplay run/cross action.
- **Fade Stop:** outside vertical converts back downhill when corner stays on top; weekly matchup tag.

These tags exist because the base concept set already created the defender behavior they punish.

# 11. PLAY-ACTION AND MOVEMENT PASSES

Play-action must look like a run the offense actually calls. The fake, line demeanor, back path, and formation should match the paired run closely enough to affect second-level defenders. Stone does not carry a large collection of decorative fakes disconnected from the run menu.

| COUNTER BOOT       | Counter                   | Cross / intermediate sail / flat      | NAKED/TURNBACK    | 11, 12, 21          | 8-16                 | Move launch point away from counter flow and attack backside contain.                    |
| OUTSIDE BOOT       | Outside Zone              | Deep sail / crosser / flat            | NAKED             | 11, 12, 21          | 8-16                 | Core edge-conflict pass paired with Outside Zone.                                        |
| LEAD POST          | Lead/Iso                  | Post / dig or cross / flat            | MAX               | 21, 22              | 15-28                | Hard under-center action designed to pull single-high safety/linebackers forward.        |
| SPLIT LEAK         | Split Zone                | Cross with delayed TE/H leak backside | TURNBACK          | 12, 13, 21          | 10-20                | Punish edge defender squeezing split action and second-level overplay.                   |

## 11.1 Shot Discipline

A shot is tagged on the call sheet with its setup. Examples: Power x2 -> Power Pass, Outside Zone strong -> Boot back, Doubles Stick/hitch -> Mills, Heavy Duo -> Post-Cross. If the setup never develops because the defense refuses to react, Stone may remove the shot instead of forcing it.

## 11.2 Play-Action Fake Standards

A fake is graded like a block. The quarterback and back must sell the same first steps used on the paired run. Linemen cannot illegally travel downfield on a true pass, so their demeanor sells the run without violating pass rules. The back does not stare at the defender he is trying to influence.

Boot direction and route spacing are paired with the run's normal pursuit. Outside Zone Boot attacks the defender chasing the stretch. Counter Boot attacks the backside player who squeezes down with pull action. Power Pass attacks safeties and linebackers who trigger downhill on guard pull and heavy surfaces.

## 11.3 Movement Pass Progression

Movement passes use a simple three-level rule whenever possible:

1. **Deep:** post/corner/over if coverage gives clean explosive.
2. **Intermediate:** cross/out/sail route.
3. **Flat:** immediate completion or QB edge run if completely uncovered.

The QB should not drift backward while reading. Gain depth, get shoulders organized, then attack the edge. A defender outside the launch path changes the call's value; the QB is not required to outrun a free edge every time.

# 12. SCREEN GAME

Screens are treated as run-game cousins: timing, angles, and blockers matter more than route-artistry. The screen menu is intentionally smaller than the supplied draft. No screen exists merely because a position can theoretically catch one.

## 12.1 Screen Sequencing

The screen call sheet tracks rush behavior. If defensive ends are screaming upfield, RB Slow and Middle rise in value. If corners are bailing, Smoke/Now becomes a cheap run substitute. If pressure is arriving through linebackers, Slip and Tunnel are preferred. A screen should answer something the defense is doing.

## 12.2 Screen Blocking Rules

- Sell normal pass protection long enough to invite rush, but do not allow free contact on the quarterback.
- Releasing linemen take angles to landmarks and defenders, not merely open grass.
- First blocker secures the most dangerous immediate threat; second/third blockers work inside-out or according to weekly screen rule.
- Receiver screens require legal blocking timing. Do not turn a good leverage throw into an offensive pass-interference penalty.
- The ball carrier follows leverage. Cutting behind a blocker before the defender commits wastes the screen design.

## 12.3 Fake Screens and Complements

Stone carries only a few complements:

**Bubble Pump Go:** after corners/safeties trigger hard on repeated bubble access, pump and attack outside vertical. Shot only.

**RB Screen Fake Cross:** back shows screen path before clearing space for intermediate cross/dig. Low frequency because the fake can slow protection timing.

**Tunnel Fake Draw:** receivers show tunnel posture while QB gives draw action. Weekly package against defenses that sprint outside to screen presentation.

## 12.4 Special Plays and Gadgets

Special plays are a small weekly folder, not a separate offense.

| **Play**         | **Structure**                                                    | **Expected Yardage** | **Carry Rule**                                                                  |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

Stone does not call a trick play because the offense is bored. It must attack a documented pursuit or coverage tendency.

# 13. ACCESS AND PACKAGED PLAYS

The 2013 system uses a small set of run/pass packages. Stone's term is **ACCESS**. These are not a giant modern RPO tree. The quarterback is normally reading one leverage or conflict defender before or immediately after the snap. The offensive line executes the called run. The throw is short enough to protect the line from lingering downfield.

| **Package**     | **Run**           | **Read**                        | **QB Rule**                                                                                                                 | **Expected Yardage** |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| INSIDE + BUBBLE | Inside Zone       | Trips-side overhang / numbers   | Hand off unless the offense has clear perimeter numbers or leverage for immediate bubble.                                   | 4-6 run / 4-8 pass   |
| INSIDE + STICK  | Inside Zone       | Apex LB over slot/Y             | If apex folds into box or widens poorly, throw immediate stick; otherwise hand off.                                         | 4-6 run / 4-7 pass   |
| POWER + SMOKE   | Power             | Corner cushion / receiver count | Take smoke only if the corner gifts space and blocker count is clean. Otherwise run Power.                                  | 3-6 run / 3-7 pass   |
| ZONE + SLANT    | Inside/Split Zone | Backside LB or overhang         | Slant replaces a second-level defender who inserts hard into run fit. This is a selective weekly package, not base offense. | 4-6 run / 5-9 pass   |

## 13.1 Access Guardrails

- One clear read. Do not turn an access call into a five-option backyard play.
- If the box and leverage are neutral, run the called run.
- Ball is out immediately on access throws.
- Do not use access merely because it is fashionable. If a defense is playing sound numbers and tackling well, return to the normal run/pass menu.
- The weekly plan may carry only two of the four packages.

## 13.2 Access Examples by Defensive Picture

**Trips Inside + Bubble vs 6-man box, 2 defenders over 3 receivers:** throw bubble if blocking leverage is clean. The defense has chosen to protect the box.

**Trips Inside + Bubble vs 5-man box, 3 defenders over 3 receivers:** hand Inside. Do not throw into equal numbers merely because Bubble is attached.

**Doubles Inside + Stick vs apex linebacker tucked into box:** if slot has immediate space and defender cannot recover, ACCESS Stick. If apex widens before snap, run Inside.

**Power + Smoke vs off corner:** Smoke is a gift only if receiver can catch and gain useful yards before support arrives. If corner is off but safety is driving downhill and blocker leverage is poor, run Power.

## 13.3 Access Practice Rule

The offense practices the run and throw as one play. The line must not react to the quarterback's decision. Receivers must block/run routes at full intent. The quarterback should make the decision early enough that the mesh and ball handling remain clean.

# 14. SIX-OFFENSIVE-LINEMAN PACKAGE

Stone does not claim to have invented using an extra offensive lineman. His distinction is making the sixth lineman a real package with complementary answers instead of a one-call declaration that "heavy run" is coming.

The sixth lineman is usually a tackle body. He may align as an attached eligible after reporting, as an extra ineligible surface, or as part of an unbalanced line according to league formation and reporting rules. The weekly plan specifies eligibility and alignment before the game.

## 14.1 6OL Families

| **Family** | **Structure**                                                         | **Core Calls**                                | **Constraint**                                                    |
|------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| HEAVY      | Extra tackle attached to create an additional edge.                   | Power, Duo, Counter, Outside, Power Pass.     | Do not make every snap a run. Carry at least one real shot.       |
| OVER       | Tackle-over/unbalanced surface. Defense must declare strength.        | Power to/away, Counter back, Toss, Boot away. | Formation legality and eligible numbers confirmed in weekly plan. |
| NUB        | Extra OL creates heavy surface opposite a reduced single receiver/TE. | Duo, Counter, play-action Post-Cross.         | Use formation to isolate backside coverage.                       |
| WING       | Extra OL attached with Y/H off him.                                   | Power, Split/Wham, Pin-Pull, TE delay screen. | H/Y must still be a credible receiver.                            |
| LEAK       | Rare eligible-tackle complement from a previously run-heavy picture.  | Block-release flat/leak only.                 | Change-up. Never build core pass game around 6OL route running.   |

## 14.2 6OL Rule

Every 6OL game plan contains at least three categories: one downhill run, one run away from the extra surface or misdirection call, and one pass. If the defense substitutes oversized personnel, the offense may HOLD the package and spread the remaining eligibles to create a pass matchup.

## 14.3 Sample 6OL Mini-Menu

A normal 6OL week should be small enough to execute cleanly:

1. Heavy Right, Power R
2. Heavy Right, Counter L
3. Over Left, Duo L
4. Over Left, Boot Flood R
5. Nub Right, Post-Cross, Max
6. Wing Left, TE Delay or Leak if the eligible-lineman rule is active

The defense should see six calls, not sixty. Stone's innovation is complementary structure, not an enormous separate 6OL playbook.

## 14.4 6OL Substitution Sequence

A preferred sequence is:

1. ROTATE into 6OL.
2. Observe whether defense answers with base, goal-line, or an extra defensive lineman.
3. Run the call selected for that response.
4. If matchup is favorable, HOLD the same eleven.
5. Shift/spread eligible players without substituting and call pass or run away from heavy surface.

This sequence turns the extra lineman into information and personnel leverage rather than a single snap of brute force.

## 15.1 Two-Point Menu

Carry six two-point calls each week: two heavy, two bunch/man answers, one movement pass, and one tendency breaker. Do not automatically recycle a goal-line call if the defense has already seen the formation and adjustment.

## 15.2 Red-Zone Structure

The field compresses vertically, so Stone changes spacing rather than merely running normal concepts into less grass.

**High red zone (20-11):** full run menu remains alive. Cross, Dagger, Sail, and Smash are still viable. Shots become more precise because safety depth is reduced.

**Low red zone (10-5):** bunch/stack, movement passes, TE matchups, quick pivots, Power/Counter, and heavy-to-space shifts rise in value. Deep crossers become shorter and faster.

**Goal line (4-in):** reduce negative-play risk. Sneak, Lead, Power, Counter, and a small play-action folder. The offense may spread 11/12 if the defense overcommits big bodies, but only with a call practiced from that field location.

## 15.3 Two-Minute Mechanics

Two-minute offense uses 10, 11, and 20 personnel most often, but Stone prefers to avoid substituting once the desired group is on the field.

- First down in bounds: center gets ball set; receivers confirm formation from signal; quarterback calls next concept.
- Completed short of first down: nearest receiver gives ball to official, not teammate.
- Clock stop available: decide before snap whether boundary completion is essential or middle-field completion plus timeout is acceptable.
- Sack: immediate clock/field awareness. Do not compound sack with procedural confusion.
- After explosive: PRESS to line only if next call is already communicated; otherwise use timeout/huddle rather than wasting the gain.

Core two-minute concepts: Spacing, Stick, Drive, Mesh, Curl-Flat, Vert, Draw, RB Screen, Smoke/Now.

## 15.4 Four-Minute Mechanics

The offense wants first downs more than rushing statistics. Stay in bounds when practical, make the defense spend timeouts, and use play-action if safeties overcommit. A predictable three-run sequence that produces a punt is not "physical football." Stone will throw a safe cross, boot, or quick conversion if the defense sells out to stop the run.

## 15.5 Third-Down Sticks Rules

Routes are taught relative to the sticks. On 3rd-and-5, a six-yard stick route cannot settle at four because a defender contacted the receiver. On 3rd-and-12, a five-yard checkdown is useful only when the catch-run matchup can realistically reach the line to gain or the protection has collapsed with no better option.

## 16.3 Box Count Rule

The quarterback does not treat every light box as an automatic audible. The called run must still have leverage and blocking numbers. Likewise, a loaded box does not automatically kill a run if the offense has the extra surface, puller, or matchup needed to block it. Box count starts the decision; front structure completes it.

## 17.4 Role Interchangeability Limits

Portability has boundaries. A 260-pound TE may own the same **job name** as a slot receiver but execute it with different leverage and speed. A back can flex into a route role only if he has practiced the release and landmark. An extra offensive lineman is not treated as a normal tight end because the chart says "eligible."

The system changes the person without pretending all people are identical.

# 18. WEEKLY GAME-PLANNING PROCESS

The playbook is larger than the weekly call sheet. The weekly plan selects what the team can execute at full speed and what attacks the opponent's rules.

## 18.1 Monday-Tuesday: Build the Defensive Map

Chart personnel, front, pressure, coverage, motion adjustment, formation adjustment, third-down package, red-zone package, and substitution tendencies. Identify the defenders the offense wants to put in conflict. Do not begin by choosing favorite plays.

## 18.2 Wednesday: Select the Core

Choose:

- 4-5 primary runs;
- 6-8 primary dropback concepts;
- 3-4 play-action/movement concepts;
- 3 screens;
- 2 access packages;
- 2-4 shots;
- 6 two-point/goal-line specials;
- one 6OL package if roster and opponent justify it.

The same concept may occupy multiple categories when it is carried from multiple presentations.

## 18.3 Thursday: Build Complements

For every major run, identify at least one complement. For every formation the defense is likely to key, identify a tendency breaker. Build the third-down and red-zone menus independently rather than assuming normal-down calls will solve those situations.

## 18.4 Friday-Saturday: Reduce

Delete calls that are redundant, poorly practiced, or require a player to perform a job he has not demonstrated. The final sheet should feel smaller than Wednesday's install. A call removed on Friday is not a failure; it is a decision.

## 18.7 Personnel Usage Planning

The staff builds a **playing-time map**, not a yardage quota. Each skill player has a list of concepts he can execute without reducing the call. # APPENDIX C. HISTORICAL GROUNDING NOTES

These notes define the historical rails used to keep Iteration I plausible for 2013. They are not claims that Stone copies any single real playbook.

1. **Concept-based Erhardt-Perkins organization.** A January 2013 ESPN/Grantland discussion of New England's offense described pass plays as concepts that can be called from many formations, with the participants changing jobs while the theory of the play remains stable. This is the historical basis for Stone's concept-portability structure.
2. **Multiple personnel as a real Payton/Saints trait.** NFL analysis of New Orleans before and around this era repeatedly emphasized varied personnel, formations, pre-snap movement, empty looks, and matchup creation. The Stone system inherits that preference but uses shorter calls and a more explicit portability matrix.
3. **11 personnel was already a league base grouping.** Retrospective NFL data placed 11 personnel at more than half of league snaps by 2013. Stone therefore does not "invent" spread personnel. His distinction is maintaining meaningful 12/21/20/heavy volume without making those groupings obvious tells.
4. **Packaged run/pass ideas existed in 2013.** Contemporary coverage of Chip Kelly's first Eagles season discussed packaged/combo plays and zone-read concepts. Iteration I therefore permits a small Access package, while withholding the much larger RPO structures that belong to later evolution.
5. **Running-back committees were already normal enough to be historically grounded.** The 2013 Patriots publicly discussed rotating multiple backs and expecting them to execute the same run game. Stone's difference is systematizing skill-player rotation across the offense, not claiming to invent committees.
6. **Extra offensive linemen predate Stone.** New Orleans used an extra offensive tackle/six-man line before 2013 to help protection and alter structure. Stone's innovation is the breadth of complementary calls carried from the package, not the existence of 6OL itself.
7. **Substitution has a cost.** NFL substitution rules allow the defense a reasonable opportunity to respond after offensive substitutions. ROTATE/HOLD/PRESS is designed around that reality: Stone can substitute heavily, then keep the same eleven to prevent another defensive matchup change.

Selected historical references used in construction:

- ESPN / Grantland, January 2013 discussion of New England's Erhardt-Perkins concept language.
- NFL.com, June and November 2013 coverage of Chip Kelly's read-option and packaged-play approach.
- Patriots.com, September-November 2013 reporting and coach comments on running-back rotation and shared run-game responsibilities.
- NFL.com analysis of the Saints' multiple personnel, empty formations, and extra offensive lineman use.
- NFL Rulebook, Rule 5 substitution procedures from the period.

**Run:** Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Outside Zone.

**Pass:** Stick, Mesh, Drive, Y-Cross, Four Verticals.

**Movement:** Outside Boot Flood.

**Screen:** RB Slow, Bubble/Smoke access.

**Situation:** QB Sneak, one goal-line play-action.

These calls receive the most repetitions and the broadest personnel portability.

## E.2 Strong Secondary Calls

Duo, Lead/Iso, Split Zone, Toss/Crack, Smash, Curl-Flat, Shallow, Dagger, Sail, Texas, Post-Cross, Mills, Snag, Spacing, play-action Power Pass, Tunnel Screen.

These are fully taught but can shrink in a weekly plan.

## E.3 Change-Up Folder

Trap, Wham, Pin-Pull, Jet Sweep, Whip/Pivot, Scissors, Out-and-Up, Sluggo, TE Delay, Throwback Screen, flea flicker, selected 6OL leak.

A change-up that does not get practiced does not get called merely because it appears in the master book.

## E.4 Why There Are No Play Numbers 1-735

The offense is not improved by assigning a new number to Mesh from Bunch, Mesh from Trips, Mesh from 12, and Mesh from 21. Those are presentations of one family. Numbering every presentation encourages players and the simulator to treat window dressing as a new play, which defeats the Erhardt-Perkins concept foundation.

The simulation should generate legal presentations from:

**Personnel x Formation x Motion/Shift x Concept x Tag x Protection**, subject to the compatibility matrices and weekly active menu.

That gives the offense genuine multiplicity while keeping the human teaching load realistic.

## E.5 Simulation Rules

1. Do not select concepts marked inactive in the weekly plan unless a game-state emergency expands the menu.
2. Do not generate a personnel presentation that the compatibility matrix marks - without an explicit roster-specific package.
3. Use expected yardage as a planning distribution band, not guaranteed output.
4. Player ability modifies the result and may remove a presentation entirely.
5. Formation and motion can alter defensive response without changing the concept identity.
6. Repeated success increases the chance Stone calls the same simple family again; the AI should not force novelty for novelty's sake.
7. Stone may abandon a planned shot if its trigger never appears.
8. Rotation changes playing time, not automatically touch share.
9. A hot player can override the planned rotation.
10. If the offense substitutes, the defense receives its normal opportunity to match; HOLD/PRESS is the mechanism for exploiting the resulting matchup without another offensive substitution.

# STONE 2013 OFFENSE: ONE-PAGE CARD

**Identity:** physical downhill run game + concept-based passing + personnel portability + vertical aggression.

**Run first principles:** Power / Counter / Inside / Outside. Duo, Lead, Split, Pin-Pull rotate in by opponent. Toss and Jet punish edge behavior.

**Pass first principles:** Stick for leverage. Mesh for man. Drive for linebackers. Y-Cross for intermediate space. Dagger for the middle. Four Verticals and Post-Cross for explosives.

**Personnel principle:** change the matchup without changing the concept.

**Rotation principle:** two or three runners can receive real playing time; no equal-share requirement. Fresh players matter only if substitutions do not reveal the play.

**Tempo principle:** ROTATE to use the roster. HOLD to hide tendencies. PRESS to trap the defense.

**QB principle:** identify point, box, shell, pressure, leverage. Take free access. Do not invent a new offense at the line.

**6OL principle:** extra lineman means a package, not a run tell. Carry run to it, run away from it, and a pass.

**Play-caller principle:** show it, stress it, punish it.
