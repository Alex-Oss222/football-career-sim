## Player playbook access and evidence rule

Every participant may possess and study the **complete active 2013 Iteration I playbook**. The phase plan controls what the staff formally installs, practices, and evaluates together; it does not ration access to pages. A player may read ahead and ask about later material. He is evaluated on what has actually been assigned and taught for the phase.

No preset personnel percentage, snap share, rep quota, touch quota, or depth-chart probability determines the result of this phase. Reps and roles are adjusted from football evidence: assignment, communication, technique, physical execution, health, correction retention, matchup, special teams, and what the player demonstrates in practices and games. The plan creates opportunities to evaluate; it does not manufacture the answer.


Training camp converts offseason teaching into competitive NFL football.

The question changes from "Can we teach this?" to:

**Can we trust this player and this unit when speed, contact, fatigue, substitution, field position, opponent behavior, and consequences are real?**

Camp is not designed to make players miserable. It is designed to make the roster and system dependable.

## Camp entry standard

Before field competition begins:

1. Verify current roster/control status.
2. Verify medical participation status.
3. Complete onboarding for every late acquisition.
4. Confirm every player has the active 2013 Iteration I playbook and current position material.
5. Deliver the individualized camp-entry expectations created after minicamp or after acquisition.
6. Conduct the medically cleared, position-adjusted camp conditioning test described in the Prowl readiness standard.
7. Record the test standard before players take it and apply it consistently to comparable football jobs.
8. If a player does not meet the entry standard, assign an individualized conditioning/retest plan. Do not use random punishment running.
9. Do not treat a failed conditioning test as an automatic depth-chart verdict.
10. Freeze the opening camp install/practice goals before evaluating players.

## Camp learning architecture

Camp operates through four progressive blocks. The exact dates and number of practices in each block are set only after the real calendar is verified.

### Block 1 — re-establish and acclimate

Purpose:

- re-establish terminology;
- verify offseason retention;
- re-establish individual fundamentals;
- confirm communication;
- rebuild practice tempo safely;
- identify players who need immediate reteaching.

Scheme emphasis:

- core offense;
- base defense;
- core special teams;
- substitution procedure;
- huddle/cadence;
- basic situations.

The staff should not interpret early rust as permanent inability without giving the player the correction/repetition cycle.

### Block 2 — pads, physical execution, and competition

As rules and medical status permit, add the physical component that offseason work could not fully test.

Evaluate:

- leverage;
- block finish/control;
- block defeat;
- contact balance;
- route release;
- pass protection;
- run fits;
- tackling technique and finish under permitted practice structure;
- ball security under pressure;
- physical response to repeated football work.

This is where some spring conclusions should change. The staff should allow them to.

### Block 3 — pressure, variation, and situation

Stress the system with:

- different personnel;
- formation/motion;
- pressure presentation;
- coverage changes;
- third down;
- red zone;
- backed up;
- two minute;
- four minute;
- sudden change;
- end-of-half/game;
- noise/communication;
- substitute entering unexpectedly;
- starter leaving a period;
- special-teams emergency jobs.

The goal is not chaos. The goal is recognizable problems.

### Block 4 — preseason/game operation

Reduce the menu enough to evaluate actual game execution.

Test:

- sideline communication;
- substitutions;
- play-call operation;
- headset/huddle mechanics where applicable;
- clock awareness;
- halftime adjustment;
- game-day special teams;
- backup readiness;
- injury substitution;
- reduced game-plan learning.

Preseason outcomes belong in game/event records, not in this plan.

## Installation rule: mastery before menu size

Training camp opens more of Iteration I, but not indiscriminately.

### CORE

Core concepts should become dependable enough to survive:

- different personnel;
- different fronts/coverages;
- ordinary pressure;
- substitution;
- field zone;
- fatigue.

If a CORE call cannot reach that standard, the staff must identify whether the problem is personnel, teaching, technique, or concept fit.

### WEEKLY

Weekly concepts can be installed and evaluated as game-plan candidates.

A weekly concept earns carry-forward if:

- its teaching burden is justified;
- the roster has players for the jobs;
- it solves a real problem;
- players can execute it without damaging core reliability.

### CHANGE-UP

Change-ups stay small.

A clever call is not valuable if the team needs excessive practice time to run it once.

### Active-iteration lock

Nothing from Stone's 2016-or-later offensive evolution can be imported because it would solve a 2014 problem elegantly. The 2014 team develops only the system Stone has actually reached.

## Offensive camp plan

### Run game

Core priorities:

- Power;
- Counter;
- Inside Zone;
- Outside Zone.

Additional families such as Duo, Split Zone, Pin-Pull, Trap, Wham, Toss/Crack, Jet, Draw, or QB sneak/follow are installed/emphasized only as the roster and weekly purpose justify.

Evaluation questions:

- Are the combinations sound?
- Are backs hitting the intended track?
- Is the perimeter correctly accounted for?
- Does the quarterback understand the run/check responsibility?
- Does the concept still work from multiple presentations?
- Which linemen/backs execute it best together?

### Pass game

Core/major families are developed as concepts, not isolated route drawings.

Evaluate:

- spacing;
- route depth;
- leverage;
- progression timing;
- protection timing;
- checkdown;
- scramble reaction;
- catch/finish;
- ball security;
- whether one position change forces the whole play to be retaught.

### Protection

Pressure periods should teach answers, not merely create sacks in practice.

Quarterback/line/back must agree on:

- point;
- slide/man responsibility;
- weak point;
- hot answer;
- back scan/check;
- adjustment versus overload/mugged presentations;
- when the correct answer is to throw behind pressure or move the launch point rather than add a blocker.

### Personnel packages

The offense should determine which roster combinations genuinely expand the system.

Questions:

- Can 11 run the full core?
- Does 12 create real run/pass multiplicity?
- Does 21/20 improve the roster's best players or just satisfy a diagram?
- Does heavy personnel still threaten pass?
- Which backs/TEs/receivers allow HOLD/PRESS without substitution?

There are no controlling personnel planning percentages. The current roster, opponent, practice evidence, health, and game performance determine which packages actually earn use.

### Tempo

NORMAL is the foundation.

ROTATE tests substitutions.

HOLD tests formation/role portability.

PRESS is used with mastered calls and only when the current eleven can operate it.

FREEZE/MILK or other tempo tools are taught as real game operations, not practice theater.

## Defensive camp plan

The defense should become difficult to play against because it is sound first, multiple second.

### Run defense

Evaluate:

- gap integrity;
- block defeat;
- edge;
- linebacker fit;
- safety support;
- pursuit;
- tackling;
- communication versus shift/motion.

Do not confuse adding a defender to the box with solving run defense.

### Pass defense

Evaluate:

- rush-lane integrity;
- pressure timing;
- leverage;
- coverage spacing;
- Cover 1 press/off man technique and help discipline;
- Nickel Even communication and fit integrity;
- 1 Robber / Robber-Rat post, underneath robber/rat and flat/force ownership;
- match/zone communication in the paired Cover 3/Quarters answers;
- route distribution;
- bunch, stack, motion, pick/rub and crossing-release responses;
- linebacker/back and tight-end man matchups;
- explosive-play prevention;
- scramble response.

### Man-capable Nickel competition

Camp is where the spring emphasis becomes a physical football test. Use Nickel Even as the central sub-package body when personnel and down/distance support it. Give Cover 1 and 1 Robber/Rat repeated competitive work with the actual corners, nickel, safeties and linebackers who have earned those assignments. Evaluate press disruption, off-man transitions, post-safety range, robber/rat timing, flat/force leverage, recovery after separation and the ability to tackle after man coverage.

Do not make one favorable corner matchup a reason to isolate every linebacker or safety. When a back, tight end, bunch or stack creates a bad man assignment, test the active book's help, bracket, personnel and Cover 3/Quarters answers. Carry the same pre-snap pictures across categories so offenses cannot identify man merely from alignment. Cover 0 remains a low-volume situational tool, not the identity.

The camp result decides what Crennel can trust in preseason and later weekly plans. The plan itself sets no snap-share or call-share quota.

### Pressure and disguise

Pressure must have a reason.

Camp can expand:

- four-man rush from different presentations;
- five-man pressure;
- overload looks;
- simulated/exchange pressure concepts present in the current defensive system;
- late safety movement;
- disguise.

If the defense busts because it cannot communicate the presentation, simplify it.

## Good, Better, Best in camp

**Good**
- player knows the job;
- communicates;
- executes base technique;
- can function in the practice.

**Better**
- correction holds;
- technique survives changing pictures and repeated work;
- player needs less rescue;
- situational awareness grows.

**Best for current camp**
- player executes fast and reliably;
- can handle adjacent responsibilities;
- stabilizes teammates;
- survives physical/situational variation;
- self-corrects routine issues.

These are phase descriptions, not permanent numeric ratings.
