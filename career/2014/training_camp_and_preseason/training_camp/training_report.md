# Jacksonville | Training camp | July 21 to August 1, 2014

<!-- sim-meta: {"event_entry": 116, "kind": "phase_output", "status": "IN_PROGRESS", "through": "2014-08-01"} -->

Jacksonville came back able to run the spring assignments without starting the teaching over, and the first two padded practices showed that the understanding mostly survives contact. The first five practices (July 25 to 29, without full pads) restored the core offense, the defensive calls and the kicking operation, then changed formations and personnel to see whether the answers still held. July 30 was the first full-pad practice and August 1 the second, with July 31 the players' protected day off. The padded work ran the sequence the coordinators set at the July 27 review, in order, with every period scripted as walkthrough, non-contact or controlled contact; no period was live to the ground, quarterbacks were not contacted and the whistle ended the rep. The physical findings below are of that class: a block secured or lost, a release won or late, a fit made or missed, the instructed tackle fitted and wrapped, not a tackle finished to the ground. The main unfinished question is whether the combinations that held on two padded days hold through the August 2 scrimmage and repeated work.

Every signed player reported on his assigned date, rookies and first-year players on July 21 and veterans on July 24. The medical staff communicated no new participation restriction at report or after either padded practice, and the position-adjusted conditioning test produced no failure requiring a retest plan. Lucas worked both padded sessions in full; his foot review remains on the performance staff's list. Bradfield, Clemons, Brown and Pasztor remain outside the program with unsigned tenders. There is no recorded coach fitness test to assess.

## Offense: putting the playbook on the field

Tice's group began with the huddle, substitutions, cadence and the protection point, then ran the retained menu against defensive looks. Power, Counter and Inside Zone shared the work with the quick and crossing concepts and the movement passes taught in spring. The useful finding was that the players could get to their jobs without a coach supplying the answer on the first rep back.

The interior line carried its spring correction into the changed fronts and then into contact. Against the double A-gap presentation and the five-man pressure, HALF went toward the overload, and the guards communicated the change in time; on July 30 and August 1 the same answer held at contact speed, with Brewster resetting the point against the mug and the slide going the right way on every recorded team rep. That settles the shared part of protection for now. The physical part was mixed, which is what Block 2 is for. Bitonio anchored against Marks on the inside combination and over-set once, opening the inside lane to Marks's counter; Yarno corrected the set angle and inside hand, and the repeat and the August 1 rep were correct. Norwell, beside Brewster at left guard in the mixed group, carried the ACE combination through to the linebacker on the recorded Power reps and lost initial leverage once to Miller in protection, recovering without the pocket collapsing. Turner held his anchor against Mosley and C.J. Wilson and lost one one-on-one rep to Donald's first step, late with his hands; in the inside-run period the ACE combination of Brewster and Turner moved Donald off the strong A-gap on the recorded reps and Donald split it once. One lost rep is not the plan's "struggles," and Turner's conditional swap was not triggered.

The relief pairings did the same work under a rush. Henne and Linsley made the point on time again, and Linsley lost initial leverage once to Mosley in the one-on-one period and recovered. Wilson worked with Shatley and Larsen, who held their recorded combination blocks with no correction. Linsley has now given the staff a better camp opening than his minicamp, but two padded days do not answer how the pair handles a collapsing pocket over a game.

Changing the offensive picture did not require a different answer on every page. Boot Flood kept its taught response to the free edge from both Wing and Ace. Y-Cross kept its staggered spacing with one tight end and with two. Hawkins supplied the correction worth following: from a reduced split on July 26, he cut the dig a yard short. His read was intact, but the route was not at its taught depth. Drake corrected it; Hawkins reached the right depth on the repeat, the following day and again on July 29. On July 30 the same dig came up short once more against Poyer's jam in the slot, this time a contact cause, not spacing; Drake kept the two causes separate, and on August 1 the depth held through the jam. Wilson also retained his Sprint Flood depth.

The running backs met resistance for the first time. Jones-Drew pressed the Power landmark and read the puller, not open grass, on the recorded reps, and his ball security held through the controlled strip attempts in the ball-security period. Anderson bounced one Inside Zone rep before pressing the interior; Spencer corrected it, and the August 1 reps were correct. Jonathan Grimes's track was correct with no correction recorded.

## Defense: playing the call together

Crennel brought back the base and Nickel Even work with the responsibilities stated before the snap. Cover 1 and Robber/Rat required the players to know the man assignment, the deep help, the underneath robber or rat, and the flat or force job. Cover 3 and Quarters were worked from similar starting pictures, so the appearance of the defense did not supply the whole answer to the offense.

The spring corrections survived the break. Rambo used the taught ROLL trigger, Harris held his path through the rub, Poyer checked the second threat before driving from the rat position, and Stanford retained his wheel leverage. Motion, bunches and stacks then tested whether the group could exchange those responsibilities while moving. The voice and hand signals arrived on time in the recorded work.

Pads added the fits. In the 9-on-7 inside-run period the Under fit was named before every snap and held on the recorded Power and Inside Zone reps: Marks owned the weak B-gap, Miller controlled the shade, Posluszny and Daryl Smith fit the strong B and weak A, and the overhang took force by call. The edge tags were used as written: on SPILL, Davis attacked the kick-out and Moore arrived as the next support defender; on BOX, Branch and Te'o-Nesheim kept the ball inside against Lewis's and Kelce's attached blocks. The one fit error came on Counter, which punishes fast flow: Telvin Smith fit the strong gap with the flow on July 30 and the wrap came through the gap he left. Bush corrected the read of the puller, and on August 1 he fit the counter correctly against the same misdirection. Tackling was taught as fit and wrap to the whistle, on bags for the finish; no tackle to the ground was scored for anyone, by design.

The edge rush was tested against a live set for the first time. Mincey kept contain on every recorded rep and won the edge once against Johnson with the long arm. Branch produced the better isolated win, beating Johnson around the corner, and on one July 30 team rep did it by leaving the lane, which the book grades as a pressure error however the rep looked. Crennel made the point once; on August 1 Branch kept contain and still produced pressure on a recorded rep. PEEL was executed correctly when the back released immediately. Edebali and Jeffcoat won and lost one-on-one reps against Leno, Lucas and Feiler, in their lanes each time.

In coverage, Crennel tested the linebacker-on-back vertical in man for the first time, as the plan asks. Daryl Smith carried Jones-Drew's wheel with the taught leverage and Lowery's post help arrived where the rule puts it; Stanford held his wheel leverage once against Jonathan Grimes and lost the top of the route once, and Bush's correction was the same leverage point as June 18. Press technique was now lawful: Talib re-routed Nicks at the line on recorded reps, Verner lost leverage to Adams at the line once and recovered to the dig, and Harris held his rub path with contact. Butler's corrected transition from press was late once against Hurns; Oden corrected it and the August 1 rep was on time. Poyer's rat vision held under the live five-man rush. Another period showed why the call matters when judging a result: the offense's hot throw reached the flat against the five-man pressure, but that was the throw the defense had elected to concede. Rush and replacement ownership were correct. Calling it a coverage bust would have misread the assignment.

The defense also operated with its usual communicator taken out of the huddle. Daryl Smith relayed the calls first; Posluszny then handled the work without him, and Stanford handled a period with both absent. The fast-motion TRAVEL signal still reached the group. Moore relayed his own packages. That gives the staff evidence about the replacement communication it actually rehearsed.

## Situations and special teams

Cousins and Henne ran two-minute and four-minute work on the clock without needing the situation restated. Cousins made the recorded boundary-or-middle decisions correctly and reached his second progression on time when the first answer was taken away. In the padded team periods he made the same decisions with a live rush in front of him and was not contacted. Wilson ran the core; Bray and Shaw worked their reduced menu in seven-on-seven. This established operation at the pace practiced; a defense that does not stop at the whistle is still ahead.

Westhoff changed personnel in the substitution work and gave Anger end-game and plus-territory punt instructions. The recorded punts went to the assigned side. Cain and Kreiter both kept the snap operation intact across the four punt families and in the field-goal work, and the emergency snapper and holder paths were rehearsed without a fault. On July 30 the operation ran against a controlled rush for the first time: both snappers' operations held, and the interior protection passed an A-gap rusher late on one recorded rep. Westhoff corrected the exchange, and the August 1 reps were clean. The coverage units released against a hold-up and kept their lanes; several players fielded punts in the catch work. No returner has been designated, and neither snapper has separated.

## Players and combinations

The open competitions received work in the same periods. Nicks and Adams took WR1 reps; Adams and Hawkins took WR3 reps. Nicks held the taught landmarks against changed leverage and, once press was lawful, won leverage against Talib with his hands while keeping the quick-concept timing. Adams had both successful and unsuccessful release paths against Talib and Verner while keeping his assignment; with contact he won the release and lost the timing on recorded reps, the extra step the book's release rule forbids. Drake corrected it, and on August 1 he kept the timing against Harris and lost one release to Talib. Thielen performed the H job against Poyer in the slot and kept his crossing depth through contact. These observations help Drake assess how they run the concepts; two padded days separate no one.

Bitonio and Norwell worked at left guard, with Turner at right guard and both alternatives rotating through; the contact evidence above is their first, and each had a secured combination and a lost rep on the record. Mincey and Branch took the Edge 1 work and handled contain and PEEL, with Mincey the steadier lane and Branch the better isolated win and the one corrected lane error. The report now has live blocking and rush results, and they do not separate the competitors yet.

Kelce's attached blocking, the promised assessment, began on July 30. His assignment and first step were right on the Power down block and the TREY combination with Johnson; his pad level rose late in the period and the closed end got into his chest on the later reps, the base and hand item from 2013. Zernhelt corrected the base, and on August 1 the early reps were better with the same late-period rise, so the item stays open as the question it was. Lewis sustained his attached blocks against Davis on the recorded reps. Brate sustained the assigned block against the reserve ends; Jensen and Hoskins were assignment-correct with mixed finish and no correction beyond the general teaching.

Reserves were not confined to watching the established players. On July 26 and 27, and again in the padded periods, the rookie groups received comparable assignments against veteran groups: receivers outside and in the slot, tight ends attached and detached, linemen against changing fronts and now against a live rush, linebackers in fit and communication work, and young corners against veteran receivers with press permitted. Lucas's set landmark held against the rookie rushers and he was beaten around the edge once by Branch; Donald's first step won one rep and the double team moved him on the others; Hurns and Gabriel had mixed release results with no correction recorded. The recorded finding for those without a specific correction is assignment correctness and a mixed physical result. It does not establish a hidden standout or a roster winner.
