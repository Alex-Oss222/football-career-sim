## 3. Narrative, two parts

### 3A. Front-office closeout

Monday, February 17. Caldwell had the verification file in two copies with Stone's checklist clipped to the front. He went down it and wrote C, S or U beside each item. Cain took a C for the expiry and a U for the value, $2.45 million in one source and $3.0 million in another. Owens took an S. Wilson and Jonathan Grimes took a U each. He struck $32.8 million and $21.8 million from the search notes, wrote 2015 beside them and initialed the margin. Cain had been on the unresolved list on February 2.

"Cain was never really in doubt," Caldwell said. "Three years from 2011, done after 2013. The one-year minimum is my offer to make, and his answer comes when he decides."

"What about Nwaneri?" Stone said.

"Base $3.775 million, roster bonus $1.0 million, through 2015. Nobody can show me the date the bonus is due."

"Then F1 has no deadline."

"It has one. Your Minnesota package sits behind the same date, and behind Seattle."

Mincey, Mosley and Lowery had more letters than numbers. Caldwell said no release saving would be booked off an estimate. Below them the worksheet carried a line for Blackmon's remaining guarantees. He said Minnesota had asked for Bates and Tice that day and been refused.

"January hasn't changed. The margin and the ball security."

Stone asked whether the tag numbers were final. Caldwell said February 28, and moved the carryover line to the top of the next page.

### 3B. Coach's own team assessment

On offense, Kirk is the starter and I'm not reopening it. What I owe Dave is eighteen interceptions turning into fewer without my cutting the sheet to nothing, and Boot Flood is still in the review pile. The line starts with Eugene: signed, or tagged at four on the third. Lane played the season and has a technical list. Brewster earned center. Maurice ran for 1,334 and I want him on two years. If Seattle pays 36 once Tate signs, Cecil and Justin go, and I would rather draft Adams at 26 than pay a veteran to be the third receiver. Thielen earned what he has. Zernhelt and I owe Kelce the blocking assessment promised in January.

On defense, Romeo's group got off the field on third down, but twenty-three sacks and fifteen takeaways don't carry a playoff game. Sen'Derrick gets paid. The edge is thin, which is why Lawrence and the free-agent end are both on the plan and neither replaces the other. Paul is a medical projection until the doctors say otherwise. Corners are where the money goes: Verner, then Talib, with Brent as the fallback.

In the kicking game, the Houston punt returns and the Cleveland kickoff belong to Mike Westhoff now. I held that job for a month and do not want it back.

On the staff, nobody left. Frank interviewed with Chicago and wasn't offered it, and Minnesota's requests for Jeremy and Mike were turned down the day their coach was hired.
