## 3. Narrative, two parts

### 3A. Front-office closeout

Friday, February 28. The league memo put the cap at $133,000,000 and carried the franchise values on its second page. Caldwell went down the position column to the offensive line, struck through the pencilled projection in the margin beside Monroe's tender line and wrote $11,654,000 on the line itself. He pulled the 2013 sheet, multiplied Monroe's base and his cap number by 1.2, and entered $4,560,000 and $6,897,000 beside the tender with a note that the 2013 row was an old transcription. The adjusted-cap line he left open.

"That's the cap," Stone said. "What's the room?"

"We don't know it yet. The carryover isn't certified, and I've got twenty-eight contracts I can't price."

Later that day Carolina's announcement on Hardy came through: franchise tag. Stone took the memo off the corner of the desk and opened it to section 3.

"Hardy was never the plan," Stone said.

"An offer sheet costs two firsts. We've spent both of ours on paper already, Donald and Adams."

"Then Te'o-Nesheim, with Allen behind him, and Babin stays if both go. Somebody still has to line up across from Lawrence."

"His status is supported. Nobody has shown me he's on the market on the eleventh."

Caldwell wrote Te'o-Nesheim's name at the top of a clean page and, under it, March 8, the first day agents could be called. Then he asked whether Seattle's two releases that day changed anything Stone wanted put in writing before the eleventh.

### 3B. Coach's own team assessment

On offense, nothing moved this week except Eugene's number. He costs $11,654,000 on the tender if nothing else gets done, and I'd rather it got done on the terms I gave Dave. Kirk starts. The line from the left side in is the one we finished with, and Adams is still the plan at 26.

On defense, Carolina took Hardy off the table, and two firsts for him was never a price we could pay; both are on the board already. Te'o-Nesheim is the end now, with Jared Allen behind him, and Lawrence doesn't replace either of them. Sen'Derrick is still a negotiation with no tag behind it. Paul is a projection until April 5.

Seattle, Cleveland, Baltimore, Arizona, New England and Dallas all put veterans on the street this week. Dave hasn't asked me about any of them and I haven't asked him. None of them is on the board I gave him.

In the kicking game, Mike Westhoff runs the unit, and Cain's minimum offer is still Dave's to make. On the staff, nobody moved this week; the twelve coaches under contract on the eleventh of February are the twelve I have.
