## Player playbook access and evidence rule

Every participant may possess and study the **complete active 2013 Iteration I playbook**. The phase plan controls what the staff formally installs, practices, and evaluates together; it does not ration access to pages. A player may read ahead and ask about later material. He is evaluated on what has actually been assigned and taught for the phase.

No preset personnel percentage, snap share, rep quota, touch quota, or depth-chart probability determines the result of this phase. The plan creates opportunities to evaluate; it does not manufacture the answer.



## 1. Purpose of the offseason program

The offseason is not a miniature training camp.

Its job is to build the body, language, technique, communication, and shared football understanding that training camp will later stress under greater speed and competition.

The sequence is:

**Build the player -> teach the job -> connect the unit -> test communication -> carry the learning forward.**

The target moves from **Good toward Better**. The staff should expect players to retain corrections across days and weeks, not merely perform one clean scripted rep.

## 2. Participation and voluntariness

The Prowl standard does not create attendance authority that the CBA does not give the club.

During voluntary periods:

- no player loses a roster spot, role, rep, or standing because he lawfully declines voluntary participation;
- coaches do not use attendance as a hidden commitment grade;
- workout bonuses or other lawful contract mechanisms are handled through proper club/contract channels, not informal threats;
- football evaluation is based on work the staff is actually entitled to evaluate;
- a player who is absent from voluntary work receives the necessary re-entry teaching when he lawfully reports later.

The plan describes what Jacksonville teaches **when lawful participation occurs**. It does not convert the plan into a mandatory summons.

## 3. Phase One: build the physical platform

Follow the exact CBA restrictions established by the governing source. Do not add football work that the phase does not permit.

Primary objectives:

- individualized playing-weight direction;
- functional strength;
- acceleration and deceleration;
- change of direction;
- position movement;
- mobility;
- rehabilitation;
- conditioning base;
- recovery habits;
- nutrition/hydration education and support.

Performance staff should give each player a clear answer to:

**What physical qualities does your football job require, and what are we building now to support them?**

No player receives a universal body target simply because another player at the position has a different frame.

Medical restrictions control all work.

### Phase One coaching restraint

Football coaches may prepare teaching material and coordinate with the appropriate staff, but they may not use The Prowl as a reason to exceed the phase's permitted football-contact or field-work boundaries.

If the rule says the football answer must wait, it waits.

## 4. Phase Two: technique and job connection

As the calendar permits individual/group football teaching and walkthrough-speed work, connect physical training to football responsibilities.

### Offensive emphasis

- stance/alignment;
- formation recognition;
- personnel and role labels;
- run-footwork fundamentals;
- route landmarks;
- ball-security technique;
- protection technique;
- center/QB/back communication;
- shift/motion mechanics where permitted;
- concept teaching without pretending walkthrough success equals team-execution mastery.

### Defensive emphasis

- stance/alignment;
- front and leverage rules;
- pursuit landmarks;
- run-fit responsibility;
- coverage landmarks;
- communication;
- rush-lane responsibility;
- tackling approach/positioning taught without creating prohibited live-contact work.

### Special-teams emphasis

- stance/start mechanics;
- protection/release technique;
- lane and leverage rules;
- return-path landmarks;
- ball handling;
- specialist operation;
- substitution responsibilities.

Phase Two is where the staff should find vocabulary problems before eleven-man work exposes them.

## Phase close and handoff

Phase One is physical preparation and rehabilitation. Retention checks involving football instruction wait until permitted; no offense-versus-defense work or live contact is introduced in Phase Two. Medical staff define each injured player's participation restrictions independently.

Before Phase Two, position coaches prepare the retained-material check and an individual correction priority from the actual 2013 evidence. During permitted instruction, ask the player to explain the assigned job, demonstrate at the allowed speed, receive a specific correction and repeat it. Revisit the correction on the next permitted workday. A correct rep remains correct.

At the handoff to OTAs, each room reports what was taught, retained, postponed and limited by medicine or legal work restrictions. The shared full-team family dinner remains one event in the offseason/OTA window, planned and recorded through OTAs, not a duplicate obligation here. Events belong in this folder's future `output.md`; current state changes only when an event actually occurs.

## 2014 decisions: P3 adopted; other proposals pending

These items answer the exit-interview questions without inventing Crennel's promised report or a player evaluation. P3 is adopted by the user’s training/film request; P1, P2 and P4 remain proposals. Any later personnel appointment still needs its own evidence and record.

### P1: defensive communication succession

Keep Daryl Smith as the on-field communication lead. Recommend Posluszny as the first backup only after medical clearance and a lawful retention check. If he is unavailable, have Crennel and Bush evaluate Stanford, then Moore, for the reserve relay job during permitted work. That order is a proposed teaching sequence, not a depth-chart award. Allen is not a durable successor because the trade instruction and April 22 retirement remove him from the post-April plan.

Crennel's proposal should name the primary and backup communicator for each actual package, the front/coverage echo, the handoff when the lead leaves, and the sideline fallback if neither designated player is on the field. First explain and walk the handoff within Phase Two limits; stress a changed look and missing lead in OTAs. Record whether the call arrived, was repeated accurately and produced the correct alignment. Separate a late staff call from a player's relay error. Do not make two players competing authorities on the same snap.

### P2: injured-player learning and return

Offer medically permitted access to meetings, approved film and position-coach clarification on the same voluntary basis as teammates. The clinician specifies whether cognitive as well as physical work is restricted. No attendance, recall deadline or contact requirement overrides that instruction. Review restrictions before adding work; a return projection is not permission. Provide a short catch-up sequence on clearance, then a lawful retest of assigned material. No lost role follows automatically from an injury or absence.

### P3: individual feedback and overdue film, adopted

Provide an individual written teaching note for every participating controlled player, including reserves and practice-squad/futures players, after each meaningful work block. State the assigned job, evidence, what was correct, the cause of any error, correction owner, next legal retest and whether an evaluation is open, parked or closed. No evidence means not observed, not failed. If several players miss the same instruction, review the teaching first.

For a needed correction, pair the player's rep with a correct example where one exists; never fabricate a matching good rep. Classify assignment, communication, technique, physical loss, processing delay, medical restriction and staff/design failure separately. Correct reps need no invented fault. Immediate re-reps occur only at the lawful speed/contact level; otherwise schedule the next permitted opportunity.
