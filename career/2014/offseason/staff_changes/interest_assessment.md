# Interest in Jacksonville's assistants after 2013: first-pass assessment

**Status:** ASSESSMENT, not canon. Nothing here happened. It estimates what the branch's own 2013 season would produce, so the user and a fuller assessment can decide what to resolve. The user has said a more thorough assessment will follow; this is the starting point.
**Prepared:** September 28, 2026, for the branch date of February 2, 2014.
**Inputs:** branch 2013 standings and receipts; each club's opening-day head coach and 2012 record (`career/2013/awards/season_honours_evidence.json`, from nflverse); Jacksonville's staff contracts and résumés (`career/2013/coaching_staff.md`, `career/2013/offseason/staff_building/staff_plan.md` and `hires.md`).

## Read this first: limits

- **The rules on permission are unverified.** The repository holds no sourced text of the league's anti-tampering policy for coaches. My understanding, which the fuller assessment must verify with the two-pass discipline:
  - a club must let an assistant under contract interview for another club's head-coach job;
  - it may refuse an interview for a job that is not a head-coach job, especially a lateral move;
  - an assistant on a playoff club could interview only in set windows (a bye week, or after elimination). Jacksonville's staff became free to interview after the January 11 Divisional loss.

  **Label: Unverified.**
- **The calendar has passed.** The branch is at February 2, 2014. In a real cycle most head-coach and coordinator hires close in January. The carousel therefore has to be resolved retroactively, and its timing checked against the verified windows.
- **Hindsight.** I know how the real 2014 coaching cycle went, and the knowledge cannot be fully set aside. I have tried to rank only on branch evidence and pre-2013 public records. Where a club's branch season differs from its real one (for example San Francisco 2-13-1, Minnesota 13-3), the branch controls. The fuller assessment should be done by someone who freezes each club's criteria before looking at candidates, as the 2013 hiring search did.
- **Branch records are noisy.** Every club carried the same strength in 2013 (`runtime/defect_register.md`, item 1), so records swung further from preseason expectations than real ones do. Owners still react to the record they got.

## 2. What that means for Jacksonville's staff

Jacksonville's 10-6 turnaround from 2-14 is the headline other clubs will read. Its units, though, were middling in the branch: 12th in points scored (356) and 16th in points allowed (346). The offense had 25 giveaways (25th) and the defense 15 takeaways (31st). Stone calls the offensive plays; Crennel calls the defense. Interest therefore follows reputations and titles more than unit results.

**Reading.** The most likely requests are for **Frank Bush and Jeremy Bates, as coordinator candidates**, and possibly **one head-coach interview for Crennel**. The requests most likely to come but to be refused are lateral ones for the position coaches. A realistic outcome is that Jacksonville loses **zero to two assistants**, and more likely one than two. This is an estimate, not a result.

## 3. Decisions the user owns before anything is resolved

1. **Permission policy for jobs that are not promotions.** Refuse lateral requests, or grant them? Refusing keeps the staff but can strain a coach who wants the move. Granting is goodwill with a real departure risk.
2. **Promotions.** Stone can grant these freely or ask a coach to stay. He cannot promise a coach a title he does not have the authority to give; the staff budget is Caldwell's (row 6).
3. **Resolution method.** Whether the league's head-coach changes and the interview requests are resolved by frozen club criteria (as the 2013 hiring search was), and whether the fuller assessment replaces this one first.
4. **Replacement targets.** Record them in [staff_plan.md](staff_plan.md) before any departure closes, as in 2013, so a departure never leaves the job to improvisation.
