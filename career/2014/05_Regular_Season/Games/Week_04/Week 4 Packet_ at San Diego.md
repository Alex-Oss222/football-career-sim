# Staff meeting brief | proposed September 22

## Jacksonville self-scout

The offense does not need rebuilding. Cousins went 18 of 31 for 296 yards, four touchdowns and one interception against Indianapolis without taking a sack. Lewis produced 4 catches for 119 yards and a touchdown. Jones-Drew ran 16 times for 74. The problem was protecting the game after Jacksonville reached 38-13.

Indianapolis then drove 62, 66 and 85 yards for three fourth-quarter touchdowns. The offensive four-minute possession was three runs for two yards and a punt. Week 4 therefore does not translate "protect a lead" into predictable run-run-run football. Y-Cross, Boot and another controlled first-down pass remain available when the defense sells out against Jones-Drew.

The tight-end emphasis is an expansion of something already working, not a target quota. Lewis just produced 119 yards. The change is to make Lewis, and Kelce if cleared, part of more early first-read and leverage decisions.

## San Diego branch evidence

San Diego is 2-1. Rivers has 809 passing yards with seven touchdowns, four interceptions and 12 sacks taken through three games. Six of those sacks are charged to King Dunlap's protection slot. That does **not** establish Dunlap as the cause, but it makes San Diego's left-side protection declarations worth testing.

Ryan Mathews left Week 3 with a branch-generated short lower-extremity instruction and is projected beyond September 28. Keenan Allen has a minor lower-extremity instruction projected to September 27. Neither projection is treated as a game-day certainty.

The strongest offensive matchup clue is at tight end. Arizona's Rob Housler, Seattle's Zach Miller and Buffalo's Scott Chandler combined for 12 catches, 169 yards and two touchdowns on 22 targets against San Diego. Three games are a small sample, but that is enough to deliberately test San Diego's TE coverage rules early.

San Diego's defense has generated pressure, with Kendall Reyes recording four sacks through three games. The compact receipts do not tell us San Diego's actual coverage or blitz percentages, so none are invented.
