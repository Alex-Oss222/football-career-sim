## 1. Week setup and game preparation

**Opponent context.** The staff had no Philadelphia game of its own to study. Jacksonville has not played the Eagles under Stone, and the only Philadelphia football on the club's record is the 2013 season: 8-8, the NFC East title on the division record, a wild-card win over Tampa Bay, a divisional win at St. Louis and the conference championship loss at Minnesota, with 318 points scored and 330 allowed. What the staff did have was public before the branch: Chip Kelly's offense plays at a tempo that denies the defense its substitution, and Billy Davis described his defense as a three-man-front structure with four-man looks, the base ends predominantly two-gap players and the structure chosen for spread width. That told the staff what kind of system to prepare for and not what Philadelphia would call on September 7. The club's declared Week 1 lineup was not in the staff's hands when the plan was built, so the week prepared for the system rather than for a named quarterback or a named front, and the opening menu was built to find the answers on the field. The players the plan had to account for were its own: Nicks out and Hoskins out under the August 28 instructions, Bouye cleared and the 46 otherwise whole.

**Stone's priorities and decisions.** Stone issued the [August 28 packet](Regular-season%20Week%201.md) as the Week 1 plan and it governed the six days within his authority. Its first direction was that no new concept family enters the plan this week: the game is won or lost through formation, shift, sequencing and protection with the taught sheet, and installation time goes to execution. Its second was that every major dropback is rehearsed against a live rush picture, with the short side of SCAT, HALF, SOLID and MAX identified before a call is approved. Its third was that twelve personnel makes Philadelphia declare how it matches Lewis and Kelce together, attached first and then shifted to space without a substitution. On the receivers, the offense runs normally with Adams at X, Thielen at H and Hawkins at Z, no target quota replaces Nicks, and the relief job belongs to Hurns, the fourth receiver, because Gabriel is inactive under Stone's standing rule. The starters are the season game depth chart of August 30 with no change: Monroe, Bitonio, Brewster, Turner and Johnson; Jones-Drew; Lewis, Kelce and Jensen at tight end; Cousins. The call sheet is the packet's: eleven and twelve personnel as the base, an opener of thirty calls built on twenty-one distinct calls with nine planned returns to a picture already shown, each return asking whether Philadelphia's first answer was a rule or a reaction; third down from Power, Counter, Mesh and the access package in short yardage through Mesh, Stick and Drive in the middle distances to Y-Cross with SOLID, Dagger with HALF, Drive and the slow screen in long; the red zone from the compact runs and the Cross high and from Bunch Mesh, Snag, the boot, Power and Counter low. On defense Crennel carries the vocabulary the unit owns, Under and Nickel Even, Cover 1, 1 Robber/Rat, Cover 3 and Quarters, the four-man rush and the installed five-man pressure, with one no-substitution escape call for base personnel and one for nickel against Philadelphia's tempo and no line game, none having been taught. Westhoff's opening operation is Scobee, Anger and Kreiter, with Hawkins returning kicks and punts and Hurns the second punt returner. What came off the menu came off before the week began, by the packet's own exclusions: the Choice tag, the Y-Iso alignment, the dropback Sail, TE Delay, the Alert Post tag, Texas and Angle, Split Leak, the static Empty and the six-lineman package, none of them taught in the 2014 program. The Friday rule, that any call with unresolved protection, formation communication or route spacing comes off and is replaced from the same situation, removed nothing, for the reasons in the reduction table below. The frozen sheet is [call_sheet.json](call_sheet.json).

## 2. Pregame media Q&A

**Reporter:** Is it exciting or daunting going into the opening game, your second opener here, on the road against a team that went to the conference championship game?

**Stone:** Exciting. Last year the opener was at home against a team we had a plan for. This one is on the road against an offense that plays faster than anything we saw in August, and the plan this week was built around that, not around a name on their depth chart. We know what kind of system it is. We find out what they call on Sunday.

**Reporter:** What's the approach in preparing for Chip Kelly's tempo with a defense that hasn't seen it live?

**Stone:** Get lined up first. Romeo has one call for base and one for nickel that we can run without a substitution, and the rule all week was simplify before you invent. Daryl relays it, the front is set, then you can disguise if there's time. If there isn't, you play the call you have.

**Reporter:** Davante Adams starts at X in his first regular-season game. What did you see in his camp that gave him the job?

**Stone:** The third-down catches at Atlanta with Kirk, the landmarks all through camp, and the fact that nothing was charged to his spot in four games. He won the competition on the whole body of work, and the chart said so on the 30th. Sunday is the first time it counts and I am not going to grade it before it is played.

**Reporter:** What's the status of Hakeem Nicks?

**Stone:** He was reassessed Thursday and nothing changed. Out, and the trainers have the date. He did not practice this week, so he was not going to play this week whatever the exam said.

**Reporter:** Did Hoskins practice Wednesday?

**Stone:** No. He was seen Wednesday and the instruction stands. He is out Sunday.

**Reporter:** How important is the leadership from the returning starters on the line with two rookies at guard?

**Stone:** Mike sets the point and Eugene has played next to a rookie before. Joel and Trai have had every rush picture we could show them since Monday and the declarations were on time. That is the part of the week I can see. The part I can't see yet is what Philadelphia's front does when it is not a scout card.
