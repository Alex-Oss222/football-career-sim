MY ASSESSMENT AND DIRECTION

The pressure idea is sound. Changing the fourth rusher can attack a protection rule while keeping seven in coverage. It does not make the quarterback's protection calls useless, and it does not require dropping our best rusher on every pressure. Split-field rules also depend on receiver spacing, releases, motion and the location of underneath help, not receiver count alone. No five coaches were identified, so I am not adopting the claim that all five have abandoned Cover 3. Our own book includes Cover 3 Match as well as split-safety families.

I will carry Quarters and 6 as the weekly coverage emphasis and keep the practiced Cover 3 escape. I will show five, six or seven threats using the work we installed last week. Eight-up is not on this week's game sheet. It needs its own teaching and coverage recovery work before I can call it.

I am continuing the defensive calling arrangement we started against San Diego. Romeo prepares the defense, gives me the situation and protection information, and transmits my call. Daryl Smith makes the front declaration. Mike Tice tracks the offensive situation and next eligible opening call; Jeremy Bates supplies quarterback and protection information; Charlie Skalaski supplies the clock, timeout, fourth-down and challenge information. Mike Westhoff directs the kicking game. Taking the defensive calls does not remove their jobs.

We lost 21-20 after playing our first turnover-free game. Cousins threw for 267 yards and two touchdowns; Jones-Drew produced 137 yards from scrimmage. Those are things to preserve. Lewis caught three of eight targets for eight yards, and Kelce had no target. We need useful tight-end routes, protection that releases the intended receiver and an honest review of the read. Eight throws alone do not establish useful involvement. I cannot diagnose a route or quarterback error from those totals.

San Diego ran for 164 yards and converted seven of thirteen third downs. Pittsburgh has run for 859 yards through four games, including 581 on 83 carries by Bell and 187 on twenty carries by Blount. That makes an automatic dime call on every down a poor plan. I want six defensive backs against passing situations while keeping the front and support capable of fitting a run. Their last three team rushing totals were 302, 232 and 257 yards. These are game-book totals, not evidence of a particular blocking scheme or a forecast of Sunday's result.

Pittsburgh is 3-1. Roethlisberger has completed 82 of 134 for 916 yards, five touchdowns and five interceptions, with nine sacks. Four interceptions came in the opener; I will not treat that game as his weekly expectation. Bell also has fifteen catches for 217 yards. Wheaton has nineteen for 225, Miller fifteen for 166 and Brown fifteen for 139. Our attention follows the actual distribution, including the back, rather than a famous receiver's name alone.
