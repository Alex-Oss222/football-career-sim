## What separates the candidates

**After August 27:** Rehearsed inside the quarter structure, Nicks with the starters and Adams with the starters and then the young group, in a sheet that builds the progression around the tight end; nothing on the record separates them. Open.

**After game 3:** A real target to each X with Cousins, Nicks's for 28 and Adams's for 16, both caught, and Nicks's one Henne possession supplied with one target, caught, short of the sticks; nothing followed the receiver and nothing changed with the quarterback in a sample of one throw each. The record charges neither man with anything. Open.

**After August 21:** Rehearsed with the neighbors changed and with both quarterbacks, as the plan asked, and the interceptions charged to no one; nothing on the record separates them. Open.

**After game 2:** Each X was thrown to in his correction series, one target apiece, Nicks's intercepted and Adams's a 16-yard completion; Nicks was also the target on Shaw's interception from the 7. The record names defenders, not causes, so two interceptions on throws to one candidate are not established as his errors and three catches by the other are three catches. Open.

**After August 13:** Rehearsed again in both arrangements; nothing on the record separates them. Open.

**After game 1:** Six first-group snaps, no target to either X and both third downs lost at the right tackle before a throw: the game did not test the comparison. Nothing changes.

Nicks has the steadier recorded timing through this work; Adams's correction has now held on its delayed retest and in series, with his releases against Talib still mixed. Three controlled-contact days and a scrimmage against their own defense do not settle WR1. Their shared opponents and actual assignments matter more than a tally of catches.
