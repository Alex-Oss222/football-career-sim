## What separates the candidates

**After game 3:** Each guard kept the same communication and technique as the surrounding pieces changed, as far as a record that charges slots on losses can show it: 23 and 26 snaps beside Brewster, beside Linsley and in a mixed tackle combination, no loss charged to either; the two sacks allowed went to the right tackle and the right guard. Nothing separates them. Open.

**After August 21:** Each guard rehearsed beside Brewster, beside Linsley and in one mixed tackle combination, as the plan asked, with the same result as before; nothing on the record separates them. Open.

**After game 2:** Both held their slots on the record across the correction block and the developmental lines, 12 and 24 snaps, no loss charged to either; the three sacks allowed went to the left tackle, the center and Feiler at left guard in the Wilson and Shaw lines. Nothing separates them. Open.

**After August 13:** The comparison the game did not give was rehearsed again in both arrangements with the same result as camp; nothing separates them on the record. Open.

**After game 1:** Both held their slots on the receipt's record (no sack or loss is charged to either guard); the first group's six snaps and the sacks at the tackle give the comparison little. Open.

Bitonio's inside counter and Norwell's initial leverage loss are different technical questions. Both have useful contact evidence, now in series, and the shared protection held with the call from the sideline. Each has a secured block and an occasional lost rep; neither has won the left-guard job from this block.
