**Location:** Jacksonville club facilities; individual fields were not specified.

Jacksonville came back able to run the spring assignments without starting the teaching over, the first two padded practices showed that the understanding mostly survives contact, and the August 2 scrimmage showed that it survives the clock, substitution and the sideline sending the calls. The first five practices (July 25 to 29, without full pads) restored the core offense, the defensive calls and the kicking operation, then changed formations and personnel to see whether the answers still held. July 30 and August 1 were the full-pad practices, with July 31 the players' protected day off; August 2 was the scrimmage, run in series with the chains and the play clock, the coaches off the field between snaps and no score kept; August 3 was the day off after it; August 4 was the padded practice that closed the block with the retests the review named. Camp block 1 is closed with the first Plan execution assessment on the record for both units. August 5 to 7 then turned the installed menu into a game operation for Tampa Bay under Stone's written plan: the opening 15 and the situational menus were installed on August 5, rehearsed on August 6 with the calls from the sideline and the play clock running, and reduced on August 7 at walkthrough pace, after which Stone met Cousins and Tice and froze the plan. The operation carried the practiced pace into a sequence of calls it had not run in that order; the only call-level findings were single, corrected items, and the one cut was the PRESS sequence for the reserve and late groups. The main unfinished question is unchanged: what the combinations do against an opponent that does not stop at the whistle, which is the August 8 preseason game. That game (Tampa Bay 30, Jacksonville 21; [output](../Preseason_Games/Game_01/output.md)) gave the first offense six snaps and two sacks at the right-tackle slot, the first defense three unexplained long completions and the reserves three scoring possessions, and it injured Johnson, Brate and Anderson. August 9 to 13 reviewed it and prepared Chicago under Stone's revised plan: the record could assign a slot to the sacks and no cause, and no defender or call to the three completions, so the corrections were ordinary protection at the right tackle with Lucas and the help and communication of the coverages the defense already owns, not a new family. Anderson cleared on his projected date, Johnson's instruction did not change and Stone held him out of Chicago regardless, Brate is out long term, and the sheet lost the pages the 2014 program has not taught.

Every signed player reported on his assigned date, rookies and first-year players on July 21 and veterans on July 24. Lucas worked every padded session, the scrimmage and the game-week work in full; his foot review remains on the performance staff's list. Bradfield, Clemons, Brown and Pasztor remain outside the program with unsigned tenders. There is no recorded coach fitness test to assess.

## Offense: putting the playbook on the field

Tice's group began with the huddle, substitutions, cadence and the protection point, then ran the retained menu against defensive looks. Power, Counter and Inside Zone shared the work with the quick and crossing concepts and the movement passes taught in spring. The useful finding was that the players could get to their jobs without a coach supplying the answer on the first rep back.

The interior line carried its spring correction into the changed fronts and then into contact. Against the double A-gap presentation and the five-man pressure, HALF went toward the overload, and the guards communicated the change in time; on July 30 and August 1 the same answer held at contact speed, with Brewster resetting the point against the mug and the slide going the right way on every recorded team rep. That settles the shared part of protection for now. The physical part was mixed, which is what Block 2 is for. Bitonio anchored against Marks on the inside combination and over-set once, opening the inside lane to Marks's counter; Yarno corrected the set angle and inside hand, and the repeat and the August 1 rep were correct. Norwell, beside Brewster at left guard in the mixed group, carried the ACE combination through to the linebacker on the recorded Power reps and lost initial leverage once to Miller in protection, recovering without the pocket collapsing. Turner held his anchor against Mosley and C.J. Wilson and lost one one-on-one rep to Donald's first step, late with his hands; in the inside-run period the ACE combination of Brewster and Turner moved Donald off the strong A-gap on the recorded reps and Donald split it once. One lost rep is not the plan's "struggles," and Turner's conditional swap was not triggered.

The scrimmage put the same interior under series work with the call arriving from the sideline. The HALF slide went to the overload on every recorded series rep, with the substitution made and the point reset while the play clock ran; that is the eighth hold across three phases and the first with no coach on the field. Bitonio's set angle held through the late series as well as the early ones, the delayed retest the position room wanted. Norwell's combination with Brewster carried through again in the mixed series and he was walked back once by Marks in protection late, recovering before the whistle. Turner's hands were on time against Donald on the recorded reps; the Brewster-Turner combination moved Donald and he split it once, as on July 30. On August 4 the three ran the ordinary program work beside Brewster with nothing new recorded. The guard evidence is now three padded days and a scrimmage of the same shape: a secured combination and an occasional lost rep for each of them.

The relief pairings did the same work under a rush. Henne and Linsley made the point on time again, and Linsley lost initial leverage once to Mosley in the one-on-one period and recovered. Wilson worked with Shatley and Larsen, who held their recorded combination blocks with no correction. Linsley has now given the staff a better camp opening than his minicamp. In the scrimmage Henne and Linsley took a mixed series behind the first tackles against the first defense: the point was on time on every recorded rep, and Linsley lost initial leverage once to Marks in the later series, the rep whistled with the rusher at the quarterback's depth, which the script records as a lost rep and not a sack. That is the pair's first evidence under a pocket that was actually moving. A collapsing pocket over a game is still a preseason question.

Changing the offensive picture did not require a different answer on every page. Boot Flood kept its taught response to the free edge from both Wing and Ace. Y-Cross kept its staggered spacing with one tight end and with two. Hawkins supplied the correction worth following: from a reduced split on July 26, he cut the dig a yard short. His read was intact, but the route was not at its taught depth. Drake corrected it; Hawkins reached the right depth on the repeat, the following day and again on July 29. On July 30 the same dig came up short once more against Poyer's jam in the slot, this time a contact cause, not spacing; Drake kept the two causes separate, and on August 1 the depth held through the jam, and in the scrimmage it held through two series against Poyer. Wilson also retained his Sprint Flood depth.

The running backs met resistance for the first time. Jones-Drew pressed the Power landmark and read the puller, not open grass, on the recorded reps, and his ball security held through the controlled strip attempts in the ball-security period. Anderson bounced one Inside Zone rep before pressing the interior; Spencer corrected it, and the August 1 reps were correct. Jonathan Grimes's track was correct with no correction recorded. The scrimmage added the backs' protection, which the padded days had not isolated: Jones-Drew scanned to the five-man pressure's extra rusher and fitted him on the recorded reps; Anderson identified the same rusher and lost the block once to Telvin Smith, a physical loss with the assignment right; Anderson's Inside Zone track pressed the interior on every recorded series rep, the delayed retest of the July 30 bounce.

Game week asked a different question of the same offense: not whether a call could be run, but whether fifteen of them could be run in Stone's order, from the huddle, with the personnel signal ahead of the call and the sideline sending it. On August 5 Tice installed the opening 15 in order against the scout look he had prepared from what Tampa Bay's new staff had shown publicly, an even four-man front with two-deep and single-high shells and the five-man pressures a defense of that kind carries, and no more Tampa Bay than that. Every call on the sheet is a retained call from Stone's 2013 book; nothing was installed new, and the pages the 2014 record names only as the retained menu (Dagger, Spacing, Split Zone, the Inside Zone and Access Bubble package, the RB Slow Screen and Power Pass) ran on their taught landmarks on the first rep back. Three operation items came out of the install and each was corrected once. The shift to Empty on the Spacing call reached its set late once, the back short of his landmark, and Cousins reset the formation as the book requires rather than snap it; the TRADE motion on the Wing picture moved the strength and Brewster's re-point came after the cadence had started on one rep, so Tice re-walked the strength reset with the point confirmed before the cadence; and the one 21-personnel substitution, Owens entering for a receiver ahead of the Counter call, arrived with the play clock low once, and Tice slowed the operation rather than steal a snap, as the book instructs. The second and first-unit combinations ran the same sheet: Norwell beside Brewster at left guard for the second first-unit possession, and Henne with Linsley behind the first tackles for their planned series, where the point was on time on every recorded rep.

August 6 was the rehearsal, with the calls from the sideline, the play clock running and the coaches out of the formation once the call was sent. The opening sequence ran clean in order through the first group's two rehearsed possessions, including the shift, the TRADE reset and the 21 substitution, each on time on the recorded reps; the HALF slide went to the overload on every recorded rep with the point reset by Brewster, the ninth and tenth holds across three phases with the call from the sideline. The HOLD sequence, the same twelve-personnel eleven moved from Ace to a flexed Doubles picture and back to Wing without a substitution, held its assignments. The PRESS sequence was the day's finding. With the first eleven and the signal-and-word mechanism the book prescribes, the mastered quick calls ran at pace without a restated formation on the recorded reps, and Henne's group did the same; with the reserve group the formation had to be restated twice before the snap, the operation the book says does not belong in PRESS. The RB Slow Screen gave a single corrected item in Henne's series, Anderson leaving ahead of the convoy once, which Spencer corrected with the first group's reps on time. On August 7, at walkthrough pace with nothing installed, the staff reviewed the opening 15, the protections, the alerts and kills, the substitutions, the special situations, the emergency personnel and the communication chain, and Stone reduced the sheet by the plan's own rule, recorded below.

## Defense: playing the call together

Crennel brought back the base and Nickel Even work with the responsibilities stated before the snap. Cover 1 and Robber/Rat required the players to know the man assignment, the deep help, the underneath robber or rat, and the flat or force job. Cover 3 and Quarters were worked from similar starting pictures, so the appearance of the defense did not supply the whole answer to the offense.

The spring corrections survived the break. Rambo used the taught ROLL trigger, Harris held his path through the rub, Poyer checked the second threat before driving from the rat position, and Stanford retained his wheel leverage. Motion, bunches and stacks then tested whether the group could exchange those responsibilities while moving. The voice and hand signals arrived on time in the recorded work.

Pads added the fits. In the 9-on-7 inside-run period the Under fit was named before every snap and held on the recorded Power and Inside Zone reps: Marks owned the weak B-gap, Miller controlled the shade, Posluszny and Daryl Smith fit the strong B and weak A, and the overhang took force by call. The edge tags were used as written: on SPILL, Davis attacked the kick-out and Moore arrived as the next support defender; on BOX, Branch and Te'o-Nesheim kept the ball inside against Lewis's and Kelce's attached blocks. The one fit error came on Counter, which punishes fast flow: Telvin Smith fit the strong gap with the flow on July 30 and the wrap came through the gap he left. Bush corrected the read of the puller, and on August 1 he fit the counter correctly against the same misdirection. Tackling was taught as fit and wrap to the whistle, on bags for the finish; no tackle to the ground was scored for anyone, by design. In the scrimmage the fit was still named before every snap, now from the sideline signal, and it held on the recorded series reps; Telvin Smith fit Counter correctly in his reserve series and again in a mixed series with the first offense, two clean repeats behind Bush's correction. The finish is still fit and wrap to the whistle; nobody has been asked to tackle to the ground, so nobody has been graded on it.

The edge rush was tested against a live set for the first time. Mincey kept contain on every recorded rep and won the edge once against Johnson with the long arm. Branch produced the better isolated win, beating Johnson around the corner, and on one July 30 team rep did it by leaving the lane, which the book grades as a pressure error however the rep looked. Crennel made the point once; on August 1 Branch kept contain and still produced pressure on a recorded rep. In the scrimmage the item came back: Branch won inside against Johnson late in his Edge 1 series and left the escape lane open, and Cousins, uncontacted, stepped up through it before the whistle. That is the same pressure error under the book's contain rule, now twice, and Crennel made the point a second time; on August 4 Branch kept contain on every recorded rep with pressure still produced. Mincey kept contain on every scrimmage rep and won the edge once with the long arm, the steadier lane again. PEEL was executed correctly when the back released immediately. Edebali and Jeffcoat won and lost one-on-one reps against Leno, Lucas and Feiler, in their lanes each time.

In coverage, Crennel tested the linebacker-on-back vertical in man for the first time, as the plan asks. Daryl Smith carried Jones-Drew's wheel with the taught leverage and Lowery's post help arrived where the rule puts it; Stanford held his wheel leverage once against Jonathan Grimes and lost the top of the route once, and Bush's correction was the same leverage point as June 18. Press technique was now lawful: Talib re-routed Nicks at the line on recorded reps, Verner lost leverage to Adams at the line once and recovered to the dig, and Harris held his rub path with contact. Butler's corrected transition from press was late once against Hurns; Oden corrected it and the August 1 rep was on time. Poyer's rat vision held under the live five-man rush. The scrimmage retested the secondary in series: Talib re-routed Nicks and lost other reps to his hands; Verner held his leverage against Adams and recovered once to the dig; Harris held the rub path; Butler's transition from press was on time against Hurns and Gabriel in the reserve series, the delayed retest held; Stanford's wheel leverage was not tested, because the script sent the back's vertical to Daryl Smith's series and called the paired Cover 3 in Stanford's, so Bush ran the man version on August 4 in the group period and Stanford held the leverage at the top of the route against Jonathan Grimes. Another period showed why the call matters when judging a result: the offense's hot throw reached the flat against the five-man pressure, but that was the throw the defense had elected to concede. Rush and replacement ownership were correct. Calling it a coverage bust would have misread the assignment.

The defense also operated with its usual communicator taken out of the huddle. Daryl Smith relayed the calls first; Posluszny then handled the work without him, and Stanford handled a period with both absent. The fast-motion TRAVEL signal still reached the group. Moore relayed his own packages. In the scrimmage the relay ran from the sideline signal through Smith, then Posluszny for a series, then Stanford for a series with both out, and each was complete; the fast-motion TRAVEL signal reached the group with the clock running. The one operation error was the reserve defense's nickel substitution arriving late once against a personnel change with the clock running; Crennel's sideline call kept the base group on the field and the paired Cover 3 answer from the same shell, and the group played it correctly. That is a substitution-timing finding for the reserve group, not a coverage bust, and it is the kind of problem the scrimmage was designed to produce.

Crennel's game-week work was deliberately narrow, as Stone's plan asked: base front football, Nickel Even, Cover 1 and 1 Robber/Rat, Cover 3 and Quarters from the same shells, and the installed five-man pressure, against a scout offense built from the personnel groupings and motions any opponent brings rather than a Tampa Bay study. The item the plan named for deliberate stress was the reserve group's nickel substitution. On August 5 the scout offense changed personnel repeatedly with the clock running; the reserve substitution was late once on the first change and on time on every recorded change after it, and on August 6, under the play clock with the call from the sideline, it was on time on every recorded change. The relay ran the full succession in both days' series: Daryl Smith, then Posluszny with Smith held out for a series, then Stanford with both out, each complete, and Moore relaying his own packages; the series without the normal communicator the plan wants in the game was rehearsed on both days. Branch's lane, the second recurred item, was a targeted retest in the edge-lane work and in the team periods: contain kept on every recorded rep on August 5, 6 and 7 with pressure still produced on recorded reps, three clean days behind the second correction, still not closed until a tackle who does not stop at the whistle tests it. The pressure replacement rules were stated and executed as called, the hot throw conceded as the stated GIVE and identified by the front. Nothing else new was recorded on the defensive side, which is the result the narrow menu was meant to produce.

## Legal work, participation and medical method

- **Report dates.** The league published the 32 clubs' camp report dates on July 14 (library item C26, publication date confirmed). Jacksonville's dates are the historical dates selected under [March 24 organization and schedule decision](../../../../docs/season_structure.md) and cited in the [plan](staff_plan.md#historical-camp-sequence-and-simulation-stops): rookies July 21, veterans July 24, first practice July 25, first full pads July 30, scrimmage August 2. They satisfy rule R8 of the [league calendar research](../../../../library/2014_league_calendar_and_financial_rules.md): a veteran may not be required to report earlier than 15 days before the first preseason game or July 15, whichever is later, and with Tampa Bay on August 8 that is July 24 exactly; rookies and first-year players may not be required to report earlier than seven days before the veteran date, and July 21 is inside that lead. Any quarterback or injured-player exception is unverified in the library, so the veteran quarterbacks reported with the veterans. The report and first-practice dates are on the plan's cited club sources; the first-full-pads and scrimmage dates are the plan's selected dates and are not separately verified in the library, so they are carried as selected, not confirmed.
- **Who reported when.** A first-year player is a player under contract with no accrued season. Of the six reserve/future players, Bray, Jerrell Jackson and Long have no credited season and reported July 21 with the 26 rookies; Murphy, D'Anthony Smith and Blake each carry one credited season and their accrual is unresolved in the [contract status register](../../00_Team_Operations/Finances/player_contracts/contract_status.md), so the club did not require them before July 24. The remaining 45 signed players reported July 24. Every signed player reported on his date; no absence is on record.
- **Unsigned tenders.** Bradfield, Clemons, Brown and Pasztor had not signed their tenders by August 4. They are not under contract, and a player without a contract may not report to camp or practice until he signs; the library's calendar files record the tender deadlines and no separate camp-participation article, so the operative basis is contract status, and Stone's May 1 instruction keeps them outside the program and the social calendar until they sign. No work, meeting or invitation is recorded for them, and no "fell behind" language attaches. Caldwell's office owns the contracts.
- **Controlled roster:** 78 throughout: 74 signed and four unsigned tenders, of the 90-player limit. No transaction between June 28 and August 4.
- **Physicals and the entry standard.** The medical staff examined each reporting group on its report day (July 21 and July 24) and refreshed football-use instructions for camp. No restriction was communicated for any player. Cornelius Lucas's foot check was consistent with the June 16 physical: no communicated restriction; the performance-staff review item is carried, because nothing on record resolves it. The position-adjusted conditioning test was written before any player took it, applied consistently by football job, and produced no failure requiring an individualized retest plan; the test is an entry standard, not a depth verdict.
- **Acclimation and contact.** The [calendar](../../Calendar.md) records the first three camp days as required acclimation with no contact; the library does not record the helmet and shell sequence within them, so it is not stated here. July 25, 26 and 27 were the acclimation days. July 28 and 29 ran short of full pads under the club's schedule, whose first full-pad practice is July 30. No contact evidence is claimed for any of the five days: every observation in those sections is of the same class as the spring's, recognition, communication, timing and spacing against an opposing look, and cannot establish blocking, a won rush, a finished tackle or a physical matchup. Off days, hours and drill rules were applied within the calendar; no day is invented or removed from the plan's July 25 to 29 interval.

Under Stone's camp standard the daily script named each period's contact: individual and walkthrough periods non-contact; the one-on-one line, release and edge periods controlled contact to the whistle; the 9-on-7 inside-run and 11-on-11 team periods controlled contact with the ball carrier fitted and wrapped and kept on his feet; tackling finish on bags only; quarterbacks never contacted; the punt operation against a controlled rush. The scrimmage ran under the same script in series with the chains and the play clock, no score kept, by the plan's instruction that the scrimmage produces an evidence-based position review and no made-up game score; it is a practice event, not a game, and no engine draw, box score or result attaches to it.
