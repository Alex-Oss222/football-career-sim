## Questions going into Atlanta

**Written before the game from Stone's plan and the week's record; preserved as the ex-ante questions.** First quarter: are the starters ready to play regular-season football, the whole operation (huddle, personnel, formation, motion, protection, cadence, substitution, normal third down and red zone, the tempo changes) from the current chart's starting points with Johnson at right tackle; whether a real target reaches either X or the Z with no scripted throw. Second quarter: can Cousins keep the offense functional when the starters disappear, coaching the count, the point, the box, the shell, the pressure, the leverage and the protection from inside the huddle behind Leno, Norwell, Linsley, Feiler and Lucas, with Adams, Hawkins, Hurns and Gabriel and Anderson and Grimes around him. Kelce, through the first half: can he separate from linebackers and beat safety leverage; can Cousins find him on the cross; can he make the stick read correct from the shifted picture; can he work in traffic on Mesh; can he become the intermediate player on the boot; can he become a real red-zone matchup; does Atlanta change its personnel or coverage because he is on the field; if a linebacker covers him, does the football go there until Atlanta gives a reason to stop. Second half: which backups and rookies handle real situations without veteran rescue, Henne quarterbacking the reserves rather than babysitting them, Wilson as the established QB3, Shaw's progression and timing beyond three dropbacks, the young tackles on real pass-protection reps, Larsen's slow screen finally presented. Defense: the normal fit on tape with the starters for more than one snap; whether the young front's pressure survives with the veteran linebackers behind it and whether the young linebackers play the defense behind the veteran front; whether the alternate safeties' and Bouye's leverage and help rules hold, and whether a third game's long completion shares a cause with Detroit's and Chicago's; the nickel under motion, Bunch, Empty and tempo with Poyer back; the backups and rookies on the narrowed sheet with no established communicator. Special teams: the kickoff coverage's lanes after the Detroit review, a return average inside 22 or touchbacks the improvement Westhoff named; each snapper's whole operation, Kreiter opening; the field goal with the operation graded before the kicker; Hawkins's security and whoever else fields a kick, named on the game book, including the recovering returner on any muff. A manageable fourth down with the developmental offense in the second half is an evaluation opportunity, weighed, not automatic.
