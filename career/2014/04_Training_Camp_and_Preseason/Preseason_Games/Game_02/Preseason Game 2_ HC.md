# August 9 to 14, 2014

## Chicago Week: Head Coach Planning Outline

### What I want from this game

This game belongs to the rookies and backups.

The established veterans have already given us NFL tape and they received the first-game exposure. Unless Game 1 creates a specific football reason to play one of them, I am protecting them here.

I want the younger roster playing enough football to get past the novelty of being in an NFL game.

I also do not want a tired reserve running twenty consecutive snaps and giving us bad evaluation tape because his technique has fallen apart.

So the operating principle is:

**Play them. Cycle them. Change the problem. Put them back in.**

I want to know whether they can come out, receive a correction, sit for a series, return with different personnel or a different situation, and still execute the same football.

My priorities:

1. Rookies and reserves carry the game.
2. Rotate before fatigue makes the evaluation worthless.
3. Test the same player in more than one football situation.
4. Stress substitutions, communication and sideline operation.
5. Give the quarterbacks different personnel packages without changing the language.
6. Use the timeouts as teaching resources instead of protecting them for a meaningless preseason endgame.
7. Get meaningful special-teams evidence from players fighting for roster value.

This is not the week for a large opponent-specific plan.

Chicago gets enough study that we understand the fronts, pressures and personnel we may face. The rest is our offense and our defense.

# Personnel stress periods inside the game

I want the substitutions to create deliberate tests.

## ROTATE

Change skill personnel between series.

Test:

- sideline communication;
- personnel signal;
- huddle count;
- defense matching;
- player's readiness entering cold.

## HOLD

After a reserve group has entered, keep them on the field and move them.

Example with 12 personnel:

1. Ace Power.
2. Trey Drive.
3. Wing Outside Zone.
4. Shift toward Empty and run Stick or Spacing.
5. Return to Ace.

Same people.

Different geometry.

If a rookie understands only where he stands in one formation, Game 2 should find it.

## PRESS

One short PRESS period for each quarterback if the unit is functioning well enough.

Menu:

- Inside Zone;
- Draw;
- Stick;
- Spacing;
- Mesh;
- Drive;
- Smoke;
- RB Screen.

Nothing that requires the quarterback to rebuild the call at the line.

# Timeout philosophy

I am using the timeouts liberally.

That does not mean wasting them.

It means I am not saving three timeouts so I can pretend the final two minutes of a preseason game are the NFC Championship.

I will spend one when it protects a useful evaluation.

Examples:

- reserve substitution is breaking down;
- quarterback and center are clearly not on the same protection;
- young group cannot hear or communicate the call;
- I want the correct personnel for a rare red-zone or third-down opportunity;
- the two-minute group needs one real sideline communication cycle;
- we are about to waste a valuable situation with ten confused players and one man sprinting onto the field.

If the problem is simply that a player is beaten, I do not call timeout to rescue him.

That rep is evidence.

If the problem is that the operation has failed before the football can even be evaluated, I am willing to stop it and fix the operation.

We have three timeouts per half.

I am comfortable using them.

# Defensive rotation

Rotate the front aggressively.

I do not want a reserve defensive lineman playing twelve consecutive high-effort snaps simply because the offense sustained a drive.

Get another group on.

Then bring him back later and see whether the technique returns with him.

Linebackers rotate through:

- fit;
- coverage;
- pressure;
- communication.

Secondary players rotate through:

- outside corner;
- nickel/STAR where qualified;
- post/half/quarter jobs where actually trained;
- press and off leverage.

No pretend interchangeability.

A player only receives a job he has practiced.

---

# Defensive communication test

The reserve defense has to survive personnel changes.

Use the actual system:

- MATCH when the bodies require it.
- HOLD when the current eleven can handle the new picture.
- MASK only when the group can communicate it cleanly.

If Chicago goes fast, exotic football disappears first.

Base fit and coverage communication stay.

I would rather play Cover 3 correctly than call something sophisticated with three defenders looking at the sideline.

---

# Special teams

This is a major special-teams evaluation game.

A backup who cannot create offensive or defensive separation can still make this roster through the kicking game.

Westhoff gets broad rotation authority inside the approved units.

I want:

- Cain and Kreiter both operating;
- young coverage players on multiple units;
- different return candidates where actual camp work supports it;
- backup protection jobs;
- emergency replacement communication.

I want to see who can leave an offensive or defensive series, get a special-teams call, line up correctly and play fast.

That is NFL roster value.

# What I am grading in Game 2

Game 1 asked whether our core football worked under real conditions.

Game 2 asks a different question:

**How much functional roster do we actually have behind the established players?**

For every rookie and reserve:

### Assignment

Does he know what he is doing?

### Portability

Can he perform the same job from another formation, personnel grouping or situation?

### Communication

Can he enter from the sideline and immediately join the operation?

### Technique

Does the practiced technique survive game speed?

### Physical execution

Can he actually win the NFL rep?

### Conditioning

Does the execution remain usable later in his playing block?

### Reset

Can he leave, receive a correction, return later and execute it?

### Special teams

Can he provide another legitimate game-day job?

### Situation

Does he understand why the call changes when the field, down, clock or score changes?

That is the Game 2 tape I want.

Not who collected the most yards against the bottom of Chicago's roster.

I want to know who can be part of an NFL game when the starters are not carrying him.
