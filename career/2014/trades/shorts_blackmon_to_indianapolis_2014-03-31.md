# Shorts and Blackmon to Indianapolis: March 31, 2014

Recorded September 30, 2026 (2014 ledger Entry 104). Branch date: March 31, 2014.

**Stone's instruction** ([memo amendment](../offseason/stone_to_caldwell_2014_offseason_decisions.md#september-30-2026-amendment-trade-shorts-and-blackmon)): trade Cecil Shorts and Justin Blackmon for draft picks, ideally one in rounds 2 to 4. Jacksonville takes the dead money.

**Method.** This follows the Arizona trade for No. 38 ([negotiation](package_i_negotiation_2014-03-20.md)). Caldwell sets the asks within Stone's instruction, and each club answers on its own need, pick capital and cap. There is no private draw. The calls below are authored simulation events, not recovered conversations. The same two players offered by any other club would draw the same answers.

## What the clubs were buying

| Player | Age | Contract | Branch 2013 | Other facts on file |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cecil Shorts | 26 | Final year: about $1,431,000 base, no guarantee | 16 games, 104 catches, 1,158 yards, 6 touchdowns | No communicated restriction. Unrestricted after 2014, so a buyer gets one cheap season and the first chance to extend him |
| Justin Blackmon | 24 | Through 2015: about $2,072,910 base in 2014. His future guarantees are void after the branch suspension | 10 games, 32 catches, 271 yards; fell to third receiver | Four-game branch suspension in 2013 and a 2012 DUI arrest before the branch. No communicated restriction |

Signing-bonus money Jacksonville has already paid cannot move to another club. Splitting the dead money was therefore not available to offer.

The range for a productive receiver with a short contract is a third or a fourth. A second needs a younger player under longer control.

## The calls (March 31)

1. **Seattle.** Tate left for Detroit and Rice was released in February. Caldwell asks for No. 36 for both players. Seattle's third belongs to Minnesota, and Seattle will not spend its second on one season of Shorts. It declines and makes no counter.
2. **Carolina.** Steve Smith was released, and LaFell and Ginn left in free agency. Carolina offers No. 119, its fourth, for Shorts alone, and passes on Blackmon.
3. **Indianapolis.** Nicks chose Jacksonville, Heyward-Bey is unsigned and Wayne is 35 in his final contract year. The Colts had about $36 million to $41 million of cap room entering free agency ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1978664-breaking-down-colts-salary-cap-situation-heading-into-free-agency); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/afcsouth/post/_/id/56659/colts-to-have-second-most-salary-cap-space)).
   - **Caldwell's ask:** No. 51, the Colts' second, for both players.
   - **Colts' answer:** no second, for three reasons: Shorts has one year left, the receiver class is widely rated deep, and Blackmon's 2013 production was low. They offer No. 82, their third, for Shorts.
   - **Caldwell:** Stone wants both moved. He asks for a pick for Blackmon on top.
   - **Colts' final:** they add No. 194, their sixth, and take Blackmon's non-guaranteed contract as a low-cost flyer.
4. **Decision.** The Colts' third beats Carolina's fourth, and only the Colts take Blackmon. Caldwell accepts.

## The trade

| Jacksonville sends | Indianapolis sends |
|---|---|
| WR Cecil Shorts | 2014 third-round pick, No. 82 (Colts' own, round 3, slot 18) |
| WR Justin Blackmon | 2014 sixth-round pick, No. 194 (Colts' own, round 6, slot 15) |

- **Physicals:** both players passed the Colts' physicals on March 31 with no communicated restriction.
- **Picks:** both are clean ordinary picks; no hold or condition in the [ownership audit](../draft/ownership_audit.md).
- **Contracts:** Indianapolis takes both contracts from March 31.

## Cap effect for Jacksonville

| Player | 2014 cap charge before | Dead money that stays with Jacksonville | 2014 change | 2015 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Cecil Shorts | $1,541,845 | $110,845 (his final bonus allocation) | Saves $1,431,000 | No commitment either way |
| Justin Blackmon | $5,048,728 | $5,951,636 (all remaining bonus allocation, 2014 and 2015, accelerates) | Costs $902,908 | His whole 2015 charge comes off |

On the two contracts alone, 2014 improves by $528,092. The offseason cap counts only the top 51 charges, though, and with two fewer players, two $420,000 minimum contracts move back inside the 51. After that, 2014 working room falls by $386,908, from $11,117,504 to $10,730,596. The gain is in 2015, where commitments drop from $108,785,637 to $102,895,454. The two picks add rookie contracts only once players are selected and signed.
