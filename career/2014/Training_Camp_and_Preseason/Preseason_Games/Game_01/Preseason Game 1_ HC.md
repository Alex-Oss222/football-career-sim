# August 4, 2014

## Tampa Bay Week: Head Coach Planning Outline

### What I want from this game

I am not treating the first preseason game like a regular-season game plan.

I want to find out whether what we have been teaching survives a real opponent, a real play clock, full tackling, substitutions, the sideline sending the call and a defense that does not cooperate with our practice script.

The score matters because we are playing a game, but I am not sacrificing the evaluation to manufacture a preseason win.

My five priorities:

1. Game operation.
2. Core offense from different pictures.
3. Communication under pressure and substitution.
4. Comparable work for the open jobs.
5. Special-teams operation under real speed.

I do not want a large Tampa-specific package. Tice can make sure we understand the fronts and pressures we may reasonably see so we do not expose the quarterback to something irresponsible. Beyond that, I want us playing our football.

No new offense goes in for this game.

---

## Offensive approach

We are going to major in what we have already installed.

### Core concepts I want called

Run:

- Power
- Counter
- Inside Zone
- Outside Zone
- Split Zone

Pass:

- Stick
- Mesh
- Drive
- Dagger
- Y-Cross
- Spacing

Complements:

- Outside Zone / Boot Flood
- Power / Power Pass
- Inside Zone / Access Bubble
- RB Slow Screen

That is enough.

I do not need Trap, Wham, Jet, Scissors, flea flicker or a large 6OL package in Game 1. Those calls can wait. I would rather see eleven players run six things correctly from different pictures than watch them survive fifteen unrelated calls.

### Formations I want tested

Primary:

- Ace
- Wing
- Trey
- Doubles
- Bunch
- Pro

Limited exposure:

- Trips
- Empty

I want the same concept to appear from more than one picture where we have already practiced it.

Formation is part of the test.

If we call Drive from Doubles and later from Trey, I am looking at whether the players still understand the concept rather than memorizing the formation.

### Motion and shifts

Use movement with a reason.

I want to see:

- ZIP or RETURN to test defensive communication and our own timing.
- TRADE to change the strength and make our offense reset correctly.
- One heavy-to-space or back-to-empty shift if the personnel running it have handled it cleanly this week.

I do not want movement on every snap.

A motion penalty or a player asking where to line up because I wanted another pretty formation tells me nothing useful.

## Personnel plan

### First offense

Cousins starts.

The first group gets two possessions, with roughly 10 to 12 offensive snaps as the normal ceiling. If the first drive goes twelve plays, I do not need another full series. If we go three-and-out immediately, they get the second possession.

I am not leaving established starters on the field simply because the quarter has not ended.

### Receiver comparison

First series:

- Nicks at the WR1/X starting point.
- Thielen in his carried role.
- Adams gets the WR3 work.

Second first-unit series:

- Adams gets WR1/X work.
- Hawkins gets the WR3 work.
- Thielen remains the constant where the call allows it.

That gives me a cleaner comparison.

I am not scripting catches for any of them.

I want to evaluate:

- release;
- correct split;
- route depth;
- timing;
- blocking;
- adjustment communication;
- whether moving the player changes what we can call.

### Interior offensive line

Open:

- Monroe
- Bitonio
- Brewster
- Turner
- Johnson

On the second first-unit possession, Norwell gets left guard beside Brewster.

Turner remains at right guard. His existing conditional move has not been triggered.

Later, I want the Henne-Linsley pairing behind the first tackles for one series if the game gives us the opportunity. That directly continues the camp test instead of giving Linsley meaningless fourth-quarter snaps with an entirely different unit and pretending the comparison is equal.

### Tight ends

Lewis remains TE1.

Kelce remains TE2.

I want Kelce attached on an early run and attached again toward the end of his playing block. His open question is not whether he can make one block fresh. It is whether the base and pad level hold later in a sequence.

Brate, Jensen and Hoskins get reserve work with actual attached and route assignments. TE3 remains open.

### Quarterbacks

1. Cousins: opening group.
2. Henne: next unit, including the planned Linsley series.
3. Wilson: reserve core menu.
4. Bray and Shaw: each gets a reduced-menu opportunity if possession count allows.

I am not reopening QB1.

With Bray and Shaw I want operation first: huddle, formation, point, cadence, decision, ball security. I do not need either one trying to win a job with a reckless vertical throw.

## Offensive communication test

Every quarterback is responsible for:

1. Point.
2. Box.
3. Shell.
4. Pressure.
5. Leverage.

Every protection call has to reach the line and the back.

If the quarterback and center disagree, I want it visible and corrected. I do not want the quarterback saving a broken call by improvising something nobody else understands.

One HOLD sequence during the game.

Same eleven, new formation.

One short PRESS sequence with only mastered calls.

No separate no-huddle offense.

I want to know whether our existing language can speed up without becoming sloppy.

# Defense

Crennel calls it.

I do not want a giant pressure menu in the first preseason game.

I want:

- base front football;
- Nickel Even;
- Cover 1;
- 1 Robber/Rat;
- Cover 3;
- Quarters;
- selected five-man pressure already installed.

The evaluations I care about:

### Edge 1

Mincey opens.

Branch gets comparable work with the first group.

For Branch, pressure alone is not enough. I am grading whether he keeps the rush lane while producing it. A quarterback stepping through the lane after Branch wins the block is a bad rep for the assignment we are teaching.

### Communication

Run the relay succession we installed:

1. Daryl Smith.
2. Posluszny.
3. Stanford.
4. Moore handles his packages.

I want at least one series where the normal communicator is not on the field.

The test is whether the defense receives the call, identifies the personnel, handles motion and gets aligned before the snap.

### Reserve substitution

The late nickel substitution from the scrimmage gets deliberately stressed during preparation this week.

In the game I do not manufacture the situation, but when Tampa changes personnel I want the reserve sideline group ready.

If the substitution is late again, that is an operation problem even if the defense survives the play.

# Special teams

Westhoff owns the technical plan.

My concern is operation and evaluation.

### Long snapper

Cain retains the starting point.

Kreiter gets real game operation.

Do not judge the competition on snap location alone. Grade:

- snap;
- protection exchange;
- hold operation;
- coverage responsibility;
- communication;
- response to a bad look.

If we only get one meaningful punt opportunity for one of them, practice evidence remains part of the evaluation. I am not inventing equality the game did not provide.

### Return game

No returner is being named before this game.

Westhoff will establish the game rotation from the players who have actually taken return work in camp.

Grade:

- fielding decision;
- ball security;
- communication;
- first vertical decision;
- use of blocks.

One long return does not automatically decide the job.

### Head-coach decisions

Fourth down, field goal versus go, fake, onside kick, timeout, challenge and unusual end-of-half situations remain mine.

There will be no preseason fake just because we want to "put it on film."

# What I am grading Friday night

Not points.

Not fantasy stats.

Not whether somebody made one highlight.

For every player and unit:

**Assignment:** Did he know the job?

**Communication:** Did the information reach him and did he pass what had to be passed?

**Technique:** Did he execute the job correctly?

**Physical result:** Did he actually win or lose the block, route, rush, tackle or coverage?

**Situation:** Did he understand down, distance, clock, field position and objective?

**Correction:** If something failed, did the next opportunity look different?

Those are separate findings.

A player can know the assignment and lose physically.

A player can win physically while violating the call.

A coach can give a bad instruction and put the player in a losing position.

I want the staff to tell me which one happened.

That is what Preseason Game 1 is for.
