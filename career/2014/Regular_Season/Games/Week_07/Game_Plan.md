# Week 7 Game Plan: Cleveland

**Head coach:** Alex Stone  
**Game:** Cleveland at Jacksonville, Sunday, October 19, 2014, 1 p.m. ET, EverBank Field  
**Briefing used:** October 12, 2014  
**Baseline:** Carry Week 6

## 1. What I want this game to look like

**The matchup I am attacking:** Make Cleveland defend one formation as several different plays. Shift Kelce and Lewis, run Outside Zone, show the same track into Boot Flood, come back with Counter, and release a back or tight end into the space the pressure leaves. Test how Dansby and Robertson exchange the crossers and how Whitner and Gipson fit the run; don't assume their answers from a stat sheet. Cleveland has nine takeaways. The ball goes to the open answer, not the most elaborate drawing.

**What I must not give them:** Five more sacks, free hits on Cousins, early interceptions to Skrine or Haden, or a missed protection count with a new left guard. Tennessee was a win, but five sacks and the ball on the ground twice are problems to investigate. Defensively, no lost edge on Tate, uncovered Cameron seam or uncontested deep release by Lee. Special teams cannot donate short fields.

**What would make me change course:** If Mingo or Kruger beats our edge help, condense the pass menu to SOLID, SCAT, movement throws and screened rush counters. If Cleveland rotates a safety into every run fit, attack behind that rotation; if the safeties stay deep, keep taking favorable run numbers. If Hoyer consistently beats our pressure picture with an immediate throw, replace the bluff with sound four-man rush and match coverage. If a medical decision changes the guard or nickel, reissue the affected assignments.

## 3. Offense: what we are calling and why

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base personnel and roles:** Use 12 and 11 as interchangeable pictures. The current order begins with Anderson at back and Kelce at tight end; Jones-Drew remains the proven Power, Counter, goal-line and four-minute answer, with no forced touch split. Kelce can attach or move; Lewis supplies the dependable attached surface, protection and receiving complement. Adams is X, Thielen H, Hawkins Z, with Hurns and Gabriel for rehearsed relief. Linsley makes the center declaration with Norwell at left guard while Bitonio is out. We keep two tight ends in the picture but do not release both against an uncovered rush threat.

**The first things I want to learn:** How Cleveland's linebackers bump or travel with a 12-to-Empty shift; whether Whitner inserts on Power and the Outside Zone track; whether Gipson stays over the post; how Mingo and Kruger rush with a chip or moving launch point; and whether the defense switches the two shallow crossers in Bunch. We test the coverage, not label it ahead of the snap.

**Opening approach:** Show Outside Zone and its Boot from the same Wing alignment. Follow Power with Counter and protected Power Pass only after a safety declares. Use SHIFT Empty and Bunch to change the count with familiar receivers; mix quick throws and the back screen into the same series. The first fifteen ideas investigate alignments, then return to the call that created a favorable defender response. Short yardage, backed-up and scoring downs take their own calls.

**Opening ideas:** 12 Wing, Outside Zone Left; 12 SHIFT Empty, Stick; 12 Ace, Power Right; 12 Trey, Y-Cross, SOLID; 12 Wing, Boot Flood Right; 11 Doubles, Stick, SCAT; 11 Trips, Inside Zone + Bubble; 11 Bunch, Mesh, HALF; 12 Ace, Counter Right; 11 Doubles, RB Slow Screen; 12 Wing, Outside Zone Left; 11 Doubles, Inside Zone Right; 12 Trey, Y-Cross, SOLID; 12 Ace, Power Pass / Post-Cross, MAX; 11 Doubles, Drive, HALF; 12 SHIFT Empty, Stick; 12 Wing, TE Delay; 11 Trey, Dagger, HALF; 12 Ace, Power Right; 11 Bunch, Mesh, HALF

### Situational changes from the standing / previous menu

**Third down:** Short: Power, Counter or the counted Inside Zone/Bubble. Medium: SCAT Stick, Mesh or Y-Cross; work the marker. Long: SOLID Y-Cross, Drive, protected Dagger, or Slow Screen against confirmed rush. Cousins states the blitz answer before the snap; five sacks in Tennessee make protection identification a practice priority, not proof of any one player's fault.

**Backed up:** Outside Zone, Inside Zone, Power and Stick SCAT. Two safe completions are better than one unprotected seven-step drop. No delayed gadget or blind reverse field.

**High red zone, low red zone, goal line:** High: protected Cross, Power Pass and gated Boot. Low: Bunch Mesh, Snag, Power and Counter, with run-pass leverage clear to Cousins. Goal line: downhill gap run first; a pass needs a covered protection and clean release. No self-inflicted grounding or delay as in the Tennessee game.

**Two-minute / hurry-up:** Stick, Spacing, Drive, Mesh and Y-Cross from a personnel group that can stay on the field. The clock assistant gets the next snap, sideline options and timeout count to me early. The quarterback retains his practiced audible authority inside this reduced menu.

**Four-minute / protect a lead:** Run at the favorable fit with Anderson or Jones-Drew, then punish the loaded box with SOLID Y-Cross or a safe Boot. Play for first downs, not three automatic runs. Everyone knows the clock cost of a sack or incompletion before the series.

**Sudden change / shot opportunities:** Secure the first snap unless the defense already gave us the exact Power Pass or Boot trigger. Do not force a deep shot because we have a short field. Against an aggressive rush, the Slow Screen is our changeup.

**Special plays:** 12 Ace, Flea Flicker, MAX: Practice only; 12 Wing, TE Delay: Keep; 12 Trey, TE Delay: Keep

**Calls not available this week:** None

## 4. Defense: what we are showing and answering

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base front and run fits:** Aaron Donald is a first-unit interior lineman alongside Sen'Derrick Marks, with Mincey and the opposite edge completing the starting four. Donald attacks a guard or the center's protection relationship from a coached 3-technique, while Roy Miller rotates into heavier run surfaces. Under is the base against Tate and attached tight ends; Bear is the short-yardage answer, Nickel Even the spread response. Donald and Marks must keep their own run gaps before any twist. Daryl Smith declares the front, with Posluszny and Telvin Smith connected to the puller and cutback.

**Coverage and rush approach:** Give Hoyer a crowd at the line without giving him an easy pre-snap answer. Show the two A-gap threats, rotate safeties late, and most often send four with 1 Robber, Quarters or Cover 6 behind it. Use an actual fifth rusher with Fire 3 only when the hot route and contain are accounted for. Lee is the first receiver in our coverage count, Cameron's seam and crossing release the second. Talib and Verner stay on their outside sides; Ball handles the nickel until Bouye is medically cleared, and Butler is the outside reserve. Donald's interior push is the point of the pressure picture, not permission to vacate his gap.

**Rush rule:** Four unless I call the five-man counter

**What we need to discover early:** Does Mack reset the point when we mug both A-gaps, and where does Tate scan? Does Hoyer throw immediately against the threat picture or hold for a developing route? Does Lee change sides or go inside; does Cameron release across the formation? Keep Joe Thomas's edge and the quarterback's escape lane accounted for on every rush.
