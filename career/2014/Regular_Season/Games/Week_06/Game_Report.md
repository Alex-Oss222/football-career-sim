## 1. Preparation and final game-day decisions

Stone's [Week 6 Game Plan](Game_Plan.md) kept the arrangement of the Pittsburgh game: Stone calling the offense and the defense, Romeo Crennel preparing the defense and sending the call in, Daryl Smith declaring the front and Mike Westhoff calling the kicking game. It made no depth or role change. Fourth downs were to follow the rule Stone gave during the Pittsburgh game from the first one on; in Week 5 he had answered the first himself and given the rule after it. Two-point tries and kickoffs stayed on his standing rules. After the four turnovers against Pittsburgh, the plan put securing the ball and defining Kirk Cousins's answer against pressure first among the week's practice priorities, and it added nothing new to the practice menu: no Throwback Screen, Halfback Pass, WR Pass or new motion family.

Monday, October 6, was the Pittsburgh review and the Tennessee introduction, and Tuesday the players' day off. Under Stone's standing practice schedule Week 6 had no padded practice, and the Wednesday, Thursday and Friday practices were in helmets. On Saturday, October 11, the team held its walkthrough and travelled to Nashville. No one was injured during the week, no new restriction was communicated and the club made no roster move.

Each of the three players under a medical instruction at the start of the week was cleared or released on the date projected for him ([medical history](../../../Team_Operations/Medical/medical_history.md#october-6-to-11-christian-jones-and-trawick-cleared-poyer-released)). Christian Jones was reassessed and cleared on Monday, took the review and every session after it in full, and was not on the league's injury report. Brynden Trawick was reassessed on Tuesday with no change, out with a projected return of October 11, and took no part in the Wednesday, Thursday and Friday practices. As Stone's plan provided, Chris Prosinski took his deep-safety work and Adrian Phillips the next safety and coverage duties while he was out. The medical staff cleared Trawick on Saturday, and he took the walkthrough in full. The independent medical process released Jordan Poyer's hold on Thursday, October 9. He had not practiced before the release and took part in full on Thursday and Friday and in the walkthrough, with no communicated limitation. The league's report listed Trawick as a non-participant all three days and questionable for the game, and Poyer as a non-participant Wednesday and full Thursday and Friday with no game designation. Because each came on its projected date, the alternatives Stone had written into the plan for a different result were not needed: Todd Davis in Jones's linebacker and coverage work, and the inactives and the punt, kickoff, dime and emergency-safety groups rebuilt without Trawick.

**Plan in force:** On offense, the same 12-personnel look shown to Tennessee several ways: Maurice Jones-Drew running at the actual box count, Marcedes Lewis and Travis Kelce shifting to test the linebackers' coverage rules, Outside Zone paired with Boot Flood and Power with Counter and Power Pass. On defense, Shonn Greene's run fits first and four rushers holding their lanes against Jake Locker unless Stone calls the five-man counter ([Game Plan](Game_Plan.md)).  
**Game-day availability:** No change from the Week 5 game-day unit. Jones and Trawick, both hurt against Pittsburgh, were cleared, and Stone's plan named the same seven inactives as in Week 5: Poyer, Malcolm Butler, Jonathan Grimes, Corey Linsley, Kasim Edebali, Taylor Gabriel and Gator Hoskins. Poyer was on the list by Stone's decision, not by medical instruction, as Butler had been in Week 5 after his own release.

## 2. Pregame media

Stone met reporters after practice on Friday, October 10, the day before the walkthrough and the flight to Nashville.

**Reporter:** Brynden Trawick didn't practice this week. Where is he?

**Stone:** He was looked at again Tuesday and nothing changed, so he didn't practice, and he's questionable. He's cleared when the doctors clear him, not before. Christian was cleared Monday and he's done everything since, so he's off the report.

**Reporter:** And Poyer?

**Stone:** The independent process released Jordan yesterday. He didn't practice before that. He went full yesterday and today, and there's no designation on him.

**Reporter:** So he plays Sunday?

**Stone:** He's eligible. You'll get the inactives Sunday with everybody else.

**Reporter:** If Trawick isn't cleared, who's next to Rambo?

**Stone:** Chris took Brynden's work at deep safety this week, and Adrian had the next safety job and the coverage duties behind it. If the doctors don't clear Brynden, we'll go back through all 46 and set it from there. I'm not going to name a starter ahead of a medical decision that hasn't been made.

**Reporter:** You were inside the Pittsburgh 30 five times and came away with 10 points, and you turned it over four times. What has to change?

**Stone:** We moved the ball, and then we gave it to them four times, and too many good drives came back with nothing. That starts with me: the calls, the plan and the spots I put players in. Tennessee is a division team. We have to protect the football, tackle and execute the situations we practice.

**Reporter:** Three of the four were Kirk's interceptions. How is he handling it?

**Stone:** You'll have to ask Kirk. I'm not going to take those apart up here one at a time and hang them on him. What we say about them stays in the quarterback room.

**Reporter:** Tennessee is 1-4, but all four losses have been one-score games, the last two by a point at Indianapolis and by three to Cleveland. What do you see?

**Stone:** That they're close, and they're at home. The record doesn't tell you much about Sunday. Shonn Greene has 81 carries in five games, so it starts with fitting the run and tackling him. Kendall Wright is their leading receiver, and you'd better know where Delanie Walker is. And you can't give Jake Locker a lane. Once he's out of the pocket, the coverage has to hold a lot longer than anybody wants it to.

## 3. The game

Jacksonville won 24-10 at LP Field with two touchdowns 2:09 apart early in the fourth quarter, after Tennessee had tied the game 10-10 early in the third on a short field. Maurice Jones-Drew finished a 91-yard drive from the Jacksonville 9 with a 3-yard run for a 17-10 lead, Aqib Talib intercepted Jake Locker on Tennessee's next possession, and Kirk Cousins found Davante Adams for a 23-yard touchdown on the following snap. Brynden Trawick intercepted Locker two snaps after that, and Tennessee's last chance ended on fourth-and-4 at the Jacksonville 21 with 3:57 left. A week after four turnovers against Pittsburgh, Jacksonville gave the ball away once.

### First quarter

Tennessee went three-and-out on the opening possession, and Andrew Hawkins returned Brett Kern's 66-yard punt 63 yards to the Tennessee 27. Jacksonville gave the field back: an intentional-grounding foul on first down, a completion to Adams that lost 4, and on fourth-and-24 at the 41 Bryan Anger punted to a fair catch at the Tennessee 7. Tennessee kept the ball for more than five minutes. Shonn Greene and Denard Robinson put it on the ground three times on that possession and Tennessee recovered all three, and the drive stalled when Jeremy Mincey dropped Robinson for an 11-yard loss on second-and-2.

Jacksonville's first touchdown came out of third-and-13 at its own 28, after Sammie Lee Hill's sack on first down, a Tennessee offside and an incompletion. Travis Kelce caught Cousins's throw and went 71 yards to the Tennessee 1, and Jones-Drew scored on the next snap at 6:01, 7-0. Tennessee answered with a seven-play drive that an encroachment penalty on third-and-4 kept alive. Mincey sacked Locker on second-and-7, Kendall Wright's 13-yard catch on third-and-15 left fourth-and-2 at the Jacksonville 46, a false start made it fourth-and-7, and Kern punted.

### Second quarter

Ropati Pitoitua and Hill sacked Cousins on Jacksonville's first possession of the quarter, and on fourth-and-3 at its own 29 Anger punted 52 yards. After a Tennessee three-and-out, Jacksonville's 12-play drive from its 35 reached the Tennessee 6, through two false starts, Marcedes Lewis's 8-yard catch on third-and-7 and a 22-yard catch by Adams on first-and-15. On second-and-4 at the 6 Wesley Woodyard sacked Cousins for a loss of 8, Hawkins got 4 of the 12 on a third-down run, and Josh Scobee's 28-yard field goal try missed with 2:32 left.

Tennessee used what remained of the half for points. A Jacksonville hands foul gave it a first down on its first snap, Locker found Kendall Wright for 26 yards to the Jacksonville 38 and moved on to the 23, and after Paul Posluszny's 10-yard sack and a false start had pushed it back to the 38, Ryan Succop kicked a 56-yard field goal at 0:38, 7-3. Hawkins returned the kickoff to the Jacksonville 43, Jones-Drew ran for 11, a 24-yard pass-interference penalty on Tennessee put the ball at the 22, and Scobee's 40-yard field goal made it 10-3 at the half.

### Third quarter

Jacksonville's first series of the second half lost ground. Derrick Morgan sacked Cousins and forced a fumble that Jones-Drew recovered, and from the Jacksonville 8 Anger's punt went 25 yards and out of bounds at the 33. A.J. Bouye was hurt at 13:26 and did not return; Alan Ball played the nickel from then on. Tennessee needed three plays: Locker hit Robinson for 14 and Greene ran the last 17 for the touchdown at 12:26, 10-10.

Jacksonville answered with 14 plays from its own 22, the most of any possession in the game. It converted twice on fourth down with Jones-Drew, at its own 40 and at the Tennessee 49, and Kelce's 13-yard catch took it to the Tennessee 22, where Bernard Pollard intercepted Cousins's first-down throw for Adams at 5:30. Tennessee made one first down before a holding call and a false start left it at first-and-25, and Kern's 49-yard punt was downed at the Jacksonville 9.

From there Jacksonville went 91 yards in eight plays. A delay-of-game penalty made it first-and-15 at the 14, C.J. Anderson caught a 26-yard pass, and late in the quarter Jones-Drew ran 25 yards to the Tennessee 35.

### Fourth quarter

Hawkins caught a 21-yard pass to the 14 on the first snap of the quarter. Jones-Drew ran 13 to the 1, was stopped by Hill for a loss of 2, and scored from the 3 at 12:42, 17-10. On Tennessee's third snap after the kickoff, third-and-6 at its 16, Talib intercepted Locker's throw for Derek Hagan and returned it 14 yards to the Tennessee 23, and Cousins hit Adams for the touchdown on the next snap at 10:33, 24-10. Wright caught a 17-yard pass on Tennessee's next first down, and Trawick intercepted Locker's next throw for him at the Tennessee 49.

Three short gains left Jacksonville at fourth-and-4 at the Tennessee 43 with 7:29 left. It went for it, and the throw for Adam Thielen fell incomplete. Tennessee then kept the ball for 3:32, converting a fourth-and-4 at the Jacksonville 37 with Justin Hunter's 10-yard catch, until Locker's fourth-and-4 throw for Greene from the 21 fell incomplete. Cousins ran 21 yards on second-and-8 to move Jacksonville off its 23, the ball came loose from Anderson on the next snap and Joel Bitonio recovered it, and the series ended in a punt fair-caught at the Tennessee 13. Tennessee ran one play before the game ended.

### The calls and decisions that shaped the game

Stone's Game Plan put fourth downs under his standing rule from the first one on, and the rule decided all eight of Jacksonville's at the distance and spot the down actually presented: fourth-and-2 or less is a go with offensive coordinator Mike Tice's first call on that down's menu; fourth-and-3 or 4 is a go on the opponent's side of midfield; otherwise it is a field goal when the kick is 52 yards or less, from the opponent's 34 in, and a punt beyond it. It produced four punts, one field goal try and three tries for the first down, two of them converted.

The first fourth down followed Hawkins's return. At fourth-and-24 from the Tennessee 41 the kick would have been 59 yards, so the rule called for the punt.

Fourth-and-8 at the Tennessee 10 before the half was a field goal under the rule, and Scobee's 28-yard try missed. Tennessee had 2:14 left and drove for Succop's 56-yarder before Jacksonville answered with its own field goal.

Both conversions came on the 14-play drive after Greene's touchdown. At fourth-and-2 at the Jacksonville 40, after Anderson was stopped for a loss of 1 on third-and-1, the call the rule names for the distance was 12 Ace, Power Right, and Jones-Drew gained the 2. He ran 9 yards on the next snap, and two incompletions for Adams then left fourth-and-1 at the Tennessee 49; the rule and the call were the same, and Jones-Drew gained 2 again. The drive went on to the 22 and ended with Pollard's interception.

At 24-10 with 7:29 left, fourth-and-4 at the Tennessee 43 fell inside the rule's second line. The rule has no term for the score or the clock, so it sent the offense, and its call for the distance was 12 SHIFT Empty, Stick. The throw for Thielen fell incomplete. Tennessee started at its own 43 and went to the Jacksonville 21 before its own fourth-and-4 failed.

## 4. Postgame media

Stone met reporters at LP Field after the game.

**Stone:** A division win on the road. Protecting the football was first on our list this week, and we turned it over once. We ran it, and the defense took it away twice in the fourth quarter. Go ahead.

**Reporter:** One turnover after four against Pittsburgh. Is that fixed?

**Stone:** No. It's better. We put it on the ground twice today and our guys got to both of them, and you don't get to count on that. The one we gave them came at their 22 at the end of a long drive, and those were points we should have had.

**Reporter:** Why go for it on fourth-and-4 at their 43, up 14 with seven minutes left?

**Stone:** That's the rule we play by. Fourth-and-3 or 4 on their side of the 50, we go. We didn't make it and they got the ball at their 43, and that's on me, not the players. Then our defense went out and stopped them on fourth-and-4 at our 21.

**Reporter:** Does the score not change that?

**Stone:** The rule is about the distance and where the ball is. It doesn't have the score in it.

**Reporter:** Kirk was on the ground a lot today. What happened up front?

**Stone:** Five's too many, and I don't know yet that it was all up front. We'll know what each one was tomorrow.

**Reporter:** Talk about the catch Travis Kelce made on third-and-13.

**Stone:** That's a punt from our 28 if he doesn't make it. Instead we're on their 1 and Maurice scores on the next snap.

**Reporter:** Maurice converted two fourth downs in the third quarter.

**Stone:** We ran Power both times and he got it.

**Reporter:** Tennessee's only touchdown came on a short field. How did you see the defense?

**Stone:** That touchdown starts with us. We went backward on our first series of the half, punted from our 8 and gave them the ball at our 33. Other than that they had a field goal from 56, and they didn't convert many third downs. Aqib's interception put us on their 23 and we scored on the next play.

**Reporter:** Trawick didn't practice this week. What did you see from him?

**Stone:** The doctors cleared him yesterday, and he played every snap on defense and took one away.

**Reporter:** Any update on A.J. Bouye and Joel Bitonio?

**Stone:** A.J. came out in the third quarter with a lower-body injury, and Alan Ball played the nickel the rest of the way. Joel was hurt on our last series and didn't come out of the game. It's lower body as well. Both get looked at again Wednesday, and that's what I have from the doctors.

## 5. Football review and next week's work

**Offense:** The causes below wait on Monday's film review; the play-by-play shows the results and the players involved, not the protection, the coverage or the reads. The runs produced both touchdowns, from inside the 5, and both fourth-down conversions on Power Right, the call the rule named; whether Jones-Drew was running at the box count the plan asked for, and how Woodyard, Zach Brown and Pollard fit Power and Outside Zone, are questions for that review. The passing game had Kelce's long catch and five sacks, after none against Pittsburgh, and two of the sacks hurt the field position: Woodyard's turned second-and-4 at the 6 into third-and-12 before Scobee's 28-yard miss, and Morgan's strip on the first series of the third quarter came before the punt from the 8 that set up Tennessee's touchdown. Whether each sack was an assignment, a lost block, a late recognition or a held ball is the review's first question. Ball security, the plan's first practice priority, improved on Week 5 to one interception, but the ball was still on the ground twice; Jacksonville recovered both. Seven possessions began or got inside the Tennessee 30 and produced all 24 points; three came away empty. Two false starts, a delay of game and the grounding put the offense behind the down.  
**Defense:** Tennessee scored its touchdown on a 33-yard field and its field goal from 56. It converted 2 of 11 third downs and failed on fourth-and-4 at the Jacksonville 21 while the game was still within two scores. Greene's 17-yard touchdown was its longest run; Tennessee's other 20 runs gained 51 yards. Locker did not run the ball, and the two interceptions came on consecutive Tennessee possessions in the fourth quarter. Wright was the target of 12 throws and caught 7 for 80; Walker caught 1 of 3 for 5. The film will also say whether Bouye's inside plan against Wright worked before he left, and whether the edges and rush lanes held as the plan asked. Three defensive fouls, the illegal contact on third-and-1, the encroachment on third-and-4 and the hands foul on the first snap of the two-minute drive, each gave Tennessee a first down.  
**Special teams and game management:** Two of Anger's four punts were fair-caught inside the Tennessee 15, at the 7 and the 13. Scobee missed from 28, made from 40 and made all three extra points. Tennessee returned three kickoffs for 53 yards and one punt for 5, and Jacksonville had no kicking-game penalty, as the plan asked. No change to the plan's calling arrangement was recorded. All eight fourth downs followed the standing rule, and no two-point or onside situation under Stone's standing rules arose.  
**What changes for the next game:** No change has been ordered. The review of Monday, October 13, still to be held, opens the Cleveland week. Its items are the five sacks (Jeremy Bates with Cousins on his answers against pressure, the work the plan had Bates recording on every shifted look, and George Yarno with the line), the interception at the 22 (Bates with Cousins, Darryl Drake with Adams), the missed 28-yarder and the 25-yard punt from the 8 (Westhoff with Scobee, Casey Kreiter and Anger), and the three defensive fouls that moved the chains (Crennel and the position coaches). Bouye's and Bitonio's reassessments on Wednesday, October 15, decide their availability, and the nickel and left guard for Cleveland are Stone's on his Week 7 Game Plan. The next chances to verify the work are the Cleveland week's practices, Wednesday's in pads, and the game on Sunday, October 19.
