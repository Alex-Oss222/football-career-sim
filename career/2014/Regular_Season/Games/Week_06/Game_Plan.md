# Week 6 Game Plan: at Tennessee

**Head coach:** Alex Stone  
**Game:** Jacksonville at Tennessee, Sunday, October 12, 2014, 1 p.m. ET, LP Field  
**Briefing used:** October 12, 2014  
**Baseline:** Carry Week 5

## 1. What I want this game to look like

**The matchup I am attacking:** Make Tennessee defend the same 12-personnel surface several ways. Run Jones-Drew at the actual box count, shift Lewis and Kelce to expose the linebackers' coverage rules, then pair Outside Zone with Boot Flood and Power with Counter and Power Pass. Test whether Woodyard and Brown widen, whether Pollard inserts against the run, and whether Morgan or Wimbley chases the backside. Those are questions to answer on the field, not assumed tendencies.

**What I must not give them:** Another game of giveaways and empty trips inside the 30. Pittsburgh got four turnovers and 17 points from them. Protect the ball, give Cousins a defined pressure answer, finish drives, and do not give Locker an open escape lane or Greene an unaccounted cutback. No cheap yards from kick-cover penalties.

**What would make me change course:** If Tennessee packs the run surface, detach a tight end, use Stick or Y-Cross and move the launch point only when the edge is secured. If its linebackers sit on the crossers, work the run and perimeter count. If interior penetration wrecks the mesh point, get to quick throws and protected passes. On defense, if our fits or replacement-pressure exchanges fail, play the clean Under or Nickel Even four-man answer immediately. Medical availability overrides every package.

## 3. Offense: what we are calling and why

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base personnel and roles:** Start from 12 and 11, not a fixed run-pass quota. Jones-Drew leads the backs; Anderson relieves him for a prepared series or protection job, with the same credible run-pass menu. Lewis is the established Y and Kelce the movable second tight end and prepared receiving Y. Adams stays at X, Thielen at H, Hawkins at Z; Hurns is general relief and Nicks takes his rehearsed X-relief series. The Monroe-Bitonio-Brewster-Turner-Johnson line stays together. Make Tennessee defend shifts without signaling the ball's destination.

**The first things I want to learn:** Who follows the tight ends when 12 shifts toward Empty; how Woodyard, Brown and Pollard fit Power and Outside Zone; whether the backside edge chases Boot; how the front handles a screen release and whether a safety sits on the dig. Bates records the pressure count and Cousins's immediate answer on every shifted look.

**Opening approach:** Show the run surface, change the picture with the same people, and return to it once we know the adjustment. Start with secure handoffs and defined reads, then use complementary play-action or the screen only when the defense earns it. A short-yardage, backed-up or scoring situation interrupts this order; an unused idea remains available. No mandatory shot and no target quota.

### Situational changes from the standing / previous menu

**Third down:** On 1 or 2, Power or the counted Trips run-bubble; on 3 to 5, SCAT Stick or properly protected Mesh; on 6-plus, SOLID Y-Cross, Drive or protected Dagger. Slow Screen is a rush answer, not a surrender throw short of the marker. Cousins identifies the hot defender and the actual line to gain.

**Backed up:** Inside Zone, Outside Zone, Power and SCAT Stick. No Flea Flicker, long-developing shift or uncovered edge. Win field position without donating the ball.

**High red zone, low red zone, goal line:** High: Y-Cross, controlled run and gated Boot or Power Pass. Low: Counter, Power, Bunch Mesh and Snag by leverage; no forced crosser. Goal line: Power Right or Left and Counter first; a pass only from a taught protection and eligible-receiver count. Review the Week 5 grounding and delay before the snap, not after the series.

**Two-minute / hurry-up:** Keep the current 11 group, work Stick, Spacing, Drive, Mesh and Y-Cross through the practiced PRESS procedure. Skalaski gives Cousins the clock and timeouts; get the next call ready on an inbounds completion. A protected shot needs both the field position and clock.

**Four-minute / protect a lead:** Jones-Drew's Power, Counter and Outside Zone are the base; retain Y-Cross or a gated Boot when Tennessee crowds them. First downs end games. Discuss an inbounds completion, sack and opponent timeouts before the series, then use the clock deliberately.

**Sudden change / shot opportunities:** Start with a secure base call unless the safety or rush has already shown the exact shot trigger. Power Pass and Boot come before the Flea Flicker. Never confuse getting the ball after a turnover with permission for a gadget.

**Special plays:** 12 Ace, Flea Flicker, MAX: Keep; 12 Wing, TE Delay: Keep; 12 Trey, TE Delay: Keep

## 4. Defense: what we are showing and answering

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base front and run fits:** Under against credible tight-end and downhill run surfaces, Nickel Even when the spacing warrants it. Use the established first-unit four-man front. Daryl Smith makes the front declaration; Posluszny and the fit linebacker key Greene's path, the puller and the backside cutback. The force defender keeps outside leverage and the plus-one fitter arrives from the correct side. Do not let a pass look erase the run fit.

**Coverage and rush approach:** Show two-high, play Quarters or Cover 6 by distribution, and use 1 Robber against a real crossing/dig picture. Four rushers with disciplined edges are the default against Locker. We want him to hold the ball to confirm the picture, not escape a vacant lane. Bouye carries Wright's inside release when Wright aligns in his nickel area; Talib and Verner keep their outside jobs. Match Walker's seam and crossing release by rule, not by assigning one linebacker two routes. Replacement rush attacks a verified slide/back scan, never a guessed weakness.

**Rush rule:** Four unless I call the five-man counter

**What we need to discover early:** Does Schwenke point the pressure when we mug the interior, and which way does Greene scan? Where does Locker launch and attempt to escape when the edges hold? Who takes Wright when he moves inside, and how does the offense distribute Walker after motion?
