# Week 8 Game Plan: Miami

**Head coach:** Alex Stone  
**Game:** Miami at Jacksonville, Sunday, October 26, 2014, 1 p.m. ET, EverBank Field  
**Briefing used:** October 26, 2014  
**Baseline:** Carry Week 7

## 1. What I want this game to look like

**The matchup I am attacking:** Miami's front has 16 sacks through six games, with Olivier Vernon and Cameron Wake setting the edges. Make that front defend the run, the keeper and the screen from related 12-personnel looks. Kevin O'Connell's wide-zone and movement-pass structure supplies the first picture; Ben Johnson's Counter, Power, condensed releases and complementary shifts supply the answer when Miami overplays it. Move Kelce and Lewis without changing the eleven. Locate Grimes and Delmas before testing the intermediate middle. Win third down with protection and spacing, not a succession of slow-developing shots.

**What I must not give them:** Free edge rushes, a disguised pressure Cousins cannot identify, and a takeaway that gives Tannehill a short field. We converted one of eight third downs against Cleveland and gained 86 yards on the ground. On defense, do not repeat Cleveland's 32 first downs or 11 third-down conversions. Miller cannot reach the cutback untouched; Wallace cannot get a free vertical release; Clay and Gibson cannot own the easy catch at the marker. Donald's penetration must remain tied to a complete run fit and four connected rush lanes.

**What would make me change course:** If Wake or Vernon defeats the protection help, use SCAT Stick, Spacing, Mesh, the Slow Screen and a protected Y-Cross before taking another long-set throw. If Miami squeezes wide zone, hit Counter or Inside Zone; if the box loads, shift Kelce out and use the access or crossing answer. If Tannehill escapes an interior rush or Miami converts short third downs, simplify the A-gap presentation, contain both edges and tighten coverage at the marker. If cornerback or left-guard availability changes, reset the job assignments before kickoff.

## 3. Offense: what we are calling and why

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base personnel and roles:** Start from 12 personnel with Kelce and Lewis attached, wing or shifted to a spread picture; rotate to 11 without announcing pass. Anderson remains the first back and presses the wide-zone and inside-zone track, with Jones-Drew available for Power, Counter, pass protection and four-minute work. No preset touch split. Adams, if cleared, is X; Thielen works H and Hawkins Z, with Hurns and Gabriel used according to the actual matchup and availability. Monroe and Johnson need help on the correct edge, not an automatic chip that removes a required route. Linsley owns the center point with Bitonio or Lucas at left guard according to the medical and practice checks.

**The first things I want to learn:** Does Miami play Wake and Vernon upfield against the same Wing wide-zone and Boot picture? Does the front slant when Lewis moves? Will Grimes stay with Adams or remain to a side? Does Delmas insert into a Power fit or hold the post? Do Miami's linebackers travel or bump with Kelce's shift, and do they exchange the Mesh crossers? Which defender owns the back when we show a Slow Screen after a dropback? Let the answers select the return calls.

**Opening approach:** Begin with wide zone and quick-game distribution to force Miami to declare front strength and coverage leverage. Repeat the formation with Boot, then Power and Counter when the backside defender runs over the top. Mix Bunch Mesh, SOLID Y-Cross and the back screen into the same personnel sequence. Take a protected Power Pass only after a safety has actually attacked the run. Opening ideas are choices in priority order, not an unchangeable first-drive script.

**Opening ideas:** 12 Wing, Outside Zone Left; 11 Doubles, Stick, SCAT; 12 Ace, Power Right; 12 Wing, Boot Flood Right; 12 SHIFT Empty, Stick; 11 Trips, Inside Zone + Bubble; 11 Bunch, Mesh, HALF; 12 Ace, Counter Right; 12 Trey, Y-Cross, SOLID; 11 Doubles, RB Slow Screen; 12 Wing, Outside Zone Left; 11 Doubles, Inside Zone Right; 11 Doubles, Spacing, SCAT; 12 Wing, TE Delay; 11 Doubles, Drive, HALF; 12 Ace, Power Left; 11 Trey, Dagger, HALF; 12 Ace, Power Pass / Post-Cross, MAX; 11 Bunch, Snag; 12 Wing, Boot Flood Right

### Situational changes from the standing / previous menu

**Third down:** Short: counted Power, Counter or Inside Zone with access only when the perimeter wins leverage. Medium: Stick, Spacing, Mesh, Drive and SOLID Y-Cross, all with the marker as the landmark. Long: Y-Cross, protected Dagger or Slow Screen after the rush declares. We were one for eight against Cleveland; first-down gains and third-down distances matter as much as the call.

**Backed up:** Wide Zone, Inside Zone, SCAT Stick or Spacing. Use the correct chip and escape route. No blind vertical, delayed screen into the goal line or gratuitous shift that risks a false start.

**High red zone, low red zone, goal line:** High: Y-Cross, protected Power Pass after the run-fit trigger, or Boot when the edge chases. Low: Mesh, Snag, Power and Counter, with an identified free defender. Goal line: downhill run and one protected pass answer; finish the center-guard exchange before choosing the play.

**Two-minute / hurry-up:** HOLD 11 or 12 for Stick, Spacing, Drive, Mesh and Y-Cross; use PRESS only after the current eleven communicates properly. Cousins carries the protection point and immediate sideline/clock answer.

**Four-minute / protect a lead:** Run toward the soundest fit, protect the football and mix quick completion or a gated Boot when Miami packs the box. Know the field position, lead and remaining time before third down; prepare the fourth-down call under the standing rule instead of discovering it after a stop.

**Sudden change / shot opportunities:** Secure a first down before chasing a highlight. Power Pass or Boot only when the safety or edge has given the exact trigger; otherwise run the base concept or the back screen into an overaggressive rush.

**Special plays:** 12 Wing, TE Delay: Keep; 12 Trey, TE Delay: Keep

## 4. Defense: what we are showing and answering

**Caller:** Alex Stone

**Base front and run fits:** Under with Donald at the attacking interior 3-technique alongside Marks, Mincey and Andre Branch on the edges; move to Nickel Even against open formations and Bear for short yardage. Roy Miller is the heavy-run alternate. Posluszny makes the front call while Daryl Smith is out, with Telvin Smith and Sio Moore in the first linebacker group. Set force before pursuit and stay square against Lamar Miller's zone cutback. Donald's first job is to own his gap, then penetrate; Marks and the ends cannot chase inside and leave Tannehill a boot or scramble lane.

**Coverage and rush approach:** Show Spagnuolo's crowded pressure surface and Flores's late rotation, but earn both with clean assignments. Present the two A-gap threats and usually send four: Donald and Marks compress inside while the edges keep the cage. Rotate between 1 Robber, 3 Match, Quarters and Cover 6 according to release distribution. Put Wallace's deep stem first in the outside coverage count, Gibson's intermediate routes next, and Clay's seam, crossing release and flat work in the linebacker-safety exchange. Use a genuine fifth man with Fire 3 only when Tannehill's hot answer and the vacated zone are covered. With injured corners and Daryl Smith out, do not make complexity the objective.

**Rush rule:** Four unless I call the five-man counter

**What we need to discover early:** Does Samson Satele redirect the line when both A-gaps show pressure? Does Lamar Miller scan inside-out or release? Do Albert and James chase defensive-line movement or keep a firm edge? Does Tannehill take the immediate throw, climb past Donald or escape the edge? How does Wallace change splits; where does Gibson settle; does Clay stay to help or release through the middle? Record the answer before dialing a counter.
