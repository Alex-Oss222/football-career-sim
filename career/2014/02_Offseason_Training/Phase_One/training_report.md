# Phase One | Jacksonville Jaguars | 2014

**Location:** Jacksonville club facilities; individual rooms and fields were not specified.  
**Dates:** April 21 to May 1; staff handoff May 2.  
**Days completed:** Eight classroom and physical-work days.  
**Work addressed:** Shared protection language, Boot Flood's free-edge answer, defensive call responsibilities, punt instruction and individual physical preparation.

## Phase One: learning the calls in the room

The first two weeks combined classroom football with individual strength, conditioning and rehabilitation work. The performance staff ran the physical work; position coaches did not coach or observe it. The record contains individual physical plans, but no measured strength or conditioning gains to report. Rackley continued his classroom work while his existing restriction governed his physical work.

## Learning the offense

The returning offense reviewed Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Stick, Drive and the Mesh work it already knew. Boot Flood needed a clearer explanation. In the first discussion, the quarterbacks and the intermediate and flat receivers gave different answers when a defender was free outside the quarterback's rollout path. Bates and Tice had not taught that situation with one consistent cue. They settled the wording, and on April 29 the players gave the same answer from a fresh diagram: the quarterback has a taught outlet or throwaway answer and is not expected to outrun that edge defender.

Protection teaching had a similar problem. Bates and Yarno were giving different instructions about resetting the point when two defenders threatened the A-gaps beside the center. They reconciled their cues on April 24. Cousins, Brewster and the backs subsequently identified the point and the calls each needed to repeat correctly on the board. This established that they understood one another in the meeting room. It did not yet establish that they could do it before a snap.

## Learning the defense

Crennel's defensive meetings connected each call to what it was meant to take away, what it conceded and how the defense would answer. That addressed the question Marks and Miller had raised about the interior rush's responsibilities. Daryl Smith, Posluszny and Stanford worked through the communication job. Stanford needed the motion answer walked through again during the first week, then gave the right answer to a different motion diagram on April 29. Posluszny could explain his front and fit calls without prompting.

## Special teams and individual preparation

The newcomers also needed Jacksonville's words translated into familiar football. The position coaches prepared room-specific word lists after the veteran newcomers asked how the protection and coverage language compared with their previous clubs. Westhoff introduced the four punt situations in meetings: field position, plus territory, backed up and end game. There was no coached kicking or snapping in this phase.


## Handoff to Phase Two

The May 2 staff review closed the classroom block. Each signed player received his position coach's teaching note; no football film packet was delivered. The coaches had resolved the two competing explanations of Boot Flood and protection identification. Phase Two must now test whether players can give those answers while moving. Rackley's physical restriction still governs his work, and the four unsigned tenders remain outside the program under Stone's May 1 instruction.

## Before the program: April 18 staff review
Stone adopted his decisions on the eight open choices ([record](../stone_april_18_2014_decisions.md); status in the [decision package](../staff_decisions.md)). The staff fitted the Phase One classroom allocation to them. Where Stone's statement of a league rule goes beyond the [verified phase rules](../../../../library/2014_offseason_phase_rules_verification.md), the verified rule governed this phase: no position coach taught or observed field work, so all punt and snap instruction stayed in the classroom.

## Phase One, April 21 to May 1

### Control, medical and participation

- **Controlled roster:** 53 players, 49 under signed contracts and 4 on unsigned tenders.
- **Unsigned tenders:** Bradfield, Clemons, Brown and Pasztor are not under signed 2014 contracts, and the repository has not established a basis for their taking part in the program. No program work is recorded for them. Caldwell's office owns the question. Bradfield's RFA offer-sheet deadline is May 2.
- **Participation:** the program was voluntary. The 49 signed players took part within their medical instructions. No absence was recorded, and attendance was not used as evidence of anything.
- **Medical:** Will Rackley's existing limit (upper extremity, minor) is a physical restriction only. Under the injured-player learning rule he did the full classroom work, and the medical staff set his strength work. Posluszny, cleared April 5, worked without restriction. No new injury or restriction was communicated.

### Onboarding

The same process was used for all eleven newcomers: the five veterans signed in March (Verner, Talib, Nicks, Hawkins and Te'o-Nesheim) and the six reserve/future players (Bray, Murphy, Jerrell Jackson, Long, D'Anthony Smith and Blake).

- **April 21 (Day 0):** each received the welcome package with the complete offensive and defensive Iteration I books and the 2014 phase roadmap. The package says the whole book is theirs to study and that only taught material is evaluated. No package went out before the program, because the verified rule bars pre-program playbook study.
- **April 23 to 25:** Stone and each position coach held the follow-up conversation in the building, as they were on site.
- **What came back:** football feedback was recorded as teaching notes, not grades. The common request from the five veterans was a translation of Jacksonville's protection and coverage words into those of their previous clubs. The relevant position coaches wrote a one-page word list for each room. Nothing private was recorded.
- **Newcomers' focus:** by Stone's rule each newcomer works on his primary Jacksonville job first. None of them was given a second position.

### What was taught

Every classroom session used Explain, Show, Walk (players explained the job back). Rep and the later steps need field work and wait for Phase Two. Examples came from the documented 2013 locators in the [Cousins review index](../../00_Team_Operations/Film/kirk_cousins_2013_review.md) and the phase outputs, alongside labeled diagrams. Where the record lacks an angle or an assignment detail, the diagram says so.

**Offense (Tice; Bates, Yarno, Spencer, Drake, Zernhelt).**
- Review: Power, Counter, Inside Zone, Stick, Drive and the taught Mesh work, together with the other assignments shown during the season.
- Boot Flood returned to the classroom (active book 10.13, 11 and 11.3): the Outside Zone pairing, NAKED protection, the deep, intermediate and flat read, and what changes when a defender is outside the launch path. The examples were the good 2013 camp work and the Week 1 interception and Week 2 sack.
- Shared protection identification for quarterbacks, centers and backs (book 8 and 8.3): the center sets the point and others echo only what changes their rule.

**Defense (Crennel; Pleasant, Bush, Oden).**
- Review of the carried front, coverage and rush packages, with TAKE, GIVE and ANSWER written on each one. The motion answers are the book's TRAVEL, BUMP, ROLL and HOLD (book 6).
- Relay rotation: Crennel and Bush rotated the communication job among Daryl Smith, Posluszny and Stanford without changing anyone's defensive role. Stanford took full-call, echo and check reps. Moore learned the call structure for his own packages.

**Special teams (Westhoff).**
- The four punt families (field position, plus territory, backed up, end game) and three-part grading (operation, kick, coverage), taught with Anger, Cain, Scobee and the core coverage players.
- Anger's 2013 punts, starting with Week 12, were reviewed from written locators.
- No kicking or snapping was coached or observed.

**Cross-training observation lanes.** These opened in the classroom for Thielen, Kelce, Poyer, Moore, Prosinski and Trawick, with each player's primary job first. Bradfield's and Pasztor's lanes wait for their participation basis.

**Physical work.** The performance staff gave each signed player an individual plan (playing weight, strength, movement, conditioning and recovery) within his medical instructions. Coaches did not attend the field work.

### Staff operation

- **Consultation:** Stone, Tice, Crennel and Westhoff met for 25 minutes on April 21 and April 28. The coordinators and position coaches held the 45-minute week-end review on April 24 and May 1. No coach-run field session took place, so there was no post-field meeting.
- **Player council:** the first council met May 1 with a rotating group of veterans, younger players and newcomers. Its one actionable point, the Boot Flood wording, was already with Bates and Tice. It did not discuss the depth chart.
- **Emergency practice lead:** not used. Stone was present throughout.
- **Film:** no individual film packet was issued in Phase One. No packet has reached coach approval: the cause reviews are open and there is no video asset. The January 31 receipts stay unverified. Classroom discussion of 2013 examples is recorded here, not in the [delivery log](../../00_Team_Operations/Film/delivery_log.md).
- **Written notes:** the individual written notes for Phase One are due at the May 2 handoff.

### Social events

All were held as scheduled. Attendance was not recorded, no football was discussed, and nothing from them became personnel evidence.

- April 21: opening team dinner for players, coaches and football and support staff. Stone spoke briefly to welcome the newcomers and thank the staff.
- April 24: family barbecue.
- April 29: players and staff cookout.
- May 1: family dinner.

## May 1 instruction: the four unsigned tenders

The user settled the participation question on May 1 ([memo amendment](../../01_Early_Offseason/stone_to_caldwell_2014_offseason_decisions.md#september-30-2026-amendment-unsigned-tenders-and-the-cross-training-list)). Bradfield, Pasztor, Clemons and Brown are not program participants and not social-event participants until they sign. Bradfield's tackle and sixth-lineman lane and Pasztor's guard and emergency-center lane are closed; Monroe and Lane Johnson take the tackle work, and Brewster, Rackley, Asper, Monroe and Johnson are the signed linemen Stone coaches. Clemons and Brown receive no receiver install; receiver teaching goes through Thielen, Nicks, Hawkins, Jerrell Jackson and later eligible additions. No "fell behind" language attaches to any of the four, and a conditional catch-up plan is ready for each on the day he signs. The cross-training list is now Lane Johnson (limited opposite-tackle familiarity), Asper (both guard spots and emergency interior communication exposure), Thielen, Kelce, Poyer, Moore, Prosinski and Trawick.

## May 2: Phase One handoff

The staff handoff was held as the plan requires, with no player workout. Each room reported what was taught, retained, postponed and limited.

- **Current profile:** the established offense and Boot Flood's return, shared protection identification, TAKE, GIVE and ANSWER, the four punt families and the relay rotation were taught in the classroom to the 49 signed players. Nothing was certified as unit execution; there was no field work. The two staff wording gaps found in Phase One were closed.
- **Individual written notes:** each position coach issued the Phase One note to each signed player in his room: what was taught, what the player explained back correctly, what was postponed to the field. The notes are teaching records, not grades, and are held by the coaches; no note went to a player on an unsigned tender.
- **Postponed:** every Rep, Correct and Retain step waits for Phase Two field work. Brewster's protection-call feedback item stays with Yarno.
- **Limited by medicine:** Rackley's physical limit continues; his classroom work was complete.
- **Open staff decisions carried to Phase Two:** none new. The Boot Flood read and the protection cue go to unopposed movement first; the backup relay needs field evidence; the film packets still lack coach approval.
- **RFA deadline:** the offer-sheet deadline for Bradfield passed May 2 with no offer sheet on record; there was nothing to match on May 7.
