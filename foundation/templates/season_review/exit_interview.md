# Exit interview template

Choose the player or staff variant below. Keep one completed meeting record at its [season-review destination](README.md). This template does not hold the meeting, advance the clock or authorize Stone's unspoken decisions. Read the actual season's game and training findings, prior commitments and the participant's role before writing. Use the active book relevant to the work being reviewed, not a later iteration.

An interview is a conversation about the season. Follow the matters the actual participants raised; do not force a question under every possible topic. A retrospective account may summarize existing notes, but must identify that basis and cannot add dialogue, a claimed film session or the player's private reaction. Use quotation marks only for recorded words. Where a view was not expressed, leave it unreported instead of writing an answer for the person.

## Player variant

# [Player] | [Team] | [Season] exit interview

**Held:** [Actual date and location or medium, if recorded]

**Participants:** [Actual player and staff present]

**Season work reviewed:** [Dates and roles actually covered; distinguish limited or interrupted opportunities]

**Record basis:** [Recorded conversation, contemporary meeting notes, or retrospective summary of named existing notes]

**Player record:** [Opening annual assessment and dated updates; link the final assessment only when it exists]

## The season he actually had

[Open with the football role the player performed and the most important question the review addressed. Relate the starting expectation to the work he received, including changed roles, surrounding personnel, injury-limited opportunities or time away when relevant. Do not retell the whole schedule or equate participation with effectiveness. Use his actual playing season, not what a same-name historical counterpart did.]

## The work the staff reviewed

[Develop the consequential strengths and limitations through representative recorded work. Explain the assigned job, what the player recognized, his technique and physical execution, and the effect on the connected unit. What became dependable? What required repeated help? Which correction held when the opponent, personnel or situation changed, and which remained inconsistent? Preserve contrary evidence. A small sample or an untested task remains limited without becoming a failed evaluation.]

[Give the staff perspectives that were actually delivered. The position coach can explain the specific technique and response to teaching; the coordinator can explain how the role worked within the offense, defense or kicking game; Stone can state his recorded team-level judgment. These are different responsibilities, not three required speeches. If reconstructing football analysis from the record, identify it once as analysis and do not imply a conversation occurred.]

## The player's account

[Record the player's expressed view of his work, role, teaching and opportunities. Follow up only on the actual issues: an instruction he found unclear, a job he felt ready to try, useful feedback, or support he lacked. Keep a disagreement visible without converting it into conflict or forcing agreement. An unexpressed view is not consent, discontent or a known motive.]

## Availability and support

[Include the communicated medical or conditioning findings that affect the transition, and who is responsible for them. Distinguish missed opportunity from poor play. The coach can discuss the available workload and football role; medical staff retain diagnosis and clearance. Link the medical owner instead of repeating a treatment history. Include other support only when discussed and relevant, without inventing private personal circumstances. Omit this heading when the meeting established nothing in this area.]

## What was agreed and what remains open

[State the actual feedback, commitments and unfinished questions. Identify who owes the next piece of work, such as a cutup, explanation, medical follow-up or development proposal, and the real timing if established. A promise to review a role is not a promised appointment; a suggested offseason exercise is not a compulsory club session or completed training. Respect the applicable contact calendar. Do not turn a coach's retention preference into a contract, roster or acquisition decision.]

[The separate final annual assessment uses this meeting alongside the season's evidence. Link it when completed and preserve the opening assessment. Do not add a new overall grade table here. A departed player receives no invented postseason interview; retain any actual departure review and its date.]

## Evidence and follow-through

[Link the game/training findings actually discussed, the opening player card, relevant medical source and actual commitment or decision owner. Keep receipt identifiers and source hashes out of the conversation. Close an actual meeting under the shared event-record procedure and add its one-line annual Record.md link; a rewrite of old notes does not create a new meeting.]

## Staff variant

# [Coach] | [Role] | [Team] | [Season] exit interview

**Held:** [Actual date and location or medium, if recorded]

**Participants:** [Actual participants and authority for this review]

**Responsibilities reviewed:** [The coach's actual assignments, including delegated work and changes during the season]

**Record basis:** [Recorded conversation, contemporary meeting notes, or retrospective summary of named existing notes]

**Coaching record:** [Link the coach's existing profile and dated contributions]

## The season's assignment

[Explain what this coach was responsible for, the players and staff he worked with and the central question the season raised about that work. Separate the coordinator's, position coach's and head coach's actual responsibilities. A unit's win-loss or statistical ranking does not identify who taught a player, selected a call or caused a breakdown.]

## Teaching and preparation

[Use recorded examples to assess how the coach explained the job, designed practice, gave feedback and checked whether learning survived a changed situation. Did the players receive consistent instruction across rooms? Did a correction address the actual cause? Which players received useful opportunities, and whose work remained underobserved? Include successful teaching as well as failures where supported. Do not infer an effective teacher solely from a productive player or invent poor effort from a slow correction.]

## Game work and staff coordination

[Review the actual preparation, personnel support, calls and adjustments within this coach's authority. Explain coordination with other rooms, sideline and booth communication, substitutions and situational preparation where those were his jobs. Distinguish a sound instruction executed poorly from an unsuitable assignment or late call. Identify what the staff learned from later evidence without rewriting the information available when the decision was made.]

## The coach's account and response

[Preserve the coach's expressed assessment, explanation, disagreement, request or idea. Include Stone's response only when the user actually gave it or an established instruction directly covers it. A head-coach review follows the actual reporting authority and cannot invent Stone's self-assessment or the owner's judgment. Label a retrospective analytical synthesis rather than staging a private exchange that was never recorded.]

## Work carried into the next year

[Record the actual retained methods, changes, unresolved player work and staff commitments. Name the responsible person and next useful check where agreed. A proposal to alter teaching, delegation, personnel or scheme remains a proposal until authorized. Contract renewal, dismissal, promotion or replacement belongs to the appropriate staff/contract owner and is not inferred from this review.]

## Evidence and follow-through

[Link the existing coaching profile, representative training/game reports and actual decision or commitment owners. Append a material change or retained assessment to the existing coaching profile according to its method, rather than creating a duplicate coaching dossier. Keep this record focused on the actual meeting. Use the shared event-record procedure for a completed review; do not create a second season-review narrative or a new central ledger.]
