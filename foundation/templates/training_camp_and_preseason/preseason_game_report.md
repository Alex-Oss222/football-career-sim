# Preseason game report template

Use the [report instructions](README.md). This is the preseason version of the [season output template](../season_output_template.md); its evidence, narrative, media and generated-box-score rules still apply. The game must have been resolved through the authorized game process. An unplayed game gets a preparation note, never a filled result or simulated highlight added by the report writer.

Read the [research and coaching questions](../../../docs/preseason_game_reports.md) and the [worked report extract](../../../docs/examples/preseason_game_review_2013_miami.md) for the intended depth and voice. The extract uses an archived game; its names, results and conclusions are not defaults for another game.

## Before writing

Read the prepared game menu, camp questions, actual participation and substitutions, game receipt and play record, medical instructions and dated coaching observations. Use the relevant active playbook entries to understand the assigned jobs. The full book is not proof that a concept was taught or called. A box score supplies outcomes; it cannot by itself supply a missed assignment, a spoken check, a player's effort or a technical cause.

Keep an immediate postgame impression distinct from a completed staff review. Do not claim to have watched film, heard the huddle or held a coaches' meeting unless that work is recorded. Give the staff's supported assessment directly, with its basis; when the cause is unresolved, identify the useful competing explanations and the next check. Do not turn an unknown cause into either a confident accusation or an empty paragraph.

Treat starters, reserves and mixed groups by their actual work. A quarterback change does not establish that the whole offense changed with him. Use recorded teammates, opponents, assignment, help and opportunity in comparisons. Late-game success counts; neither the quarter nor a depth-chart label proves the quality of the opposition. Do not assume equal snaps or invent snap counts.

The rendered report combines a selected game story with a coaching review. Usually 900 to 1,400 words of football prose, excluding the box score, is enough; write less when the evidence is thin. These are not quotas. Name the players who explain the game and the unresolved jobs, including quiet blocking, coverage and special-teams work. Do not force a best player, a culprit, matching praise and criticism, or a note on every active player. Keep the main headings below; combine related player findings into natural paragraphs. Author instructions and research citations stay outside the game narrative.

## Report to render

# [Team] | Preseason game [number] | [Opponent, date and venue]

**Final:** [Verified result from the game receipt.]

**Review:** [Immediate postgame assessment, or completed staff review with its actual evidence-through date.]

## What the staff wanted to see

[Briefly identify the camp questions the game was meant to test, the prepared menu, personnel opportunities and material workload limits. Explain why a player or combination received that work when the reason is recorded. Distinguish the plan from who actually played. Do not assume every starter plays the same number of series or that game three always has a fixed rehearsal role. Include the recorded pregame media exchange when there is one.]

## How the game unfolded

[Open with how the team played as a whole: what sustained it, what repeatedly hurt it and how those things shaped the result. Follow the meaningful action in connected prose. Select the drives, stops, mistakes, adjustments and substitutions that changed the game or answered an evaluation question. Show offense, defense and special teams interacting through possession, field position and the situations they left each other. Include the opponent's contribution. Avoid a touchdown-only recap or a possession-by-possession log.]

[Explain what happened when the personnel changed, if the record supports a comparison. A productive opening group and an uneven relief group belong in the same game story, with the actual reasons and context. Describe a later correction or continued problem only when it occurred. Keep exact clocks, calls, down-and-distance and technical causes to what the record establishes. End the story at its last material consequence; the assessment follows.]

## How the units played

### Offense

[Explain which parts of the prepared offense functioned and which broke down, with named players and representative evidence. Assess the actual opening, relief and mixed combinations. Could they line up, handle a changed defensive picture and run the intended call? Did the run blocks and back's track fit together? Did the quarterback, protection and routes provide the same answer at the same time? Describe drive sustainability and the relevant third-down, red-zone or clock situations, including failures that did not become turnovers. Use game statistics to explain the finding, not to replace it.]

[Separate recognition, technique, matchup and decision errors. A sack can involve the protection call, a lost block, the back, the route answer or the quarterback's timing. Attribute the cause only where the evidence supports it. A designed unblocked edge is not automatically a bust. A completion does not clear a late read or wrong route; an incompletion can include a correct decision. Explain what support made a combination work and what that support cost the rest of the call.]

### Defense

[Explain the defense's collective play and the changes with its actual personnel. Connect front and gap control to linebacker fits and the force/support defender; connect rush lanes and pressure to coverage and help. Assess block defeat, tackling and pursuit where observed, along with motion/bunch exchanges, leverage and the response to changed formations. Explain whether an explosive play was an isolated loss or part of a repeated problem only when the wider record supports that distinction. A tackle total, sack or interception cannot stand in for the whole assignment. Credit the defender whose sound job allowed someone else to make the play.]

### Special teams

[Assess the kicking units as football units: snap/hold/protection, kick instruction and placement, releases, coverage lanes, blocks, return decisions and the resulting field position. Distinguish specialist execution from the protection or coverage around him. Name reserves whose work mattered. Include actual substitution or emergency-operation findings. A made kick does not prove protection was sound; participation alone does not earn coverage credit.]

## Communication and game operation

[Explain whether the team could get the call from the sideline into the huddle, make its shared declarations and get the correct people aligned before the snap. Use specific recorded examples of protection agreement, coverage exchanges, substitutions, tempo, clock handling or a replacement taking over communication. Describe what held together and what needed rescue, including timeout or play-menu costs when established. Account for coach-to-player communication as well as player-to-player communication.]

[Do not label every bust a communication problem. Distinguish a call not received, conflicting instructions, a correctly understood job executed poorly and a cause that is still unclear. A clean penalty line or a win does not establish clean communication. If only one unit has usable evidence, state that limit once and assess that unit without awarding the whole team a communication verdict.]

## Players who stood out and questions still open

[Use named paragraphs with the [player-assessment method](player_assessment.md). State what the player did well, what he did poorly where supported, and what that means for his particular job. Include meaningful contrary evidence in the same assessment. Connect the game to the previous camp question: was it answered, partly answered, contradicted or never tested? Show how an error or successful technique held up on another opportunity when recorded. Do not merely repeat the scoring plays or the unit section.]

[Give the reader the distinction that matters: dependable work, a useful performance earning another look, a recurring limitation, or an unresolved comparison. Those are judgments to express in normal prose, not mandatory labels or grades. A receiver can help through spacing or blocking without a catch; a defensive back can execute his coverage without a target. Credit those jobs only with actual evidence. A productive reserve with a narrower menu has established something useful within that menu, not mastery of the entire offense. Playing little, being medically limited or never encountering the planned test is not failure.]

## Stone's decisions and the next practice

[Record consequential choices Stone actually made, why the recorded football problem prompted them and what happened afterward. Separate the choice from its execution. Consider the staff's teaching, call, personnel support and sideline operation alongside the players. Do not rewrite Stone's intentions from the final score or invent a private reaction.]

[Give each material correction its responsible coach and next useful opportunity: film/meeting clarification, a technique period, a combination rep, a situation or a later game exposure as appropriate. Explain what would count as improvement in that job. Use that season's actual staff; a proposed correction is not a delivered lesson, and a planned retest is not a successful rep. Distinguish a recommendation for different work from Stone's actual role decision. Link any actual role or availability change to its owner. Include recorded postgame media in Stone's established voice without manufacturing quotes in a retrospective rewrite. End the football review with the next work or the consequential choice that pauses the bulk turn.]

## Box score

[Insert the complete generated box score for both teams using the repository's supported preseason receipt/rendering path. Preserve the game's receipt and provenance. Do not hand-fill a number or route the preseason game through a regular-season statbook just to populate this section. If the production path cannot provide the required record, report the specific blocker.]
