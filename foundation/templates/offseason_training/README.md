# Offseason training reports

Use these phase versions of the [offseason output template](../offseason_output_template.md) for the report in chat and the readable opening of `training_report.md`. The writing and evidence guidance below also applies to [training camp](../training_camp_and_preseason/README.md). The individual templates add the questions particular to their phase; they do not create another resolution process.

## Choose the phase

| Work being reported | Template | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| Phase One | [Classroom and conditioning](phase_one_report.md) | Can the players explain their jobs, and what physical preparation is documented? |
| Phase Two | [Unopposed field teaching](phase_two_report.md) | Can they carry out the assignment while moving with their unit? |
| Rookie minicamp | [Introduction to the team](rookie_minicamp_report.md) | What did the actual newcomers show in their first assigned work? |
| OTAs | [Team practice](otas_report.md) | Does the work hold when the opposing unit changes the picture? |
| Mandatory minicamp | [Spring review and summer work](mandatory_minicamp_report.md) | What can the group run now, and what remains for camp? |

Phase One and Phase Two have separate folders, plans and reports. Rookie minicamp is its own event on the verified post-draft calendar and can overlap Phase Two. A particular club's chosen agenda does not become a league rule. Verify the season's rules; these templates neither authorize contact nor create a new-head-coach minicamp.

## Read the work before writing

Read the current state to locate the career, then the requested event's dates, actual participants and restrictions, staff plan, completed observations and relevant active-book entries. Use the people and evidence at that event's date. Read the [assessment method](../training_camp_and_preseason/player_assessment.md) for interpreting individual and shared assignments.

A plan explains intent; the completed record establishes what happened. For authorized new work, follow the existing practice, authority and continuity rules before writing its report. For a closed phase, use the existing observations without rerunning it, adding a representative rep or borrowing later results. Real-world research explains practice methods; it supplies no Jacksonville performance.

## Write the football

Lead with the most useful supported assessment of the group. Follow with a short account of the work that produced it: the meaningful classroom, individual, group, team or situational periods, their purpose and any participation limit that changed the opportunity. Count actual days and distinguish separate sessions when recorded. Physicals, staff reviews and planned sessions are not additional practices. Do not infer rep totals, contact intensity or field location from a normal NFL schedule.

Within offense, defense and special teams, connect the players' jobs. Explain how the formation and call set their responsibilities, what each relevant player did and what that allowed the next player to do. A late protection declaration can affect the center, guard, back and quarterback; give that shared problem one coherent account. Identify whether a failure came from the call, instruction, recognition, communication, technique or the matchup only as far as the evidence supports.

Cover the position rooms that did consequential work, including the line, reserves and established players. Explain why a combination or unfamiliar position was tried and what the actual opportunity revealed. Do not turn the report into a playbook catalogue or a row for every roster member. A quiet, correctly executed job can matter as much as a conspicuous play.

For a block of practices, explain the important movement across its dates: what the group brought in, what a later session clarified, and what remained inconsistent or untested. Describe the coach's response and the later work when recorded. A change of instruction, demonstration, personnel combination or workload may matter; a correction is not compulsory. An immediate clean repeat and retention on another day support different conclusions. Neither guarantees continued improvement.

Use plain football language. Explain an unfamiliar call where it matters and name the actual people involved. Describe good work directly. A completion can conceal poor spacing; a correct assignment can still lose. When the cause is unclear, keep the observation and the uncertainty together. Do not force a mistake, comeback, standout, rivalry or improvement to give the report a story.

## Carry the work forward

End with the few questions that will actually shape the next work. For each, retain the dated source, the responsible coach or unit, the specific next opportunity and what that opportunity should clarify. Link the existing observation or a section of this report; do not repeat the whole account. A short paragraph is enough for one question. When several need comparison, an optional table can use:

| Work carried forward | Evidence and current finding | Coach or unit responsible | Next opportunity and question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Specific taught job] | [Dated report/section link and what it established] | [Existing responsibility; label a proposed assignment] | [Authorized work, or a clearly proposed test; no prewritten result] |

Separate a recommendation, an authorized trial and an actual role decision. Bring Stone a decision only when the next football action genuinely requires one. Routine coaching under the adopted plan does not need renewed approval. If the next session is not established, describe the useful opportunity without inventing its date.

## Evidence and voice

- State the phase's opposition and contact limits near the start; repeat them only when they change a conclusion. Classroom recall, rehearsal, non-contact opposition and physical execution are different evidence.
- Attribute an actual staff assessment to its source. Label an interpretive reconstruction where no attributed judgment exists. Do not invent a meeting, quote, player explanation or Stone decision to make the report human.
- Keep medical information to communicated participation instructions. Separate medical availability, conditioning and football technique. Voluntary absence and family attendance are not commitment evidence.
- Use the active books and assigned teaching. Access to the complete playbook does not establish that every page was taught or tested. Later real-life outcomes never serve as a private answer key.
- Keep hashes, engine badges, receipt checks and approval bookkeeping out of the football account. Mention an administrative issue only when it changes participation or the next action.

## Use in the LLM chatbox

Render the filled template directly as normal Markdown as well as saving it; a file link alone is insufficient. Replace all bracketed instructions. Keep the recognizable section order while adapting headings to the work. Give each unit the space its evidence warrants, without equal-length sections, a minimum number of anecdotes or a fixed word target. A short conditioning update and a three-week teaching review should differ in depth. Answer a narrow follow-up from the saved evidence without replaying the whole report or advancing time.

The saved `training_report.md` is the single owner of the activity and coaching observations. Its opening account summarizes the work; dated supporting detail preserves unique evidence without repeating it in a separate phase assessment file. Necessary continuity comments follow the [phase record instructions](../phase_record_template.md) and remain outside the rendered report.

There are exactly two full player assessments each year: the opening baseline, maintained with dated material updates, and a separate final assessment after the season. Preserve the opening judgment. Routine teaching findings stay in the phase report; a changed personnel view gets a source-linked note on the existing annual card. An actual competition uses its existing [battle card](../position_battle_card_template.md). None of these updates creates a fresh whole-player assessment or automatically changes a grade. New arrivals receive an opening assessment when they join.
