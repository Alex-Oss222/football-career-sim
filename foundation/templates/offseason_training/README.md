# Offseason training reports

Adopted at the user's September 30, 2026 request. These are the training-phase versions of the [offseason output template](../offseason_output_template.md). Use them for the report in chat and the readable opening of `training_report.md`. The general offseason financial, draft and job-status tables do not belong in an ordinary practice report.

## Choose the phase

| Work being reported | Template | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| Phase One | [Classroom and conditioning](phase_one_report.md) | Can the players explain their jobs, and what physical work is actually documented? |
| Phase Two | [Unopposed field teaching](phase_two_report.md) | Can they line up and carry out the assignment while moving? |
| Rookie minicamp | [Introduction to the team](rookie_minicamp_report.md) | What did each newcomer learn, and what needs more teaching? |
| OTAs | [Team practice](otas_report.md) | Does the work hold when the other unit changes the picture? |
| Mandatory minicamp | [Spring review and summer work](mandatory_minicamp_report.md) | What can the group run independently, and what still needs work in camp? |

Phases One and Two may share a season file. Give each its own account. Rookie minicamp is a separate event on the verified post-draft calendar; it is not automatically a third week between those phases. These templates do not apply to padded training camp or authorize a new-head-coach minicamp.

Continue with the [training camp and preseason templates](../training_camp_and_preseason/README.md) when those phases are reached. Use the shared [player-assessment method](../training_camp_and_preseason/player_assessment.md) in every phase: connect the active playbook's taught job to the actual work, the coach's correction, needed support and the next useful test. Keep each phase's own evidence limits. The format carries forward each season; roster names, dates, active books and findings do not.

## Write the football

Start with the staff's supported read of the work. Use ordinary football language: who lined up where, what was called, what the player saw or did, the effect on the play, the coaching correction and the later response. Explain an unfamiliar call where it matters. A named formation is useful only when its spacing, eligible receivers, motion or matchups explain the observation. Do not list the playbook as a substitute for reporting practice.

Give offense, defense and special teams their own attention. Cover the position rooms within those accounts, including the line and reserves; do not force a highlight for every player. Use a short player table only when several follow-ups are easier to compare. The individual records retain the full roster's notes.

Describe good work directly. A correct assignment can still lose; a completion can conceal poor spacing. A conceded flat throw is not automatically a coverage bust. When several players give different answers, check whether the coaches taught different things. If the cause is unknown, say that. No required mistake, redemption, standout, conflict, improvement or fixed number of examples.

Replace abstractions with their evidence: "Turner called the change after the cadence had started" says more than "processing-timing item." Explain whether a correction held immediately, on a later day, or has not been checked again. Do not turn that distinction into a status ladder in the prose.

## Evidence and voice

- Read the phase plan for intent and the dated output for results. Use the active books only. Real-world practice research explains the activity, never how a branch player performed.
- For a closed phase, summarize its existing evidence. Do not rerun it, add a representative rep, invent a quote, or use a later phase to improve its ending. Future opportunities belong in the final paragraph as future work.
- For authorized new work, resolve it under the existing practice, authority and continuity rules before writing the report. Templates select what to explain; they never select outcomes. A missing result is a gap to resolve or disclose, not a blank to fill with a plausible success.
- Attribute a staff judgment as a judgment and a player explanation as something actually offered. Do not script Stone's words, emotions or choices. Use direct dialogue only when the record supports the words. Specific coaching does not require profanity, a speech or an invented meeting scene.
- State the non-contact or unopposed setting once near the start. Repeat a limitation only where it changes a conclusion. Never turn spring line work into proof of blocking power or rush wins, or classroom recall into field execution.
- Keep medical information to the communicated participation instructions. Voluntary absence and family attendance are not effort or commitment evidence.
- Leave hidden mechanics, badges, hashes, receipt checks and approval bookkeeping out of the chat report. Preserve any existing assessment and its evidence in the supporting record. Bring up an unresolved administrative item only when it actually changes the next football action.

## Use in the LLM chatbox

Read the current state to orient the career, then the requested phase's dates, participants, plan, completed observations and relevant active-book entries. When reviewing an older phase, use evidence through its own close, not today's roster or later development.

Render the filled template as normal Markdown, not a fenced block, JSON object or questionnaire. The headings are containers for connected prose, not fields the user must complete. Replace bracketed instructions, adapt headings to the actual work and omit empty optional sections. A normal phase review usually fits in 500 to 900 words; this is a reading target, not an event quota. A combined Phase One/Two review can be longer. Answer a narrow follow-up from the existing record without replaying the entire report or advancing time.

Close with the next football work and only a real decision needing Stone. If none is pending, say what the staff will work on next within the authorized plan. Do not ask for already-adopted calls, reapprove routine drills or resolve a new role yourself. A request to read an old report never advances the master clock.

The saved `training_report.md` remains the event owner, including its existing `sim-meta` header. The four rewritten 2014 reports retain their original detailed session records inside an expandable section so dates, individual observations, old anchors and closed decisions remain available. Show the opening report in chat; consult the detailed record for follow-ups. New reports should keep supporting detail only where needed, without duplicating the whole narrative. Review `player_assessments.md` against the full report before refreshing its receipt.
