## 1. Purpose and authority

This document records the competition, era, rules, calendar, labor or eligibility system, financial structure, technology, historical conditions, social environment, verified sources, and factual divergence point for one coaching simulation.

It does not decide the protagonist head coach's background, personality, authority, or choices. Those belong in Document 3, together with the head coach's own contract. It does not carry the live roster, current injuries, depth chart, staff workload, player or non-protagonist-staff contract commitments, or detailed current financial reconciliation. Those belong in Document 4. It does not carry the changing date, record, current week, pending decision, or derived decision-facing resource summary. Those belong in Document 5. It does not carry an accumulating event, correction, or supersession history. That belongs only in Document 6.

This document never contains hidden simulator facts. Do not place opponent plans, unexpressed thoughts, undisclosed front-office decisions, private medical information, future outcomes, random seeds, or secretly predetermined reactions here. If the platform supports private state, essential hidden state remains there. Otherwise, leave the matter undetermined until a plausible event resolves it.

Once the user confirms the simulation mode, the confirmed fields in this document are stable canon. Amend a locked field only when:

1. The user expressly changes the simulation mode.
2. An official rule or fact applicable on the simulation date is corrected.
3. A deliberate fictional alteration is approved and entered in the divergence register.
4. A contradiction audit identifies a higher-authority record.

Never silently blend rules from different leagues, seasons, collective bargaining agreements, or historical periods.

### 1.1 Authoring master and active-only runtime edition

Before mode lock, this file can serve as an authoring master containing templates, competing candidates, audit findings, research notes, and quarantined comparators. It is not a runtime document.

Immediately after the user confirms the mode lock:

1. Create a new version of Document 2 marked `ACTIVE-ONLY EDITION`.
2. Retain only the selected competition, league, season, team, start date, divergence policy, applicable rules, applicable calendar, historical conditions, and sources that support active canon.
3. Remove all unused template branches, rejected settings, unchosen candidates, migration commentary, audit findings, provisional research, and unresolved legacy claims.
4. Remove every actual-future comparator dated after the divergence point.
5. Preserve only a concise statement that post-divergence actual history is noncanonical. Do not preserve a list of what actually happened later.
6. Close the authoring master as a research artifact outside the six runtime canonical documents.
7. Record creation and supersession of the active edition once in Document 6.

The audit report, provisional research, source-comparison working notes, and this document's `Provisional Legacy Candidate` section are never loaded during simulation play. They can be consulted only during a paused audit, correction, migration, or new mode-lock procedure. Normal play reads the active-only edition.

### 1.2 Team, league, or governing-body change

A career move to another team, league, governing body, season ruleset, or materially different historical jurisdiction closes the current sourcebook version. Do not overwrite it in place.

1. Record the closing date and reason in Document 6.
2. Create a new Document 2 version with `Supersedes` pointing to the closed version.
3. Reverify the team, venue, location, calendar, rules, labor or eligibility system, finances, technology, social setting, and source scope.
4. Repeat the mode-lock validation and obtain user confirmation for any variable that changes the simulation premise.
5. Generate a new active-only runtime edition before play resumes.

A same-league team change still requires versioning and re-locking because organization, venue, market, schedule, authority environment, and dated factual snapshot change even if the league rulebook does not.

## 2. Evidence and canon labels

Every material claim entered here must carry one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified pre-divergence fact** | Supported by an identified reliable source, applicable before the divergence, and available through a permitted channel | Fixed unless corrected by better evidence |
| **User canon** | Expressly created, approved, or corrected by the user | Fixed simulation fact, even when it differs from reality |
| **Post-divergence simulation event** | Occurred after initialization inside the simulation | Canon only when closed with a date in Document 6 |
| **Attributed report or assessment** | A dated public or communicated claim by an identified source | Evidence of the claim, not automatic objective truth |
| **Labeled inference** | Best-supported conclusion from incomplete evidence | Usable only with its reasoning and uncertainty visible |
| **Unresolved legacy claim** | Appears in supplied material but lacks adequate support or conflicts with other material | Quarantined and unusable until resolved |
| **Undetermined** | Not yet fixed and not safe to infer | Leave open; do not invent precision |
| **Quarantined actual-future comparator** | Real event after the divergence point | Authoring/audit context only; removed from active runtime edition |

A source can verify that a real event happened. It cannot convert a post-divergence real event into simulation canon.

### 11.1 Competitive financial rules

**Populated 2026-09-17 with verified 2013 NFL figures** (2011 CBA era). Full sourcing and independent verification notes in `library/2013_league_calendar_and_financial_rules.md`.

- Salary cap or roster-cost limit: $123,000,000 per team for the 2013 league year (up from $120.6M in 2012). Confirmed independently across NFL.com, ESPN, CBS Sports, and NFL Network's own year-by-year cap history.
- Salary floor or minimum team salary: No single-season per-team floor. Under the 2011 CBA, each team must spend at least 89% of the aggregate salary cap in cash, measured as an aggregate over the four-year period 2013-2016 (and again 2017-2020) — not a single-season requirement. Falling short in any one season is not itself a violation; any shortfall at the end of the four-year window is paid directly to players. There is no separate single-year 95% league-wide requirement specific to 2013 alone (an earlier draft of this entry stated one; it was corrected during verification — the closest such figure, a 99% league-wide cash floor, applied only to 2011-2012, a different rule and different years).

The kickoff moved to the 35 in 2011, not 2010. See the [NFL's 2011 change report](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-moves-kickoffs-to-35-yard-line-touchbacks-unchanged-09000d5d81ee38c1).

### D.2 Overtime

- Preseason and regular season: one 15-minute pure sudden-death period. The first score by safety, field goal, or touchdown wins. Each club has two timeouts. The game can end tied if the period expires scoreless.
- Postseason: modified sudden death adopted for the 2010 postseason. An opening-possession touchdown ends the game. If the opening team scores a field goal, the opponent receives an opportunity to possess. Thereafter, the next score can end the game. Postseason play continues in 15-minute periods until a winner, with three timeouts per half of overtime.
- All overtime replay reviews are booth initiated. Coaches cannot challenge.

See the NFL's [2010 postseason overtime explanation](https://www.nfl.com/news/postseason-overtime-rules-09000d5d81d817d7).

### D.3 Replay and challenges

- Before the two-minute warning of either half, a coach can initiate a review.
- After the two-minute warning and throughout overtime, only the replay assistant initiates.
- Each team receives two challenges. If both are successful, it receives a third.
- A challenge requires an available timeout. A successful challenge restores it.
- The referee reviews at a field-level monitor.
- Reversal requires indisputable visual evidence.
- The review period is limited to 60 seconds from the referee beginning monitor review.
- Scoring plays were reviewable, but were not automatically reviewed in 2010.
- Turnovers were not automatically reviewed in 2010.

Automatic review of scoring plays began in 2011, as confirmed by the [NFL report on the 2011 change](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-moves-kickoffs-to-35-yard-line-touchbacks-unchanged-09000d5d81ee38c1). Automatic turnover review began in 2012, as confirmed by the [NFL report on the 2012 adoption](https://www.nfl.com/news/owners-vote-to-adopt-playoff-ot-rules-in-regular-season-09000d5d827ecefd).

### D.4 Material 2010 rule changes

The NFL's [contemporaneous rule-change report](https://www.nfl.com/news/owners-approve-new-player-safety-rules-weigh-expanding-ot-rule-09000d5d8171f72f) records:

- Expanded head and neck protection for defenseless players.
- The ball becomes dead at the progress spot when a runner loses his helmet.
- The umpire moves behind the offensive backfield.
- A defender cannot line up directly across from the snapper on a field goal or try.
- Certain dead-ball personal fouls on the final play carry to the next-half or overtime kickoff.
- Contact with a videoboard, guide wire, or sky camera creates a dead ball and replayed down.
- A final-minute replay stoppage can create a 10-second runoff if the clock otherwise would have run, subject to timeout relief.

Use the exact official rulebook if a live decision turns on a detail not resolved by these summaries.
