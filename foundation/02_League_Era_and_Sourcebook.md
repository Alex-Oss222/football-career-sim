## 11. Financial and resource structure

### 11.1 Competitive financial rules

**Populated 2026-09-17 with verified 2013 NFL figures** (2011 CBA era). Full sourcing and independent verification notes in `library/2013_league_calendar_and_financial_rules.md`.

- Salary cap or roster-cost limit: $123,000,000 per team for the 2013 league year (up from $120.6M in 2012). Confirmed independently across NFL.com, ESPN, CBS Sports, and NFL Network's own year-by-year cap history.
- Salary floor or minimum team salary: No single-season per-team floor. Under the 2011 CBA, each team must spend at least **89%** of the aggregate Salary Caps in cash over the four-year period 2013-2016 (and again 2017-2020). Separately, clubs **league-wide** must reach **95%** cash spending over the same four-year period. Neither figure is a 2013-only spending requirement. Falling short in one season is not itself a violation; the four-year period controls.
- Accounting period: League year runs from the 2013 league-year start (March 12, 2013, 4:00 PM ET) through the following year's league-year start.
- Cap treatment of bonuses: For ordinary veteran Player Contracts, signing bonuses are prorated straight-line over the contract term to a maximum of **5 years** under 2011 CBA Article 13 §6. Rookie signing bonuses are separately limited to a maximum of **4 years** under Article 7. Do not apply the rookie four-year maximum to veteran contracts.
- Dead money or termination accounting: If a player is released, waived, or traded on or before June 1, remaining unamortized signing bonus generally accelerates into the current League Year. After June 1, future-year unamortized signing-bonus amounts generally count in the following League Year. Before June 1, a club may designate up to two qualifying terminated contracts per League Year for June-2 accounting treatment. In a trade or waiver assignment, the acquiring club does not inherit the old club's signing-bonus proration. Apply the exact Article 13 §6 timing rather than treating every transaction as immediate current-year acceleration.
- Incentive treatment: `[PENDING FURTHER RESEARCH — likely-attained vs. not-likely-attained incentive cap treatment was not part of this research pass]`
- Top-51 offseason accounting: From the start of the League Year through the first day of the regular season, Article 13 applies the offseason Top-51 rule: ordinary Paragraph 5 salary from contracts outside the club's 51 highest-valued contracts/tenders is largely excluded except for specified excess amounts, while applicable bonus proration and other cap items still count under the CBA. Do not sum a 90-man offseason roster as 90 full cap charges.
- Rookie or entering-player pool: 2011 CBA rookie wage scale. All drafted rookies receive fixed, unalterable 4-year contracts (undrafted rookies get 3 years); only first-round picks carry a club option for a 5th year. Compensation is slotted via a Rookie Compensation Pool tied to exact draft position. Signing bonuses are prorated straight-line over a maximum of 4 years. A club cannot raise a drafted rookie's annual salary by more than 25% of his Year-One Rookie Salary (the "25% Rule").

### Historical class sourcing, simulated order, and post-selection continuation

This governing rule applies to every draft or equivalent selection event, in every simulated year, regardless of mode.

**Historical class sourcing, through the last real year with a documented class.**

- Prospect pool: use a **contemporaneously verified operational eligibility pool**, not a hindsight list of who was eventually drafted or signed. For 2013, `library/2013_draft_pool_registry.md` contains the NFL's complete 73-player special-eligibility list and defines conservative senior-pool coverage. Absence from the registry never proves ineligibility; before an unlisted player can be selected, verify his eligibility from a pre-selection source and add that evidence.
- Information timing: `library/2013_draft_information_gates.md` controls when declarations, combine invitations/results, pro-day results, medical/workout updates, and final boards become available. A final April snapshot may not leak backward into January or February.
- Permitted prospect data: real name, position, school, verified eligibility, physical measurables, pre-draft testing, and dated pre-draft scouting opinion available by the current simulation date.
- Prohibited prospect data: any real post-selection outcome — career statistics, awards, injuries, bust or star status, and any later real-world team change. This is an actual-future comparator quarantined under §4.3 and must never be used to select, grade, or narrate a prospect, including indirectly through an evaluator's uncannily accurate hunch.

**Simulated order, every simulated year.**

- Selection order is computed only from the simulation's own standings, tiebreakers under §5.3, and any picks already traded inside the simulation. It is never copied from the real historical order for that year.
- Real pre-draft trades are not imported, for any year. All pre-draft trade activity — including trades that move selection ownership before the event itself — is generated fresh inside the simulation under the Trades rule (§9.2) and the autonomy rule below, whether or not a real trade occurred on the corresponding real date.

**Selecting club behavior.**

- Each club evaluates the real (or, once applicable, procedurally generated) prospect pool against its own simulated need, cap or resource situation under §11, and its documented draft philosophy (best-player-available versus need-based, risk tolerance, positional-value model).
- Public pre-draft boards are evidence about contemporary perception, not a universal baseline every club must copy. A club may differ where scheme, role, medical comfort, interview evidence, scouting access, or documented organizational priorities provide a football reason. Unsupported private certainty remains unknown rather than being invented.

**Autonomy for organizations other than the protagonist's.**

Trades, extensions, releases, and free-agent moves between two or more organizations that do not include the protagonist's are resolved by the simulator on its own initiative and never require user confirmation, consistent with Document 1 §3.3. This covers pre-draft and draft-day trades of picks or players between other clubs. A transaction becomes subject to Document 1 §3.1's consequential-decision control only when the protagonist's organization is itself a party to it.

**Post-selection continuation.**

From the moment a player is selected, every later event concerning him — trade, signing, extension, release, performance, and development — is resolved entirely from simulation-native conditions from that point forward. His real-world career after his actual selection year is never consulted, imported, or used as a hidden outcome. His in-simulation trajectory runs from his real physical and skill profile at the time of selection, developed under Document 1 §6.3's uncertainty framework, exactly as if he were a simulation-generated player from that moment on.

**Procedural generation once the documented record is exhausted.**

Once the simulation's calendar passes the last real year with a documented class, generate each later class procedurally rather than inventing prospects ad hoc. Sample position-and-round distributions — physical measurables, school-level tier, and real historical bust/hit base rates — from the aggregate of the documented real classes, then evaluate the resulting prospects under the identical demonstrated-tools/projection/uncertainty framework used for real prospects; never assign a generated prospect a hidden true rating a real prospect would not also have. Record the sampling method and its source distribution once in Document 6 and apply it consistently to every later class rather than re-deriving it each year.
