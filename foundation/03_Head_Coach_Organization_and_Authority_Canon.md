## 10. External hiring market and search-cycle procedure

This section governs every coaching or front-office search: the protagonist's own staff, the protagonist as a candidate elsewhere, and every other organization's search. Its purpose is authenticity without turn-by-turn narration. A search is realistic because it follows the real calendar and real procedural constraints below, not because every phone call in it is dramatized.

### 10.1 Calendar gating

A search may only reach a given stage during the Document 2 §6 period in which that stage is actually possible:

| Search stage | Earliest eligible period | Real-world constraint modeled |
|---|---|---|
| Vacancy created (firing, resignation, retirement) | Any period, but in-season firings are rare; most head-coach and coordinator vacancies open on or after the day following each club's final game | A team already eliminated may act immediately; a team still alive in the postseason ordinarily does not |
| Initial contact / permission request | After the vacancy exists | A candidate currently on a staff still active in the postseason cannot be interviewed without that club's permission, and ordinarily not until that club's season ends |
| Interviews | After permission (if required) is obtained | Scheduling depends on both organizations' calendars and the candidate's current commitments |
| Offer and acceptance | Any point after interviews the employer treats as sufficient | Competing processes may resolve at different speeds |
| Public announcement | Immediately after acceptance | Introductory press conference and staff-building follow on realistic lag, not the same instant |

Document 2 §6 must carry named periods (offseason coaching-search window, combine, free agency, draft, offseason program, training camp, preseason, regular season, each postseason round) so this table has something concrete to gate against; add them there if not already present.

### 10.2 Compressed-turn procedure

A search is resolved in a small, fixed set of user-facing decision points, never a running interview-by-interview narration, regardless of how many candidates or organizations are actually involved.

1. **One vacancy/opportunity turn.** When a search becomes relevant to the protagonist — his own staff, or his own candidacy elsewhere — present everything currently known in a single turn: the situation, the decision-maker, the realistic timeline, and, if he is a candidate, what is known about the competing field. Do not spread this across several turns of incremental reveal.
2. **One candidate-slate turn**, when the protagonist is hiring. Present the full realistic slate at once, not one name at a time, with enough on each candidate (background, fit, likely cost, competing interest from other clubs) for the user to choose who to pursue and in what order in a single reply.
3. **Routine steps proceed without a turn.** Initial outreach, permission requests, scheduling, reference and background calls, and other implementation directly following from the user's choice in step 2 are carried out and only summarized, under Document 1 §3.2; the user is not asked to approve each call.
4. **User decision points are limited to genuine branches**: which candidates to pursue, who to interview first, who to offer, how to respond to a competing bid or a candidate's counteroffer, and whether to accept or reject when the protagonist himself is the candidate. Each such branch is one turn.
5. **Absent a genuine complication** (a leak, a rival bid, a scandal, a rescinded permission), a full search — from vacancy to hire — should resolve in roughly three to five user-facing turns, not twenty. A complication earns its own turn because it is itself a consequential decision point, not because the search is inherently long.
