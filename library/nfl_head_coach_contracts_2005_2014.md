# Library: NFL head-coach contracts and extensions, real practice 2005 to January 2014

**Research status:** research pass 1 (with a same-session re-check, labelled pass 1b) on October 18, 2026, then a separate skeptical verification pass, pass 2, the same day. Pass 2 could run no new web searches (the session's search budget was already spent), so it reopened every source it could reach by address, treated what it could not reopen as unconfirmed, and recounted every team record and the one draft-pick number with its own code. After pass 2 the file holds 81 claims: 37 Confirmed, 11 Corrected and 27 Unverified, and 6 Derived readings of the other rows rather than outside claims (tally and every change in section 12). This file is research only. It changes no canon, roster, staff, contract, cap, calendar or state fact, and it decides nothing for any club or coach, Jacksonville and Stone included.

**What it covers:** how real NFL clubs handled head-coach contracts before the January 2015 cycle: when extensions were signed (offseason or in season, how many years were left, after what results), how long first contracts and extensions ran, whether terms were disclosed, salary figures named outlets reported, guarantees and offsets when a coach was fired, what a firing cost, how clubs treated a coach entering the last year of his deal, how a club and a coach's agent negotiated, and what happened to the assistants' contracts when the head coach went. The interview-permission and compensation rules are in [the 2014 hiring rules](2014_coaching_hiring_and_anti_tampering_rules.md); assistant ladders and assistant contract terms are in [the coaching career path](nfl_coaching_career_path.md), section 6.

**Labels**

- **Confirmed:** two or more independent publishers agree, or a club or league statement is matched by an independent outlet. For a money figure, Confirmed means the same figure was *reported* by two publishers. No figure in this file was confirmed by a club, and none is presented as what a coach was actually paid.
- **Corrected:** pass 1, a search summary, a source, or one of the research queries' own premises was wrong. The fix and the reason are stated in the row and listed in section 11 (pass 1 and pass 1b) or section 12 (pass 2).
- **Unverified:** one route only, sources that conflict, a later source applied to the era, or nothing found.
- **Derived:** arithmetic or a reading of the rows in this file, made here. It is not a sourced fact and carries no label of its own beyond that.
- **Repo:** taken from a repository file, whose own label is kept.

The labels shown are pass 2's. A row that pass 2 could not reread is **Unverified** even when pass 1 read two publishers in search text, and the row says so. **Confirmed** after pass 2 means that pass 2 opened at least one page stating the claim and that a second, independent publisher agrees (reread in pass 2, or read by pass 1 and independent of pass 2's page). Reprints of one wire story count as one publisher.

Every row about a named coach is **real history, an example only**: it stops at the point it describes, it is not branch canon, and it is never an answer key for any branch extension, firing, hire or contract.

### 1.2 What the extension rows show (Derived)

- Sixteen extensions in 1.1 have a known old end date (C2, C4, C5, C7, C9, C17 to C20, C22, C24 to C26, C28 to C30). Twelve were signed with one season left, three with two left (C7, C9, C18), and one after the contract had already run out (C22, Lewis). C13 (Payton) and C31 (Billick) are left out because the old end is disputed or unknown. C28 is Unverified.
- Of the eighteen dated agreements, fifteen were reached between January and July. C18 was reported an hour before the 2013 opener and said to have been agreed months earlier; C9 was announced three days before the 2011 opener; C13 was agreed in December during a suspension year. None of the named coaches was extended during a regular season he was coaching; Cincinnati's offer during the 2009 season was not signed (C21).
- Most extensions added one to three seasons beyond the old end (C2, C7, C9, C17 to C20, C24 to C26, C28, C29); C4 added three beyond the 2013 option. Two added four (C5, C30). C13 was a new five-year deal from a disputed end.
- A playoff season was not required. Coughlin was extended one year after 8-8 (C25); Kubiak and Tomlin after 9-7 seasons that missed the playoffs (C19, C2); Lewis re-signed after 4-12 (C22). The largest reported raises followed a Super Bowl appearance (C7 and C26 after wins, C30 after a loss); Coughlin's second title brought a two-year extension with no reported raise (C29).
- One-year extensions avoided a lame-duck year without a long commitment (C25, C28); one coach turned such an offer down because he wanted more security (C78).

## 10. What this file does not establish

- No league-wide base rate of extension timing, contract length or salary: every count above is Derived from named examples, not a study.
- No first-time head-coach salary for the January 2012 hires (Schiano, Philbin, Allen and Pagano; Mularkey was a former head coach, C39), and none for any later hire (section 4). Pass 2 added one-route figures for 2006 and 2009 first-time hires (C75, C81).
- No source for how a negotiated parting was paid (Fisher, C58, where Wikipedia's "buyout" is one route), how the Jaguars' 2012 dispute ended (C73), or whether Baltimore's 2007 staff was paid out (C71).
- No standard guarantee or buyout term. The cases show clubs paying the remainder less offsets (C53 to C56). One era report says "Most NFL coaching contracts are guaranteed", against one report of an extension that guaranteed years not guaranteed before (both C79); the one buyout term found is in a declined 2007 offer (C78). Each rests on one publisher.
