# Library: 2011 CBA practice, rest and rookie-renegotiation rules for the 2014 season

**Status:** Research pass 1 and a separate, skeptical pass 2 completed October 20, 2026. This file records the rules of the August 4, 2011 NFL-NFLPA collective bargaining agreement (effective "from August 4, 2011 until the last day of the 2020 League Year", Article 69 §1, printed p. 253) that governed four questions in the 2014 season: regular-season and postseason padded practices, the players' bye-week break, regular-season days off, and when a rookie contract may first be renegotiated or extended. It is research only. It changes no canon, practice record, roster, contract, calendar or state fact, and it decides nothing about any branch week.

**Scope note:** rules only. No real club's 2014 practice schedule, padded-practice count, bye-week plan or day-off log is recorded, and no real player's contract, renegotiation or extension is recorded. Where a source names a real player, it is cited for the rule it states and the player is not named here.

**Method:** Pass 1 opened the full text of the 2011 agreement and quoted each operative provision with its article, section and printed page. Pass 2 researched every claim again from scratch, without relying on the pass 1 citation, through a second copy of agreement text, reputable outlets and club sites, and compared each result with pass 1 word for word. Search-engine summaries were not accepted on their own: where a summary attributed text to the 2011 agreement, the text itself was checked (see correction 2 in section 6).

**Labels:**

- **Confirmed:** the 2011 text (S1) and at least one second, separately published route agree. A second route may be a later agreement's text with the same words, which shows that the words carried through 2014 and beyond, or an outlet or club site that describes the rule.
- **Corrected:** a source, a search summary or a repository wording was wrong or looser than the text; the fix and the reason are given in section 6.
- **Unverified:** only one source was seen, the sources disagree, the point is an inference from the text, or it rests on absence of evidence. Section 7 says exactly what is unknown.

**Access (read before relying on this file):** The 2011 agreement PDF (S1) was downloaded in full from the NFLPA's host on October 20, 2026 and its text extracted with PyMuPDF; every quotation below comes from that text. A second copy of the 2011 PDF on the NFL's labor site, the 2020 agreement PDF on the NFLPA's host, and the Cowboys, Cardinals, Steelers, NBC Bay Area, NBC San Diego, Niners Nation and Harvard Football Players Health Study pages were refused by this environment's egress proxy (HTTP 403), and steelersdepot.com did not respond. No block was routed around: no mirror, archive or alternate host was tried. Sources marked "search text only" were read through the search engine's result text, not by opening the page. Over the Cap (S2), the Atlanta Journal-Constitution (S3) and two ProFootballTalk articles (S4, S5) were opened.

**Page key:** printed page = PDF page minus 15 in S1 (for example, PDF page 158 carries printed page 143). S1's PDF metadata gives a creation and modification date of August 5, 2011.

## 1. Regular-season and postseason padded practices (Article 24, Section 1)

Article 24 is titled "Regular Season and Postseason Practices" and begins on printed p. 143.

| # | Rule | Exact words (2011 text) | Source | Pass-2 result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Regular-season total: fourteen padded practices for all players | "During the regular season, padded practices for all players shall be limited to a total of fourteen" | S1, Art. 24 §1(a), p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S3 (AJC) "Limit of 14 padded practices during the regular season"; S6 (search text) 14 |
| P2 | Split: eleven in the first eleven weeks, three in the remaining six | "eleven of which must be held during the first eleven weeks of the regular season, and three of which must be held during the remaining six weeks of the regular season" | S1, Art. 24 §1(a), p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S3 "11 of which must be held during the first 11 weeks ... with remaining three during the final six weeks". S4, a pre-signing report, said "last five weeks"; superseded (correction 1) |
| P3 | Two padded practices in one week: allowed in one week only, and that week must fall in the first eleven | "Subject to the foregoing rules, each Club may hold two padded practices during the same week during one week of the regular season, provided that such week falls within the first eleven weeks of the regular season." | S1, Art. 24 §1(a), p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S3 "club may hold two padded practices during only one week", inside its first-eleven-weeks clause; S6 (search text) "only once ... during the first 11 weeks" |
| P4 | The club picks the days | "The Club may choose the days of the week on which such practices shall be held." | S1, Art. 24 §1(a), p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words |
| P5 | Definition: a practice at which players are required to wear helmets and shoulder pads | "For purposes of this Article and Article 23, a “padded practice” shall be defined as a practice in which players are required to wear helmets and shoulder pads, in addition to any other equipment required by the Club, subject to the exceptions set forth in Article 23, Section 6(b)." | S1, Art. 24 §1(c), p. 143 | **Confirmed** for the definition. S2 has the same words except that its cross-reference reads "Article 23, Section 7(e)", the later agreement's numbering. No 2011 to 2014 outlet describing the definition was opened |
| P6 | Exceptions: optional helmets or pads for quarterbacks, kickers, punters and long snappers; a precautionary helmet ordered after a head injury; helmets for the helmet-radio quarterback and defensive signal caller | "it shall not be a violation ... nor shall it constitute a “padded practice,” if: (i) quarterbacks, kickers, punters, and/or long-snappers only wear helmets and/or shoulder pads during practice at the option of the player; (ii) a player who, because of a head injury, is directed by the Club physician to wear a helmet as a precautionary measure at all practices; or (iii) the quarterback and/or the defensive player who receives signals from the coaching staff via helmet communication wear helmets during the team period in which helmets are used for such communication." | S1, Art. 23 §6(b), p. 141 | **Confirmed.** S2 Art. 23 §7(e) same words |
| P7 | A padded practice cancelled within sixty minutes for a reason beyond the club's control does not count | "In the event that a Club begins a padded practice but such practice is cancelled within sixty minutes of its commencement due to inclement weather or for any other reason beyond the Club’s control, such practice shall not count as a padded practice under this Article or Article 24." | S1, Art. 23 §6(a), p. 141 | **Unverified** (S1 only; S2's Article 23 has no such sentence and no other source was found) |
| P8 | On a padded day, on-field team activity is limited to three hours, with up to thirty minutes of position work before it and a no-helmets, walking-pace walkthrough outside it | "On days when padded practices are permitted under Subsection (a) above, on-field Team activity for all players shall be limited to a maximum of three hours per day, including “first period” (i.e., stretching and calisthenics)"; the thirty-minute position-coach period and a walkthrough "no helmets and walking pace" do not count | S1, Art. 24 §1(d), p. 143 | **Confirmed** for the three-hour limit and the thirty-minute proviso (S2 same words; S3 "limited to three hours plus a walk-through with position coaches not to exceed 30 minutes"). S2's walkthrough definition ("Pro Bowl practice pace", a jointly approved video) and its extra last sentence are later text (section 5) |
| P9 | Postseason: one padded practice a week, from the week after the club's last regular-season game | "Clubs participating in the postseason may hold one padded practice per week, on a day of the Club’s choosing, commencing with the week following the Club’s last regular season game." | S1, Art. 24 §1(b), p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S3 "One padded practice per week in postseason" |
| P10 | Enforcement and film: violations of Section 1 go through the Article 21 §8 fine procedures; clubs film all regular-season and postseason practices and keep the film until thirty days after their season | "Any violation of Section 1 of this Article shall be governed by the same procedures, and shall be subject to the same fines and fine procedures, as set forth in Article 21, Section 8"; "Clubs shall film all regular and postseason practices shall maintain a copy of such films until thirty days after the end of their season." (the missing "and" is in the original) | S1, Art. 24 §§3 and 4, pp. 143-144 | **Confirmed.** S2 same §3 words; S2 §4 adds "and" and a reference to Article 21 §8(h). Fine amounts are not recorded in this file |

**Reading the padded-practice rule (inferences, labelled):**

- **Weeks.** The agreement does not define a "week". Eleven plus six is seventeen, the number of league weeks in the 2014 regular season (Week 1 opening September 4 and Week 17 on December 28, per `2014_nfl_calendar.md`), so the split reads as league weeks, a club's bye week included. *Unverified (inference from arithmetic).*
- **No carrying over.** "Must be held during" ties eleven to the first eleven weeks and three to the last six. The text gives no way to move an unused first-period practice into the last six weeks, and S3 and S6 describe the same split. *Unverified (reading of the words).*
- **Most in any week.** Outside the single permitted double week, a club holds at most one padded practice a week; the double week, if used, counts toward the eleven. *Confirmed as arithmetic from P1 to P3.*
- **"Shells".** The agreement does not use the word. A practice at which players are required to wear helmets and shoulder pads is a padded practice under P5 whatever the staff calls it, unless a P6 exception applies; a helmets-only practice without shoulder pads is not. *Confirmed as a reading of P5; the word "shells" is not the agreement's.*

## 2. The bye-week player break (Article 24, Section 2; Article 35, Section 4)

| # | Rule | Exact words (2011 text) | Source | Pass-2 result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | At least four consecutive days off in a regular-season bye week | "During any regular season bye week period occurring during the term of this Agreement, players will be given a minimum of four consecutive days off." | S1, Art. 24 §2, p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S7 (search text) "at least four consecutive days off for the players during the bye"; S8 (search text) four consecutive days off during the bye week |
| B2 | The four days must include a Saturday and a Sunday | "Such four-day period must include a Saturday and a Sunday unless the Club is scheduled to play a game on the Thursday following the bye week" | S1, Art. 24 §2, p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S7 (search text) "which must include Saturday and Sunday" |
| B3 | Thursday exception: a club playing the Thursday after its bye may require players to report on the Sunday before that game, and the four days are then Wednesday to Saturday | "in which case players may be required to report to the Club on the Sunday preceding the Thursday game. In such an event, the four-day period shall be Wednesday through Saturday." | S1, Art. 24 §2, p. 143 | **Confirmed** for the words (S2 same words). No 2011 to 2014 outlet describing the Wednesday-to-Saturday version was opened |
| B4 | No other exception | The Thursday game is the only exception stated in Article 24 §2 | S1, Art. 24 §2, p. 143 | **Confirmed** (S2 states the same single exception). See U5 for a first-round postseason bye |
| B5 | Treatment and rehabilitation: an injured player may be required to take treatment the medical staff deems reasonably necessary | "Any injured player may be required to undergo medical or rehabilitation treatment during such four-day period provided that such treatment is deemed reasonably necessary by the Club’s medical staff." | S1, Art. 24 §2, p. 143 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words. The allowance is for an injured player, not for every player (correction 6) |
| B6 | The bye-week days are governed by Article 24 §2 and are not counted in the regular-season monthly rate | "During any regular season bye week, all players will be permitted days off in accordance with Article 24, Section 2 of this Agreement."; Article 35 §2 counts days off "(not counting days off during a bye week)" | S1, Art. 35 §§4 and 2, p. 165 | **Confirmed** for §4 (S2 same words). The "not counting" clause is in the 2011 §2 only, which differs from S2 (see D1) |

## 3. Regular-season days off (Article 35, Days Off)

| # | Rule | Exact words (2011 text) | Source | Pass-2 result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **No weekly day off.** The 2011 agreement sets a rate of at least four days off per month, from the first regular-season game until the club's last regular-season or postseason game, chosen by the club, bye-week days not counted | "Commencing with the first regular season game and continuing until the last regular season or postseason game played by the respective Club, all players will be permitted days off at least at the rate of four days per month (not counting days off during a bye week) as determined by the Club." | S1, Art. 35 §2, p. 165 | **Unverified** (S1 is the only opened source for the 2011 wording). S11 (search text only) says a four-days-a-month standard dates from the 1977 agreement, which is consistent. S2's Article 35 §2 sets a different, later rule (one day off each calendar week); it is not the 2014 rule (correction 2) |
| D2 | On a day off, an injured player may be required to take medical treatment and quarterbacks may be required to attend coaches' meetings | "During the 24-hour period constituting a day-off, any injured player may be required to undergo medical treatment and quarterbacks may be required to attend coaches meetings." | S1, Art. 35 §3, p. 165 | **Confirmed.** S2 §3 same words except "day off" for "day-off" |

The preseason day-off rule (Article 35 §1, one day off in every seven from the veterans' reporting date, at least five in all) is outside this file's scope and is noted only so that it is not confused with D1.

## 4. Rookie contract renegotiation (Article 7, Section 3(k))

Article 7 is "Rookie Compensation and Rookie Compensation Pool"; Section 3 is "Rookie Contracts" (from printed p. 24); subsection (k), "Renegotiations", is on printed p. 28.

| # | Rule | Exact words (2011 text) | Source | Pass-2 result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A drafted rookie's contract may not be renegotiated, amended or altered in any way until after the final regular-season game of his third contract year | "A Rookie Contract for a Drafted Rookie may not be renegotiated, amended or altered in any way until after the final regular season game of the player’s third contract year." | S1, Art. 7 §3(k)(i), p. 28 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S5 (PFT, July 25, 2011) "incoming players will not be able to renegotiate contracts before the end of the third season on their rookie contracts"; S9 (search text, June 2013) a 2011 draftee's club could not begin renegotiating until after the 2013 season. The task's wording "after the final regular-season game of his third contract year" is exact |
| R2 | An undrafted rookie's contract: the same bar, until after the final regular-season game of his second contract year | "A Rookie Contract for an Undrafted Rookie may not be renegotiated, amended or altered in any way until after the final regular season game of the player’s second contract year." | S1, Art. 7 §3(k)(ii), p. 28 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words; S5 "Undrafted players can renegotiate after their second year." The task's wording "after his second" is correct |
| R3 | A permitted renegotiated or extended contract is no longer a Rookie Contract and leaves the rookie rules | "For the avoidance of doubt, any permissible renegotiated or extended Player Contract shall not be considered a Rookie Contract, and shall not be subject to the rules that limit Rookie Contracts." | S1, Art. 7 §3(k)(iii), p. 28 | **Confirmed.** S2 same words |
| R4 | Who is a Rookie, a Drafted Rookie and an Undrafted Rookie | "“Rookie” means a person who has never before signed a Player Contract with an NFL Club. The first Player Contract signed by such person is a “Rookie Contract.”" (p. 3); "“Drafted Rookie” means a person who is selected in the current League Year’s Draft or whose Draft rights are held, or continue to be held, consistent with this Agreement, by an NFL Club that selected the Rookie in a prior Draft." (p. 1); "“Undrafted Rookie” means a Rookie who was eligible for but not selected in a College Draft for which he was eligible" (p. 4) | S1, Art. 1, pp. 1, 3, 4 | **Unverified** (S1 only; the definitions were not looked for in a second copy) |

**Reading the renegotiation rule (inferences, labelled):**

- **An extension is barred too.** "Renegotiated, amended or altered in any way" covers any change to the contract, and an extension changes its term; R3 speaks of "renegotiated or extended" contracts together. S5 reads the rule the same way (early new deals "wouldn't be able to make those deals now"). *Confirmed as a reading (S1 words, S5).*
- **"Contract year" is not defined** in Article 1. On the usual reading that a rookie's first contract year is the League Year in which he signs: a player drafted in 2011 first becomes eligible after the final 2013 regular-season game; one drafted in 2014, after the final 2016 regular-season game; an undrafted rookie who signed in 2013, after the final 2014 regular-season game. *Unverified (inference; a mid-season or later signing could count its contract years differently, and no source settles it).*
- The bar is on altering the contract. It does not by itself say when talks may begin, and nothing here sets a market value, a delay cost or a likelihood of agreement.

## 5. Changes between 2011 and 2014, and later rules not to import

- **2011 to 2014.** No source found an amendment to Article 7 §3(k), Article 23 §6, Article 24 or Article 35 between the August 2011 signing and the 2014 season. S1 is the original August 2011 text (PDF dated August 5, 2011), not a consolidated later version. *Unverified (absence of evidence, not proof).*
- **Over the Cap's text is a later agreement.** S2's pages do not name the agreement they reproduce, but they refer to "the 2020 League Year" (four times in Article 23 and five in Article 7) and number the padded-practice exceptions Article 23 §7(e). Their Article 24 §1(a), §1(b) and §2, Article 35 §§3 and 4 and Article 7 §3(k) match the 2011 words; the rows above use that match as a second route.
- **Later rules seen in S2 that do not apply in 2014:**
  - Article 35 §2: "at least one day off each calendar week (i.e., Sunday-Saturday) of the regular season", at least three days off after a Thursday game, and the four-days-per-month rate kept for the postseason only.
  - Article 24 §1(d): a walkthrough at "acceptable walkthrough pace (i.e., Pro Bowl practice pace), as demonstrated in a video jointly approved by the parties", and a closing sentence applying §1(d) on days with no padded practice.
  - Article 24 §1(e): a list of prohibited drills in padded practices.
  - Article 23 §7: a five-day camp acclimation period, padded camp practices of no more than 2.5 hours, and a 90-minute interval between practices.
- **Search text on the later agreement** (S12, search text only) also describes a training-camp total of sixteen padded practices. The 2011 text has no such total (a search of S1 for "sixteen" finds none in Article 23). It is a later rule and is not recorded as a 2014 rule.

## 6. Verification: corrections

1. **Pre-signing report superseded (S4).** ProFootballTalk's July 22, 2011 report, written before the agreement was signed, said that "during the last five weeks of the regular season, teams can practice in pads one day a week in only three of those weeks". The signed text (S1), the later text (S2) and S3 all say three in the remaining **six** weeks. S4 is not used for the split.
2. **Search summaries presented a later agreement as the 2011 agreement.** Several pass-2 search summaries called Over the Cap's text "the 2011 NFL CBA" or "the actual CBA text", and one concluded from it that the four-days-per-month rate applied only to the postseason. The text refers to the 2020 League Year and differs from S1 in Article 35 §2 (section 5). For 2014 the regular-season rule is D1: four days per month, with no weekly day off. Do not cite Over the Cap as the 2011 text without checking the words against S1.
3. **Search summary on the 1977 agreement.** A summary first offered the Harvard appendix (S11) as a source for the 2011 four-days-a-month rate; a second search showed it describes the 1977 agreement. It is kept only as consistent background for D1, not as a second source for the 2011 words.
4. **Week 5 note on Over the Cap, made more precise.** The Week 5 game output and the defect register cite Over the Cap's Article 24 §1 as agreeing with §1(a) and §1(c) of the 2011 agreement. That holds for §1(a) word for word; §1(c) differs only in its cross-reference (Article 23 §7(e) in place of §6(b)), and the page reproduces the later agreement. The football conclusion in those closed records (fourteen; eleven and three; one double week inside the first eleven; helmets and shoulder pads) is unchanged. Closed records are not edited by this file.
5. **Rookie-renegotiation wording tightened (`docs/event_inserts.md` §D).** That page says drafted rookies are ineligible "until after their third contracted regular season" and undrafted rookies "until after their second". The effect matches R1 and R2, but the exact rule is "until after the final regular season game of the player's third [second] contract year", and it bars any amendment or alteration, not only a renegotiation. This file does not edit that page.
6. **Bye-week treatment wording tightened (`docs/bye_week.md` §4).** That page says medically necessary treatment "may be required by the medical staff". The text allows it for "any injured player" and requires the treatment to be "deemed reasonably necessary by the Club's medical staff" (B5). The rest of that page's Article 24 summary (four consecutive days including Saturday and Sunday, the Thursday Wednesday-to-Saturday exception, fourteen padded practices split eleven and three, one double week inside the first eleven, helmets plus shoulder pads) matches S1. This file does not edit that page.
7. **The 2014 Niners Nation explainer cited by `docs/event_inserts.md` §G was not confirmed.** Its page was refused (S10), and its search text showed rookie-contract lengths and options but no renegotiation sentence. R1 and R2 rest on S1, S2, S5 and S9 instead.

## 7. Verification: unverified and open points

- **U1 (P7).** The sixty-minute cancellation rule is seen only in S1; whether a cancelled practice is "beyond the Club's control" is not defined further.
- **U2 (P2, weeks).** "Week" is not defined; reading the split as league weeks including the bye rests on 11 + 6 = 17.
- **U3 (D1).** The 2011 four-days-per-month wording was opened in one source only. "Month" is not defined (calendar month or a running period), and the text does not say how the rate applies to a partial month at either end of the season.
- **U4 (bye days and quarterbacks).** Article 35 §3 lets a club require quarterbacks to attend coaches' meetings "during the 24-hour period constituting a day-off". Article 24 §2 mentions only injured players' treatment, and Article 35 §4 sends bye days to Article 24 §2. Whether the quarterback allowance reaches the four bye days is not stated. `docs/bye_week.md` says no required meeting may fall in the four days; the agreement neither confirms nor rules out a quarterback exception.
- **U5 (postseason bye).** Article 24 §2 speaks of a "regular season bye week". No 2011 provision found gives a separate rule for a first-round postseason bye; during the postseason D1's monthly rate continues and P9 allows one padded practice a week.
- **U6 (R4 and contract years).** The Article 1 definitions were not checked in a second copy, and "contract year" is undefined.
- **U7 (amendments).** No 2011 to 2014 amendment to these articles was found; absence is not proof.
- **U8 (B3).** The Thursday exception's words are confirmed by two texts, but no 2011 to 2014 report describing a club using it was opened.
