## The five candidate teams — situational dossiers

### Jacksonville Jaguars

- **Why the job opened:** Owner Shad Khan fired GM Gene Smith Dec. 31, 2012 (four seasons; criticized for the Blaine Gabbert pick — corrected below — and free-agent overpays including Torry Holt, Aaron Kampman, Paul Posluszny, Clint Session, Dawan Landry, Laurent Robinson, Aaron Ross). New GM David Caldwell (hired Jan. 8, 2013, corrected from "Jan 9" — from the Atlanta Falcons' director of player personnel) then fired HC Mike Mularkey on Jan. 10, 2013, two days after his own hire, after a 2-14 season (the worst in franchise history at that point — Jacksonville's 2020 season, 1-15, later became the actual worst).
- **Correction:** Blaine Gabbert was drafted **10th overall** in 2011 (Jacksonville traded up for him), not 8th as first reported.
- **2012 record / draft position:** 2-14; picked 2nd overall in the 2013 draft (Kansas City, also 2-14, picked 1st on a strength-of-schedule tiebreaker).
- **Owner:** Shahid "Shad" Khan. Ownership timeline, corrected for precision: agreement to buy Nov. 29, 2011; NFL owners approved Dec. 14, 2011; sale formally closed Jan. 4, 2012. ("Bought the team Dec. 2011" collapses a step — use "owner since Jan. 2012" or the full sequence.)
- **GM:** David Caldwell, hired Jan. 8, 2013.
- **Cap situation:** Reported as roughly $27.9M in cap space as of March 21, 2013 (Bleacher Report, citing Spotrac) — **flagged unverifiable as a precise figure**; other contemporaneous reporting for the Jaguars that offseason ranges from about $22M to $40M depending on the exact date and method. Directionally, Jacksonville was reasonably well positioned relative to the league (several teams had far more room — Cincinnati ~$55M, Cleveland ~$49M — and roughly 10 teams were over the cap that offseason), but do not present "$27.9M" as a precise, load-bearing number.
- **Roster context:** An unsettled QB situation (2011 first-rounder Blaine Gabbert, sacked 40 times as a rookie) and star RB Maurice Jones-Drew fresh off a 38-day 2012 contract holdout (fined roughly $800K, negotiated down from ~$1.6M) were the central roster problems a new head coach would inherit.
- **Real hire:** Gus Bradley (Seattle DC, architect of the "Legion of Boom"), hired Jan. 17, 2013 (see correction above), introduced alongside GM Caldwell.

### Arizona Cardinals

- **Why the job opened:** Ken Whisenhunt fired Dec. 31, 2012 after 6 seasons (45-51 record) when a 4-0 start collapsed into 11 losses in the final 12 games (including a 58-0 loss to Seattle, the franchise's worst-ever), missing the playoffs a third straight year. GM Rod Graves fired the same day.
- **2012 record / draft position:** 5-11; picked 7th overall in the 2013 draft (used on OG Jonathan Cooper).
- **Ownership/front office:** William "Bill" Bidwill, principal owner and chairman since 1972/2007 respectively; son Michael Bidwill has served as team president (day-to-day operations) since 2007. Steve Keim promoted from VP of player personnel to GM Jan. 8, 2013, replacing Graves.
- **Cap situation:** Entered the 2013 offseason roughly $723,000 over the cap (per ESPN's John Clayton), driven by Kevin Kolb's ~$62.1M contract; releasing Kolb saved about $7.5M in cap room and left roughly $6M in dead money. WR Larry Fitzgerald carried a confirmed $10.25M cap hit for 2013, the offensive centerpiece.
- **Roster context:** A failed multi-year QB carousel (Kolb, John Skelton, 6th-round rookie Ryan Lindley — the 2012 team cycled through all three amid a 9-game losing streak) was the defining problem.
- **Real hire:** Bruce Arians, hired Jan. 17, 2013, on a 4-year deal, immediately after serving as Indianapolis's interim HC for the 2012 season (filling in for Chuck Pagano's leukemia treatment, going 9-3).

### Chicago Bears

- **Why the job opened:** Lovie Smith fired Dec. 31, 2012 by GM Phil Emery despite a 10-6 record — Chicago lost the final playoff spot to Minnesota (also 10-6) on a **division-record tiebreaker**, becoming the first team since the 1996 Washington Redskins to start 7-1 and miss the playoffs. Smith had missed the postseason in 5 of his last 6 seasons (only 2010 made it).
- **Correction:** Phil Emery was a **first-year** GM at the time (hired Jan. 28-29, 2012, fired Smith 11 months later), not a "2nd-year GM."
- **2012 record / draft position:** 10-6, missed playoffs; picked 20th overall in 2013 (used on OG Kyle Long, Oregon).
- **Ownership/front office:** Virginia Halas McCaskey, principal owner since 1983; son George McCaskey, Chairman of the Board since May 2011. Phil Emery remained GM through 2013 (not fired until Dec. 2014).
- **Cap situation:** The league cap was confirmed at $123M; a franchise tag on DT Henry Melton carried a confirmed $8.45M cap hit. The Bears' own cap-space figure is **not cleanly confirmed** — contemporaneous reporting for this specific team ranged from roughly $14.5M (mid-February, pre-official-cap) down to as little as $2.7-5.4M by late March after free-agent moves; treat "roughly $12M" as a plausible snapshot at one point in time, not a precise fact. The crunch made re-signing LB Brian Urlacher and Nick Roach difficult; both in fact left that offseason (Roach to Oakland, Urlacher retired unsigned).
- **Roster context:** Protecting QB Jay Cutler behind a weak offensive line was the clear organizational priority — reflected directly in the Kyle Long pick.
- **Real hire:** Marc Trestman, hired Jan. 16, 2013 (see correction above), from the CFL's Montreal Alouettes (2008-2012, 59-31 record, back-to-back Grey Cups 2009-2010).

### Philadelphia Eagles

- **Why the job opened:** Andy Reid fired Dec. 31, 2012 by owner Jeffrey Lurie after 14 seasons (1999-2012), following a 4-12 season — the worst under Reid and Philadelphia's worst since 1998. Lurie's exact quote: "it is time for the Eagles to move in a new direction. Coach Reid leaves us with a winning tradition that we can build upon."
- **2012 record / draft position:** 4-12; picked 4th overall in 2013 (used on OT Lane Johnson, Oklahoma). Note: Oakland's original 3rd-overall slot was traded to Miami pre-draft, so "Oakland picked 3rd" refers to assigned draft-slot order by standings, not to Oakland being the team on the clock at pick 3.
- **Ownership/front office:** Jeffrey Lurie, owner/Chairman-CEO since 1994; Howie Roseman, GM since Jan. 2010.
- **Cap situation:** QB Michael Vick's 2013 cap hit was confirmed at $16.9M pre-restructure (on a $15.5M base salary). In Feb. 2013, Lurie/Roseman/new coach Kelly restructured rather than released him — reworked into a $3.5M signing bonus with the final two contract years voidable, functionally a one-year deal. Roseman on the process: new coach Kelly "came out of it liking the quarterback position, liking the talent of the quarterbacks on this roster" — Nick Foles (2012 3rd-round pick, #88 overall) being the other piece of that QB room.
- **Roster context:** The QB situation (Vick's cap-crushing deal plus developing Foles) directly framed the search that led to a spread-option college hire; RB LeSean McCoy and WR DeSean Jackson were the other key offensive pieces.
- **Real hire:** Chip Kelly, agreed Jan. 16, 2013, from the University of Oregon (nationally prominent spread/option offense).

### San Diego Chargers

- **Why the job opened:** Norv Turner and GM A.J. Smith both fired Dec. 31, 2012 by team president Dean Spanos after a 7-9 season — the third straight year missing the playoffs (Turner's first losing season with the team since 2003), amid real fan discontent (four TV blackouts that season, three consecutive, from unsold tickets).
- **2012 record / draft position:** 7-9; picked 11th overall in 2013 (used on OT D.J. Fluker, Alabama — their first selection of that draft).
- **Ownership/front office:** Alex G. Spanos, majority owner since 1984; son Dean Spanos running day-to-day operations as President/CEO since the early-to-mid 1990s (sourcing splits between 1993 and 1994 — not fully resolved to a single year). Tom Telesco hired GM Jan. 9, 2013, from the Colts' VP of football operations, becoming the youngest GM in franchise history at 40. Alex Spanos's personal, active role in this specific search could not be independently confirmed (his declining health was only disclosed publicly years later, in 2016) — Dean Spanos is the figure consistently credited as running it.
- **Cap situation:** A tight 2013 cap situation; that year the team restructured QB Philip Rivers's contract (converting $5M of base salary to bonus, freeing roughly $3.33M in 2013 room) but pushed larger cap hits into 2014-2015.
- **Roster context:** An aging core around Rivers — sacked 49 times in 2012 behind a weak offensive line, with TE Antonio Gates also aging — made protecting Rivers the clear top need, directly reflected in new GM Telesco's first pick (Fluker).
- **Real hire:** Mike McCoy, agreed ~Jan 14, announced Jan. 15, 2013, from Denver, where he had been offensive coordinator coming off a season with Peyton Manning.

## The full 2013 coordinator/assistant carousel (market color)

All seven new 2013 head coaches filled their coordinator staffs within days to about two weeks of their own hires (roughly Jan. 10 - Jan. 22, 2013):

| Team | HC | OC | DC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buffalo | Doug Marrone | Nathaniel Hackett (his Syracuse play-caller) | Mike Pettine (ex-Jets DC) |
| Cleveland | Rob Chudzinski | Norv Turner (former Chargers/Redskins/Raiders HC) | Ray Horton (ex-Cardinals DC — see below) |
| Jacksonville | Gus Bradley | Jedd Fisch (ex-Miami Hurricanes OC, former Seahawks QB coach under Bradley) | Bob Babich (ex-Bears defensive staff; Bradley's old boss at North Dakota State) |
| San Diego | Mike McCoy | Ken Whisenhunt (former Cardinals HC) | John Pagano (retained) |
| Chicago | Marc Trestman | Aaron Kromer (ex-Saints OL coach) | Mel Tucker (new to Trestman) |
| Philadelphia | Chip Kelly | Pat Shurmur, hired Jan 20 (former Browns HC 2011-12) | Bill "Billy" Davis (21 years NFL experience, ex-Browns LB coach) |
| Arizona | Bruce Arians | Harold Goodwin (came with Arians) | Todd Bowles (ex-Eagles DC) |
| Kansas City | Andy Reid | Doug Pederson (Reid's former Eagles QB coach) | Bob Sutton (13 seasons Jets LB coach/DC) |

**Ripple-effect replacements** at the teams these hires left behind:
- **Denver** promoted QB coach Adam Gase to OC (Jan. 17, 2013) after McCoy left for San Diego.
- **Carolina** promoted QB coach Mike Shula to OC (Jan. 18, 2013) after Chudzinski left for Cleveland — having also interviewed Pat Shurmur and Hue Jackson, ultimately choosing Shula partly for his rapport with Cam Newton.
- **Seattle** hired Dan Quinn (Florida's DC, former Seahawks D-line coach) as DC the same day Bradley left for Jacksonville.
- **Indianapolis** hired Stanford OC Pep Hamilton (who had coached Andrew Luck as a senior) after Arians left for Arizona.

**Best market-color storyline: Ray Horton.** Arizona's outgoing DC interviewed for both the Browns' and Bills' head-coaching jobs (Cleveland one night, Buffalo the next), interviewed twice for the Cardinals' own vacancy, and reportedly believed he had it once Mike McCoy left for San Diego — but Arizona hired Bruce Arians instead. Horton then landed as Cleveland's DC (Jan. 18, 2013); Arizona filled his old job with Todd Bowles. Useful as a template for how a real, sympathetic near-miss HC candidacy actually plays out in this market.
