Tight ends coach.

### New England Patriots, 2007-2008

Passing game coordinator / tight ends, working alongside offensive coordinator and quarterbacks coach Josh McDaniels.

He called selected preseason quarters and halves as a staff-development assignment. McDaniels remained the regular-season play-caller.

New England went 16-0 in the 2007 regular season before losing Super Bowl XLII, then 11-5 in 2008 without a playoff berth.

### New Orleans Saints, January 2009-April 15, 2012

Offensive coordinator under Sean Payton.

This is a branch divergence from real history. In this timeline Stone holds the offensive-coordinator role, while Pete Carmichael Jr. remains heavily involved with Drew Brees and the passing game.

Stone did not replace the existing offense with a new system on arrival. Payton remained the principal offensive authority and game-day caller through the opening part of the 2011 season. Stone coordinated the weekly offensive planning process, consolidated run- and pass-game work, managed situational sections and installation, and prepared the final menu with Payton retaining final approval.

### New Orleans Saints, 2012 season

Interim head coach during Sean Payton's suspension.

Stone held the full team-level interim role throughout the 2012 playing season. The real-world Joe Vitt / Aaron Kromer interim-head-coach sequence is not imported into this branch.

Stone retained primary offensive play-calling and the Saints' offensive-coordinator authority while serving as interim head coach. Pete Carmichael Jr. handled substantial weekday passing-game/offensive coordination as a senior offensive staff member but did not hold the offensive-coordinator title while Stone remained in New Orleans. User canon establishes that Carmichael becomes Saints offensive coordinator only after Stone leaves. Steve Spagnuolo controlled and called the defense. Greg McMahon controlled the technical special-teams operation. Aaron Kromer remained a major part of the offensive-line and running-game structure. Joe Vitt returned to his assistant head coach / linebackers role after his league suspension.

Stone had final day-to-day football authority over meetings, practice structure, depth chart, playing time, team discipline, and game management. He participated substantially in roster evaluation, draft preparation, free-agent discussions, and role definition, but did not possess final acquisition, contract, or draft authority.

New Orleans went 12-4, finished second in the NFC South behind Atlanta, earned a Wild Card berth, won its Wild Card game, and lost in the Divisional Round on January 13, 2013.

The opponent and final score of that Divisional Round loss are not established in canon.

## 5. New Orleans offensive record

### 2009

New Orleans went 13-3, won the NFC South, and defeated Indianapolis in Super Bowl XLIV.

The offense finished first in the NFL in points and total yards, scoring 510 points and producing 6,461 yards. The running game rose from 1,594 yards in 2008 to 2,106 in 2009.

Payton called the games. Stone coordinated the full offensive plan. Aaron Kromer carried explicit running-game responsibility, and Pete Carmichael Jr. remained closely involved with Brees and the passing game.

### 2010

New Orleans went 11-5 and lost 41-36 at Seattle in the Wild Card round.

The offense finished sixth in total yards and third in passing. The Saints scored 384 points, committed 31 turnovers, and Brees threw 22 interceptions.

### 2011

New Orleans went 13-3 and won the NFC South.

The offense produced 7,474 yards and 547 points. Brees threw for 5,476 yards and 46 touchdowns.

After Payton suffered a leg injury on the sideline at Tampa Bay in Week 6, Stone took over the offensive calls during the game. Stone then called the offense against Indianapolis the following week while Payton underwent surgery. New Orleans won 62-7.

Stone remained the primary game-day offensive caller for the rest of the season and postseason. Payton continued to contribute ideas and situational direction, Kromer supplied run-game input, and Carmichael remained deeply involved with Brees and the passing game.

Over the final 10 regular-season games, New Orleans went 9-1.

The Saints beat Detroit 45-28 in the Wild Card round and lost 36-32 at San Francisco on January 14, 2012.

Across Stone's first three Saints seasons as offensive coordinator, New Orleans went 37-11 in the regular season and 4-2 in the postseason.
