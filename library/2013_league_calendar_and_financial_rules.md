**Status:** Research pass completed and independently verified 2026-09-17. This file is the sourcing/verification backup for the real-data values now populated into `foundation/02_League_Era_and_Sourcebook.md` §6 (calendar) and §11 (financial rules). If those two documents' values and this file's values ever disagree, treat that as a defect to fix, not a real disagreement — Document 2 is the canonical location; this file exists so every number there stays traceable to a source and a verification note rather than becoming an unsourced fact over time.

**Method:** every fact below was researched, then independently re-verified by a second pass that searched for corroborating sources without trusting the first pass's citation. Facts that a first pass got wrong, or that the second pass could not independently confirm, are marked as corrected or unverifiable below rather than silently fixed or silently kept — this is the project's own top-stated priority (never present an invented or approximate number as if it were precise and sourced) applied to the data-gathering step itself.

## Salary cap and financial rules

**2013 salary cap: $123,000,000 per team**, up from $120.6M in 2012, announced to all 32 teams around February 28, 2013. Confirmed independently across NFL.com, CBS Sports, ESPN, Yahoo Sports, Bleacher Report, Fansided, and NFL Network insider Tom Pelissero's published year-by-year cap history. (A few 2013-era sources cite "$123.9M" — that is the $123M cap plus a separate ~$900K per-team minimum-salary-benefit add-on for total player cost accounting, not a different cap figure; do not treat it as a discrepancy.)

**Cap/cash floor — corrected during verification.** The first research pass reported a single-season 2013 league-wide 95% cash-spending requirement. Independent verification could not confirm this and found it does not hold up: every source that discusses cash-spending floors ties both the 89% team-level figure and any league-wide figure to the same four-year aggregate window (2013-2016, then 2017-2020), not to 2013 alone. The only single-year figure that exists is a 99% league-wide cash floor for 2011-2012 specifically — a different rule, different years, not applicable to 2013. **Correct statement:** each team must spend at least 89% of the aggregate salary cap in cash over the four-year period 2013-2016 (and again 2017-2020); falling short in any single season within that window is not itself a violation, and any shortfall is resolved (paid directly to players) only at the end of the four-year period.

**2011 CBA rookie wage scale** (Article 7, Section 3): drafted rookies get fixed, unalterable 4-year contracts (3 years for undrafted free agents); only first-round picks carry a 5th-year club option; compensation is slotted via a Rookie Compensation Pool tied to exact draft position; signing bonuses prorate straight-line over a maximum of 4 years; a club cannot raise a drafted rookie's annual salary by more than 25% of his Year-One Rookie Salary (the "25% Rule"). Confirmed against three independent sources (Over The Cap's hosted CBA text, capandcontracts.com, and a general NFL-CBA explainer), all agreeing on every element.

**General veteran signing-bonus proration and dead-money timing — corrected 2026-09-18.** Under 2011 CBA Article 13 §6, an ordinary signing bonus is prorated straight-line over the contract term to a **maximum of five years**. The four-year maximum elsewhere in this file is specific to Rookie Contracts. If a player is released, waived, or traded on or before June 1, remaining unamortized signing bonus generally accelerates into the current League Year. After June 1, future-year unamortized signing-bonus amounts generally move into the following League Year. Before June 1, a club may designate up to two qualifying terminations per League Year for June-2 accounting treatment. The acquiring club in a trade does not inherit the old club's signing-bonus proration. Primary source: 2011 CBA Article 13 §6, reproduced by Over The Cap: https://overthecap.com/collective-bargaining-agreement/article/13/section/6

**Roster and practice-squad rules, 2013:** 53-man active roster; 46 dress on game day (7 inactive), a rule dating to 2011; practice squad capped at 8 players (this did NOT expand to 10 until August 2014 — do not apply the 10-player rule to any 2013 date). Confirmed via multiple team sites, NFL.com, the Washington Post, and the NFLPA's own press release on the 2014 change.

## A note on team-specific cap-space figures

Team-by-team "cap space available" figures reported for early 2013 (e.g., for Jacksonville or Chicago) turned out to be genuinely unstable across sources — they move by several million dollars depending on the exact date and which transactions (cuts, restructures, tag decisions) had already happened when a given outlet published. See `archive/2013_coaching_market_historical_comparator.md` for the historical research notes on later per-team figures; those figures are not part of the January 14 hiring snapshot. Do not treat any single-team cap-space dollar figure from this research as precise in the way the league-wide cap and tag tables above are — those are official, single, undisputed numbers; team cap space that offseason was a moving target reported inconsistently even by contemporaneous sources.
