# Library: 2013 NFL Draft Class — Pre-Selection Scouting Snapshot

**Status:** Research rebuild completed 2026-09-18 with a separate verification pass.  
**Hard cutoff:** **2013-04-25, before the first selection of the 2013 NFL Draft.**  
**Runtime purpose:** preserve what evaluators could reasonably have known and argued before the draft, not what later happened.

> **HARD QUARANTINE — NO HINDSIGHT:** This file contains no actual 2013 selection order, no team destinations, no post-draft transactions, no NFL statistics, no awards, no later injuries, no later reputation, and no bust/star framing. Nothing that happened after the draft began may be used to rank, grade, narrate, or secretly calibrate a prospect. A later database may not be used as a hidden answer key. If a claim cannot be supported from evidence available before the first selection, it does not belong here.

## 1. How to use this file

This is a **historical pre-draft information library**, not a list of true talent values.

- A board rank records one evaluator's view at a dated point in the process. It is evidence about contemporary perception, not objective truth.
- Disagreement is preserved. Do not average competing boards into a fake exact consensus grade.
- Measurables are combine or explicitly labeled pro-day/workout results available before the draft. DNP means the player did not perform that drill at the combine.
- The Steelers Depot combine tables are a contemporaneous live-results log and explicitly warn that their figures, especially early 40 times, should not all be treated as official. Where NFL.com published an official time for a major prospect, the NFL.com figure controls.
- Scheme projection matters. A prospect may rank differently as a 4-3 end, 3-4 outside linebacker, nose tackle, three-technique, slot receiver, boundary corner, etc.
- Medical, availability, and off-field matters appear only when they were publicly documented before the draft and materially affected contemporary evaluation. They are not converted into hidden character or durability scores.
- **Date gate:** during the simulation, information only becomes available when it existed in-world. January 2013 does not know February combine results, March pro-day results, or an April final board. By the morning of April 25, 2013, the full snapshot below is eligible knowledge.

## 2. What the class looked like before anyone was selected

The useful historical picture is not "weak class" or "strong class" as a single label. Contemporary evaluators saw an unusual distribution of strength.

1. **The top of the class was trench-heavy.** Mayock's final overall top 11 contained three offensive tackles (Eric Fisher, Luke Joeckel, Lane Johnson), two guards (Chance Warmack, Jonathan Cooper), three defensive front-seven edge players (Dion Jordan, Ezekiel Ansah, Barkevious Mingo), and three interior defensive linemen (Sharrif Floyd, Sheldon Richardson, Star Lotulelei). [MAYOCK-100]
2. **There was no settled elite quarterback.** Geno Smith was the most common QB1 on major late boards, but the ordering behind him varied widely and even Smith was not universally QB1. Josh Norris ranked Tyler Wilson first at the position; Mayock finished Smith first, EJ Manuel second, Matt Barkley third, Ryan Nassib fourth, and tied Tyler Bray/Mike Glennon fifth. [MAYOCK-POS] [NORRIS-QB]
3. **Offensive tackle had the cleanest top-end agreement, but not a unanimous OT1.** Joeckel and Fisher were both treated as top-of-board players. Mayock ultimately put Fisher first overall; Brandt put Joeckel first overall and Fisher fifth. Lane Johnson was the consensus third member of the top tackle tier and was viewed as the premier athlete of the three. [MAYOCK-100] [BRANDT-125] [MCGINN-OL]
4. **Interior offensive line was unusually prominent.** Warmack and Cooper were not merely good guards; both sat in the top eight of Mayock's entire board. Their contrast was central to the discussion: Warmack as the more power-oriented finisher, Cooper as the more mobile space player. [MAYOCK-100] [MCGINN-OL]
5. **The defensive front had depth and role diversity.** Floyd, Richardson and Lotulelei offered different interior profiles, while Jordan, Ansah, Mingo, Werner, Carradine, Datone Jones and others created real scheme-dependent ordering on the edge. [MAYOCK-POS] [SI-DEF] [NORRIS-DL]
6. **Wide receiver offered several different archetypes without one universal WR1.** Tavon Austin's explosive multi-alignment value pushed him to Mayock's WR1 and No. 12 overall by the final week. Cordarrelle Patterson carried rare open-field traits but a much less developed route profile. DeAndre Hopkins, Keenan Allen, Robert Woods and Justin Hunter appealed for very different reasons. [MAYOCK-POS] [MAYOCK-100] [NORRIS-WR] [MCGINN-WR]
7. **Cornerback was deep and the top changed late.** Dee Milliner had been the near-consensus top corner for much of the process. Mayock moved D.J. Hayden to CB1 on April 18 after Hayden's medical return and workout, while other major evaluators still had Milliner or Xavier Rhodes higher. That disagreement is part of the class, not noise to erase. [MAYOCK-POS] [BRANDT-125] [MCGINN-DB]
8. **Running back lacked a universally dominant top prospect.** Eddie Lacy was the most common RB1, but Giovani Bernard, Montee Ball, Andre Ellington, Le'Veon Bell and Christine Michael produced very different role projections. [MAYOCK-POS] [NORRIS-RB]
9. **Tight end had a clear top name but disagreement behind him.** Tyler Eifert was broadly TE1. Ertz, Kelce, Escobar and McDonald changed order depending on how an evaluator valued inline blocking, movement skill and receiving projection. [MAYOCK-POS] [NORRIS-TE]

## 3. Quarterbacks

### Consensus shape

There was no Andrew Luck/Robert Griffin-style top quarterback consensus in 2013. Geno Smith was the **most common final QB1** among major national boards used here, but evaluators disagreed sharply about both the ordering and the overall value of the group. Mayock's final order was Smith, EJ Manuel, Matt Barkley, Ryan Nassib, then Tyler Bray/Mike Glennon tied. Josh Norris was a major dissenting voice, ranking Tyler Wilson first, Smith second, Zac Dysert third, Glennon fourth, Nassib fifth, Barkley sixth and Manuel seventh. [MAYOCK-POS] [NORRIS-QB]

**Simulation use:** do not create one secret "QB order." A quarterback-needy club should weigh scheme, required processing structure, movement needs, tolerance for mechanical development and risk. The real 2013 disagreement was substantial.

## 4. Running backs

### Consensus shape

Eddie Lacy was the clearest common RB1, but this was not viewed as a class with one uncontested blue-chip back. Mayock ranked Lacy #18 overall, then Montee Ball #52, Giovani Bernard #63, Andre Ellington #75, Christine Michael #87 and Le'Veon Bell #94. Josh Norris preferred Bernard second and Ellington third, while Mayock had Ball second. [MAYOCK-100] [NORRIS-RB]
