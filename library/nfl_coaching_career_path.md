# Library: the NFL coaching career path, from position coach to head coach (2013 to 2015 rules)

**Research status:** research pass 1 completed October 17, 2026; a separate skeptical verification pass completed October 18, 2026. Of the 80 claims the first pass made, the verification pass confirmed 40, corrected 11 and left 29 unverified (section 10). This file is research only. It changes no canon, roster, staff, contract, calendar or state fact, and it decides nothing for any club or coach.

**Information window:** real-world rates and practice come from sources published before the January 2014 hiring cycle, or from studies whose data end before it; studies whose windows run later are marked *Later* and are not applied. Rules are those of the January 2014 cycle as verified in [the 2014 hiring rules](2014_coaching_hiring_and_anti_tampering_rules.md), checked against the dated January 2015 and January 2016 league windows. Rules adopted after the 2014 season are listed in section 5.4 only so that they are not applied.

**Purpose:** a reference for any coaching character who is, or is going to be, a position coach working toward a head-coaching job: the rungs, how long they take, how a hiring year runs, where careers fail, and which of those moments must stop for the user. Alex Stone's own yearly cycle and contract milestones are in [the season cycle and career milestones guide](../docs/season_cycle_milestones.md).

**Labels**

- **Confirmed:** two or more independent publishers agree, and at least one is from the era (2013 to 2015) or states that the rule ran unchanged into it.
- **Pattern:** documented in more than one place but by one publisher only, or by later sources only, or with no era text seen.
- **Unverified:** one route only, an undated source, an inference, or no source found.
- **Later:** dated after the 2014 season; listed so that it is not applied.
- **Repo:** taken from a sourced repository file, whose own label is kept.
- **Derived:** arithmetic or a reading of repository records made here, not a sourced fact.

**Access limits (read before relying on this file):** both passes read most outside sources through search-result text (title, address and extracted passage). The verification pass opened six pages: five on nfl.com and one on Wikipedia. Fetches to footballperspective.com, the University of Iowa, Newswise, 49ers.com, CBS Sports and the Arizona State research hosts failed by DNS or were refused by the network proxy, and espn.com returned empty pages. **No block was routed around**: no archive, mirror or alternate host was tried. A session with page access should open the Football Perspective and University of Iowa pages first and upgrade or correct the labels.

**Hindsight boundary (read first):** no real coach's hire, firing, extension or job move in the January 2014 cycle or later is recorded here as a fact. Other clubs' coaching and front-office changes stay branch-generated (AGENTS.md, historical league rails, rule 2). Where a useful source names such a coach, it is cited for general practice only, or withheld. Three items found by the research passes were withheld for this reason: a January 2014 report of one club's internal decision on assistant contract lengths, a dollar total for fired coaches that appears to include a January 2014 dismissal, and a report on a January 2014 head-coach contract. Aggregate studies are used only for the general shape of a career; none is an answer key for any branch decision.

## 1. The ladder

| Rung | What the job is in practice | Usual way off the rung | Label and source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry: college graduate assistant, NFL coaching intern or fellow | The usual way into coaching. The NFL's minority coaching fellowship began with Bill Walsh bringing minority coaches into the 49ers' 1987 training camp. In 2013 it was the Bill Walsh NFL Minority Coaching Fellowship, placing coaches with clubs for the offseason and camp. It is now the Bill Walsh NFL Diversity Coaching Fellowship; the year of that rename was not found | A full-time staff job, usually quality control | 1987 origin and 2013 name: **Confirmed** (NFL.com, opened; NFL Football Operations; SWAC, July 26, 2013; UAlbany, August 12, 2013). A 49ers.com history dates a formal program to 1986. Placement length: **Pattern** |
| Quality control coach, or offensive or defensive assistant | The first NFL staff job for most coaches: breaking down film for the position coaches and coordinators, charting tendencies, preparing for games two or three weeks ahead | Assistant position coach or position coach | **Confirmed** (Wikipedia, opened, citing the New York Times, September 26, 2009, and the Denver Post, January 25, 2011; ESPN Washington blog) |
| Assistant position coach | The second coach in a room, such as assistant offensive line | Position coach | Title is ordinary staff structure. Its career weight: **Unverified** (no source) |
| Position coach | Owns one room: its teaching, meetings and the evaluation of its players | Coordinator, a coordinator-level title (passing-game or run-game coordinator), or occasionally head coach directly | Ordinary structure. Direct hires: section 3 |
| Coordinator, and titles such as assistant head coach | Runs one side of the ball or the kicking game; may or may not call plays | Head coach | In the 2013 to 2015 rules every coordinator, co-coordinator, play-caller and "assistant head coach" title was an assistant title with no protected status: **Confirmed** (Repo: rules file T1, T3, T5) |
| Head coach | The only protected promotion: once an assistant's own season was over, his club could not block a head-coach interview | A second head-coaching job, a coordinator job, or out of coaching | **Confirmed** (Repo: rules file T2) |
| College route | College position coach, then college coordinator, then college head coach, then NFL head coach | NFL head coach | College head coaches were about 10 percent of NFL head-coach hires since 2000 and since 2005: **Pattern** (Football Perspective, December 2013, one publisher) |

## 2. Timelines

**Years per rung.** No study giving the usual number of years as a quality control coach, an assistant position coach, a position coach or a coordinator was found in either pass (**Unverified: not found**). A simulation that needs those durations must label them as its own modelling choice. One coach's résumé is not a base rate.

**Age at a first head-coaching job.** No era figure is confirmed. Two figures seen are not used: a "mean age about 49 with 13 years of professional coaching experience", linked in one search summary to coverage of a 2013 University of Iowa study but not found on a second search, and an average of 45.3 for 21 coaches, which describes the head coaches then in office who were first-time hires, not one cycle's hires (both **Unverified**). Later studies with their own averages are **Later** and not applied.

**The one documented path in this repository** is Stone's ([Document 3 §1.4](../foundation/03_Head_Coach_Organization_and_Authority_Canon.md), user-confirmed). Ages are **Derived** from his confirmed date of birth, September 21, 1963.

## 3. Where head coaches came from (data before the January 2014 cycle)

| Window | Breakdown | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| 1990 to 2013 hires | 159 head-coach hires; 12 went to interim coaches made permanent. The rest were grouped by the job held the season before: former NFL head coach, offensive coordinator, defensive coordinator, college head coach, quarterbacks coach, offensive line coach, other. In 21 of 54 former-head-coach hires the coach had not been in the NFL the season before | **Pattern** (Football Perspective, December 2013) |
| Hires since 2000, through the January 2013 cycle | Former head coaches 32.2 percent; offensive coordinators 23.0; defensive coordinators 23.0; college head coaches about 10 | **Pattern** (same publisher) |
| Hires since 2005 | Offensive coordinators 28.3 percent; former head coaches 26.4; defensive coordinators 18.9 | **Pattern** (same publisher) |
| January 2013 candidate field (five searches, 40 candidate pairings) | Sitting coordinators were 72.5 percent of pairings and 50 percent of the eight hires. One position coach appeared as a candidate (2.5 percent), and he was a former NFL head coach | **Repo** (rules file §7.1 Confirmed; §7.3 Pattern) |
| January 2013 coordinator openings at the 24 clubs that kept their head coach | 17 openings at 14 clubs: 7 filled by lateral or displaced coordinators, 4 by in-house promotion, 3 by college coordinators, 2 by sitting position coaches from other clubs and 1 by a displaced position coach. 4 of the 17 hires came on or after February 1, including both sitting position coaches | **Repo** (that file's own counts over its Confirmed entries: [2013 retained-club coordinator turnover](../career/2014/01_Early_Offseason/staff_changes/2013_retained_club_coordinator_turnover.md)) |
| Direct hires from a position-coach job | Head coaches were hired straight from a quarterbacks-coach job before 2014 (a 1999 hire and a 2008 hire) | **Pattern** (Washington Times, October 4, 2008, one era publisher) |

**Reading for a position coach (Derived):** the ordinary route to head coach ran through a coordinator job. A direct jump from position coach happened, mostly from quarterbacks coach, but rarely. The usual way a position coach moved up was a coordinator title at another club, granted only with his employer's consent, or an in-house promotion when his own coordinator left.

## 5. The hiring cycle in era

### 5.1 The annual sequence

| Stage | Rule | Label |
|---|---|---|
| End of a club's season | Its assistants become requestable when its season ends. For a non-playoff club that is its Week 17 game. Black Monday, the Monday after Week 17, is the customary day for dismissals, not the legal start | Rule: **Confirmed** (rules file W2, §9 item 1). Custom: **Confirmed** (NFL.com and Fox 17, December 31, 2012) |
| Head-coach requests | Cannot be refused once the assistant's season is over. Whether the duty runs only through March 1 is unclear | Refusal bar: **Confirmed** (T2). March 1 limit: **Unverified** (T6) |
| Every other request | Lateral, including position coach to coordinator. The employer club may grant or refuse, finally, with no appeal | **Confirmed** (T3) |
| Playoff clubs | Bye clubs: head-coach interviews only through the Wild Card games. Wild Card winners: only between the Wild Card and Divisional games. No first interview after the Divisional weekend. Super Bowl assistants: one second interview, no later than the Sunday before the game. One interview per inquiring club while the club is alive | **Confirmed** (W4 to W9) |
| The hire | No contract agreed, signed or announced while the employer club is still playing. An agreement in principle with a coach of a live club is not a hire | **Confirmed** (X1, X2) |
| Head coaches under contract | Unavailable during their season; afterwards only with their club's consent. Draft picks or cash may change hands for a head coach only | **Pattern** (H1 to H3) |
| Compensation for losing an assistant | None | **Confirmed** (C2) |
| Coaches who resign under contract | Not free without their club's consent | **Pattern** (E5) |
| Expiring assistant contracts | The employer may keep exclusive rights through the second Tuesday after its last game | **Unverified** (E4); if used, the rules file §6 gives the dates |
| Tampering | Contact outside these rules is a matter for Commissioner discipline | Penalty wording for 2013: **Unverified** (D1) |
| Development programs | The league's Career Development Symposium for aspiring head coaches and general managers returned May 6 to 8, 2013 at the Wharton School after running 1998 to 2008; the 2015 edition was March 19 to 21 in Phoenix | **Confirmed** (NFL.com, opened; Ravens.com; Texans.com; NFL Football Operations) |

### 5.3 Moves below head coach

- A position coach's move to a coordinator job at another club is lateral and needs his employer's consent (T3). The first protection for coordinator interviews dates from May 2020 and does not apply.
- In the January 2013 cycle, both sitting position coaches hired as coordinators by clubs that kept their head coach were hired after February 1 (section 3). A position coach's best window often opens after the head-coach carousel settles.
- Jacksonville's standing default for requests about its own assistants grants a head-coach request, grants a position coach's move to a coordinator job and refuses same-title moves. The user may change it ([carousel method](../career/2014/01_Early_Offseason/staff_changes/carousel_method.json), stage 4).
- Rates at which employers grant lateral requests, and how often assistants are asked about, were not found (rules file §7.5). Any probability for them is a labelled modelling choice.

## 8. Stopping points for a career that starts as a position coach

The repository's rules decide when a career event pauses for the user and when it resolves on its own: Document 3 §10.2 and §10.3, its Authority Map, the AGENTS.md hiring-search and staff-building tasks, and the protagonist-blind rule (Document 1 §9.1). This table applies them to a protagonist who is a position coach. It is a **Derived** design reading, not an outside fact. Every probability or decision behind an automatic row reads only the job, the coach's record and contract and the clubs' situations, never which coach is the protagonist.

| Event | Who decides | Stops for the user? | Resolves on its own |
|---|---|---|---|
| His own room: teaching plan, corrections, player evaluations and recommendations | He does, within his head coach's plan | **Yes**, through his weekly and phase plans | Practice and game results, through the normal resolution rules |
| Depth chart, play-calling and staff changes above him | His head coach and coordinator | No | Their decisions, resolved from their own records and the football evidence |
| His head coach is fired | The club | No for the firing | The club's search; the new head coach's staff choices, from the new coach's needs |
| The new head coach keeps, reassigns or releases him | The new head coach, within his contract | **Yes** only for his response: stay in a reassigned role, or seek another job once free | His contract's guarantee and offset |
| Another club asks to interview him for a coordinator job | His club (lateral request) | **Yes**, if his club grants it: whether to interview | His club's grant or refusal |
| Another club asks to interview him for a head-coach job | The rules: his club cannot refuse once its season is over | **Yes**: whether to interview | Permission and the playoff windows |
| An offer at any rung, from his club or another | He does | **Yes, always**, unless a written instruction holds an exact acceptance or walk-away rule | Nothing. A deal with a coach whose club is still playing is not a hire until it is eliminated |
| His own club promotes from within | The head coach offers; he decides | **Yes**: accept or decline | The vacancy that created the chance |
| His contract expires | He does | **Yes**: re-sign, or become a free coach | The expiry date (the second-Tuesday rule is Unverified) |
| An interim head-coach appointment during a season | The club appoints; he decides | **Yes**: accept the role | The vacancy |
| He is released or out of work | He does | **Yes**: a written job-search brief | The free-coach rules (any club, no permission) |
| He reaches coordinator, then head coach | His employer | Each new job brings its own authority map and its own stops | The same rules at every assistant rung; head-coach requests stay unrefusable after each season |

**The stopping points in order, for a position coach aiming at head coach (Derived):**

1. Each weekly and phase plan for his own room.
2. The end of each season: his exit review with his head coach, and his own contract status.
3. His head coach's job decision, if the club changes coaches, and his response to the new staff.
4. Any outside request: coordinator (if his club grants it) or head coach (always permitted after his season).
5. Every offer, at every rung, including an in-house promotion or an interim appointment.
6. Contract expiry: re-sign or become free.
7. On reaching coordinator: the same points again. Most head-coach candidates were sitting coordinators (section 3), and his play-calling role, if any, becomes part of every offer he weighs.

A position-coach protagonist needs an authority map unlike Document 3's, in which the head coach, not the protagonist, holds the staff, depth-chart and play-calling rows, and an employer evaluation frozen before his own case is read. That is a future build question, not part of the current career.
